Words for cannabis rise and fall with the times. The [Google Books Ngram Viewer](https://books.google.com/ngrams/) counts how often each word appears in the books Google has scanned, as a share of all words printed each year; this site follows the names of the plant and the drug in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian and Russian since 1800.[@1] The curves can be read beside the atlas entries on [marijuana](/culture/language/marijuana/), [Indian hemp](/culture/language/indian-hemp/) and [hashish](/culture/language/hashish/), which writers tried at the [Club des Hashischins](/culture/traditions/club-des-hashischins/) in 1840s Paris.

Books are counted by language, not by country, and the scanned books are not a random sample of what was printed.[@1] "Hemp" also means rope and cloth, so its curve is mostly about the crop. The atlas's [language](/culture/language/) section tells the story behind each word, from [ganja](/culture/language/ganja/) to [420](/culture/language/420/).
