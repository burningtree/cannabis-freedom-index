How rich a country is sets what it can spend on police, courts or a regulated market. The [World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD) measures income as everything a country produces in a year per person, at prices that make a dollar buy the same everywhere.[@1] In 2025 [Singapore](/country/sg/), [Ireland](/country/ie/) and [Luxembourg](/country/lu/) are richest, at over $125,000 a person, and [Burundi](/country/bi/), the [Central African Republic](/country/cf/) and [Somalia](/country/so/) poorest, near $1,000; the median is about $17,000.

The chart of [what goes with it](/charts/#what-goes-with-it) sets income and other [development indicators](/datasets/development/) beside each score. Income does not decide the law: Singapore is rich and among the harshest, while [Uruguay](/country/uy/), far poorer, was the [first country to legalise, in 2013](/history/uruguay-legalisation-2013/). Where cannabis is a cash crop for poor farmers, each profile's [black market](/context/black-market/) section says so.
