How free people are from torture, political killings and forced labour, and how far they may move, worship, speak and associate, is estimated every year by the country experts of the [V-Dem Institute](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/civil-liberties-index-vdem), from 0 to 1; Our World in Data publishes it as its human rights index.[@1] In 2025 [Estonia](/country/ee/), [Sweden](/country/se/) and [Denmark](/country/dk/) score highest, at 0.96, and [North Korea](/country/kp/), [Afghanistan](/country/af/) and [Eritrea](/country/er/) lowest.

The series runs back to 1900, so a profile can show how a country's liberties rose or fell beside the history of its cannabis laws.[@1] A country can respect civil liberties and still treat cannabis as a crime: Sweden is among the highest here, and its [drug strategy](/history/sweden-drug-strategy-renewed-2006/) aims at a drug-free society. Compare the [Human Freedom Index](/datasets/hfi/) and each profile's [beyond the penalty](/context/consequences/) section.
