Cannabis is also simply a plant, wild in some places and grown in many more. The [Global Biodiversity Information Facility](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?taxon_key=2984538) gathers every record of it shared by herbaria, museums, botanical surveys and citizen scientists, by the country where it was found.[@1] Records exist for 134 countries; the most come from [Russia](/country/ru/), the [United States](/country/us/) and [Sweden](/country/se/), and the oldest herbarium sheets go back centuries.

The numbers say as much about who records plants as about where cannabis grows: recent records are mostly photographs from apps such as iNaturalist.[@1] For the plant's local varieties and the people who keep them, see each profile's [genetics and heritage](/context/genetics/) section and the atlas entry on [landraces](/culture/language/landrace/); for the crop grown legally, see [hemp grown](/datasets/hemp/).
