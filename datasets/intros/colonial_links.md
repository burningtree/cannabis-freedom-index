Who once ruled a country can still be read in its laws: the British Empire wrote the name [Indian hemp](/culture/language/indian-hemp/) into the laws of its colonies, and many keep it today. This list lets the scores be compared by former ruler. [CEPII](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=6) records which countries ruled each country as a colony for a long time.[@1] Of the 204 places here, 55 were once ruled by the United Kingdom, 26 by France and 18 by Spain; 31 have no long colonial ruler recorded.

The [empire chart](/charts/#empire) compares the scores of former colonies by their old ruler. Short or light rule is listed apart, and the data was compiled for trade studies, not for law.[@1] Each profile's history shows how its first laws arrived, and the atlas tells the story of [Russell Pasha](/culture/people/russell-pasha/), the British commandant of the Cairo police who told the League of Nations that hashish was a minor vice.
