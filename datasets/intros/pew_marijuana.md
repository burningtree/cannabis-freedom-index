Laws follow what people believe, and in 2025 the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2026/03/05/in-25-country-survey-americans-especially-likely-to-view-fellow-citizens-as-morally-bad/) asked adults in 25 countries whether using marijuana is morally acceptable, morally unacceptable, or not a moral issue.[@1] The share who call it morally wrong runs from 91% in [Indonesia](/country/id/), 83% in [Nigeria](/country/ng/) and 82% in [Türkiye](/country/tr/) down to 19% in [Canada](/country/ca/), 23% in the [United States](/country/us/) and 24% in [Australia](/country/au/). The median country sits near half.

The three countries at the top treat cannabis as a crime, Indonesia with death still possible for aggravated sale, while Canada, at the bottom, has allowed it since its [Cannabis Act of 2018](/history/canada-cannabis-act-2018/). The survey cannot say which came first, the law or the belief.
