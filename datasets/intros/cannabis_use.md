How many people use cannabis is the first thing most readers ask, and the [UN Office on Drugs and Crime](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/data-and-analysis/world-drug-report-2026-annex.html) collects the answer from governments' national surveys: the share of people who used cannabis in the past year.[@1] This site applies each share to a population of the same ages, which gives the [number of users](/map/users/) on the map and the [chart of use](/charts/#use). All 220 places have a figure, and 93 have more than one survey, so many profiles can show use over time.

The surveys are not made to be compared. They ask different age groups, in different years, and some include children, so a high share may only mean a younger or a more recent survey.[@1] Where a country has no survey, a regional median stands in, and it is marked as an estimate. For how people actually get cannabis in each country, see the [recreational use](/context/recreational/) and [black market](/context/black-market/) sections; for schoolchildren, see [use at 15 and 16](/datasets/youth-use/).
