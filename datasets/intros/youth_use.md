Use among 15- and 16-year-olds is the one cannabis figure built for comparison: the same age group, mostly asked in school surveys such as Europe's [ESPAD](/datasets/espad-lifetime/), and reported by governments to [UNODC](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/data-and-analysis/world-drug-report-2026-annex.html).[@1] It covers 51 countries, and the median is 8.5% who used cannabis in the past year. The highest shares include [Liechtenstein](/country/li/) and the [United States](/country/us/), at about a quarter; among the lowest are [Mexico](/country/mx/) and [Egypt](/country/eg/), under 2%.

The price of comparability is coverage. Countries that survey other age bands, Germany, France and Spain among them, are left out, and survey years run from 2003 to 2024.[@1] A survey of pupils also misses young people who are not in school. Read it beside [how easy students say cannabis is to get](/datasets/espad-availability/) and beside the adult figures in [cannabis use](/datasets/cannabis-use/).
