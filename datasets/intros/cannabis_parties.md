In a few countries, people who want cannabis legal have founded parties of their own. [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/), the open database behind Wikipedia, records parties that campaign on cannabis, with their country and the years they were founded and dissolved.[@1] It lists 22 parties in 9 countries, from [Australia](/country/au/), with Legalise Cannabis Australia and its state parties, and [New Zealand](/country/nz/), with the Aotearoa Legalise Cannabis Party, to [South Africa](/country/za/), with the Dagga Party, and the [United States](/country/us/).

Wikidata is written by volunteers and far from complete: parties whose name does not mention the plant, such as Israel's Ale Yarok, are found only if their ideology is recorded.[@1] The [parties chart](/charts/#parties) shows when they were founded. In South Africa change came instead from the [Constitutional Court in 2018](/history/south-africa-constitutional-court-2018/); the atlas profiles campaigners such as [Keith Stroup](/culture/people/keith-stroup/) and [Marc Emery](/culture/people/marc-emery/).
