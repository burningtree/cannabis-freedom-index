A law is only as strong as the state that applies it. The [World Bank's governance indicators](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators) estimate how well a state works: the quality of its public services and civil service, and how far it can carry out the policies it makes.[@1] On a scale of 0 to 100, [Singapore](/country/sg/), [Japan](/country/jp/) and [Luxembourg](/country/lu/) score highest in 2025, and [South Sudan](/country/ss/), [Haiti](/country/ht/) and [Sudan](/country/sd/) lowest; the median is about 52.

It measures capacity, not freedom: an effective state can enforce a harsh law thoroughly, as Singapore does, or run a [legal market](/context/production/) well.[@1] The [chart of state capacity](/charts/#state-capacity) sets it beside each score. A weak state may have a strict law on paper and a [black market](/context/black-market/) in practice; see also the [corruption perceptions index](/datasets/corruption/).
