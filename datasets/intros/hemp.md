Hemp is cannabis grown for fibre and seed, with little THC, and the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL) has recorded how much of it each country harvests every year since 1961.[@1] In 2024 the largest areas were in [France](/country/fr/), [North Korea](/country/kp/) and [China](/country/cn/), each about 20,000 hectares; 39 countries have a figure. The crop was once grown widely, as the atlas shows in [Italian hemp rope-making](/culture/traditions/italian-hemp-rope-makers/) and [Hmong hemp cloth](/culture/traditions/hmong-hemp-cloth/).

The records are patchy: some countries report only fibre or only seed, some only the area, and many stop for years.[@1] Where fibre and seed overlap, the larger area is shown. The [hemp chart](/charts/#hemp) follows the crop since 1961, and each profile's [hemp and CBD](/context/hemp/) section gives the THC limit that separates hemp from the drug, such as the 0.3% the [United States adopted in 2018](/history/us-farm-bill-2018/).
