How often the police record someone for using or carrying cannabis is the plainest measure of how a law is enforced. The [European Union Drugs Agency](https://www.euda.europa.eu/data/stats2026/dlo_en#displayTable:DLO-03-2-0) publishes these offences for cannabis alone, separate from dealing and growing for sale.[@1] Per 100,000 people, [Spain](/country/es/) records the most, about 596 in 2024, followed by [Belgium](/country/be/) and [Austria](/country/at/); [Malta](/country/mt/), which [legalised in 2021](/history/malta-legalisation-2021/), records almost none. In Spain, having or using drugs in public is punished with a fine of at least €601 under its [public safety law of 2015](/history/spain-public-safety-law-2015/), not as a crime.

An offence is not a conviction, and countries count offences or offenders and define them differently, so a country is best compared with itself over time.[@1] The [chart of who is punished](/charts/#punished) follows the series, and each profile's [arrests and prisoners](/context/arrests/) section explains what an offence leads to there. Outside Europe, the nearest figure is UNODC's [police contact for drug possession](/datasets/police-possession/).
