Legalisation is meant to move buyers from illegal sellers to legal shops, and Canada measures whether it has. Each year [Health Canada](https://health-infobase.canada.ca/cannabis/) asks people who used cannabis where they usually got it: a legal store or website, friends and family, an illegal seller, their own plants, or elsewhere.[@1] The 2018 survey came before the first legal stores opened under the [Cannabis Act](/history/canada-cannabis-act-2018/), so it has no legal figure. In 2019 37% bought legally; by 2023 it was 73%, while the share buying from illegal sellers fell from 28% in 2018 to 3%.

The [sourcing chart](/charts/#sourcing) draws the shift year by year. The same questions in other countries would show how much of the market a law has reached; for that, see each profile's [recreational use](/context/recreational/) and [black market](/context/black-market/) sections, and for what the legal trade pays to the state, [government revenue from cannabis in Canada](/datasets/canada-revenue/).
