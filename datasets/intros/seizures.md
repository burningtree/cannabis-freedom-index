Seizures show where cannabis moves and where the authorities stop it. [UNODC](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/data-and-analysis/world-drug-report-2026-annex.html) collects the kilograms of herb and resin each government seizes in a year; this site covers 167 countries from 2015 to 2024.[@1] The largest totals come from [Namibia](/country/na/), [Tanzania](/country/tz/) and [Paraguay](/country/py/), each over a million kilograms in 2024, figures that may include plants destroyed in the field.

A seizure is not a measure of use: a transit country can seize far more than its people smoke, and a large total may mean a strong police force as much as a large trade.[@1] Plants, oil and seeds are left out. Each profile's [black market](/context/black-market/) section explains where the country's cannabis comes from, and the history records large finds such as the [transit seizure at Singapore's Pasir Panjang in 2026](/history/singapore-pasir-panjang-transit-seizure-2026/).
