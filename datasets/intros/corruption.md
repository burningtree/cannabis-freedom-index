Where officials can be paid, a drug law is enforced unevenly. [Transparency International](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi) scores how corrupt experts and business people judge each country's public sector, from 0, highly corrupt, to 100, very clean, from up to 13 surveys and assessments.[@1] In 2024 [Denmark](/country/dk/), [Finland](/country/fi/) and [Singapore](/country/sg/) are seen as cleanest, and [South Sudan](/country/ss/), [Somalia](/country/so/) and [Venezuela](/country/ve/) as most corrupt; the median of 180 countries is about 40.

It says nothing about cannabis itself, but much about how a law is likely to be enforced: by the book, or by whoever pays.[@1] The [corruption chart](/charts/#corruption) sets it beside each score. Each profile's [black market](/context/black-market/) and [beyond the penalty](/context/consequences/) sections tell what enforcement looks like in practice; compare [government effectiveness](/datasets/government-effectiveness/).
