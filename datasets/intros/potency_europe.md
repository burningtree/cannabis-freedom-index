Cannabis has grown stronger, and potency is one of the arguments in every debate about the law. The [European Union Drugs Agency](https://www.euda.europa.eu/data/stats2026/ppp_en) collects the mean THC content of herb and resin tested at retail level, mostly from seized samples.[@1] Across 28 countries the median is about 11%; the strongest recent herb or resin is reported from [Finland](/country/fi/), [Spain](/country/es/) and [Estonia](/country/ee/), at 15–17%.

Samples and laboratory methods differ between countries, so a country is best compared with itself over time.[@1] The [chart of THC per euro](/charts/#thc-per-euro) sets potency against the [street price](/datasets/prices-europe/). Where a country sets a limit for legal products, it is in the profile's [hemp and CBD](/context/hemp/) section; the plant's chemistry is explained in the atlas under [CBD](/culture/language/cbd/) and [indica and sativa](/culture/language/indica-and-sativa/).
