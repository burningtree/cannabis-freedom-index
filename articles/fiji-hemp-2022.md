Fiji’s parliament approved and signed a hemp exemption on 29 July 2022, setting a THC limit of no more than 1%. The amendment excluded qualifying hemp and plant parts derived from it from the drug schedule’s references to the cannabis plant. The government presented hemp as a potential export crop for farmers.

## The budget linked hemp to farming and exports

In the budget address earlier that month, the government described industrial hemp as an economic opportunity and proposed changes to the drug and customs rules. It planned to allow imports for cultivation and export once independent laboratory testing confirmed the required THC content.

The government also proposed working with the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime on growing trials at Fiji National University’s agriculture school. Its stated plan was to obtain seeds from Canada and eventually distribute them to farmers, including in Kadavu.

Those were the agricultural ambitions behind the amendment. They put testing and a supply of suitable seeds at the centre of the proposed industry.

## The exemption depended on THC concentration

The [2004 drug law](/history/fiji-drug-offences-law-2004/) listed cannabis plant material, seed, fruit and oil, alongside other controlled forms. The amendment changed how the schedule referred to the plant and its parts.

Its wording covered hemp, or plant parts derived from hemp, with a THC concentration that did not exceed 1%. This made the legal distinction depend on both the material involved and its concentration.

The budget speech had described a level below 1%. The enacted exemption included the boundary itself: no more than 1%.

## A person claiming hemp had to prove it

The amendment also introduced an evidentiary rule for prosecutions. If someone claimed that seized material was hemp within the limit, the law presumed it was cannabis exceeding 1% until that person proved otherwise.

For a farmer or trader facing a prosecution, calling a crop hemp therefore required evidence that it met the legal test. The government’s emphasis on laboratory testing was directly relevant to that distinction.

The signed amendment provided for commencement through a separate ministerial notice in the Gazette, Fiji’s official publication for legal notices.

## Hemp entered a wider cannabis policy debate

The exemption concerned an industrial crop, while unauthorised cannabis possession and cultivation continued to fall under the drug law. Courts were also developing [new sentencing guidelines for growers](/history/fiji-cultivation-guidelines-2023/).

By [December 2024](/history/fiji-medicinal-cannabis-bill-promised-2024/), the government was promising legislation for a medicinal cannabis industry. Hemp had given [Fiji](/country/fj/) an earlier route to distinguish a low-THC agricultural crop within its cannabis controls.
