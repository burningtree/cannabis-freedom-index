On 13 August 2019 an amendment to New Zealand's Misuse of Drugs Act came into effect. It did not change what is illegal. It changed what the police are supposed to weigh before prosecuting someone for possessing or using a drug.

## Three changes

The health ministry's review of the amendment summarises it in three parts.

- It affirmed that police may use discretion over whether to prosecute for personal possession and use, and required them to consider, among other things, whether a health-based response would be more beneficial to the public interest.
- It allowed temporary orders classifying new and potentially harmful substances.
- It classified two synthetic cannabinoids, AMB-FUBINACA and 5F-ADB, under the Act; the first had been detected in most of a surge of deaths in 2017 and 2018.

The first is the one that touches cannabis. The discretion applies to possession and use of every drug.

## What the police did with it

On the day the amendment took effect the police updated their instructions and opened a referral pathway: an officer can offer to refer a person to the national alcohol and drug helpline, and if the person agrees they receive text messages over a week inviting them to make contact.

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Possession and use cases closed without court, August 2019 | 66% |
| The same, first quarter of 2021 | 82% |
| First offences dealt with out of court | 90% |
| Referrals to the helpline, August 2019 to May 2021 | 959 |
| People who then made contact | 147 |

The review is careful about cause. It says it is not clear how much of the rise in out-of-court outcomes can be attributed to the amendment, and it notes that referrals are recorded only when the person consents, so the number of offers declined is unknown.

## Discretion runs both ways

Because the amendment tells the police what to consider and not what to do, the outcome depends on practice. Justice ministry figures reported in 2026 showed 3,438 people charged with possessing cannabis in 2025, against 2,287 in 2023, the highest number since the change.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [New Zealand](/country/nz/) from 13 to 18 with the [2019 amendment](/history/new-zealand-police-discretion-2019/). Possession rises from 1 to 2 and enforcement from 3 to 4: possession remained an offence, but a warning or referral became the ordinary outcome of a first case.
