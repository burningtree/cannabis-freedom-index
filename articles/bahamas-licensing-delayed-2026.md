The Bahamas enacted a framework for medical cannabis in 2024. By July 2026,
however, the application system needed to put that framework into practice had
still not opened.

## A target slips

The Bahamas Cannabis Authority had aimed to begin accepting licence applications
at the end of June or in early July. Its chairman, Dr Lynwood Brown, told *The
Tribune* that unresolved internal matters had taken longer than expected.

Brown described the delay as a cautious regulatory choice: the government and the
Ministry of Health and Wellness wanted the system properly prepared before inviting
applications. He did not give a replacement opening date.

The delay matters because the [2024 Cannabis Act](/history/bahamas-cannabis-act-2024/)
cannot create access by itself. The country's current profile records that the
Act's medical-use and licensing provisions have not been brought into force.

## How applications are meant to work

When the online portal opens, an applicant will first choose a licence category.
The options described by Brown include personal medical use and commercial roles
such as cultivation, manufacturing and dispensing.

Applicants will upload business licences, information about principals and other
supporting documents. Compliance officers will check each application and ask for
missing information. Police and other relevant agencies will then help vet it
before the Cannabis Authority's board approves it, asks for more information or
refuses it.

## Pharmacies need another licence

Ordinary pharmacy status will not be enough to dispense cannabis. A pharmacy will
need a separate licence from the Cannabis Authority and a dedicated dispensary area
physically separated from its existing operation.

Every dispensary must also be overseen by a licensed, registered pharmacist. Brown
said the separation is intended to maintain controls and limit unnecessary exposure
of minors visiting pharmacies to cannabis products.

## A framework without supply

The Authority exists, and the law anticipates cultivation, manufacturing,
dispensing and medical use. Yet no application can pass through the system while
the portal is closed. For patients, the promised legal route therefore remains a
plan rather than an available service.
