Venezuela's 1993 drug-law reform specified twenty-gram cannabis limits and reduced the separate criminal possession sentence from six to ten years to four to six. Consumers holding their own dose continued under treatment and supervision, a route already present in the 1984 law.[@1,2]

## Treatment already had a legal basis

The [1984 law](/history/venezuela-drug-law-1984/) had covered consumers and people holding a dose for immediate personal use. A judge assessed that dose with forensic reports. Its security measures included rehabilitation, detoxification, social readaptation, supervision and expulsion of non-resident foreign consumers.[@2]

The 1993 reform retained that distinction between consumer cases and criminal possession. Its personal-dose article expressly referred to up to twenty grams of cannabis and two grams of cocaine, with expert reports informing the judge's decision.[@1]

## A separate possession offence remained

The law also used those quantities in the possession offence for purposes outside personal use and the other specified activities. That offence carried four to six years in prison. The explanatory memorandum says the earlier sentence had been six to ten years.[@1]

Lawmakers explained that judges had made serious errors applying the possession rule and had failed to develop consistent quantity limits. They introduced the amounts to provide greater legal certainty, while acknowledging that a skilful distributor might benefit when evidence established only possession.[@1]

The distinction depended on the facts of the case as well as the amount found. Personal use remained a separate route with treatment measures.[@1]

## Treatment could still mean living in a centre

The consumer provisions allowed admission to a rehabilitation or specialised therapy centre. They also listed detoxification, social readaptation, supervision and expulsion of a foreign consumer who was not resident.[@1]

The law described admission as making the drug-dependent person reside in an appropriate establishment. Detoxification could occur with or without admission. The treatment route therefore included both residential and non-residential interventions.[@1]

## Lawmakers described an implementation failure

The explanatory memorandum said the executive had not supplied the facilities and sufficient professional posts needed to apply the earlier treatment measures. It reported that this had produced criminalisation of consumers in practice; some judges declined to use the measures because institutions were unavailable.[@1]

That account explains the gap between a treatment provision on paper and what happened to users. The [2005 law](/history/venezuela-possession-penalties-2005/) later reduced the separate possession offence to one or two years. The [2010 law](/history/venezuela-drug-law-2010/) continued an individual assessment for consumer cases alongside criminal possession and trafficking offences.[@3,4]
