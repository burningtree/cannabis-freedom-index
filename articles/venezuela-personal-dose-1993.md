In 1993 Venezuela reformed its Organic Law on Narcotic and Psychotropic Substances. The reform kept possession for personal use inside the law but changed what follows from it: prison gave way to what the law calls "social security measures".

## The personal dose

Article 75 defines the amount.

| Substance | Personal dose |
|---|---|
| Cannabis sativa | up to 20 grams |
| Cocaine or its derivatives | up to 2 grams |

## What happens within the dose

A person found with no more than that is not sent to prison. Article 76 lists the measures that apply instead:

- internment in a rehabilitation or specialised therapy centre;
- cure or detoxification;
- social readaptation of the user;
- supervised release and follow-up;
- expulsion from the country, for a foreign user who is not resident.

The Transnational Institute's guide to the law stresses the limit of the change. Possession for personal use is still sanctioned, by referral to treatment, and that treatment can mean compulsory admission to a specialised centre. The first measure on the list is a form of confinement.

## Why the index moved

Two scores rise by one point each. Possession goes from 1 to 2: holding up to 20 grams no longer leads to a prison sentence, but it is still dealt with by the state and can end in compulsory treatment. Enforcement goes from 1 to 2 for the same reason. Consumption stays at 1, because the user remains the object of the measures; cultivation and sharing are not touched by the personal-dose articles, and there is no lawful access. The total moves from 9 to 14, and no later event in the timeline has changed it.
