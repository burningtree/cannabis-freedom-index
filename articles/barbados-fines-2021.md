On 5 March 2021 Attorney General Dale Marshall brought a short bill to the House of Assembly of Barbados. It amended the Drug Abuse (Prevention and Control) Act to let the police deal with a small amount of cannabis by a ticket. The bill has three sections and a form.

## The ticket

The new section 6A lets a police officer serve a fixed penalty notice on anyone the officer has reasonable cause to believe has "no more than 14 grammes of cannabis". The notice offers the chance to discharge "any liability that would arise out of a conviction" by paying the penalty.

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Fixed penalty | $200, paid to the clerk of the magistrate's court |
| Time to pay | 30 days; part payment is not accepted |
| If paid | the police take no proceedings and liability to conviction is discharged |
| If not paid | a separate offence, with a fine of $1,000 on summary conviction |
| If the person denies having cannabis | prosecution for possession in the ordinary way |

## Under 18, and addiction

A person under 18 must also attend the National Council on Substance Abuse within 14 days for an assessment, and undergo whatever counselling it recommends. An officer who has reasonable cause to believe an adult is addicted to cannabis may send that adult to the Council too. Failing to attend is punished in the same way as failing to pay.

## Old convictions

A second new section, 6B, looks backwards. A person convicted of possessing not more than 14 grams who has served the sentence "shall be deemed to be rehabilitated", and the conviction is deemed to be expunged.

## A dissent from the government benches

Possession stays an offence under section 6(2); the ticket is a way of settling it. That was the complaint of Trevor Prescod, a government backbencher, as Barbados Today reported the debate. He called the fine "merely a revenue collection measure" under which the state would "receive $200 from each man", described the change as "an inch", and said people who could not pay would end up in court as before.

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 1 to 3: up to 14 grams now ends with a $200 payment and no conviction. Enforcement rises from 2 to 4, because the Act gives the police a ticket in place of an arrest and a charge and wipes out earlier convictions for the same amount. Consumption rises from 1 to 2. Growing and sharing are not mentioned in the bill and stay at 1, and access stays at the level set by the [medical law of 2019](/history/barbados-medical-2019/). The total moves from 12 to 22.
