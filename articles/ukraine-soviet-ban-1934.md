On 27 October 1934 the Soviet leadership prohibited growing Indian hemp anywhere in the
Union without permission. The decree, signed in the Kremlin by Kalinin, Molotov and
Enukidze, is the legal beginning of cannabis prohibition on Ukrainian soil — then the
Union's largest hemp-growing republic.[@1,3]

## What the decree said

Sowing opium poppy and Indian hemp was banned except for crops serving exclusively the
medical and scientific needs of the USSR.[@1] The agriculture commissariat was to fix
the permitted poppy area every year by 15 August, and the land organs of the republics
would issue the permits.[@1] Unauthorised sowing drew criminal liability with mandatory
confiscation of the crop, and each republic was ordered to write matching articles into
its criminal code.[@1] The ban took effect from 1935 and repealed a milder 1927 poppy
measure.[@1]

## Why it bit Ukraine hardest

Ukraine grew hemp on an industrial scale: Soviet Ukraine kept over 150,000 hectares
under the crop into the 1950s, mostly for oil and fibre rather than smoking.[@3] The
all-Union hemp area stood near 600,000 hectares in 1940 and shrank for decades
afterwards.[@2] For Ukrainian villages the 1934 decree turned an everyday fibre crop
into a licensed exception.

## A ban that lasted generations

The prohibition stayed in force through the war, the post-war hemp decline and
independence. Unauthorised growing remained a crime in every successor code, and only
the [2024 medical law](/history/ukraine-medical-2024/) created a licensed route back —
medical, indoor and tracked, a narrower door than the one the decree closed in 1934.
