On 15 April 1983 an amendment to Malaysia's Dangerous Drugs Act came into force. It is the amendment that made death the fixed penalty for trafficking and presumed anyone holding 200 grams of cannabis to be a trafficker. The day after, a companion law on the treatment and rehabilitation of drug dependants took effect.

## Trafficking, and what is presumed

Section 39B forbids trafficking in a dangerous drug, offering to, or doing anything preparatory to it. The sentence, as the amendment left it, was death.

The Act does not leave trafficking to be proved in the ordinary way. A person found with more than a set weight is presumed to be trafficking until they prove otherwise, and for cannabis and cannabis resin that weight is 200 grams.

## Below the line

| Cannabis | Section | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Any amount, or the plant's seeds | 6 | up to 5 years, or a fine up to RM20,000 |
| 20 g to under 50 g | 39A(1) | 2 to 5 years, and 3 to 9 strokes |
| 50 g or more | 39A(2) | 5 years to life, and at least 10 strokes |
| 200 g or more | 37(da) | presumed trafficking |
| Trafficking | 39B | death; since 2023, death or life with at least 12 strokes |
| Growing a plant | 6B | life, and at least 6 strokes |
| Using | 15 | up to 2 years, or a fine up to RM5,000 |

Growing has no lesser tier. One plant carries imprisonment for life and whipping, and a person found on the land is presumed to be the grower.

## Forty years of amendments

The notes printed with the Act record how it was adjusted afterwards.

In 1986 the maximum fine for simple possession was doubled, from ten to twenty thousand ringgit, and the minimum whipping for 50 grams or more went from six strokes to ten.

An amendment that took effect in March 2018 lowered the whipping that accompanies a trafficking sentence other than death, from fifteen strokes to twelve.

On 4 July 2023 the Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act rewrote the trafficking section. A court may now choose between death and imprisonment for life.

## Why the index moved

The [1983 amendment](/history/malaysia-mandatory-death-1983/) took [Malaysia's](/country/my/) total from 9 to 2, where it remains. Possession, growing, sharing and use all fell to 0: a presumption that turns 200 grams into a capital charge leaves no level of the scale below it, and anything passed to another person is trafficking. The 2023 change made death a choice for the judge, not an end to it, so the scores did not move back.
