Belarus began a tougher drug crackdown on 1 January 2015, bringing children as young as fourteen within criminal responsibility for drug supply. The regime also included a twelve-to-twenty-five-year prison range where unlawful supply caused someone’s death through drug consumption.

## Synthetic-drug poisonings drove the crackdown

President Alexander Lukashenko signed the emergency decree at the end of December 2014. Its opening called for stronger action to protect health, children and young people, and national security.

A 2021 assessment involving Belarusian ministries and the National Academy of Sciences traced the tougher policy to a surge in drug crime and poisonings involving synthetic drugs known as spice. The report examined whether the response met tests of necessity and proportionality.

The decree gave the Interior Ministry a coordinating role and required the government to draw up a national action plan. It also called for regular reporting on enforcement, prevention and rehabilitation.

## Fourteen-year-olds could face supply charges

The assessment identified lowering the age of criminal responsibility for supply to fourteen as one of the decree’s central changes. It also described stronger controls over drug analogues and responsibility for entertainment venues and internet resources that allowed unlawful drug activity.

These changes reached beyond the synthetic substances that had prompted the emergency response. Cannabis cases were prosecuted within the same drug-offence system.

For families, the distinction between personal possession and supply mattered greatly. Each attracted a different sentencing range, with additional offences for aggravated supply.

## The longest term concerned supply causing death

The criminal code’s later consolidated text set two to five years in prison, or restriction of liberty, for unauthorised personal possession. Supply offences had their own escalating ranges.

The twelve-to-twenty-five-year range applied when supply caused a person’s death through consumption. Organised-group supply and specified laboratory production had a separate ten-to-twenty-year range in the 2019 text.

Growing drug-containing plants was also a separate offence. Its basic penalties included a fine, short custody, or restriction of liberty or prison for up to three years.

## Families later pressed for shorter sentences

The [Mothers 328 campaign](/history/belarus-mothers-328-hunger-strike-2018/) brought prisoners’ families into the public debate. [Lower supply minimums](/history/belarus-article-328-amended-2019/) took effect in October 2019.

In [March 2025](/history/belarus-drug-sentencing-options-2025/), judges gained further sentencing options, including short custody for personal offences and restriction of liberty for basic supply. [Belarus’s profile](/country/by/) sets out those later changes alongside the offences that remained in force.
