Cuba’s replacement Penal Code took effect on 1 December 2022. Law 151 had been adopted on 15 May and published on 1 September; its final provisions required a ninety-day interval before commencement. Those three dates describe different stages of the same reform.

The new code continued to prohibit unauthorised cannabis possession, cultivation and supply. Its [actual wording](https://bwcimplementation.org/sites/default/files/resource/Cuba_PenalCode_2022_0.pdf) is nevertheless more precise than a general description of “four to thirty years or death” for cannabis: simple possession has its own offence and penalty.

## Possession and cultivation have different ranges

Article 236(b) sets six months to two years in prison, a fine of two hundred to five hundred quotas, or both, for simple possession of cannabis or its derivatives without the required authorisation or prescription. Quotas are the law’s fine units, not a peso amount that can be read directly from this provision.

Article 235(1)(d) covers unauthorised cultivation and knowing possession of seeds or plant parts, with four to ten years. It also provides for confiscation of a grower’s land or loss of usufruct or occupancy rights. The code supplies no personal home-growing allowance in that provision.

The reference to authorisation or prescription does not by itself establish an operating medical-cannabis programme. A criminal-law exception and a lawful route through which patients can obtain a product require separate evidence.

## Supply and premises

The ordinary four-to-ten-year provision also covers trafficking and providing drugs to another person. Its wording does not make payment a necessary element of every form of supply.

It includes operating premises intended for people to gather and consume illicit drugs. This helps explain why a prohibition regime cannot be assessed solely through the penalty for possessing a small amount: a place organised around communal use can involve a different provision.

## The aggravated provision expands

Article 235(2) provides ten to thirty years, life imprisonment or death for specified aggravated circumstances. These include abuse of public office, international trafficking, using someone under eighteen, activity at specified institutions or youth settings, organised crime, relatively large quantities and a recorded similar conviction.

Compared with the [1999 wording](/history/cuba-law-87-1999/), the code broadens the listed circumstances and raises the child-related age boundary from sixteen to eighteen. It also adds life imprisonment as an express alternative in this range. These are changes to the aggravated framework, not a new death penalty for simple possession.

## Reading the reform accurately

The [previous code](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/legislation/details/900) already distinguished simple cannabis possession from cultivation and trafficking. The 2022 law preserved that basic distinction while replacing and renumbering the provisions.

[elTOQUE’s explanation of commencement](https://eltoque.com/codigo-penal-sanciones-por-nuevos-delitos) confirms the December start date. For the [Cuba profile](/country/cu/), the new text provides a firmer basis for describing penalties than an undifferentiated list of maximum sentences. Prison remains possible for small-scale possession, while the capital provision requires a separate aggravated classification.
