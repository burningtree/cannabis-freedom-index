Andorra’s October 2020 amendment made a personal cannabis offence punishable by arrest **and** a fine of €500–1,200.[@1] That combination mattered: the [2013 wording](/history/andorra-personal-use-wording-2013/) had allowed arrest **or** a fine of up to €600.[@2] The amendment also increased the financial penalty for public or group consumption.[@1]

## Personal use remained a criminal matter

The earlier provision already covered consuming cannabis, and growing, importing or possessing it for personal use.[@2] Those acts belonged to the Criminal Code’s minor contraventions rather than a lawful personal-use allowance.[@2] The 2013 rule’s alternative penalties meant a fine could stand on its own, with €600 as its ceiling.[@2]

The replacement wording adopted in 2020 retained the same personal-use acts but required both forms of punishment, adding a €500 minimum and doubling the previous fine ceiling.[@1] It therefore tightened the consequences within an existing criminal prohibition, rather than creating a new offence for a previously permitted activity.[@1,2]

## Sharing a joint had a separate rule

The amendment also rewrote article 500, covering consumption in public premises or in a group.[@1] It set arrest together with a €600–1,500 fine and expressly included supplying another person for joint consumption.[@1] A group meant two or more people, so the provision was not limited to a large gathering or an organised business.[@1]

The current code keeps that distinction between personal use and public or shared consumption.[@3] Someone reading only the personal-possession fine would therefore miss the separate provision that can apply when two people use cannabis together.[@3]

## Arrest did not mean a single fixed prison term

The code provides several forms of arrest, including detention during time off, at home or for part of each day.[@3] The sentence determines which form applies, so describing the offence solely as a monetary ticket omits a separate criminal penalty.[@3]

The law was adopted on 29 October 2020, while its final provision generally delayed commencement until one month after publication.[@1] Adoption and the date when the new punishment could operate were therefore different steps.[@1]

## The later code retained the penalties

The [replacement code passed in July 2026](/history/andorra-new-criminal-code-2026/) retained €500–1,200 for personal cannabis offences and €600–1,500 for public or group consumption.[@4] Its general commencement was delayed four months after publication.[@4] The [Andorra profile](/country/ad/) consequently distinguishes the code still operating in October from that deferred replacement.[@3,4]
