Kazakhstan’s new criminal code eased two parts of cannabis law when it replaced the Soviet-era code on 1 January 1998. The personal-possession offence covered acquisition or keeping only in the legally defined large band, and the separate crime of repeated small possession disappeared. Ordinary unauthorised growing gained a fine alternative and a lower prison ceiling.[@1,2,3]

## Small holdings lost a separate repeat offence

The earlier code allowed criminal prosecution when someone bought or kept a small personal quantity again within a year after an administrative penalty. A person could therefore move from the administrative system into criminal proceedings without the authorities having to prove an intention to supply another person.[@2]

The replacement’s personal-acquisition and keeping offence required a large quantity and carried up to three years in prison. Its drug chapter contained no equivalent of the old separate repeat-small-possession offence. The quantity dividing the bands therefore became central to whether personal acquisition or keeping fell within that crime.[@1,2]

Other drug activities had their own criminal rules. The new code grouped manufacture, transport and supply separately from the narrower personal-acquisition and keeping provision, with heavier penalties. Users still faced legal restrictions on obtaining and handling cannabis.[@1]

## Growing gained a fine alternative

The old code punished growing the named prohibited kinds of hemp with up to five years in prison. The replacement offered a fine of 500–700 monthly calculation units, an income-based fine or up to two years in prison for ordinary unauthorised cultivation. Calculation units were the statutory measure used to set monetary penalties.[@1,2]

Growing as a group, repeatedly or in a large quantity still carried three to eight years. The new ordinary tier therefore made the sentencing choices less severe while retaining criminal liability for an unauthorised home grow.[@1]

## Earlier sentences had to be reviewed

The law bringing the code into force repealed the old code from the same date. It required review of earlier sentences where the replacement removed criminal liability, reduced punishment or otherwise improved a person’s legal position. Pending cases concerning conduct the new code no longer treated as criminal were to be closed.[@3]

These were instructions to revisit cases. They connected a change in the statute to people already serving sentences or facing prosecution.[@3]

## Another reform followed in 2011

The change followed the [1991 repeal of use penalties](/history/kazakhstan-use-penalties-labour-camps-1991/), which had left repeated small possession criminal. Public use acquired a new administrative fine in [2006](/history/kazakhstan-public-drug-use-fines-2006/), while the large-possession offence survived until the [2011 reform](/history/kazakhstan-humanisation-2011/). Together, these changes explain several distinct stages behind [Kazakhstan’s present rules](/country/kz/).[@4,5,6]
