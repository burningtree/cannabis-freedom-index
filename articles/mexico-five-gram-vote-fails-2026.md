In February 2026 Mexico's Supreme Court came within one vote of ending the 5-gram limit — and
left it standing. Five of nine justices voted to strike the limit from the health law with
general effect; six were needed.[@1]

## The man with 14 grams

The case belonged to a homeless person detained on 7 February 2021 in Ciudad Juárez, Chihuahua,
with 14 grams of marijuana — nine over the legal line.[@1] The court granted him individual
protection: for his case, the judge must weigh whether the cannabis was for personal use
instead of applying the 5-gram limit automatically.[@1] The justices were analysing article 478
of the General Health Law, which excludes from the offence only someone holding an amount "equal
to or less than" the permitted limit.[@1]

## A pattern, not an accident

It was the second time the court had protected someone over the line without moving the line
itself: in May 2022 it had likewise ruled that penalising possession of more than 5 grams was
unconstitutional in the individual case.[@2] The 2021 declaration had already removed the legal
obstacle to adult personal use, but Congress never passed the regulatory law — so the 5-gram
rule from the old regime still decides, on paper, who is a consumer and who is a criminal.[@1]

The February vote keeps that paper rule for everyone except the person who challenged it.
