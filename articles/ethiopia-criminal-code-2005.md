Ethiopia's replacement criminal code separated drug possession, growing and
supply for private use from trafficking for the first time, giving the
personal-use case its own ceiling of seven years in prison. In force from
9 May 2005, the code replaced the [1957 penal code](/history/ethiopia-penal-code-1957/)
while preserving the application of special criminal laws.

## One article replaced a single supply offence

The [1957 penal code](/history/ethiopia-penal-code-1957/) had punished
unauthorised production, circulation and supply together, with at least
three months of simple imprisonment. The new code's article 525 split that
single chain of conduct into two tracks: private use by oneself or another,
and trafficking.

The personal-use track covered planting, buying, receiving, making,
possessing, selling and delivering a narcotic for private use, as well as
unlawful use itself. Its punishment was rigorous imprisonment of up to
seven years with a fine of up to 50,000 birr.

## Trafficking started where personal use stopped

Supply with the intent to traffic drew rigorous imprisonment of not less
than five years and a fine of up to 100,000 birr. Organised or
professional dealing, repeat offences and other aggravated cases started
at ten years with a fine of up to 200,000 birr. The article set those
minimums without stating a maximum term for them.

The old code's host offence survived the rewrite in substance: the [1999
drug law](/history/ethiopia-drug-control-law-1999/) had already set seven
to fifteen years for allowing illicit narcotic drug use on one's premises,
and the new code kept premises offences within its reach.

## The split still shapes Ethiopian cannabis law

Article 525 remains the working law for cannabis in Ethiopia. Personal
possession, home growing and passing cannabis to another person for their
own use all sit under the seven-year ceiling, while any intent to traffic
moves the case onto the five-year track. Those distinctions became part
of [Ethiopia's cannabis law](/country/et/) and still define what a court
must decide: whether the cannabis was for private use or for the market.
