Belarus’s revised environmental rules listed Cannabis sativa among plants barred from introduction and establishment from 31 December 2016. The change placed hemp within a plant-control list alongside species such as giant hogweed and Canadian goldenrod.

## The environment ministry rewrote a plant list

The Environment Ministry adopted the amendment in October 2016, with the revised list published in the national legal gazette the following month. First Deputy Minister I. V. Malkina signed it.

The ministry changed an existing rule dating from 2008. It removed the reference to wild-growing plants from the title and an opening clause, then replaced the attached list.

Cannabis sativa appeared by its scientific name as the sixth entry. The list also included opium poppy, boxelder and several other plants. Its heading concerned the introduction and acclimatisation of plants: bringing them in and establishing them in a new setting.

## Hemp was named without a low-THC qualification

The cannabis entry named the species without giving a THC limit or distinguishing fibre varieties from drug varieties. Someone considering a hemp crop therefore encountered an environmental restriction expressed in terms of the plant itself.

This sat alongside the [2012 narcotics law](/history/belarus-narcotics-law-2012/). That law prohibited sowing or growing plants and fungi containing controlled drugs, while allowing exceptions established elsewhere in legislation.

The two texts approached plants from different directions. The environmental list controlled their introduction and establishment; the narcotics law controlled cultivation of plants containing drugs.

## Drug cultivation had its own criminal penalties

The criminal code separately punished sowing or growing drug-containing plants for sale or to obtain drugs. Its basic cultivation offence offered a fine, short custody, restriction of liberty for up to three years or prison for the same period.

Repeat and group offences attracted heavier penalties. Producing or possessing drugs after growing plants could also raise questions under the separate drug-manufacture and possession offences.

For a person growing cannabis to obtain a personal supply, these cultivation controls were the immediate legal obstacle. The environmental listing added another rule governing the plant’s establishment.

## Later sentence reforms left plant controls in place

Belarus [reduced supply minimums in 2019](/history/belarus-article-328-amended-2019/) and [expanded sentencing options in 2025](/history/belarus-drug-sentencing-options-2025/). Both changes concerned punishment within the drug-offence system.

The Health Ministry’s controlled-drug list, consolidated through February 2025, continued to name marijuana, hashish and cannabis oil among drugs not used for medical purposes. [Belarus’s profile](/country/by/) brings together the plant controls, cultivation penalties and later sentencing changes.
