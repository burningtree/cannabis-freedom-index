Equatorial Guinea's drug decree of 15 September 1993 expressly covered possession, cultivation,
use and unpaid gifts. It is a useful historical source, but it cannot by itself establish every
penalty applying today.

## What the decree covered

Article 1 prohibited production, sale, use and trafficking. Article 4 included cultivation,
manufacture, processing, transport, possession, donation and facilitating illegal use. It prescribed
medium-term imprisonment and a fine of 500,000 to 50,500,000 CFA francs. The English translation
does not translate that prison category into a number of years. An unsupported one-to-twenty-year
range should not be substituted for it.

Article 5 provided aggravated punishment for specified conduct involving minors, schools, military
or prison facilities, adulterated drugs and people in rehabilitation. It also provided professional
disqualification where a physician or public official abused their position.

## A medical and scientific exception

Article 2 reserved authorisation of controlled-drug imports, exports, sales and medical or scientific
use to the Health Ministry. Article 3 referred prescribing and dispensing requirements to the
national pharmaceutical selection committee under Decree 7/1991.

Those powers do not demonstrate that patients could obtain cannabis flower or oil, or that a
cannabis programme was operating. They also do not authorise recreational growing clubs.

## Dates and the later code

The decree was signed on 15 September 1993. Its final provision made commencement depend on
publication in national information media; the reviewed copy does not establish that publication
date. The history entry therefore identifies the signing event explicitly.

The decree repealed article 433 of the then-applicable Penal Code. A later UN report states that
Equatorial Guinea's new Penal Code entered into force on 10 December 2022. Its relationship to the 1993
drug provisions still needs a verified, complete legislative comparison. The historical fine and
prison category should not be represented as a confirmed 2026 tariff.

A newspaper account of open cannabis smoking in 2000 remains historical reporting. It cannot
establish present enforcement, current arrest rates or permission to possess cannabis.
