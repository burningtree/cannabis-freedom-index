Uruguay was first, but it has three and a half million people. Canada was the first
large economy to legalise cannabis, and unlike Uruguay it set out to build a
commercial market.

## Forty-six years late

Canada [banned cannabis](/history/canada-ban-1923/) in 1923 almost without debate.
For decades it hardly mattered: police recorded 25 cannabis arrests between 1930 and
1946. By 1972 there were 12,000 a year. That year the
[Le Dain Commission](/history/canada-le-dain-1972/) recommended ending the possession
offence, and the government did not act.

What moved first was medicine. Courts forced the government to open
[medical access](/history/canada-medical-access-2001/) in 2001, and in 2013 that
became a commercial system of licensed producers, an industry in place before
legalisation.

## The Act

Justin Trudeau campaigned on legalisation and won the 2015 election. A
[task force](/history/canada-task-force-2016/) reported in December 2016, Bill C-45
was introduced on 13 April 2017, the Senate passed the final text on 19 June 2018 and
it took effect on 17 October 2018. About seven in ten Canadians supported it.

| | Federal rule |
|---|---|
| Possession in public | 30 g for adults of 18 or over |
| Home growing | Four plants per household |
| Sale | Licensed shops and online; provinces design the system |
| Promotion | Nothing that appeals to young people; no sponsorship |

## A federal law, thirteen systems

Much was left to the provinces. Most set the age at 19, Alberta at 18 and Quebec at
21. Quebec created a state sales monopoly and banned home growing, and in
[2023](/history/canada-murray-hall-2023/) the Supreme Court upheld the ban. Edibles,
extracts and vapes [followed](/history/canada-edibles-2019/) a year after flower.

The Act did not clear old records. People convicted of simple possession had to wait
five years and apply for a record suspension.

## Did it replace the illegal market?

Slowly. Legal cannabis cost about $10.23 a gram at first against $5.59 on the street,
and one estimate gave the illegal market 86 per cent of sales in 2019.

| Year | Users who usually bought legally |
|---|---|
| 2019 | 37% |
| 2020 | 54% |
| 2021 | 64% |

By the third quarter of 2020 Canadians were spending more on legal cannabis than on
illegal. Federal and provincial governments had collected more than 5.4 billion
dollars in cannabis taxes by August 2025.

In the index the Act moved every score for [Canada](/country/ca/): possession from 1
to 6, growing from 1 to 4, enforcement from 3 to 6, sharing from 1 to 5, consumption
from 1 to 5 and access from 2 to 6.
