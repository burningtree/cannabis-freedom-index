The Dangerous Drugs Act 1967 received royal assent on 27 October 1967. Its long title names its main business: "An Act to provide for the control of drug addiction and to make further provision with respect to drugs." Most of its sections deal with doctors and addicts. One short section, headed "Further powers to search and to obtain evidence", changed what a police officer could do to anyone in the street.

## Section 6

If a constable "has reasonable grounds to suspect that any person is in possession of a drug" in contravention of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1965 or the Drugs (Prevention of Misuse) Act 1964, the constable may:

| Power | Wording |
|---|---|
| Search the person | "search that person, and detain him for the purpose of searching him" |
| Search a vehicle | search any vehicle in which the constable suspects the drug may be found, and require the person in control to stop it |
| Seize | take and keep anything found that appears to be evidence of an offence under either Act |

No warrant is needed and no arrest has to come first. The test is the officer's own reasonable suspicion.

## Two smaller changes in the same section

Search warrants for premises had named the particular constables allowed to carry them out. The section replaced those words, so that a warrant now authorised any constable "acting for the police area in which the premises are situated".

The power to arrest a suspected drug offender whose name and address were unknown was widened to cover a suspect whose stated name and address the officer was "not satisfied" were true.

## The rest of the Act

Section 1 let the Home Secretary make regulations requiring doctors to notify the authorities of patients they considered addicted, and barring doctors from prescribing specified drugs to addicts without a licence. Section 7 raised the maximum for unlawful import and export of drugs under the Customs and Excise Act from two years in prison to ten.

## Why the index moved

Nothing about what a cannabis user may lawfully do changed; possession was already an offence. The change is in enforcement, which falls from 4 to 2: from this Act on, an officer's reasonable suspicion was enough to stop, detain and search a person or a car. The total moves from 15 to 11. The next step in the timeline is the [Misuse of Drugs Act](/history/uk-misuse-of-drugs-act-1973/), in force from 1973.
