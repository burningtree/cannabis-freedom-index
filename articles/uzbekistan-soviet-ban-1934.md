Moscow prohibited sowing Indian hemp and the opium poppy across the Soviet Union in the autumn of 1934. From the start of 1935 only crops grown exclusively for the Union's medical and scientific needs stayed lawful; every other sowing became a crime, punished with confiscation of the crop.[@1] Uzbekistan, then a Soviet republic, came under the ban with the rest of the Union, and this is the first restriction in its recorded history.

## Kalinin and Molotov sign a Union-wide ban

The decree came from the Central Executive Committee and the Council of People's Commissars, signed in the Kremlin on 27 October 1934 by Mikhail Kalinin and Vyacheslav Molotov with secretary Abel Yenukidze, as No. 110/2466.[@1]

Its first article is a single prohibition: sowing the opium poppy and Indian hemp is forbidden on the territory of the USSR, except for sowings whose harvests go exclusively to covering the medical and scientific needs of the USSR.[@1] Around that exception the decree built a quota system: each year by 15 August the Union agriculture commissariat, agreeing with the foreign-trade commissariat and the republic health commissariats, set the area to be sown for the coming year, and permits within that area came from the republic land organs under a Union instruction.[@1]

## Prison and confiscation for unauthorised sowing

Article 5 ordered criminal liability for sowing either plant without permission, with mandatory confiscation of the crop, and told the central executive committees of the republics to write matching additions into their criminal codes.[@1] A final article repealed a 1927 accounting measure for poppy sowing, replacing paperwork with prohibition.[@1]

## Why the entry is dated 1935

The decree expressly took effect in 1935, so the ban counts here from the year it began operating rather than the day it was signed.[@1] The same Union decree stands behind the first-ban entries of the other Soviet republics, from Russia to Kazakhstan; the text named no republic because it covered them all.

## What came next

The permit system never became an ordinary trade. Later Soviet rules only sharpened the penalties: [a 1987 edict](/history/uzbekistan-hemp-growing-penalties-1987/) set up to five years in prison for sowing Indian hemp and named southern kinds, and the [national code of 1995](/history/uzbekistan-personal-possession-code-1995/) kept ordinary growing punishable after independence.
