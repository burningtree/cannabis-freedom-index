In 1993 Hungary added a mechanism to its criminal law that has shaped how the country treats cannabis users ever since. It is called *elterelés*, diversion: a person caught with a small amount of an illegal drug for personal use, who admits the offence, may choose a treatment or prevention programme in place of prosecution.

## How it works

- The programme lasts six months and must be completed within nine.
- While it runs, the criminal procedure is suspended.
- If it is completed, the charges are dropped and the procedure ends with no criminal record.

Possession is not legalised by this. The offence remains, and the person is offered a choice between the sanction and the programme, which the overview read here calls a form of quasi-coerced treatment.

## Three kinds of programme

Before starting, the person is assessed by a psychiatrist, clinical psychologist or general practitioner and sent to one of three tracks.

| Track | For whom |
|---|---|
| Treatment of dependence | people showing symptoms of drug dependence |
| Other drug treatment | people who are not dependent but have drug-related health or social problems |
| Preventive consulting | people with neither; counselling sessions every two weeks |

The third is by far the most common, and it is not treatment in the medical sense. Later rules fixed it at two hours every two weeks for six months, twenty-four hours in all.

## Who ends up in it

A study of the sixteen largest providers in 2016 and 2017 drew the typical participant: a man of about 28 with at least a secondary education, in work, and an occasional user of cannabis, less than once a month. Cannabis is the main reason for entering for more than three quarters of those in diversion. In 2020 almost 80% of everyone in drug treatment in Hungary, 3,384 people, were there because of it.

An evaluation in 2009 found that 53% of clients had seen no problem with their own drug use before starting, and that 80% mainly expected to avoid prison.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Hungary](/country/hu/) from 8 to 12 with the [1993 amendment](/history/hungary-diversion-1993/). Enforcement rises from 1 to 3: possession stays a crime, but an ordinary user can end the case without a conviction. The rule was narrowed in [1999](/history/hungary-diversion-restricted-1999/) and restored in [2003](/history/hungary-diversion-widened-2003/).
