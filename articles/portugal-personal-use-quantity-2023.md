From 1 October 2023, someone holding more than Portugal’s ten-day reference amount could still have the case handled administratively if the drugs were exclusively for personal use.[@1] Exceeding the reference became evidence for investigating purpose, with the judicial authority required to end criminal proceedings when exclusive personal use was established.[@1]

## The old quantity boundary had criminal consequences

The [2001 decriminalisation law](/history/portugal-decriminalisation-2001/) had left a dispute over possession beyond its ten-day reference.[@2] In [2008](/history/portugal-ten-day-supply-ruling-2008/), the Supreme Court resolved conflicting decisions by holding that the earlier criminal personal-consumption provision survived for quantities above that amount.[@2]

That ruling distinguished a personal-consumption offence from trafficking.[@2] A person could be holding drugs for themselves and still fall outside the administrative tier because the quantity exceeded the reference.[@2] The 2023 law replaced that quantity-based result with an express route back to administrative proceedings.[@1]

## Courts had to assess the purpose

The amendment provided for a case to be closed, an indictment to be refused or an acquittal entered, depending on the stage of proceedings, followed by referral to a dissuasion commission.[@1] It retained criminal liability for personal growing and allowed punishment for that offence to be waived.[@1]

The law also required regular review of the technical quantity rules, where possible every six months or when scientific evidence or consumption indicators required intervention.[@1] Its commencement clause made the change effective on the first day of the month after publication.[@1]

## The Constitutional Court upheld the rules

In [May 2025](/history/portugal-personal-use-constitutional-ruling-2025/), the Constitutional Court declined to strike down the amended possession provisions.[@3] It explained that the requirement to determine personal purpose still operated with the rule that unresolved doubt favours the accused.[@3] The judgment left the framework for investigating purpose and referring personal cases to commissions in place.[@3]

## Administrative proceedings still had consequences

Cannabis could still be confiscated, and a commission assessed the person’s circumstances under the administrative law.[@4] Qualifying first proceedings had to be provisionally suspended; available sanctions depended in part on whether the person was dependent.[@4] The [Portugal profile](/country/pt/) explains those procedures together with the separate criminal rules for growing and supplying cannabis.[@1,4]
