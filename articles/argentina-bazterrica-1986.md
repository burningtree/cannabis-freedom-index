In 1986, three years after the end of military rule, Argentina's Supreme Court held for the first time that the state may not punish an adult for holding drugs for personal use. The ruling is named after the defendant, a rock guitarist. It lasted four years as law, and it supplied the reasoning the court returned to in 2009.

## Two cases

Gustavo Bazterrica was a well-known guitarist, a member of the rock band Los Abuelos de la Nada. Police found 3.6 grams of marijuana and 0.06 grams of cocaine. Alejandro Capalbo, whose case was decided with his, was stopped in a taxi with 54 grams of marijuana in his jacket pocket.

Both had been convicted under Law 20.771 of 1974, which punished possession "even if intended for personal use".

## The reasoning

The court declared that provision unconstitutional. Article 19 of the Constitution places private actions that harm no one beyond the reach of the authorities. The court held that the law intruded on conduct of that kind, so long as it involved no concrete danger or harm to the rights or property of others.

## How it was undone

The legislature did not follow. The 1988 Vienna convention pushed towards stricter control, debate on a new drug law dragged on, and in 1989 the incoming government of Carlos Menem enacted [Law 23.737](/history/argentina-law-23737-1989/), which again made possession for personal use a crime.

In 1990 the Supreme Court upheld the new law in the Montalvo case. It reasoned that there is no protected privacy once conduct is outwardly expressed, and that possession of any quantity can affect public order, morals or third parties.

| Year | Case | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| 1986 | Bazterrica and Capalbo | punishing personal possession is unconstitutional |
| 1990 | Montalvo | the 1989 law's punishment of personal possession is valid |
| 2009 | [Arriola](/history/argentina-arriola-2009/) | unconstitutional again, on the Bazterrica reasoning |

A Supreme Court ruling in Argentina binds only the parties, which is one reason the question could swing three times without the statute changing.
