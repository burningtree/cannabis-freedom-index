In 2021 the Norwegian government proposed a drug reform that would have taken the use and possession of small amounts out of the criminal law. The Storting did not give it a majority. A year later the Supreme Court took what parliament had agreed on while rejecting the bill, and turned it into a rule of sentencing.

## The case

A man born in 1987, dependent on heroin since the age of twenty and with many earlier drug convictions, was found on 19 February 2020 with about four grams of heroin. The court of appeal had given him fourteen days in prison. On 8 April 2022 the Supreme Court decided his appeal, HR-2022-731-A, together with two similar cases.

## The reasoning

The question, as the court put it, was what weight the Storting's handling of the reform proposal should have for sentencing. The bill had failed, but a cross-party majority had said during its passage that punishing people who are dependent on drugs serves little purpose. The court treated values expressed by the Storting as an authoritative statement, and held that the courts should follow such signals on sentencing even when they did not end in legislation.

## The rule

The judgment's own summary, in paragraph 70, is that the acquisition, possession and storage of up to five grams of heroin, amphetamine or cocaine for own use by a person dependent on drugs "shall henceforth in general lead to a waiver of sentence" under section 61 of the Penal Code. The same goes for the use itself. The man was found guilty and given no punishment.

## The prosecutors' instructions

On 13 May 2022 the Director of Public Prosecutions turned the three judgments into binding guidelines.

| Stage | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Reporting | consider carefully whether there is reason to report and pursue the matter at all |
| Dependency | without indications, assume the person is not dependent; where there are indications, doubt counts in the suspect's favour |
| Coercive measures | none, beyond seizing drugs that are visible |
| Decision | use or holding of a small amount by a dependent person is closed with an unconditional waiver of prosecution |
| Amounts | up to 5 g for heroin, cocaine and amphetamine; for other drugs, the thresholds proposed in the failed bill, until the courts say more |
| Slightly above | a short suspended sentence |

The Director added, "for the sake of good order", that the judgments do not touch drugs meant for resale.

## Who is not covered

The rule depends on dependency, and the guidelines tell the police that, without indications to the contrary, a suspect is to be taken as not dependent. People who use cannabis without being dependent on drugs are outside the judgments. The Storting came back to the subject in [2025](/history/norway-drug-reform-passed-2025/).

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 1 to 2 and enforcement from 2 to 3: for one group of users, holding a small amount no longer leads to punishment, and the police have lost their coercive powers in those cases. Consumption stays at 1, because for everyone else use is still an offence. The total moves from 12 to 17.
