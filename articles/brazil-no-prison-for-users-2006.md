On 23 August 2006 President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed a drug law that did two things at once. It took prison away from people who hold drugs for their own use, and it raised the sentence for everyone else. It came into force forty-five days after publication and replaced the laws of 1976 and 2002.

## Three penalties, none of them prison

Article 28 covers anyone who acquires, keeps, transports or carries drugs for personal consumption. It lists the penalties:

- a warning about the effects of drugs;
- community service;
- attendance at an educational programme or course.

The second and third last at most five months, or ten for a repeat offender. If a person refuses without good reason, the judge may reprimand them and then fine them, between 40 and 100 day-fines. The judge must also order the state to make free treatment available.

The same article gives the same treatment to a person who sows, grows or harvests plants to prepare a small quantity for their own use.

Possession stayed in the criminal law. A separate article says that for this offence there is no arrest in the act: the person is taken before a judge, or signs an undertaking to appear.

## The line the law did not draw

The law sets no quantity. To decide whether a drug was for personal use, the judge looks at its nature and amount, the place and conditions of the act, the person's social and personal circumstances, and their conduct and record.

Everything else in the law depends on which side of that judgment a person falls.

## What lies on the other side

| | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Possession or growing for personal use (Art. 28) | warning, community service or a course |
| Offering a drug occasionally, without profit, to share with someone one knows (Art. 33 §3) | 6 months to 1 year |
| Trafficking, including supplying free of charge (Art. 33) | 5 to 15 years |

Trafficking covers eighteen verbs, among them keeping, carrying and supplying "even free of charge". A first offender of good record who belongs to no criminal organisation may have the sentence reduced by between a sixth and two-thirds.

Between the two the law placed a small middle offence: sharing with a friend, to use together, without profit.

## Why the index moved

The [law](/history/brazil-no-prison-for-users-2006/) raised [Brazil's](/country/br/) possession score from 1 to 2 and its enforcement score from 1 to 2, taking the total from 9 to 14. Possession remained a crime, which keeps it below the level of decriminalisation, but a crime with no prison attached is milder than one that usually ends in a fine. The scores for growing and sharing did not move: growing a small amount for oneself got the same penalties as possession but, like possession, no quantity to rely on, and the lighter sharing offence still carries detention.
