A dispute over the definition of cannabis accompanied the expansion of Dutch hashish
imports. Stephen Snelders describes a year of litigation ending with September 1972
sentences for three Rotterdam businessmen.

The Hague court held that hashish fell under the Opium Act even when it could not be
proved to have been made from dried cannabis tops. Two defendants received eighteen
months and one nine months. The case concerned a 110 kg seizure the previous year.

The chapter treats this as an early sign of larger criminal enterprises entering the
trade. We record the interpretation as enforcement context for the
[Netherlands](/country/nl/). The chapter does not reproduce the judgment or give a case
number, so this account is attributed to Snelders rather than presented as a separately
verified judicial holding. It does not establish the start of the national prohibition.
