Algeria published detailed procedures in August 2007 for drug users to avoid prosecution through prescribed treatment or medical supervision.[@1] Medical assessments, progress reports and completion certificates linked the person's care to the prosecutor's decision.[@1] Seized drugs and plants still had to be confiscated.[@1]

## A route already provided by the drug law

The [2004 drug law](/history/algeria-law-04-18-2004/) had allowed non-prosecution for qualifying users who followed its treatment or medical-supervision conditions.[@1,2] It also left illicit use and personal possession punishable with prison, a fine, or either penalty alone.[@1,2] The decree set out how the treatment route would be handled.[@1,2]

If a prosecutor learned that a person had already undergone the prescribed care after the alleged drug use, a medical report could support a decision not to prosecute.[@1] The prosecutor could still require examination by a specialist.[@1]

## Assessment decides the form of care

When the case suggested drug dependence, the prosecutor ordered examination by a specialist.[@1] If the person needed detoxification in an establishment, the prosecutor directed them to the designated facility.[@1] Where that level of care was unnecessary, the person could instead be placed under medical supervision for the period indicated by the examination.[@1]

The decree therefore provided more than one setting for care.[@1] It connected the decision to the medical assessment, with the prosecutor remaining responsible for the legal handling of the case.[@1]

## The doctor reports progress

Once the person entered the prescribed care, the treating doctor sent the prosecutor a certificate giving its starting date and probable duration.[@1] The doctor monitored progress and reported regularly.[@1] If treatment stopped, the establishment's director or responsible doctor had to tell the prosecutor immediately.[@1]

At the end, a certificate confirmed that the person had followed the course or medical supervision.[@1] A copy went to the prosecutor for the non-prosecution decision.[@1] Confiscation of the seized substances and plants remained required in these cases.[@1]

## Later amendments keep treatment in the law

The decree was signed on 30 July and published on 5 August 2007.[@1] Another decree in the same Gazette addressed medical and scientific authorisation for controlled-drug operations.[@1]

The [2023 amendment](/history/algeria-law-23-05-2023/) retained treatment-linked non-prosecution and added a protection for minors who completed detoxification.[@3] For people using drugs in [Algeria](/country/dz/), the medical route continued alongside criminal penalties for use and personal possession.[@3]
