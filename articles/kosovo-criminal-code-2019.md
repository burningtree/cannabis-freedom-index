Kosovo’s new criminal code took effect on 14 April 2019 and kept prison possible for cannabis possession. A first-time offender with less than three grams of cannabis or hashish for personal use faced a fine or up to one year; ordinary unauthorised possession carried a fine and one to three years.[@1]

## The amount and previous offending mattered

The lower tier depended on being a first-time offender and possessing less than three grams for personal use. For someone meeting those conditions, the court could choose a fine or imprisonment. Under the ordinary offence, the code prescribed a fine together with imprisonment.[@1]

The code also required confiscation of the drugs. Its possession article set no specific €250–300 fine amount. What it specified was the distinction between the ordinary penalty and the lower first-offence tier.[@1]

## The rule came from the earlier code

The [criminal code effective from January 2013](/history/kosovo-criminal-code-2013/) already contained the small-amount distinction. The 2019 replacement carried it forward, along with the ordinary one-to-three-year possession range.[@2,1]

Behind both codes stood the [2008 drug law](/history/kosovo-narcotics-law-2008/). That law placed cannabis, resin, extracts and tinctures in Table I, where permitted activities were limited to scientific research, education and police purposes. Its ordinary medical-prescribing rules concerned other tables.[@3]

## Suppliers and growers faced separate offences

Buying or holding drugs with intent to sell or distribute carried a fine and two to eight years. Distribution and sale carried a fine and two to twelve years, while unauthorised import or export carried a fine and three to ten years.[@1]

The code also expressly punished growing cannabis for drug production with one to ten years. Plants and cultivation equipment were subject to confiscation. The first-offence possession tier therefore offered no growing allowance.[@1]

## Critics argued for regulation

In October 2019, Pristina consultant Kushtrim Shaipi argued in Kosovo 2.0 that regulated legalisation could serve Kosovo better than penalisation. He criticised police checkpoints and raids in bars and clubs as ineffective enforcement strategies.[@4]

Police continued pursuing suspected supply. During [operation Starti in January 2025](/history/kosovo-operation-starti-2025/), officers searched four Pristina locations, seized 25.2 kg of suspected marijuana and arrested four men over suspected drug and weapons offences.[@5]

The [Kosovo profile](/country/xk/) follows those separate rules for possession, cultivation and supply. For users holding small amounts, the practical legal distinction remains the threshold and the first-offence condition.
