A Viennese pharmacy journal reported a Russian import prohibition covering **Indian cigarettes with Cannabis indica** on **1 February 1878**. The notice provides contemporary evidence of a restriction on a named medicinal product, while leaving the underlying Russian instrument and its starting date unresolved.

## A list of permitted and prohibited medicines

The item, headed *Pharmaceutische Nachricht aus Russland*, appears on pages **45–46** of the *Pharmaceutische Post*. It attributes its information to the *Pharmaceutische Zeitung*.

The report first lists patent medicines admitted to import into Russia. It then introduces a separate prohibited list. Near the beginning of that second list are *Cigarettes indiennes au Cannabis indica*. This explicit identification distinguishes the finding from a generic pharmacy law whose application to cannabis would merely be assumed.

The prohibited list continues through many unrelated preparations, including tooth preparations, herbal remedies, anti-asthmatic products and other proprietary medicines. A further paragraph addresses prohibited pharmaceutical preparations. The structure establishes a broader medicine-import regime; it does not present a cannabis-only law.

## What the 1878 date means

The journal establishes that the restriction was being reported by **1 February 1878**. It supplies no Russian decree number, enactment date or commencement provision. The database therefore records the dated report as historical context, without pretending that a new prohibition necessarily began that day or even that year.

The same distinction applies to the journal's source attribution. The underlying *Pharmaceutische Zeitung* item has not been independently retrieved. The contemporary notice is evidence actually read, but it is not the Russian government's original legal text.

## A later trade-gazette corroboration

The Swiss *Schweizerisches Handelsamtsblatt* returned to Russian pharmaceutical imports in **September 1886**. In its non-official section, issue **87**, dated **18 September**, it printed a list of foreign patent medicines whose importation was prohibited. The entry for Indian cigarettes with Cannabis indica appears at the top of page **612**, under a heading beginning on page **611**.

This independently published contemporary list corroborates the product restriction in 1886. It does not, by itself, prove uninterrupted enforcement throughout the intervening years or resolve when the prohibition originally began.

## Limits of the finding

Neither list establishes a general Russian offence of possessing cannabis, cultivating hemp, or supplying every cannabis medicine domestically. Nor does the 1878 entry name a manufacturer. The finding is consequently retained as a product-import restriction documented by contemporary reports, with no invented nationwide score change.
