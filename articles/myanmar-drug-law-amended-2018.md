Myanmar removed a three-to-five-year prison penalty for failing to
register for drug treatment in February 2018. The replacement process
still let police refer suspected users for assessment and courts order
six months in a treatment or rehabilitation centre.

## The older offence linked treatment to imprisonment

Under the [1993 drug law](/history/myanmar-narcotics-law-1993/), drug
users had to register for treatment and follow health ministry directions.
Failure could lead to prison. The amendment removed that registration
offence and replaced the requirement with an obligation to undergo
medical treatment at a prescribed or recognised centre.

Police were to send someone suspected of drug use for a preliminary
medical examination and, if identified as a user, treatment. Someone
could therefore enter the process through a police referral as well as
through seeking care.

## A court could compel six months of treatment

When a person failed to undergo examination and treatment, a police
officer could apply for a court order requiring a bond: a formal promise
to comply with the treatment conditions.

If the person refused to give that promise or broke a condition, the
court had to send them to a health or rehabilitation centre for six
months. The health ministry was responsible for providing the treatment
during that period.

Breaking the prescribed rules of a centre could then lead to a conviction
and unpaid community service. The amendment specified 240 to 360 hours,
worked at two hours a day under supervision. Examples included cleaning,
planting trees, repairing roads and bridges, and welfare work with
social groups.

## A higher cannabis threshold still left possession punishable

The amendment raised the quantity presumed to be held for sale from
75 to 100 grams of cannabis, cannabis essence or their combined weight.
Reaching that quantity brought the statutory presumption of a sale
purpose into play.

Below it, unauthorised possession still carried five to ten years in
prison. Possession for sale remained punishable with at least ten years.
Removing the registration offence therefore left separate drug-possession
charges available against a person found with cannabis.

## The new policy proposed voluntary care

Six days after the amendment, the government released its
[first national drug-control policy](/history/myanmar-public-health-drug-policy-2018/).
That document recommended transforming compulsory treatment into
voluntary services and expanding access to care and rehabilitation.

The law and the policy left [Myanmar's drug users](/country/mm/) facing
different approaches: a stated direction towards voluntary care alongside
legal powers to order treatment and punish possession.
