Jamaica's Dangerous Drugs (Amendment) Act took effect on 15 April 2015 and changed
the legal position of ganja across personal possession, cultivation, religious use
and medical activity. It did not create unrestricted adult sales, but it removed
arrest and criminal records for small possession and legalised limited household
growing.

## A ticket instead of a criminal case

Possession of two ounces or less ceased to be an arrestable offence. Police could
issue a fixed-penalty notice for J$500, payable within 30 days, but the person
would not be charged, sent to court or given a criminal record for the possession
itself.

People under 18, and adults whom police believed to be dependent on ganja, were
also referred to the National Council on Drug Abuse for counselling. More than two
ounces remained a criminal offence carrying a possible fine, imprisonment and a
criminal record.

Public smoking was prohibited in much the same way as tobacco smoking and could
also bring a J$500 ticket. Smoking at a private, non-commercial residence was not
an offence, subject to the possession rules.

## Five plants and sacramental use

Each household could legally grow up to five ganja plants on its premises. Where
several households shared one property, each household had its own five-plant
allowance.

The amendment also created specific protection for Rastafarian practice.
Rastafarian adults and organisations could seek authorisation to cultivate ganja
as a sacrament, and registered places of worship could allow sacramental smoking.
Qualifying Rastafarian events could be declared exempt from arrest or prosecution
for possession, smoking and transport within the conditions set for the event.

## A regulated medical industry

The law established the Cannabis Licensing Authority to license and supervise a
lawful industry for medical, therapeutic and scientific ganja, as well as hemp.
It also provided medical possession exceptions and a permit route for visiting
medical users.

## In the index

The reform raised [Jamaica's](/country/jm/) possession score from 1 to 3,
cultivation from 1 to 4, enforcement from 2 to 4, sharing from 1 to 2, and
consumption from 1 to 3. Access stayed at 0 because licensed retail had not yet
opened; that changed with the [first herb house in 2018](/history/jamaica-first-herb-house-2018/).
