France extended its narcotics controls to colonial [Algeria](/country/dz/) by a decree of 9 February 1917.[@1] A French government account sent to the League of Nations later identified that decree as the measure applying the metropolitan rules on poisonous substances to Algeria.[@1]

## Kif already had a local history

The administration described earlier attempts to regulate kif, its name for finely chopped cannabis.[@1] Councils in Algiers had discussed restrictions in 1881, and councils in Constantine and the colonial government considered the issue again in 1915.[@1] These discussions concerned preparation, use, sale and circulation.[@1]

The report also described a crop that mattered to small growers.[@1] Cannabis had been cultivated in some communities south of Oran and Constantine, where limited land made larger-scale agriculture difficult.[@1] Harvests were sold particularly at Biskra and neighbouring oases, providing income for the growers.[@1]

## French drug rules reach the colony

The 1917 decree applied the French decree of September 1916.[@1] According to the government's later account, the resulting system covered import, export, trade, possession and use of poisonous substances.[@1] Algeria's rules were described as following those in metropolitan France.[@1]

The legal description of the drug mattered in court.[@1] The administration said the word “hashish” had caused interpretation disputes before some courts.[@1] A [further colonial decree in January 1932](/history/algeria-cannabis-controls-1932/) applied the updated French system and named cannabis, resin, preparations, extracts and tinctures explicitly.[@1]

## Enforcement claims meet a question

The colonial administration credited strict police and customs enforcement of the 1932 rules with greatly reducing, or nearly eliminating, cultivation and use.[@1] The League Secretariat added a footnote questioning whether the French decree expressly covered cultivation at all.[@1] It observed that cultivation was not specifically mentioned in the metropolitan text.[@1]

The report supplied examples of seizures: 20 kg at La Calle in 1926, 80 kg at Tebessa in 1927 and 18 kg at El Oued in April 1928.[@1] Its figure for Algeria as a whole in 1931 was 660 g.[@1] The administration reported these as seizures for the named places and dates.[@1]

## Different ways of using the plant

The same account described smoking mixtures around Souk-Ahras and cannabis drunk as an infusion or chewed around Tlemcen.[@1] It also recorded a mixture containing honey, oil, cloves, cannabis and opium called madjoun.[@1] The controls therefore reached practices that extended beyond a single product sold as hashish.[@1]
