Moldova's government approved rules on 29 April 2026 allowing authorised farmers to use industrial hemp for more products, including stalks and biomass.[@1] The rules put authorisation and monitoring with the national food-safety agency, ANSA, and provided for domestic sales and exports.[@1]

## Farmers gained more uses for their harvest

The government's announcement described an expansion beyond fibre, seed and oil to products such as briquettes, industrial oils and permitted cosmetics.[@1] Applicants needed lawful ownership or use of agricultural land and had to identify their crop, variety, location and intended uses.[@1] Growers would keep production records and report annual output to ANSA.[@1] This built on the [2024 authorisation route](/history/moldova-hemp-authorisation-2024/) while giving the crop a wider commercial purpose.[@1]

## The crop still had a statutory ceiling

The new controlled-substance law defines industrial and horticultural hemp by a THC content no higher than 0.3%, determined by an accredited laboratory.[@2] Its definition covers industrial, horticultural and food uses and excludes medical, recreational and experimental uses from that hemp category.[@2] Article 14 places authorised hemp cultivation with ANSA and restricts growers to varieties listed in Moldova's, the EU's or EU member states' plant catalogues.[@2]

Seed supply follows that authorisation system too: distributors may provide seed intended for cultivation only to permit holders.[@2] The law also requires landholders to destroy spontaneously growing controlled plants and bear the costs of destroying unauthorised crops.[@2] These requirements make a farmer's authorised crop different from an unrestricted right to plant cannabis at home.[@2]

## Inspection follows the crop into flowering

The April announcement specified representative samples collected during flowering, with analysis by accredited laboratories, including laboratories abroad.[@1] ANSA could inspect and require correction of breaches or withdraw authorisation.[@1] The statute separately provides for annual crop estimates and reporting between authorisation holders and the responsible agencies.[@2]

## Recreational offences continued alongside hemp farming

Moldova continued to prohibit growing cannabis to obtain narcotic substances under its [new controlled-substance law](/history/moldova-drug-law-2026/).[@2] The [August 2026 penalty changes](/history/moldova-drug-penalties-2026/) retained separate personal-cultivation and supply offences, while raising the fine for small personal possession.[@3] A lawful industrial harvest therefore depends on its purpose, authorisation and variety as well as the THC limit.[@2] The [Moldova profile](/country/md/) records those agricultural permissions alongside the penalties that still apply to unauthorised recreational activity.[@2,3]
