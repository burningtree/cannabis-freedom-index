The idea that hashish turns men into killers has a date and an author. It was put
forward in Paris in July 1809 by France's leading scholar of Arabic, as a piece of
etymology.

## The lecture

Antoine-Isaac Silvestre de Sacy told the Institut de France that the word "assassin"
came from "Hachichin", hashish-eater. That, he argued, was the Arabic name of a sect of
drugged killers who had operated in Persia and Syria in the twelfth and thirteenth
centuries, sent out to murder by a leader who controlled them with the drug.

The story was not new to French readers. A century earlier the Thousand and One Nights
had associated hashish with Oriental trickery. Sacy gave it the authority of philology.

It was almost certainly wrong. The sect in question, the Nizari Ismailis, probably did
not use hashish at all.

## What the pharmacists heard

Four months later the journal of the French Society of Pharmacy summarised the lecture
and drew a practical conclusion. Hashish, it said, produced an ecstasy like opium's, and
could drive users to "brutal actions that arise from dementia and delirium". Then:

> But cannabis deserves the attention of pharmacists and chemists.

A substance so powerful must contain something useful. The supposed danger was read as
an invitation.

## Two legacies

The first was medical. By the 1840s the main apothecaries of Paris were selling dozens
of hashish medicines.
[Jacques-Joseph Moreau](/history/france-moreau-du-hachisch-1845/), who cited Sacy's
assassins, proposed hashish as a way of producing temporary madness in order to study
it. Paris hospitals used hashish tincture against
[cholera](/history/france-hashish-cholera-treatment-1848/) in 1848.

The second was legal, and lasted longer. The association of hashish with the Orient,
irrationality and violence passed into medical textbooks as established fact. The
Egyptian delegate who brought cannabis into international law in
[1924](/history/geneva-egypt-proposal-1924/) argued from the same premise. And the
chapter's author traces it into the French drug law of
[1970](/history/france-use-criminalised-1970/), whose sponsors blamed the country's
cannabis problem on "undesirable foreigners".

The index lists the lecture for context. It changed no law in [France](/country/fr/),
but few single texts have done more to shape what later laws assumed.
