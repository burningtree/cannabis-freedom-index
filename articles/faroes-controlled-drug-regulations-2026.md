New Faroese controlled-drug regulations took effect on 15 June 2026, keeping cannabis within a system of medical, scientific and agricultural permissions.[@1] They replaced the January 2025 regulations and set out who could receive controlled drugs, grow cannabis and handle low-THC commercial crops.[@1]

## A separate route for the farmer

A grower seeking a commercial low-THC crop must apply for an annual permit, covering identified land with each continuous plot measuring at least 0.3 hectares.[@1] The crop must contain no more than 0.2% THC.[@1] Applications go through Búnaðarstovan, with a police opinion before the medicines authority decides after consultation with the Chief Pharmacist.[@1]

The permission comes with oversight: crops can be sampled and analysed, and permits can be withdrawn.[@1] Buying, receiving, possessing and processing plants grown under the permit are allowed, but processing cannot produce preparations above 0.2% THC.[@1] The cannabis definition separately excludes hemp seeds and isolated fibre, while growing the plant remains regulated.[@1]

## Medical growing requires secure premises

The regulations provide licensed growing purposes connected with medical-cannabis production, pharmaceutical raw materials and propagation for other authorised growers.[@1] Medical crops must be grown in specified areas or premises, separated from other cultivation or manufacture.[@1] An approved security manager supervises the work and access, and the medicines authority can check the background of the operator and security manager.[@1]

Distribution of plants and plant parts is restricted to authorised growers or overseas businesses holding the necessary medical-growing permissions.[@1] The text also preserves separate arrangements for scientific work at state laboratories and institutions.[@1] These permissions describe controlled activities and their safeguards, without establishing that a commercial medical grower is actually operating in the islands.[@1]

## Patients use the prescribing route

Patients may receive and possess controlled medicines through a lawful prescription under the regulations.[@1] The Chief Medical Officer confirms that a [medical-cannabis trial scheme](/history/faroes-medical-cannabis-trial-2019/) has operated since April 2019.[@3] The authority explains that trial products face different quality, safety and efficacy requirements from approved medicines, leaving more limited knowledge of possible risks.[@3]

## Recreational offences still reach small amounts

The underlying [drug-control law](/history/faroes-euphoriant-substances-order-1956/) punishes its ordinary offences with a fine or up to two years in prison.[@2] Its sentencing rules give extra weight to specified supply circumstances, including sales at youth events.[@2] The 2026 regulations continue to reserve controlled-drug use for medical and scientific purposes, while the [Faroe Islands profile](/country/fo/) records the separate hemp permits and the limited patient route.[@1,3]
