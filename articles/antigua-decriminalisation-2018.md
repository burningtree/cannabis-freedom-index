The Misuse of Drugs (Amendment) Act, 2018 is a short law of seven sections. The House of Representatives passed it on 6 February 2018 and the Senate on 15 February; the Governor-General assented on 1 March and it was published in the Gazette on 5 March. Its central sentence is the new section 6A: a person in possession of a maximum of 15 grams of cannabis or cannabis resin "is not guilty of an offence".

## What the Act allows

| Subject | Rule |
|---|---|
| Possession | up to 15 g of cannabis or resin is no offence, and "no penalty capable of being imposed under this Act" applies (section 6A) |
| Growing | the head of a household, owner or tenant may grow "not more than four plants" per household on the property (section 8(1A)) |
| Old convictions | any record of a conviction involving 15 g or less "shall be regarded as spent and expunged" (section 39) |
| Children | a person under 18 found with cannabis must take part in a drug counselling programme (section 6C) |

The bill as the House debated it was reported by IDPC with a limit of 10 grams. The Act as assented says 15.

## What stays an offence

Section 6B bans smoking cannabis in a public place, and makes it an offence for the owner or manager of such a place knowingly to let people sell, supply or smoke it there. Public places include bars, restaurants, clubs, government offices and tourist establishments, apart from areas the management marks out for smoking. A private home not used for business is not a public place.

The ladder for public smoking is a police warning the first time, a $500 violation ticket the second, and a fine of up to $1,500 on conviction from the third. None of these goes on a criminal record. Selling and supplying are untouched.

## The politics

Prime Minister Gaston Browne backed the bill, calling cannabis "part of the culture of the country". IDPC reported that he recalled seeing the police use excessive force against Rastafarians over alleged cannabis offences.

## Why the index moved

This is the largest single step in the country's timeline. Possession rises from 1 to 4, because 15 grams is lawful for anyone, with no ticket and no fine. Cultivation rises from 1 to 4: four plants per household are lawful. Enforcement rises from 2 to 6, since both acts are outside the criminal law and past convictions are wiped. Consumption rises from 1 to 3, held back by the ban on smoking in public. Sharing stays at 1 and access at 0: the Act opens no lawful way to buy. The total moves from 11 to 36. The licensed medical and sacramental system came with the [Cannabis Act](/history/antigua-cannabis-act-2018/) ten months later.
