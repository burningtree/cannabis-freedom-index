Berar’s Hemp Drugs Law was issued in 1897 and came into force on 1 April 1898, according to the district gazetteer. Separating the making of the orders from their commencement corrects the timeline without discarding the familiar name of the legislation.

## Powers in the 1897 law

James H. Mills describes orders issued by the British Resident in November 1897 covering production, sale, possession and import of intoxicating drugs in the Hyderabad Assigned Districts. They empowered the Resident to prohibit hemp cultivation or permit it subject to a licence.

A power to issue a prohibition is not identical to proof that the prohibition took effect on the same day. The distinction is important here because the later administrative account supplies a commencement date and describes implementation under a subsequent administrative year.

## Commencement in April 1898

The Buldana district gazetteer explicitly places the law’s commencement on 1 April 1898. The history entry now uses that effective date, while retaining its original identifier ending in 1897 and explaining the difference.

The source is a retrospective official account reproduced on the Government of Maharashtra’s gazetteer website. It is evidence for the administrative chronology, but the original commencement instrument has not been independently retrieved for this article.

## Cultivation and the supply route

The gazetteer reports cultivation prohibition and imports restricted to the Khandwa storehouse in the administrative year 1898–1899. Its account also describes the adoption of the Central Provinces duty and wholesale system.

Those details support a shift towards controlling supply through authorized imports rather than continued local cultivation. They do not independently identify the precise calendar day of the cultivation-ban implementing rule. Assigning that separate prohibition automatically to 1 April would go beyond the passage.

## Earlier controls still matter

Berar had already introduced [cultivation licences in 1875](/history/india-berar-cultivation-licences-1875/) and revised the [fee structure in 1884](/history/india-berar-cultivation-fee-1884/). The later law therefore belongs within an existing regulatory history, rather than marking the first governmental involvement with cannabis there.

The event remains regional context for [India](/country/in/). The correction clarifies commencement and implementation without creating a duplicate event for the same law, inventing a day for every subsequent rule, or applying a Berar restriction to the whole subcontinent.
