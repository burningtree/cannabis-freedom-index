Andorra’s Constitutional Court upheld a cannabis trafficking conviction involving flower sold as CBD. Its judgment of 14 July 2025 left a resident facing three years’ imprisonment, twelve months of it immediate, a €500 fine and five years’ expulsion from the country.[@1]

## Low THC did not keep flower outside the law

The seized flower weighed 503.2 g and contained 0.3% THC. The court accepted the criminal courts’ interpretation that cannabis remained controlled irrespective of its low THC concentration.[@1] The appellant said he used it therapeutically and had previously bought similar products openly in Andorra; the judgment recorded that no doctor had prescribed cannabis to him.[@1]

The court also accepted the evidence supporting a supply purpose, including the quantity and how the flower was concealed in the vehicle. It dismissed the constitutional appeal in full.[@1] For a buyer, the consequence was substantial: a product marketed as CBD could still bring the penalties associated with cannabis trafficking.[@1]

## A parliamentary opening had already closed

The legal dispute unfolded alongside attempts to change the rules on CBD sales and consumption. Andorra Endavant brought a bill to parliament in 2024, but the government gave an unfavourable opinion on 26 June.[@3] Parliament rejected consideration on 15 July, leaving that proposed route to authorised products closed.[@3]

The vote preceded the constitutional decision by a year. The parliamentary record and the later judgment show two separate routes through which reform was sought: changing the legislation and challenging the conviction under constitutional rights.[@1,3]

## Expulsion reached beyond the prison sentence

The public broadcaster reported that the family intended to take the case to the European Court of Human Rights after the constitutional appeal failed.[@2] Its account highlighted both imprisonment and expulsion: the conviction affected the resident’s ability to return to his life in Andorra.[@2] The broadcaster also reported that the court rejected complaints about defence rights and judicial impartiality.[@2]

## Medicinal cultivation stayed on a separate track

By September 2026 the government was discussing a future indoor medicinal crop with a single cultivation licence. A separate cannabis bill still had to be brought to parliament, according to the contemporary account.[@4] Agricultural diversification supplied the setting for that proposal, with farmers’ collective participation part of the wider reform.[@4]

That later [cultivation plan](/history/andorra-medicinal-cultivation-plan-2026/) followed the failed [CBD bill](/history/andorra-rejects-cbd-bill-2024/) and the court case. Together they explain the distance between proposals for a new industry and the criminal law encountered by cannabis users in [Andorra](/country/ad/).[@1,3,4]
