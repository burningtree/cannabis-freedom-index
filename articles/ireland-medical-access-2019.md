On 26 June 2019 Ireland's health minister, Simon Harris, signed the legislation that set up the Medical Cannabis Access Programme. It was announced as a five-year pilot, more than two years after the minister had first promised an access scheme.

## Who it is for

A medical consultant may prescribe a listed cannabis-based treatment for a patient under their care who has not responded to standard treatments, for one of three conditions:

- spasticity associated with multiple sclerosis;
- intractable nausea and vomiting associated with chemotherapy;
- severe, treatment-resistant epilepsy.

The decision, Harris said, rests with the consultant in consultation with the patient.

## How supply works

The programme works through listed products. It lets commercial suppliers whose products meet set requirements apply to have them listed and sold on the Irish market. Possessing, supplying or importing the products needs a controlled drugs licence, processed by the Health Products Regulatory Authority.

That design made access depend on suppliers coming forward. Harris said at the signing that it was "going to take a little bit of time to fully establish the scheme", and patients were not expected to reach it before the autumn.

## What it did not change

The minister was explicit: "there are no plans to legalise cannabis in this country." The scheme was described as compassionate access where conventional treatment has failed, and the offences and penalties for unauthorised supply and possession were left as they were.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Ireland](/country/ie/) from 11 to 12 with the [2019 programme](/history/ireland-medical-access-2019/). Only access changed, from 0 to 1: a lawful prescription route now existed, limited to three conditions, a consultant's prescription and whatever products suppliers chose to register.
