On 27 November 2019 the Senate of Barbados passed two cannabis bills in one sitting. The House of Assembly had passed the Sacramental Cannabis Bill five days earlier. Between them they opened two narrow doors in a ban that dated from [1936](/history/barbados-dangerous-drugs-act-1936/): one for patients, one for Rastafari.

## Medicine by prescription

The Medicinal Cannabis Industry Act sets up a licensing authority, a board and an appeals tribunal. Section 25 states the rule for patients: a person may use medicinal cannabis only where a medical practitioner has prescribed it. For a minor, a doctor must certify that it is necessary and a parent or guardian must consent in writing. Pharmacies and licensed "therapeutic facilities" dispense it; where a prescription runs over three months, they may hand over at most a 30-day supply at a time.

Using medicinal cannabis without a prescription is an indictable offence with a fine of $100,000, ten years in prison, or both.

## Licences

| Licence | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Cultivator (four tiers, from under 1 acre to over 25) | growing, harvesting, drying, packaging |
| Processor (three tiers) | manufacturing products |
| Retail Distributor | running a therapeutic facility that dispenses to patients |
| Import, Export, Transport | moving cannabis and planting material |
| Research and Development, Laboratory | research and testing |

Applicants must be adults who are citizens, permanent residents, immigrants or CARICOM nationals, or a company at least 30 per cent owned by such people. A licence runs for five years and cannot be transferred.

## Sacrament at a place of worship

The Sacramental Cannabis Act lets an adherent of the Rastafarian religion use cannabis as a sacrament "in a place of worship", in assembly with other Rastafarians. The person in charge of the place applies to the minister for a permit, which allows cannabis to be grown, prepared, smoked and stored on the premises, though not within 600 metres of a school. A separate one-day permit allows worship at a public place, to which each person may carry up to 14 grams.

Selling the cannabis, carrying it outside a permitted event, importing it or letting a minor use it brings a fine of $10,000, two years in prison, or both.

## Why the index moved

Only access changes, from 0 to 1: there is now a lawful route to cannabis, but it runs through a doctor's prescription and a licensed dispenser. The sacramental permit covers a congregation at its own premises and gives an individual no right to hold or grow cannabis at home, so possession, cultivation and consumption stay where they were. The total moves from 11 to 12. Possession by everyone else was dealt with [sixteen months later](/history/barbados-fines-2021/).
