Brittney Griner, a double Olympic champion, spent the American off-season playing for
UMMC Ekaterinburg. On 17 February 2022 customs officers at Moscow's Sheremetyevo
airport found vape cartridges with cannabis oil in her luggage. A week later Russia
invaded Ukraine, and for ten months the cartridges were an item in the diplomacy
between Washington and Moscow.

## The law she met

Russian law has two tiers. Up to 6 g of cannabis or 2 g of hashish is an
administrative offence: a fine, or up to 15 days' detention, and deportation for a
foreigner. Above that, possession is a crime under Article 228. The threshold was set
at 20 g in [2004](/history/russia-decriminalisation-2004/) and cut to 6 g in
[2006](/history/russia-six-grams-2006/).

Griner's case was different in kind. She had carried the oil across a border, and
was convicted of smuggling as well as possession, with a maximum of ten years.

## The case

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 17 February 2022 | Detained at Sheremetyevo |
| 3 May | The United States classifies her as "wrongfully detained" |
| 7 July | Pleads guilty; says she packed the cartridges by accident |
| 4 August | Nine years in prison and a fine of one million roubles |
| 25 October | Appeal rejected |
| 17 November | Confirmed to be in penal colony IK-2 in Mordovia |
| 8 December | Freed in exchange for the arms dealer Viktor Bout |

Her lawyers submitted an American doctor's recommendation of medical cannabis for
pain and her clean anti-doping tests. Prosecutors asked for nine and a half years.
The defence said comparable cases averaged about five.

> I made an honest mistake, and I hope in your ruling it does not end my life.

## How unusual was it?

For a foreigner, not very. In June 2022 a Russian court gave Marc Fogel, an American
teacher, 14 years for bringing in vape cartridges and cannabis flower. For Russians,
Article 228 is among the most used in the criminal code: about 50,800 convictions in
2024, roughly one in ten of all convictions. In 2025 the Supreme Court itself
[proposed softening](/history/russia-supreme-court-proposal-2025/) the penalty for
personal possession.

Nothing in Russian law changed, so this entry moves no score. It is here because it
shows how the law works on a visitor: a fraction of a gram, carried across a border,
brought a sentence close to the maximum, and what ended it was a trade between
governments. [Russia's profile](/country/ru/) has the rules as they stand.
