On 24 September 2001 Haiti gave itself the drug law it still lives under. The Senate had
passed the trafficking bill on 19 June, the Chamber of Deputies on 7 August, and
promulgation that September made it the frame for every cannabis case since.[@1]

## What it bans

Article 10 bans growing the cannabis plant outright and makes landholders destroy
plantations, with permits only for medical or scientific purposes under article 43.[@1]
Offering, selling or holding drugs for supply draws up to thirty years under article 49;
giving drugs to someone for their own use draws five years under article 50.[@1]

## The small-quantity exception

Article 63 is the law's softer core: growing, buying or holding a quantity small enough
for personal consumption brings up to six months and a 5,000-to-100,000-gourde fine — or
either — and the court decides what counts as small.[@1] The judge may order a
detoxification cure or other care instead of, or besides, the penalty.[@1]

## Still the frame

A 1975 narcotics decree came before it; nothing has replaced it since.[@1] The court
decides the small quantity case by case, the police count seizures by the kilo, and the
security crisis decides how much of the law reaches the street.
