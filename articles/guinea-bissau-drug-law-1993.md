Guinea-Bissau's 1993 drug law replaced an early post-independence statute with detailed rules for
trafficking, possession, cultivation and personal consumption. Cannabis and cannabis resin appear
expressly in its highest-risk schedule.

## Replacing the 1976 law

Decree-Law No. 2-B/93 is dated 28 October 1993. Its preamble says Guinea-Bissau had adopted
Decree-Law No. 1/76 soon after independence, but that sixteen years of experience called for both
revised measures and institutional changes. The new text was also intended to harmonise national
law with international drug-control conventions.

The annex places cannabis and cannabis resin in Table I. Article 3 applies to unauthorised acts
involving plants and substances in Tables I and II, including cultivation, production, sale,
purchase, transfer, transport, import and possession. The published edition gives a range of two
to twelve years, while noting in a footnote that the official gazette text omitted the lower
number.

## A separate personal-use rule

Article 20 covers someone who consumes, cultivates, acquires or possesses a small quantity for
personal consumption. A cannabis derivative other than cannabis oil carries one to six months in
prison. Cannabis oil, treated as a high-risk substance, carries two months to one year.

The article supplies no fixed gram threshold. It turns instead on whether the small quantity can
be regarded as intended for personal consumption. A waiver is possible only where three listed
conditions are all met: the person is below legal adulthood, is not a repeat offender, and
solemnly promises before a magistrate not to offend again.

The law separately gives up to two years where prohibited conduct is carried out exclusively to
obtain drugs for the accused's own use. That reduced provision stops applying when the amount
exceeds an average individual's five-day supply.

## Treatment and sharing

Voluntary users seeking health care are promised anonymity and medical confidentiality. A court
may suspend a dependent offender's sentence on condition of treatment. Sharing remains distinct:
facilitating illicit use of a high-risk drug can bring up to three years.
