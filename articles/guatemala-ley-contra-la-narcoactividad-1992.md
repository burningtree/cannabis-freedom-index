Guatemala’s drug law, signed on 24 September 1992, prescribed four months to two years in prison plus a fine for acquiring or possessing drugs for personal consumption. It set much longer terms for unauthorised cultivation and trafficking, making the purpose of possession central to a cannabis user’s exposure to punishment.

## The older code had grouped several acts together

The [1973 penal code](/history/guatemala-penal-code-1973/) prescribed three to five years plus a fine for unauthorised cultivation of drug-producing plants. Its trafficking section used that same sentence range for importing, selling, supplying, transporting and keeping drugs.

The 1992 law created a detailed system of drug offences. Its stated purposes included protecting health, addressing trafficking and carrying out Guatemala’s commitments under international drug treaties.

Seeds, flowers, plants and plant parts from which drugs could be extracted came within its definitions, extending the offences to cultivation and planting material.

## Personal consumption depended on quantity and circumstances

A person acquiring or holding drugs for their own consumption faced a prison term and a fine of 200 to 10,000 quetzales.

To count as personal use, the seized amount had to be reasonable for immediate consumption. The other circumstances also had to support the conclusion that the drugs were for that person’s own use.

The law expressed the boundary through purpose and circumstances, giving no fixed cannabis quantity in grams. A small amount therefore still required an assessment of what it was intended for.

## Growing and supply carried much longer terms

Unauthorised sowing, growing or harvesting of drug-producing seeds, plants, flowers or plant parts carried five to twenty years in prison and a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 quetzales.

Trafficking carried twelve to twenty years and a fine of 50,000 to one million quetzales. Its wording covered acquisition, transfer, import, export, storage, transport, distribution and supply, alongside sales.

Those broad supply offences affected people handing drugs to others as well as businesses or organised sellers. Unauthorised manufacture and extraction had another prison range, eight to twenty years, together with a fine.

## Reformers later sought cannabis exceptions

In [2016](/history/guatemala-adult-cannabis-bill-2016/), lawmakers proposed private adult cannabis use, five home plants and licensed supply. Their bill would add cannabis exceptions to the possession, growing and distribution offences.

It received [committee opposition](/history/guatemala-regulation-rejected-2016/). The distinction between personal consumption and trafficking remained central to [Guatemala’s cannabis rules](/country/gt/).
