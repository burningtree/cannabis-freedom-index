Colombia's latest attempt to regulate adult-use cannabis cleared its first
legislative test in May 2026. The House of Representatives' First Committee
approved the bill unanimously, but several stages still stood between that vote
and a legal market.

## The ninth attempt

Bill 023 of 2025 was the ninth cannabis-regulation proposal debated by Congress
since 2020. Every earlier proposal had been shelved during the legislative process.

Representative Alejandro Ocampo presented the new bill as a way to keep cannabis
away from children, reduce the illegal market and include rural, Indigenous and
Afro-Colombian communities that have long lived with the crop.

After the committee vote, the proposal was due for a second debate in the full
House. Approval there would send it to the Senate. *Infobae* reported that the
crowded parliamentary calendar could push further debate into the legislature
beginning on 20 July 2026.

## A regulated market

The bill proposes a complete chain from cultivation to licensed sale. Dispensaries
and specialised pharmacies could sell to adults in person or online, using identity
checks and digital traceability. Advertising, promotion and sponsorship would be
barred outside digital spaces directed only at adults.

The proposal would impose a 20% consumption tax. At least half of legally
distributed flower would have to come from associations of traditional ethnic and
peasant growers, and 70% of cultivation licences would be reserved for those
communities.

## Growing and clubs

An adult could grow up to 20 plants at home for non-commercial personal use.
Commercial growers would need a licence and would be subject to agricultural,
environmental and cultivation-plan requirements.

Non-profit cannabis clubs could have between 10 and 350 registered adult members
and grow up to 200 plants solely for them. Retail purchases would be capped at 20 g
of flower or 5 g of concentrates per day, with a larger registered exception of up
to 500 g of flower in justified cases.

Minors would be excluded from production, distribution and use. Consumption would
be restricted within 100 metres of schools, playgrounds, sports centres and
religious sites, and driving under the influence would remain prohibited.
