In February 2020 Malawi's parliament passed a bill legalising cannabis cultivation for
medicine and industry, and the president assented to it as Act No. 6 of 2020 on
30 April 2020.[@1,2] Growing, processing, selling and exporting cannabis became legal
with a licence from a new Cannabis Regulatory Authority — while doing any of it
without one drew up to 25 years in prison and a $70,000 fine.[@1,2]

## Tobacco's replacement

The motive was the export ledger. Tobacco dominated foreign exchange, and cannabis —
Malawi Gold is among Africa's most famous sativas — promised a successor crop.[@3]
The Act licensed the full chain (cultivate, process, store, export, distribute,
research) for medicinal, industrial and scientific ends, and pointedly nothing
recreational: licensing bodies stress the law does not advocate or accept
adult use.[@1,3]

The statute built a detailed machine: licence types for each activity, production
practices set by the regulator, a registry tracking qualified patients and their
caregivers, and industrial hemp defined at no more than 1% THC.[@1] Contravention sat
under two hammers — K50 million and 25 years under the new Act, and the old
Dangerous Drugs Act's fine plus life maximum behind it.[@1]

## A slow start

Clinicians welcomed the opening but warned the country would only benefit if its
medical and scientific community took the lead, making the case for CBD therapies
including for complicated malaria.[@5] Licensing moved first: by April 2021 the
Authority had issued 86 licences to 35 companies and cooperatives for industrial hemp
production.[@4] The hemp itself disappointed growers — buyers were scarce and imported
seed costly — which is why parliament returned in 2024 to bring the local chamba
variety into the licensed system.[@4]

[Malawi's profile](/country/mw/) now reflects the scheme the 2020 Act built and the
[2024 amendment](/history/malawi-local-chamba-2024/) extended, with recreational use
still fully illegal.
