On 1 July 1990, Czechoslovakia stopped treating possession of a drug for one's own use as a criminal offence. The reform did not legalise production or supply, but it drew a sharp new line between a person holding a substance for themselves and conduct carried out for someone else.

## The old offence

The 1961 criminal code punished a person who, without permission, manufactured, imported, exported, procured for another or possessed narcotic substances or poisons. The Adiktologie legal history says the basic offence could bring up to two years' imprisonment, corrective measures or a fine, with higher terms for aggravated cases.

That formulation carried the controlled-drug approach of the communist period into the final months before the political system changed. Personal possession could fall within criminal liability because the text did not require possession to be for another person.

## The July amendment

Act No. 175/1990 amended section 187 with effect from 1 July. It extended the terminology to psychotropic substances, but, crucially, removed punishment for keeping a controlled substance for oneself. Under the amended wording, possession had to be on behalf of another person to constitute the offence.

The reform followed the fall of the communist government and a broader liberalisation of society. The Czech government's drug-policy history also records that the federal government created a national narcotics advisory commission in 1990 as it began developing a more comprehensive policy response.

## What remained prohibited

This was decriminalisation, not a regulated cannabis market. Unauthorised production, import, export, procurement for others and possession for others remained within the criminal law. There were no licensed adult-use shops, clubs or rules authorising home cultivation.

The personal-possession rule lasted for the remainder of Czechoslovakia and the first six years of the independent Czech Republic. On 1 January [1999](/history/czechia-recriminalisation-1999/), Czech law again made possession for personal use criminal when the amount was “greater than small.”

## What moved in the index

The change raised [Czechia's historical](/country/cz/) possession score from 1 to 3, enforcement from 2 to 5 and consumption from 2 to 4. Cultivation remained 1, sharing 1 and access 0. The score increase reflects removal of the possession crime, while the unchanged low scores preserve the continuing bans on growing, supplying and legal access.
