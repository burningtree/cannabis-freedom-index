An Austrian Finance Ministry clarification reported in **1905** separates two questions often combined in accounts of early cannabis controls: whether hemp cigarettes infringed the fiscal monopoly at home, and whether they could be imported from abroad.

The contemporary *Laibacher Zeitung* identifies an order dated **12 April 1905**. Its report appeared on **27 May 1905**, in issue 122, page **1088**. The database uses the reported order date; the complete ministry instrument and any separate commencement provision have not been retrieved.

## Domestic manufacture and sale

The notice says that production and sale of so-called Indian or hemp cigarettes within the country were not prohibited under the existing monopoly regulations. Consequently, fiscal objections were not to be raised on those grounds.

The German word **Verschleiß** here concerns sale or distribution. Translating it as personal use would expand the reported clarification beyond its commercial and fiscal subject. The notice does not discuss a general right to consume cannabis, or the conditions under which a patient could obtain a medicinal preparation.

Nor does the report establish that the ministry repealed an earlier blanket ban. It describes the position under existing monopoly rules. For that reason, this event is classified as a clarification rather than a newly established general legalization.

## Imports remained conditional

The same paragraph states that bringing such cigarettes in from abroad required special permission from the fiscal authority. Importers also had to pay the customs and licence charges prescribed for tobacco cigarettes.

This creates a clear distinction between domestic trade and import access. The absence of a domestic monopoly prohibition did not mean that imports were unrestricted. The newspaper gives no amount for the charges and does not identify a particular applicant, permission or completed shipment.

## Limits of the evidence

The report establishes a fiscal distinction, not the complete pharmaceutical law governing every cigarette or ingredient. It does not say that a fiscal permission displaced health controls, prescription requirements or restrictions on a named foreign manufacturer's product.

The entry is therefore unscored context in the [Austrian](/country/at/) chronology. Its narrow wording preserves the useful finding: early regulation could treat locally made hemp cigarettes and imported products differently, without settling every question about their medicinal supply or personal use.
