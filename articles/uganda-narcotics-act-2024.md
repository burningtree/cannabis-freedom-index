Uganda passed the same drug law twice. The first version was struck down on 5 May 2023, when the Constitutional Court found that Parliament had lacked a quorum when it voted. The court stressed that this did not make cannabis legal. Parliament passed a 94-clause replacement that August, and the President assented to it on 2 February 2024.

## What the Act says about possession

Section 4 makes it an offence to have any narcotic drug in one's possession. The penalty is a fine of up to 50,000 currency points, or three times the market value of the drug if that is more, or up to twenty years in prison, or both. A currency point is 20,000 shillings, so the fine can reach a billion.

There is no smaller offence for a small amount, and no mention of personal use. The exceptions are for licence holders, doctors, pharmacists, and patients with a prescription.

## The other offences

| Offence | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Possession (Section 4) | 20 years |
| Trafficking (Section 5) | life |
| Using, or being found where drugs are used (Section 6) | 10 years |
| Growing without a licence (Section 11) | 5 years; life for a second offence |

Two details matter for ordinary users. Section 6 punishes not only smoking, inhaling, sniffing or chewing a drug but being found, without lawful excuse, in a place people resort to for it, and possessing a pipe or other utensil.

And the Act's definition of trafficking includes "giving" and "supplying". Passing cannabis to a friend without payment is the same offence as selling it, and carries life.

## A licence for growers

The Act is not only prohibition. The health minister may issue a licence to cultivate or gather a prohibited plant, on conditions the minister sets by regulation. Growing without one brings a fine of up to 120 currency points, three times the crop's value, or up to five years.

Section 1 leaves the start to a date the internal affairs minister appoints. Statutory Instrument 42 of 2025 appointed 14 April 2025.

## Why the index moved

The score moves on [14 April 2025](/history/uganda-narcotics-commencement-2025/), the day the Act came into force, and lowers [Uganda's](/country/ug/) possession score from 1 to 0 and its sharing score from 1 to 0. Twenty years with no lesser tier is a law under which prison is a realistic outcome for a small amount, and a gift that counts as trafficking is the lowest level of the sharing scale. Growing stays at 1, because the separate five-year offence is far lighter than trafficking. The [February 2024 assent](/history/uganda-narcotics-act-2024/) is recorded on its own, with no score change. The Constitutional Court had said in 2023 that striking down the previous Act did not legalise cannabis, so the years before April 2025 stay on the earlier ban.
