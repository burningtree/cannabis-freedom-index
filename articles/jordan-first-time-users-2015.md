Jordan began sending people caught using drugs for the first time to treatment without prosecution on 16 May 2013. Police had to arrange the referral within twenty-four hours, and the incident would leave no judicial precedent against the person.

## Ministers argued that treatment would protect first-time users

The change amended the [earlier drug law](/history/jordan-drug-law-1988/), which punished personal drug use and allowed treatment in place of a sentence. Someone who sought treatment before being caught already had protection from prosecution.

In March 2013, Interior Minister Awad Khleifat presented the new proposal as a social reform informed by studies and experience in Arab and Islamic countries. He said a treatment centre could offer education and advice from specialists, psychologists and religious figures.

The Senate debated the proposal under its president, Taher Masri. It approved the version sent by the Lower House despite its own legal committee’s recommendation to keep the existing law.

## Being caught for the first time triggered a treatment referral

The amendment covered a person caught using narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances for the first time. Cannabis users came within that drug-control system.

Instead of bringing a public prosecution, the authorities had to send the person to the Anti-Narcotics Department’s specialist centre or another centre approved by the Interior Minister. The twenty-four-hour deadline ran from arrest.

The change therefore reached people already in police custody. It added a route to treatment after detection alongside the older protection for people who came forward voluntarily.

## A special register recorded the referral

The centre would enter the person’s name in a special register, under instructions issued by the Interior Minister. The incident would carry no judicial precedent.

For a first-time user, the immediate consequence shifted from exposure to a criminal prosecution to a required treatment referral. The law still involved arrest, official registration and the treatment system in that person’s life.

Its protection depended on being caught using drugs for the first time. Repeat offending remained subject to the wider drug law.

## The replacement law returned the decision to a court

In [2016](/history/jordan-first-offence-prosecution-2016/), Jordan replaced the older law. Personal drug use again carried a prison term and a fine, with a court able to substitute inpatient or outpatient treatment.

The replacement kept first-offence record protection and the exemption for seeking treatment before being caught. Those protections continued through the [2021 amendment](/history/jordan-drug-penalty-tiers-2021/), which helps explain the distinction between prosecution, treatment and records in [Jordan’s current rules](/country/jo/).
