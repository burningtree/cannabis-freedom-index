Taiwan's constitutional justices ruled on 20 March 2020 that the cannabis-growing law needed room for minor cases. A five-year minimum could punish a person raising a very small crop for themselves too severely. The ruling required a more proportionate sentence for that kind of case while leaving cultivation subject to criminal law.[@1,2]

## Six pots and a five-year sentence

The challenge followed the conviction of Huang Hsien-chang, a Tainan resident who had grown six potted cannabis plants at home. He received five years in prison and argued that the punishment was too harsh for growing for his own consumption. His case exposed how little room the statutory minimum left for a judge to distinguish personal growing from more serious conduct.[@3]

Even the ordinary provision for mitigating unusually sympathetic cases could reduce the minimum only to two and a half years. That still prevented the less restrictive outcomes the justices considered relevant to a minor offence, including a suspended sentence or conversion to a fine.[@1]

## Judges needed room to distinguish cases

The court accepted the legislature's reason for prohibiting cultivation. Its objection concerned the same heavy minimum being applied regardless of the seriousness of the conduct. Very small quantities grown only for personal use were its example of circumstances requiring a different response.[@1,2]

The justices gave lawmakers one year to revise the law. They also supplied an interim remedy: if the deadline passed without an amendment, courts could reduce the statutory punishment by up to half in minor cases. This mattered to defendants whose cases reached judges before a replacement provision took effect.[@1]

## A lower band followed in 2022

When rumours spread that cannabis was about to become legal, the Judicial Yuan issued an explanation in April 2020. Police would continue investigating cultivation; deciding whether a case was minor and what sentence followed remained a judicial task. A few plants did not become a protected allowance.[@2]

The [legislative amendment](/history/taiwan-personal-cultivation-amendment-2022/) eventually created a separate band for minor personal-use growing: one to seven years, with an optional fine up to NT$1 million. Parliament voted in April 2022; the amendment was promulgated and took effect on 4 May.[@4,5]

The distinction now turns on purpose and circumstances, rather than a fixed number of legal plants. In [Taiwan](/country/tw/), the ruling's lasting effect was to give courts a less severe sentencing route for the minor personal grower.[@2,4]
