Argentina's Supreme Court unanimously protected private personal drug possession in the *Arriola* judgment of 25 August 2009. The ruling concerned several men carrying small marijuana cigarettes and revived a constitutional principle the same court had abandoned nineteen years earlier.

## Small quantities, criminal convictions

Police proceedings in Rosario led to five men being convicted under Article 14, second paragraph, of Law 23.737. The cannabis consisted of one to three hand-rolled cigarettes per person, in quantities the trial court accepted were unequivocally for personal consumption.

Each received a suspended one-month prison sentence and conditions that included avoiding drugs and people associated with their supply or use. The defence argued that punishing this conduct invaded the private sphere protected by Article 19 of Argentina's constitution.

## The constitutional boundary

The Supreme Court agreed. It declared the possession offence unconstitutional when applied to personal use in circumstances that created no concrete danger or harm to other people's rights or property.

The court treated personal autonomy and dignity as limits on criminal law. It also rejected the idea that an abstract danger to public health was enough to punish private conduct, while stressing that distribution and conduct harming or endangering others could still be prosecuted.

## Reversing the judicial pendulum

The decision restored the approach of [*Bazterrica* in 1986](/history/argentina-bazterrica-1986/), which had protected personal possession. Parliament then enacted [Law 23.737 in 1989](/history/argentina-law-23737-1989/), and the court's 1990 *Montalvo* decision upheld punishment under that law.

*Arriola* reversed *Montalvo*, but did not repeal Article 14. The statute remained on the books, and the judgment set a constitutional rule for cases whose circumstances show private personal use without concrete harm. Courts still had to assess the facts of each prosecution. The ruling established no fixed gram limit and did not legalise sale or cultivation.

## What moved in the index

The ruling raised [Argentina's](/country/ar/) possession score from 1 to 3, enforcement from 2 to 3 and consumption from 1 to 3. Cultivation and sharing stayed at 1, while access stayed at 0. Those restrained gains reflect a judicial protection dependent on the circumstances of each case, not a legislative decriminalisation or a legal route to cannabis.
