Is it a crime to sell something that is legal to own, to a customer who may use it to
break the law? In 2012 Italy's highest court answered for cannabis seeds: no.

## The question

Growing cannabis is an offence in Italy. Seeds are not themselves a drug. Whether
selling them, with or without advice on how to grow, could be a crime went to the
united sections of the Court of Cassation, the court's most authoritative formation.

## The answer

The court ruled on 18 October 2012 and published its reasons on 7 December.

Offering seeds for sale, it held, is an unpunishable preparatory act. A seller cannot
know what the buyer will do with a seed, and the criminal law does not reach back that
far from the offence of cultivation.

It then dealt with the instructions. Selling seeds together with detailed growing
advice is not the drug-law offence of inducing others to use drugs. But the court left
one door open: depending on the facts, it may be prosecuted as incitement to commit a
crime under the ordinary criminal code.

## What it changed

The ruling gave Italian seed shops a clear footing. Selling the seed is lawful; what
the seller says about it can still be a risk.

## Elsewhere

Other countries drew the line differently. [Portugal](/history/portugal-seeds-law-2003/)
put seeds in its drug table in 2003; Oman and Tanzania moved against them in
[2026](/history/oman-narcotics-law-2026/).
