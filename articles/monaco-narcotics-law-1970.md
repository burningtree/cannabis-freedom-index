Monaco’s 1970 narcotics law made personal drug possession and use punishable by six months to three years in prison, a fine, or either penalty alone. Using drugs in a group fell under a much harsher offence, with three to ten years’ imprisonment in the original text.[@1]

## Personal use had its own offence

The law replaced parts of the [1918 poisonous-substances legislation](/history/monaco-poisonous-substances-law-1918/), while retaining existing implementing rules until new ones were issued.[@1] It defined controlled drugs by ministerial classification, allowing the detailed list of substances to sit outside the criminal statute itself.[@1]

For an individual user, the important distinction was the purpose of possession. Holding drugs for personal use belonged to the six-month-to-three-year offence; supply and facilitating another person’s use belonged to the heavier penalty range.[@1] Providing a place for someone else to use drugs could be punished whether or not money changed hands.[@1]

## The punishment could follow a person beyond prison

The original law required confiscation of seized drugs and allowed confiscation of equipment used to make, transport or use them.[@1] For the heavier offences, courts could also bar a person from the relevant profession and exclude a foreigner from Monaco for five to twenty years.[@1]

[Later amendments](/history/monaco-trafficking-penalty-structure-1987/) separated five-to-ten-year cultivation and supply penalties from ten-to-twenty-year import and export penalties.[@2] That distinction matters in a small territory where crossing the border is an ordinary part of daily life: the statutory import offence has a different penalty range from personal possession.[@2]

## Treatment entered the law alongside punishment

The [1985 amendments](/history/monaco-drug-treatment-orders-1985/) added medical examination and treatment procedures. A judge could order treatment for a minor, and adult defendants could also be required to undergo treatment alongside proceedings or a sentence.[@2]

The distinction between the maximum penalty and an actual sentence remained visible decades later. In April 2025 a pensioner with a clean record received one month suspended, two years of probation and treatment obligations after prosecution involving cannabis, CBD spray and chewing gum.[@3]

## A separate rule covers authorised medicines

Current regulations prohibit cannabis, its resin and derivatives, but contain specific exceptions for authorised pharmaceutical medicines and permissions for research or industrial varieties.[@4] The [2019 medicines exception](/history/monaco-authorised-cannabis-medicines-2019/) and continuing criminal prosecutions form different parts of the same legal system.[@3,4] The [Monaco profile](/country/mc/) sets out those present distinctions and the current penalty tables.[@2,4]
