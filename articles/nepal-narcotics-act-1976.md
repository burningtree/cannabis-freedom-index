In the 1960s and early 1970s cannabis was sold over the counter in Kathmandu. Government-licensed shops in Jhochhen Tole served travellers arriving on the overland route from Europe, and the street took the nickname it still has, Freak Street. Within three years that trade was first unlicensed and then criminal.

## Two steps

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| [1973](/history/nepal-shops-closed-1973/) | under sustained pressure from the United States and the United Nations, Nepal began revoking licences to produce, buy and sell cannabis |
| 1976 | the Narcotic Drugs (Control) Act criminalised cultivation, production, sale and consumption |

The first step took away the legal market. The second made the plant itself unlawful to grow or use. Nepali Times, the source for this article, notes one carve-out in the Act: "select religious purposes".

## Fifty years of the same Act

The 1976 Act is still the law. The newspaper describes several attempts to change it.

- **2020.** A former minister, Sher Bahadur Tamang, registered a private bill to legalise cannabis for its medicinal and industrial value. It stalled as governments changed.
- **2024.** The finance minister, Barshaman Pun, announced in the budget speech that medicinal cultivation would be legalised and commercialised. Nothing followed.
- **2026.** Gandaki Province passed its own law for licensed medicinal and industrial cultivation, with industrial cannabis limited to under 0.3 per cent THC. Recreational use stays illegal, and it is unclear how a provincial licence can work while the federal Act stands.

One campaigner quoted by the paper, Saurav Dhakal, calls illegal cultivation across Nepal "an open secret".

## Why the index moved

This is the largest fall in Nepal's timeline. Possession drops from 6 to 1, cultivation from 3 to 1 and sharing from 3 to 1, because growing, producing and selling all became crimes. Enforcement drops from 6 to 2. Consumption drops from 5 to 2 and not further, on account of the religious exception. Access had already gone to 0 when the shops closed. The total moves from 43 to 12.
