Bosnia and Herzegovina approved cannabis-based medicines at the end of 2025. Six
months later the people responsible met to establish why no patient had received
any.

## What had been decided

On [29 December 2025](/history/bosnia-medical-use-approved-2025/) the state's
Council of Ministers moved cannabis, its resin, extracts and tinctures from the
table of prohibited substances to the table of substances under strict control.
That made a cannabis-based medicine legally possible. It did not say who may
prescribe it, for what, or in what dose.

Those questions belong to the entities, in rulebooks on prescribing and dispensing
medicines.

## The meeting

A conference of health ministers on 8 April 2026 confirmed that the rulebooks
needed amending. A round table followed on 23 April. On 6 July the Ministry of
Civil Affairs called everyone back, because, in the words of the report, "no
concrete activities" had been undertaken since.

Present were the Federation's health ministry, the state medicines agency, the
pharmacists' chambers of both entities, the association of paediatric neurologists,
members of the Federation's parliament, and patients.

## What was agreed

- The Federation's health ministry is to draft the amendments by 1 August 2026.
- Within fifteen days it is to meet specialists in oncology, neurology, paediatric
  neurology, infectious diseases and anaesthesiology.

The rulebook has to settle the indications, the medical specialties allowed to
prescribe, registers of doctors and patients, doses and pharmaceutical forms, and
how the medicines are dispensed.

## Why the delay blocks everything

The medicines agency set out the chain. A country must send the International
Narcotics Control Board an annual estimate of its needs before a controlled drug
can be imported. Without a defined system of prescribing and distribution, the
agency cannot produce that estimate. Without the estimate, no import can be
approved. Growing cannabis for medicine remains prohibited, so imports are the only
source.

The paediatric neurologists asked for speed, pointing to patients with
drug-resistant epilepsy, for whom such medicines have been used elsewhere in Europe
for years. Once the framework exists, the pharmacy chambers said, they could prepare
matching formulations for both entities without delay.

No report found says whether the August deadline was met.
