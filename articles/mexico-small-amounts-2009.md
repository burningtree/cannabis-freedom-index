Mexico's 2009 drug-law reform created a national table of quantities treated as being for immediate personal consumption. For cannabis, the ceiling was five grams.

## A rule against prosecution

Article 478 of the amended General Health Law directed prosecutors not to bring the personal-possession offence against a consumer or drug-dependent person holding no more than the table amount for strict personal use.

The exception did not apply everywhere. It excluded cases in schools, care facilities, police or detention facilities, and within 300 metres of their boundaries. The decree took effect on 21 August 2009, the day after publication in the official gazette.

## Five grams, with a sharp edge

Article 479 set five grams as the maximum dose of cannabis for immediate personal consumption. The same table specified much smaller limits for heroin, cocaine, LSD, MDMA and methamphetamine.

Possession did not become generally legal. Above the personal-use ceiling, Article 477 provided ten months to three years in prison for possession below 1,000 times the table amount when the circumstances did not indicate sale or supply. Possession intended for commerce or supply was punished more severely.

The decree also defined supply broadly as directly or indirectly transferring possession by any means. Giving cannabis away therefore remained outside the personal-possession protection.

## Health referral, not a legal market

When prosecutors declined a case, they had to tell the consumer where treatment or prevention services were available and report the decision to the state health authority. The health authority was then to summon the person for guidance and urge participation in a programme. Treatment became compulsory for a drug-dependent person after a third report.

Nothing in the reform authorised cultivation, shops or another lawful source. It established a non-prosecution threshold inside a continuing prohibition and left police and prosecutors involved in determining whether its conditions were met.

## What moved in the index

For [Mexico](/country/mx/), the reform raised possession from 1 to 2, enforcement from 2 to 3 and consumption from 1 to 2. Cultivation and sharing remained at 1, and access remained 0. The limited gains reflect protection from prosecution at or below five grams, not legal possession or a regulated supply system.
