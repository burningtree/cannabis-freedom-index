Transnistria’s own drug law in 2002 prohibited growing cannabis for unlawful consumption or the illegal drug trade. Authorised cultivation of controlled plants belonged to state enterprises operating within government quotas. People who owned or used land also faced a duty to destroy prohibited plants growing there.[@1]

## Growing depended on purpose and permission

The president signed the law on 7 June. It organised the control of drugs, medicines and the plants from which they came, and provided for commencement on official publication. Its cultivation section named hemp separately from its prohibitions on opium poppy and coca.[@1]

For cannabis, the wording connected the ban to unlawful consumption or illegal drug circulation. Other paragraphs put authorised growing within state quotas and left decisions about banning particular varieties to the executive authorities. Health and interior authorities were also to set the procedures for permitting cultivation and industrial uses.[@1]

Those distinctions mattered for a person growing plants. Permission depended on the purpose and the regulated activity. The law gave ordinary household growers no general permission to raise cannabis for their own consumption.[@1]

## A landholder could be made to destroy plants

The law reached land as well as the person cultivating a crop. Its destruction rules required legal entities and individuals who owned or used land to destroy listed plants there. If a person failed to act, the authorities could arrange destruction at that person’s expense and pursue the applicable liability.[@1]

The wider law separated controlled substances into lists with different permitted uses. The strictest list could be used for the specified scientific, educational, expert and operational purposes; medical prescribing belonged to the rules for the second and third lists. Access depended on classification and authorisation.[@1]

This placed growing within a system of quotas, licences, permitted purposes and destruction duties. A claim that a plant had a useful purpose still had to fit the legal route provided for it.[@1]

## Courts explained what sowing meant

In [December 2004, the Supreme Court gave judges guidance on drug cases](/history/transnistria-court-guidance-2004/). Citing the 2002 law, it described sowing as planting seeds or seedlings without permission on any land, including vacant land. It said the sowing offence was complete even if the plants never emerged.[@2]

The growing offence later changed. The [2012 criminal-code reform](/history/transnistria-small-grow-possession-reform-2012/) made the cultivation crime depend on a legally defined large scale. The [Transnistria profile](/country/xt/) now distinguishes that criminal tier from the administrative penalty for growing below it. The 2002 law supplied the controlled-growing system within which those later rules developed.[@1,3]
