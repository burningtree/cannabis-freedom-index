The BVI House of Assembly approved a bill on 2 July 2020 offering a route to remove some historic cannabis convictions involving up to fifty grams of cannabis or ten grams of resin.[@1,2] It followed the [medical cannabis licensing bill](/history/bvi-cannabis-licensing-bill-2020/) passed two days earlier and still required assent before becoming law.[@1]

## Old records could obstruct work

Health minister Carvin Malone linked the proposed relief to the difficulties faced by people already convicted as the territory considered medical reform.[@3] He argued that a cannabis record could prevent someone obtaining a police certificate or finding employment, even as future medical users might avoid the same barriers.[@3]

The proposal addressed the consequences of past convictions alongside the separate plan for a licensed cannabis industry.[@2,3] Its quantity limits were part of the proposed record-clearing route.[@2]

## Existing law still criminalised small amounts

A [reported 2015 case](/history/bvi-cannabis-possession-fine-2015/) illustrates the punishment faced before the bills.[@4] A visitor admitted possessing 15.2 grams discovered in his luggage after arriving from the US Virgin Islands.[@4] The magistrate imposed a $5,000 fine, payable immediately or six months in prison, despite noting his cooperation and lack of previous convictions.[@4]

The drug law separately prohibited possession and presumed possession for supply above fifty grams of cannabis or resin unless the accused proved otherwise.[@5] Ordinary possession remained an offence below that amount.[@5] The bill's proposed fifty-gram cannabis and ten-gram resin limits therefore concerned relief from past records, alongside an existing criminal regime.[@2,5]

## The UK asked for further consideration

In [December 2020](/history/bvi-cannabis-bills-held-2020/), Governor Augustus Jaspert said the UK Foreign Secretary had instructed him to refer the record-clearing bill for further consideration.[@6] He explained that UK ministers were concerned about trafficking and regional security.[@6]

The licensing bill faced its own preliminary steps involving the UK Home Office and a formal agreement about regulatory responsibility.[@6] Both proposals consequently depended on further decisions after the House votes.[@6]

## The promised relief remained unresolved

Premier Natalio Wheatley later [outlined a possible route for doctors](/history/bvi-medical-cannabis-way-forward-2023/) under existing legislation in January 2023.[@7] That statement concerned medical administration and approval arrangements.[@7]

The July 2026 official legal review still recorded both 2020 bills as awaiting assent.[@2] People following the reform debate in the [British Virgin Islands](/country/vg/) therefore faced an unresolved promise of relief from historic cannabis convictions.[@2]
