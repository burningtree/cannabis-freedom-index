In 2018 Malta passed two cannabis laws within weeks. The first, enacted on 26 March, let doctors prescribe
cannabis to patients. The second let companies grow and process it, for medicine and research at home
and for export.

## For patients

The first law amended the Drug Dependence (Treatment not Imprisonment) Act, the statute that had
[stopped prosecutions](/history/malta-depenalisation-2015/) for small possession three years earlier.

| | The 2018 rules |
|---|---|
| Who may prescribe | Doctors, including family doctors |
| Conditions | Chronic pain, spasticity in multiple sclerosis, side effects of chemotherapy |
| Permission | A control card approved by the Superintendent of Public Health |
| Where | Pharmacies, on prescription |
| Form | Non-smoked preparations only |

Patients who did not use the medicine as prescribed could lose their card. The Malta Cannabis Social
Club reminded patients that smoking it was "not recommended and is illegal".

## For investors

The second law, the Production of Cannabis for Medicinal and Research Purposes Act (Chapter 578 of the
Laws of Malta), set up a licensing system. A company first needs a letter of intent from Malta
Enterprise, the state investment agency; the Medicines Authority then assesses it and inspects its
facility against European good manufacturing practice. As the law went through parliament, Malta
Enterprise said it had already approved five production projects, three Canadian and the others
Australian and Israeli, worth €30 million and promising 185 jobs.

## Slow uptake

Medical use grew slowly. In November 2019 the health minister, Chris Fearne, said more than 400 people
were [using medical cannabis](/history/malta-medical-patients-2019/) and 32 doctors had recommended it.
"This is still a small number," he said, and he called for more research to win doctors' trust. He also
warned about counterfeit products.

The 2018 laws kept cannabis a medicine, available only through doctors and pharmacies. Three years
later [Malta](/country/mt/) went much further: in December 2021 it became the
[first EU country to legalise](/history/malta-legalisation-2021/) possession and home growing for adults.
