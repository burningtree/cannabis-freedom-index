On 28 March 2024 Malawi's parliament passed the Cannabis Regulation (Amendment) Bill
to bring the country's own cannabis — chamba — inside the licensed system.[@1] The
bill's sponsor, Lilongwe South MP Peter Dimba, presented it as a foreign-exchange
play: studies suggested home-grown chamba could earn $200 million a year at first,
rising to $700 million as the industry matured, more than double tobacco's
take.[@1,2]

## Why the old law was not enough

The 2020 Act had legalised only medicinal and industrial cannabis, and the hemp it
envisaged disappointed. A Mchinji cooperative leader told VOA his industrial hemp took
a long time to find any buyer, with imported seed expensive, while high-THC cannabis
had buyers on the ground.[@2] Tobacco, meanwhile, still supplied about 13% of GDP and
60% of foreign exchange even as prices slid under anti-smoking pressure — the crop
cannabis was meant to replace.[@2]

The amendment therefore regulated "a locally produced variety", in the Nation's
words, and ironed out operational problems in cannabis production.[@1]

## Legalisation with the brakes on

The debate's most quoted lines were warnings, not promises. Leader of the House
Richard Chimwendo Banda told parliament the bill "does not in any way legalise
chamba smoking": growing chamba without a licence, or being found in possession of
it, remains an offence.[@1] He had previewed the posture days earlier, promising to
protect young people while facilitating the economic benefit.[@2] Anti-drug campaigners
objected anyway — Drug Fight Malawi's director said teen use had doubled after the
2020 hemp law and would soar further under high-THC growing.[@2] The opposition DPP
boycotted the deliberations.[@3]

Rastafari spokesman Patrick Galawanda hoped the change would stop arrests of members
found with cannabis used for spiritual and medicinal purposes — but the statute kept
the licence as the line between legal and criminal.[@2,4] Presidential assent was not
confirmed in the sources read.

[Malawi's profile](/country/mw/) reflects the amended scheme: licensed medical,
industrial and scientific production — including the local variety — with
recreational possession still a crime, and the [word behind the law](/culture/language/chamba/)
unchanged.
