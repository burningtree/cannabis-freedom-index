On February 15, 2017, Kenya's Senate heard a petition from Gwada Ogot seeking
cannabis decriminalisation and legalisation. The official Hansard records a request
for legislative change, a regulator and relief for people imprisoned for cannabis
offences. It records a proposal, not a change in law.

Neil Carrier places the broader initiative in 2015. The timeline entry now uses the
documented 2017 Senate presentation; the earlier account does not establish an exact
initial submission date.

## A proposed regulatory system

Ogot sought removal of cannabis from the scheduled-drug framework and action on the
provision criminalising possession for personal consumption. He also proposed a
body to regulate the sector and an amnesty for cannabis prisoners.

His argument connected industrial and medicinal uses with social and economic
benefits. These were claims advanced by a petitioner, rather than clinical findings
or a government commitment to establish a market.

## A second presentation in November

The November 29 Hansard records a petition from Sammy Gwada Ogot seeking withdrawal
from the Single Convention, decriminalisation and release of prisoners. A senator
recalled a similar petition in the previous Senate. The records therefore should
not be compressed into one uninterrupted legislative hearing or a bill that had
already passed a stage.

The proposal's account of treaty history is also not adopted uncritically here.
Kenya's [1964 ratification](/history/kenya-single-convention-1964/) was after
independence, contrary to the petition's description.

## The right to be heard

Some senators wanted the November petition rejected on moral grounds. Others argued
that Parliament should examine it even if they opposed cannabis use.

Senator Halake expressly declined either to support or oppose the petition while
asking colleagues to investigate the social circumstances of drug use. The Speaker
directed referral to the Health Committee once it was constituted. That direction
was a procedural decision, not approval of decriminalisation.

## A separate reform initiative

[Ken Okoth](/culture/people/ken-okoth/) later pursued a
[Marijuana Control Bill](/history/kenya-marijuana-control-bill-2018/) in the National
Assembly. Carrier discusses both efforts as openings for debate in a system where
cannabis had long been treated principally as an enforcement matter.

Neither the petition's presentation nor its committee referral changed Kenya's
cannabis rules. No score change is attributed to this event.
