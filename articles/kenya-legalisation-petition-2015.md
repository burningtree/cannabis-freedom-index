In 2015 a private citizen asked Kenya's Senate to legalise cannabis. It took the
senators two years to debate the petition, and most of them were affronted to be
discussing it at all.

## The law

Cannabis has been restricted in Kenya since the colonial
[opium ordinance of 1914](/history/kenya-ban-1914/), which followed complaints such as
that of a [provincial commissioner in 1908](/history/kenya-commissioner-asks-for-ban-1908/).
It remains the drug Kenyan police act on most: 776 people were arrested for cannabis
in 2016, against 92 for heroin and 11 for cocaine.

## The petition

Gwada Ogot asked that cannabis be removed from the list of scheduled substances and
that a regulator be set up to oversee a legal market. He argued mainly from medicine.
The plant, he wrote, "is God's gift to mankind just as the many minerals he has put in
store for Kenyans".

He also made a historical argument: legalisation would undo a colonial injustice, the
banning of something Kenyans had used for centuries.

A member of parliament, Ken Okoth, had made the economic case in the National
Assembly: the government should stop wasting money on sugarcane and legalise marijuana
instead.

## The debate

The Senate debated the petition in February 2017 and again in November.

It drew attention when one senator, a presidential hopeful, admitted to having used
cannabis in his youth, and his colleagues joked that this explained his decision to
run. Others were indignant. Senator Sam Ongeri said:

> Cannabis sativa should never find its way for discussion in this honourable August
> House.

It would be morally wrong, he added, to allow it because the United States had found
it medicinal. Two senators, by contrast, cited their own encounters with medical
marijuana in America as a reason to take the petition seriously.

The chapter's author notes that the views expressed leaned heavily on conservative
morality and paid little attention to economic or cultural arguments.

## What came of it

Nothing in law. Okoth went on to draft a
[Marijuana Control Bill](/history/kenya-marijuana-control-bill-2018/) in 2018, which
ended with his death the following year.

The index lists the petition for context; [Kenya's](/country/ke/) score did not move.
It is here because it shows where the argument stands in much of Africa: the case for
reform is now being made in parliaments, in terms of medicine, money and colonial
history, and is still being answered in terms of morals.
