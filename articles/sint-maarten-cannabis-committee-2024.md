Sint Maarten created a government committee in February 2024 to prepare cannabis legalisation for medical, scientific, religious and recreational purposes.[@1] Its work ranged from studying the effects of criminalisation to proposing conditions for growing, testing, distributing and consuming cannabis.[@1]

## Punishment and revenue drove the review

The regulation creating the committee pointed to people continuing to be punished for cannabis possession and cultivation.[@1] It also linked reform to the government's interest in new income and improving its finances, while acknowledging gaps in local research and government capacity.[@1]

Health minister Omar Ottley had already [called for study proposals in September 2022](/history/sint-maarten-cannabis-study-call-2022/), asking local and international organisations to examine economic, social and medical effects.[@2] The new committee gave three ministries a shared role in developing the next steps.[@1]

At the time, the existing [prosecution guidance](/history/sint-maarten-prosecution-guideline-2018/) allowed confiscation and possible conditional dismissal for some people with up to 25 grams, with exceptions including minors and indications of dealing.[@3]

## Three ministries shared the work

The committee could have up to nine members, with representatives from health, justice and economic affairs and a chair from the health ministry.[@1] It was tasked with reviewing consultants' research, developing a vision for reform and making recommendations to the Council of Ministers.[@1]

Its work included examining the social, health, legal and economic effects of the current approach.[@1] The regulation also called for comparisons with other jurisdictions and consideration of conditions on the French side of the island.[@1] Monthly meetings, quarterly reports and the ability to invite outside experts supported that work.[@1]

## Rules for a whole industry were on the agenda

The committee's remit covered cultivation, manufacture, processing, laboratory testing and distribution, alongside consumption and concentrates.[@1] It was also to consider changes to offences involving cannabis possession and use.[@1]

Medical use was one part of a wider brief that also included scientific, religious and recreational purposes.[@1] The resulting recommendations were to go to ministers for decisions about reform.[@1]

## Parliament heard proposals as ministers urged care

In [March 2026](/history/sint-maarten-cannabis-framework-hearing-2026/), Native Nations SXM presented MPs with a proposal involving licensing, product-safety rules and an independent cannabis oversight commission.[@4] Parliament later announced a continuation of the meeting after the March session was adjourned.[@5]

Prime minister Luc Mercelina [urged caution that August](/history/sint-maarten-pm-cannabis-caution-2026/), calling for a clear policy choice, health safeguards and the capacity to supervise any industry.[@6] Those choices remained central to the debate about cannabis reform in [Sint Maarten](/country/sx/).[@6]
