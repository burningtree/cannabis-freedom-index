Slovakia became independent on 1 January 1993 with a criminal code inherited from Czechoslovakia, under which holding a drug for one's own use had not been a crime since [1990](/history/slovakia-decriminalisation-1990/). Twenty-one months later it changed that. Act No. 248/1994 was passed on 18 August 1994, published on 17 September and took effect on 1 October.

## Three words

The Act is a long amendment to the criminal code. Point 78 rewrites the first paragraph of section 187. The new text punishes anyone who, without permission, produces, imports, exports, transports, procures for another, "or for himself or for another keeps" a narcotic or psychotropic substance, a poison or a precursor.

The 1990 wording had required the possession to be for another person. The words "for himself" put the user back inside the offence.

## The penalty

| Offence under section 187(1) | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Producing, importing, exporting, transporting, procuring for another, or keeping for oneself or another | one to five years in prison, or a fine |

The paragraph draws no line between a small amount and a large one, and none between cannabis and other drugs. The fine is the only lighter outcome it allows.

The same Act added precursors to the offence, raised the penalties in the neighbouring section 188a, and told the government to list by regulation which substances count as narcotics and psychotropics.

## The contrast with Prague

The Czech Republic kept the 1990 rule for another five years and, when it changed it in 1999, punished possession only above a "small" amount. The Slovak paragraph has no such threshold. The two countries had shared one drug law since 1938.

## What followed

A reform of the criminal code in [2024](/history/slovakia-penal-reform-2024/) reduced the sentences for small quantities of cannabis.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Slovakia](/country/sk/) from 25 to 9 with the [1994 Act](/history/slovakia-possession-crime-1994/), the largest fall in its record after the first ban. Possession drops from 3 to 1, enforcement from 5 to 1 and consumption from 4 to 1, because a user caught with any amount now faced a sentence of one to five years or a fine.
