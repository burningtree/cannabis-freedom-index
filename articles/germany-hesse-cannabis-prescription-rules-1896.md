New Hessian rules governing strong medicines came into force on **1 October 1896**. Their schedule explicitly covers several cannabis preparations while making an exception for **Indian-hemp extract intended for external use**.

The original government gazette makes it possible to distinguish the prescription rule, the exception and the quantities governing repeat dispensing.

## Three dates in the original publication

The notification is dated **5 June 1896** and appears in issue **19** of the *Großherzoglich Hessisches Regierungsblatt*, published in Darmstadt on **15 June**. Its opening paragraph fixes commencement at **1 October**.

The ministry states that it is publishing the provisions in accordance with a **Federal Council resolution of 13 May 1896**. It also says that the new provisions replace those of **15 August 1891**.

This source establishes the Hessian implementation and commencement. It does not establish that every German state used the same notification or began applying the rules on the same day.

## Prescription controls and an express exception

Section 1 requires a written, dated and signed prescription for supplying the listed drugs and preparations, and preparations containing them, to the public as remedies. It recognizes prescriptions from doctors, dentists and veterinarians, with veterinary prescriptions confined to animal treatment.

The schedule names **Cannabinon**, **Cannabinum tannicum**, **Extractum Cannabis Indicae** and **Tinctura Cannabis Indicae**. Directly beneath the extract entry, it excepts external use. No corresponding external-use exception appears beneath the cannabis-tincture entry.

The exception is therefore tied to a particular preparation and use. It does not mean that all cannabis medicines became available without prescriptions. Conversely, describing the entire schedule as an unconditional prohibition would erase a qualification printed in the law itself.

## What the scheduled quantities govern

Section 3 addresses repeat supply of medicines for internal use without a fresh instruction each time. Subject to additional provisions, it permits this when the original prescription expressly authorizes repetition with the required limits, or when the medicine's total content of the scheduled substance does not exceed its listed quantity.

For example, the extract entry carries **0.1 gram**, while the tincture entry carries **2 grams**. These figures belong to the dispensing framework. They are not general possession allowances, cultivation limits or quantities for unrestricted first purchases.

Section 5 preserves a prescriber's power to prohibit repetition. Section 8 leaves the separate rules governing trade in poisons unaffected.

## Historical scope

The notification also regulates medicine containers and labels. Its direct evidence concerns pharmacy dispensing in the **Grand Duchy of Hesse**, with explicit cannabis entries and a commencement date. It does not establish actual enforcement frequency or the first-ever Hessian cannabis control. The superseded 1891 text has not been compared in this pass, so the external-use exception is documented without claiming that it was newly introduced in 1896.
