Grenada was, in its attorney general's words, "one of the last jurisdictions" in the
region to act. When it did, it allowed more than most of its neighbours and stopped
at the same point: no shops.

## A second attempt

A first bill, tabled in [2021](/history/grenada-bill-2021/), died with the
dissolution of Parliament. The party that won the 2022 election had promised reform,
and the new prime minister, Dickon Mitchell, put it in terms of police time: "We
don't want to keep on wasting state resources arresting people for a spliff."

The bill passed the House on 20 January 2026 with support from both sides, and the
Senate on 3 February. It was published as Act No. 1 of 2026 on 20 February. The one
open argument was the age: Mitchell preferred 18, found himself "in the minority",
and the Act settled on 21.

## What the Act allows

- **Possession:** up to 56 g of cannabis or 15 g of resin is no longer an offence.
  Dealing is presumed from 71 g.
- **Growing:** four plants or fewer at a household, each household counted
  separately, are outside the growing offence. The agriculture ministry says a grower
  registers with the Cannabis Secretariat and the ministry before planting, and that
  the rules for signing up would follow. The Cannabis Commission told people to
  ignore private groups that charge a fee to sign growers up.
- **Under 21:** no arrest, charge or record. Children are referred to child
  protection and those aged 18 to 20 to counselling; the cannabis is forfeited.
- **In public:** smoking near the entrance of a public place brings a fixed penalty
  of 300 East Caribbean dollars and no record. Within 100 yards of a school it is a
  crime with up to seven years on summary conviction.
- **Rastafari:** sacramental use is exempt, and adherents cannot be prosecuted over
  cannabis in a registered place of worship, once the minister makes the rules.
- **Old convictions:** those for amounts now legal are expunged, on application or on
  the board's own motion, and pending cases are discontinued.

## What it leaves out

Nobody may sell. The Act exempts licensed medical and scientific supply, but the law
that would issue licences does not exist yet; ministers called it a second phase.
Until then the only lawful source is one's own plants.

In the index, possession rose from 1 to 5, growing from 1 to 4, enforcement from 2 to
6 and consumption from 1 to 3. Sharing and access did not move. A grower is told to
register first, and the ministry's March notice said the rules for that registration
would follow.
