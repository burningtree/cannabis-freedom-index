Two years after the United States taxed marijuana out of legal existence, the mayor of
New York asked doctors to find out whether what Congress had been told was true. Their
answer, in 1944 — from the first official body to question prohibition in America — was that most of it was not.[@1,2]

## The commission

Fiorello La Guardia, a strong opponent of the
[Marihuana Tax Act](/history/us-marihuana-tax-act-1937/), appointed a commission in
1939. The New York Academy of Medicine prepared its report, which was published in
1944 after more than five years of research.[@1,2]

## What it found

The report set out thirteen conclusions about marijuana in New York. Among them:

- smoking it did not cause addiction in the medical sense;
- it did not lead to morphine, heroin or cocaine addiction;
- it was not the determining factor in major crimes;
- it was not widespread among schoolchildren, and not linked to juvenile delinquency;
- no single organised group controlled its sale;
- the publicity about its catastrophic effects in the city was unfounded.

It also described who used it. Distribution and use centred on Harlem, most smokers
were Black or Latin American, it was cheap, and smokers said it gave them a feeling of
adequacy.

## The reaction

Harry Anslinger, head of the Federal Bureau of Narcotics, called the report
"unscientific" and denounced the mayor, the Academy and the doctors.[@2] He ordered that no
further marijuana research proceed without his personal permission, and had the
American Medical Association prepare a study reflecting the government's position.[@1]

Nothing in the law changed. Congress added mandatory minimum sentences in
[1951](/history/us-boggs-act-1951/) and raised them in
[1956](/history/us-narcotic-control-act-1956/).

## Its afterlife

The report was the evidence the AMA's witness had asked for in 1937 and not been
given. In 1972 the [Shafer Commission](/history/us-shafer-commission-1972/) said the
earlier claims about marijuana had been largely false, and recommended
decriminalisation. That was not acted on either.
