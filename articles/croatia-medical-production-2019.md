Croatia opened a domestic medical cannabis production route in April 2019, allowing cultivation and manufacturing by authorised companies.[@1] The amendment also replaced the industrial hemp permission system with registration before sowing for qualifying varieties.[@1]

## Patients already had a prescription route

The [2015 prescribing rules](/history/croatia-medical-2015/) allowed medicines containing THC, dronabinol or nabilone for symptoms associated with multiple sclerosis, cancer, epilepsy and AIDS.[@2] Prescriptions could cover at most thirty days, with a maximum of 7.5 grams of THC over that period.[@2]

The rules required doctors to specify the dose, preparation, pharmaceutical form and method of use.[@2] They also provided for standardised plant preparations and dried flowering tops for compounded inhalation medicines.[@2] The 2019 production framework added a route for domestic companies to supply that medical chain.[@1]

## Companies needed both medicines credentials and approval

A company seeking to grow medical cannabis needed a manufacturing licence for medicines or active substances and approval from the Health Ministry.[@1] Manufacturing cannabis products required approval from the medicines agency, [HALMED](https://www.halmed.hr/).[@1]

The amendment assigned the cultivation and manufacturing decisions to those separate authorities.[@1] It therefore connected medical cannabis growing to the existing pharmaceutical system, with licensed legal entities applying for authorisation.[@1] The ministry also had to notify the Interior Ministry of cultivation approvals.[@1]

## Industrial hemp used a separate register

Industrial hemp was defined through EU-listed varieties containing no more than 0.2% total THC.[@1] Growers had to enter the Agriculture Ministry’s electronic register before sowing.[@1] The register recorded the grower, land parcels, planted area and variety, and changes had to be reported within fifteen days.[@1]

This agricultural route covered qualifying industrial hemp, while the medical route required the medicines manufacturing credentials and approvals.[@1] The [Croatia profile](/country/hr/) sets out both routes alongside the criminal rules for unauthorised growing.[@1]

## Authorisation did not immediately produce a stocked market

A [September 2025 market survey](/history/croatia-medical-market-2025/) reported that no cannabis medicine was on sale and no company had completed the authorisations needed to produce or distribute one.[@3] That account describes a continuing gap between the legal production framework and practical patient supply.[@3]

Police meanwhile continued enforcing the illegal market: the [national report for 2025](/history/croatia-cannabis-seizures-2025/) recorded more than a tonne of marijuana seized and 3,978 cannabis plants.[@4]
