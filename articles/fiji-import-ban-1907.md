In 1905 the colony of Fiji banned bringing Indian hemp across its borders. Ordinance No. 21 prohibited importing Indian hemp and anything made from it — gunjah, bhang and chavas by name — plus anything the colony's chief medical officer judged capable of substitution for them.[@1] Cannabis had likely arrived with the Indian indentured workers first brought to the colony in 1879.[@3]

## The colony had legislated once before

The 1905 ban was not Fiji's first cannabis law. The legislation review points back to a Gunjah Prohibition Ordinance of 1886, numbered 11 — its text has not been traced, so what it covered is unknown, but the colony had legislated about the drug almost twenty years earlier.[@1] Later summaries misdated the 1905 measure to 1907, the year the London journal reviewing it was more widely cited; the review itself covers laws passed in 1905.[@1,3]

## A net drawn deliberately wide

The ordinance named the preparations the reviewers expected smugglers to try: gunjah, bhang and chavas, the Indian forms of the drug the indentured workforce knew.[@1] Then it added a catch-all — "any article which in the opinion of the Chief Medical Officer of the Colony is capable of substitution therefor" — so renamed or unfamiliar preparations could not slip through.[@1] The test was the medical officer's opinion, not a fixed chemical list — the decision moved from the courtroom to the colony's doctors.[@1] The reviewers of the empire's 1905 laws picked the measure out as characteristic: in their survey of the year, Fiji stood for banning the import of substitutes for bhang.[@2]

## Import only, until 1938

The review records an import ban; it says nothing about possession or growing, and the ordinance left the domestic side of the drug alone as far as the record shows.[@1] That changed with the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, which took effect on 1 January 1938 and prohibited importing or exporting Indian hemp and its seed, banned growing the plant, and made growing, possessing or selling it an offence.[@4] The 1905 border ban thus became the first step toward the full prohibition that independent [Fiji](/country/fj/) inherited and kept extending — through the 2004 drug law to the [counter-narcotics rewrite](/history/fiji-counter-narcotics-law-signed-2026/) of recent years.[@3,4]
