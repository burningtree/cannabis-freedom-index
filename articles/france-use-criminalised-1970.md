France's law of 31 December 1970 made the use of any classified narcotic a criminal offence, whether it happened in public or in private. It also embedded an unresolved second idea: the user was both an offender and a patient.

## From social use to any use

The previous framework came from a 1916 law that prohibited drug use "in society." The 1970 law went further. Simple use could bring one year in prison and a fine of 50 to 250,000 francs, without distinguishing private from public use or occasional from regular use.

The National Consultative Commission on Human Rights describes this as a decisive expansion of prohibition. Conduct affecting the user directly was treated as an offence against public order, and the statute became the basis of France's modern drug policy.

## The politics around the law

The law followed the growth of cannabis and LSD use among young people in the 1960s and the student movements associated with that period. Authorities increasingly framed drugs as a public-order issue.

France was also confronting its role in the French Connection heroin route to the United States. Abroad, American policy was moving toward a declared war on drugs. The commission's review identifies all three as part of the setting in which the French law was adopted.

## Offender and patient

The result was a compromise between the justice ministry's preference for repression and the health ministry's preference for compulsory medical supervision. The law pursued punishment of use and trafficking while also offering care.

Its health provisions placed users under the supervision of the health authority. Depending on the case, a person could face a fine or imprisonment, or compulsory care. The law did not clearly settle how authorities should choose between those tracks, leaving its penal and medical logics side by side.

## What moved in the index

For [France](/country/fr/), the expansion from social use to all use lowered possession from 2 to 1, enforcement from 3 to 2 and consumption from 2 to 1. Cultivation and sharing remained at 1, while legal access remained 0. The movement reflects a broader use offence and the possibility of prison, tempered by the law's treatment alternative.
