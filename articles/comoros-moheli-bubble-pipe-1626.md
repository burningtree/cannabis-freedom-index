Thomas Herbert's account of the voyage begun in 1626 describes the people of
Mohelia — Mohéli in the Comoros — as esteeming tobacco and "drinking" it in
long canes or pipes called bubble bubbles.[@1]

Duvall identifies this as the earliest documentation of a bamboo water pipe
anywhere — identical pipes were smoked in South Africa around 1790 — and roots
the global bamboo-pipe tradition, the ancestor of the modern bong, in
southeastern Africa rather than Southeast Asia.[@2]

The pipe came before the ban by two and a half centuries: Cape Verde yields an
even earlier coconut water pipe from 1672, and the calabash water-pipe
tradition carries the form across the region to this day.[@2]
