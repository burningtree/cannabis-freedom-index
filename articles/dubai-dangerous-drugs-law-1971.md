The Emirates banned drugs on paper in 1955, ignored the ban for fifteen years, then wrote their own laws in 1970 and 1971.[@1] Britain's regulation named Indian hemp among its prohibited drugs but was never applied by the Trucial rulers; Abu Dhabi's and Dubai's acts were the first prohibitions that bit.[@1]

## A British regulation nobody applied

As drug smuggling through the Trucial States grew in the 1950s, Britain issued a special Dangerous Drugs Regulation in 1955 with ten articles.[@1] Only the al-Maktum Hospital in Dubai and the American Mission Hospital in Sharjah could sell or supply drugs freely; everyone else needed the Political Agent's licence, imports and exports needed his permission, and penalties ran to three years or 5,000 Indian rupees.[@1] The listed drugs were raw opium, coca leaves and Indian hemp, each with preparations.[@1] The rulers never applied it and were never asked to copy it — they preferred Islamic law, and the Political Agent agreed the combination was adequate.[@1]

## Open sale in the 1960s

In the 1960s opium, hashish and qat sold openly, especially in Dubai and Sharjah, with opium and hashish the drugs most used rather than merely traded.[@1] Small quantities of qat came from Yemen and Africa on dhows.[@1] Users had their own names: the opium user was the teryagi, the hashish user the hashash, and Iranian users were Bajgiriyah after an opium method.[@1] Enforcement was personal rather than legal — one dealer was punished by the Ruler of Sharjah for importing opium and hashish from Iran to sell to elderly users who believed the drugs improved sexual activity.[@1]

## The local laws of 1970 and 1971

In 1970 Abu Dhabi issued the first locally made Dangerous Drugs Act, covering most drugs available except qat, which followed on 16 July 1973.[@1] Penalties were five years in prison or a fine up to 10,000 Bahraini Dinars, and the law applied only in Abu Dhabi.[@1] In 1971 Dubai issued its own law with the same penalties, and the remaining emirates, having none of their own, mostly applied Dubai's.[@1] Smuggling expanded through the decade as the new federation's trade and communications grew.[@1]

The paper ban of 1955 gave way to real ones in [1970 in Abu Dhabi](/history/abu-dhabi-dangerous-drugs-act-1970/) and [1971 in Dubai](/history/dubai-dangerous-drugs-law-1971/), whose law the future federation inherited — the rest of the story is on [the Emirates' page](/country/ae/).[@1]
