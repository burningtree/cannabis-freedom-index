Montenegro published a drug law on 10 June 2011 that combined prevention, treatment, cultivation controls and licensed drug production in one framework.[@1] Parliament had adopted it on 26 May, and President Filip Vujanović promulgated it on 3 June.[@1]

## Personal possession brought a misdemeanour fine

In the version consolidated through 2013, holding drugs for personal use contrary to the law carried a €100–€2,000 misdemeanour fine.[@1] Drugs held unlawfully were confiscated.[@1] The law also distinguished a medicine containing a controlled drug: a patient could possess the quantity prescribed by a doctor for personal treatment.[@1]

Prevention and care formed part of the same framework.[@1] It assigned responsibilities for identifying drug problems, treatment, rehabilitation and social reintegration, including cooperation with organisations providing assistance.[@1]

## Hemp growers needed a buyer

Food and industrial hemp followed a controlled agricultural route.[@1] Growers needed both a cultivation permit and a contract with a registered purchaser for the entire crop, which had to be handed over within four months of harvest.[@1] Scientific legal entities had a separate exception after registering their growing land with the phytosanitary authority.[@1]

The [detailed permit rules effective in July 2014](/history/montenegro-hemp-permits-2014/) covered hemp containing at most 0.2% THC in dry plant material.[@2] Applications required the variety, quantity of certified seed, plot and area, purchase-contract number and planned sowing and harvest dates.[@2] Hemp land had to be reported by 1 May each year.[@2]

## Supplying another person could mean prison

The misdemeanour possession rule sat alongside separate Criminal Code offences.[@1,3] Under the later code consolidation, selling drugs or buying, holding or transporting them for sale carried two to ten years.[@3] Giving drugs to someone for consumption, or providing premises for use, carried six months to five years in the basic case.[@3]

The [Montenegro profile](/country/me/) sets out those separate possession, growing and sharing consequences.[@1,3] A personal-possession fine therefore gives no permission to supply friends or operate a recreational shop.[@1,3]

## Police recorded both types of proceedings

The Interior Ministry’s report for 2025 lists 247 criminal complaints covering 308 people for drug offences across all substances.[@4] It separately records 955 misdemeanour complaints against 957 people.[@4] The same report describes the [Trnovo operation](/history/montenegro-operation-trnovo-2025/), where officers found about 13,100 cannabis plants and arrested two people in August 2025.[@4]
