A school survey presented in Kyrenia on 21 August 2026 reported that 1.2% of the 1,049 students studied had used cannabis at least once.[@1] The findings gave northern Cyprus a recent account of young people's experience alongside a criminal system that offers eligible users supervised treatment.[@1,2]

## Students answered about more than cannabis

The ESPAD study covered the 2025–2026 period and included alcohol, tobacco, electronic cigarettes and gambling.[@1] Lifetime use of any illegal substance was reported at 1.8%.[@1] The results were presented at a scientific panel organised by the Prime Ministry's drug commission, with researchers discussing the findings and emerging substances.[@1]

The panel also set out plans to repeat the survey in 2027 and develop an anonymous digital early-warning system.[@1] Those were future monitoring plans; the published figures described the students surveyed in the preceding study period.[@1]

## A user could already be offered supervision

The [2016 supervised-release law](/history/northern-cyprus-drug-treatment-option-2016/) lets eligible people convicted of possession or use accept monitoring, treatment or rehabilitation before sentencing.[@2] A psychiatric assessment helps the court choose a period of one to three years, and the person must then follow the board's programme.[@2] The law requires blood and urine tests at least once every fifteen days, with a fee exemption for state-laboratory tests where Social Services certifies poverty.[@2]

Successful completion means no sentence and no conviction entered on the record.[@2] Breaching the programme can return the person to the ordinary criminal process.[@2] Growing and supply convictions are excluded, as are specified repeat cases and certain non-citizens.[@2] For cannabis users, the benefit therefore depends on the offence, eligibility and completing the requirements.[@2]

## The programme was still operating in September

An [official September 2026 account](/history/northern-cyprus-treatment-programme-report-2026/) of an addiction symposium reported 218 supervised-release participants and described reintegration as part of the commission's work.[@3] This was an all-drug programme figure, covering a different population from the students in the school survey.[@1,3] The same official account described cooperation with Ankara University on research, toxicology and wastewater analysis to improve monitoring.[@3] Together, the school findings and programme reports show the information and services developing around the criminal rules recorded in the [Northern Cyprus profile](/country/xn/).[@1,2,3]
