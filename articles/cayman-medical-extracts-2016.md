Cayman opened a lawful route to prescribed cannabis extracts and tinctures in November 2016. A doctor licensed locally could prescribe them for a patient under that doctor’s care and determine the dose. The change linked patients to authorised doctors, pharmacists and importers.[@1]

## A prescription changed what patients could use

Cannabis possession and consumption were already criminal offences under the [1973 drug law](/history/cayman-modern-drug-law-1973/). The medical reform added an express rule making prescribed extracts and tinctures lawful for medical or therapeutic use. It placed the patient’s treatment and dosage under a licensed medical doctor’s responsibility.[@1]

The published bill explained the practical purpose: allowing cannabis oil to be imported and dispensed for medicinal purposes. It proposed a specific group of authorised people, including doctors, pharmacists, retail pharmacies, lawful importers and patients holding the required prescription.[@2]

## November marks the legal start

The official legislation history records the amendment as enacted on 24 October 2016 and commencing on 18 November. That start date matters when following the route from a legislative decision to lawful treatment.[@1]

The permission concerned extracts and tinctures. The general offences covering unauthorised possession, growing, supply and consumption remained part of the drug law. Patients needed the prescribed product and the medical relationship described in the exception.[@1]

## Imports connected the law to pharmacies

By 29 August 2017, Canadian producer Cronos Group reported that its subsidiary Peace Naturals had completed the company’s [first medical-oil shipment to Cayman](/history/cayman-cronos-medical-oil-shipment-2017/). The buyer was Caribbean Medical Distributors, working with Professional Pharmacy Services at CTMH Doctors Hospital in George Town.[@3]

That announcement identified a concrete supply chain: a Canadian producer, a Cayman distributor and a hospital pharmacy. It showed how an authorised imported product could reach the local medical system after the prescription reform. The company presented the shipment as a new step in its international distribution business.[@3]

## Recreational reform followed a different route

The later debate concerned people who used or possessed cannabis outside that medical exception. After the April 2025 referendum, Premier André Ebanks told Parliament that voters had backed decriminalisation and that the question would go to the Law Reform Commission.[@4]

The commission’s [July 2026 consultation](/history/cayman-cannabis-reform-consultation-2026/) focused on recreational possession and use because medical and therapeutic use was already permitted. It asked about quantities, home growing and clearing old convictions. The [Cayman profile](/country/ky/) follows both the existing prescription route and that continuing reform process.[@5]
