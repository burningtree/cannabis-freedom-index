On 27 October 1986 the United States brought back the mandatory prison sentence for drug crimes. The Anti-Drug Abuse Act undid the choice Congress had made in 1970, when it repealed the fixed minimums of the 1950s.

## Two deaths and an election

The law is also called the Len Bias Law. Bias died a cocaine-related death on 19 June 1986, and Don Rogers another eight days later. Crack cocaine had recently appeared in American cities.

The Speaker of the House, Tip O'Neill, told the Democratic leadership to draft an omnibus drug bill. In September and October the Democratic House and the Republican Senate each pushed stricter proposals, with midterm elections weeks away.

## What it contained

| | |
|---|---|
| Mandatory minimum sentences | new ones for drugs, marijuana included |
| Cocaine | 5 years without parole for 5 g of crack, or for 500 g of powder |
| Analogues | controlled substance analogues prohibited |
| Money | the Money Laundering Control Act, enacted as Title I |

With the Comprehensive Crime Control Act of 1984, which had created a commission to write binding sentencing guidelines, it greatly increased the number of drug offences that carried a mandatory minimum.

For cannabis the fixed terms applied to large-scale distribution.

## What followed

Two years later a second Anti-Drug Abuse Act authorised the death penalty for members of criminal organisations who took part in murder. In 1994 a three-strikes law mandated life imprisonment after a third serious conviction, where a serious drug offence could count among the earlier ones.

The hundred-to-one difference between crack and powder cocaine stood until 2010, when the Fair Sentencing Act reduced it to eighteen to one.

## Why the index moved

The [act](/history/us-anti-drug-abuse-act-1986/) lowered the [United States](/country/us/) total from 16 to 14. Possession fell from 2 to 1 and enforcement from 3 to 2. The states that had decriminalised in the 1970s kept their laws, which is why the fall is small, and arrests for marijuana rose for the next two decades.
