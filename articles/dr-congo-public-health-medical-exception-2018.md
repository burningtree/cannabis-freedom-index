A century after the Congo banned cannabis for smoking, the law cracked a medical door open.
Law 18/035 of 13 December 2018, organising public health, allowed narcotics cultivation,
production, possession and sale for medical and scientific purposes under authorisation,
tasked the health minister with setting the permission conditions, and required records and
access for inspections.[@1]

The law specified commencement three months after promulgation, but the conditions took
longer: licensing rules followed through the ministerial order of 27 February 2021, which
set five-year authorisations for Congolese organisations operating at industrial scale, with
medical varieties capped at 0.2% THC and high CBD content.[@2]

The exception never became a patient programme. The 2021 rules kept every non-medical
purpose prohibited, a revised order followed in 2022 keeping the same shape, and no
operating patient supply was verified — the framework licenses production without reaching
patients.[@2]
