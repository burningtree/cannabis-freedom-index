On 13 October 2006 a rewritten set of drug offences took effect in Bulgaria's penal code. It ended two years in which the law made no allowance at all for a small amount, and replaced them with a rule that is still in force: one to six years in prison for possession, or a fine in a minor case. The fine is a criminal conviction all the same.

## Two years without a small amount

Until 2004 Bulgarian law spared a person found with a single dose for personal use. That exemption was removed, and from then on holding any quantity of any drug was prosecuted like the rest. Organisers of Sofia's yearly cannabis march still describe the law they protest as "the 2004 law", under which, they say, a single cigarette can bring one to three years.

## What the courts saw

| Year | People convicted of drug crimes |
|---|---|
| 2000 | 232 |
| 2003 | 525 |
| 2004 | 743 |
| 2005 | 921 |
| 2006 | 1,587 |

The figures, from the National Institute of Statistics, cover all drugs and all drug offences. The number rose by 72% between 2005 and 2006. Most of the prosecutors' work was possession: in 2006 they monitored 4,223 proceedings for acquiring or keeping drugs, against 204 for dealing and 9 for production. Cannabis was, as the national report put it, the most widely used drug in the country.

## The amendment

The amending act was published in State Gazette 75 of 2006. It revised every drug offence in the code, in order to bring it into line with a European Union framework decision on drug trafficking. Possession moved into its own paragraph of Article 354a, with a separate provision for the minor case. A later count in the same report lists 1,448 proceedings under that paragraph and 313 people sentenced.

## A fine that is still a conviction

The minor case did not end prison for small amounts. In February 2012 a court set up for organised crime sentenced a young man to eight months and a fine of 10,000 leva for 0.54 grams of cannabis, worth 3.54 leva. An activist waved a joint at ministers in parliament in protest. Six years later the Sofia marchers were still asking for the same two things: no prison for small amounts, and no crime in growing a little at home.

## Why the index moved

Before [the amendment](/history/bulgaria-penal-code-2006/) every possession case was a serious crime with no lighter route, which the index scores at the bottom for possession and enforcement. Afterwards a fine became the usual outcome for a small amount, so [Bulgaria's](/country/bg/) possession and enforcement scores rose a little. They stay low because the fine leaves a criminal record and prison remains possible, as the [2012 sentence](/history/bulgaria-half-gram-sentence-2012/) showed.
