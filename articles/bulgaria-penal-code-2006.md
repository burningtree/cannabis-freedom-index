Bulgaria rewrote its drug offences in October 2006, separating ordinary possession from production and distribution and providing a fine-only penalty for minor possession cases.[@1,2] Ordinary possession of high-risk drugs could still bring one to six years in prison and a fine.[@1] The reform gave courts a lighter category for minor cases while retaining substantial criminal penalties.[@1]

## Possession and distribution received different ranges

The national drug report described the changes as a complete revision of the drug offences, bringing the code into conformity with the EU framework decision on drug trafficking.[@2] The revised provisions took effect on 13 October 2006.[@1]

Production, processing or possession for distribution, and distribution itself, carried two–eight years for high-risk drugs, together with a fine.[@1] Ordinary acquisition or possession had its own lower range, while the minor-possession provision allowed a fine up to 1,000 leva.[@1] Cannabis growing remained a separate offence, with a reduced tariff for minor cultivation cases.[@1]

## Prosecutors handled many more possession cases

The 2007 national report recorded 4,223 proceedings monitored by prosecutors for acquiring or keeping drugs, compared with 204 for dealing in 2006.[@2] Those totals concerned drug offences generally, rather than cannabis alone.[@2] They illustrate the scale of possession work within the system the amendment was changing.[@2]

The report also described cannabis as the country’s most widely used illicit drug.[@2] Its account of the legislation and prosecution workload therefore places the new minor-case provision within a much broader criminal enforcement system.[@2]

## A minor-case fine and administrative release are different outcomes

A fine imposed under the criminal minor-possession provision is distinct from release from criminal responsibility under Article 78a.[@1] That separate mechanism permits an administrative sanction when the statutory conditions are met, including requirements about previous convictions and the seriousness of the offence.[@1] It also has exclusions, including specified circumstances involving drug use.[@1]

The court’s chosen route consequently matters when describing the outcome: a fine does not by itself establish that someone received a criminal conviction.[@1] The [Bulgaria profile](/country/bg/) now distinguishes those possibilities.[@1]

## Prison sentences continued after the reform

In [February 2012](/history/bulgaria-half-gram-sentence-2012/), Drugreporter described an eight-month prison sentence and a 10,000-leva fine for 0.54 g of cannabis.[@3] Activist Radoslav Stoyanov protested in parliament by holding up a joint and asking MPs to intervene.[@3] The case showed why the existence of a minor-case provision still left campaigners concerned about prison for small quantities.[@3]
