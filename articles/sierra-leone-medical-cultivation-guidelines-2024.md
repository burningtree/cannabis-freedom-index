Sierra Leone's Pharmacy Board set out a licensed route for growing and processing medical cannabis in guidelines effective from 17 May 2024.[@1] The document focused on production for export, with requirements for applicants, planting material, secure premises and consistent product quality.[@1]

## An older permission power gained practical rules

The legal basis came from the [2001 pharmacy law](/history/sierra-leone-pharmacy-drugs-act-2001/), which required the Board's written consent to cultivate plants yielding narcotic drugs.[@2] The guidelines expressly relied on that power for cannabis cultivation and the production of cannabis and extracts for medical and scientific purposes.[@1]

The same pharmacy law contained severe cannabis offences, including a minimum fifteen-year sentence for an individual convicted under it.[@2] Permission for licensed production therefore operated alongside criminal restrictions on unauthorised cannabis activity.[@1,2]

## Applicants needed more than a field

Applications could come from licensed companies, registered manufacturers, and approved government or nongovernmental organisations running health programmes.[@1] They had to submit business and tax documents, an application form and a fee to the Board.[@1]

The guidelines required applicants and relevant business associates to be suitable people to hold a licence.[@1] Staff suitability mattered too: the Board treated employees as a possible route for cannabis to leave the authorised supply chain.[@1]

Security policies had to deter entry, detect breaches and set out a response.[@1] Failure to meet the security requirements could lead to a licence being revoked.[@1]

## The crop had to be traceable

Seeds and other planting material had to carry information identifying their species, variety, chemical type and origin.[@1] The rules called for traceable material and female cuttings for production, with monitoring for male plants and unwanted varieties.[@1]

The quality requirements followed the crop through cultivation, harvesting, drying and packaging.[@1] Manufacturers had to follow good agricultural, laboratory and manufacturing practices, while the Board's inspectorate and enforcement staff checked licensed sites and applicants.[@1]

## Export production shaped the route

The guidelines described cannabis as a starting material for pharmaceutical products and identified export production as their purpose.[@1] Their emphasis was on reliable quality, patient safety and preventing diversion from licensed operations.[@1]

For [Sierra Leone](/country/sl/), this placed a documented medical production route within a wider system of criminal drug controls.[@1,2] The practical work for applicants was to secure permission and satisfy the Board's requirements at each stage from planting material to processed product.[@1]
