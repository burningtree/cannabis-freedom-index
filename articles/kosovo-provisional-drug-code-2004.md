Kosovo’s provisional criminal code entered into force on 6 April 2004, setting separate prison ranges for holding drugs for sale, distributing them and bringing them across the border.[@1] For a cannabis supplier, the conduct involved determined which offence and sentencing range applied.[@1]

## Holding for sale and distributing

Buying or possessing controlled drugs with intent to sell or distribute, or offering them for sale, carried a fine and one to five years in prison.[@1] Actually distributing, selling, transporting or delivering them with the intention of further distribution or sale carried a fine and one to eight years.[@1]

Importing or exporting controlled drugs without authorisation carried a fine and three to ten years.[@1] The code required confiscation of the drugs, making their loss a further consequence alongside the sentence imposed on the supplier.[@1]

## Aggravating circumstances raised the range

Article 229 provided a fine and three to fifteen years where specified aggravating circumstances applied.[@1] These included acting as part of a group, involvement by a law-enforcement officer, using or threatening violence or a weapon, and exploiting a child.[@1] The list also addressed particularly vulnerable victims and using a humanitarian shipment or vehicle to transport drugs.[@1]

Growing, producing or processing controlled substances for sale or distribution fell under a separate article, with a fine and one to ten years.[@1] Aggravated production cases could bring three to thirteen years, and drugs and production equipment were subject to confiscation.[@1]

## The code also covered places of use

A manager or owner who permitted or tolerated drug use in premises open to the public faced three months to five years.[@1] Unlawfully facilitating acquisition or use through one’s function carried six months to five years.[@1] These provisions extended criminal exposure beyond the person directly selling a substance.[@1]

## Later laws separated personal possession

The [2008 drug law](/history/kosovo-narcotics-law-2008/) placed cannabis, resin, extracts and tinctures in Table I, restricting activities to scientific research, education and police purposes.[@2] Research institutions needed licences to acquire and use these substances; the limited institutional exceptions did not establish ordinary patient access.[@2]

The [code effective in January 2013](/history/kosovo-criminal-code-2013/) expressly prescribed a fine or up to one year for a first personal-possession offence involving less than three grams of cannabis or hashish.[@3] It retained higher penalties for supply and required confiscation even under the small-possession provision.[@3] The [Kosovo profile](/country/xk/) distinguishes that personal-possession tier from the separate growing and supply offences.[@3]
