The Isle of Man's first local medicinal cannabis dispensing service opened its
prescription route on 1 December 2022 at Karsons Pharmacy in Onchan. The Department
of Health and Social Care announced the launch the preceding day, describing a
pilot intended to gather evidence for future services.

For patients, the significance was practical: a private prescription could be
fulfilled through a pharmacy on the island. That was a different milestone from
changing the legal scheduling of a medicine.

## Legal permission came earlier

The DHSC's 2019 consultation explained that
[rescheduling on 1 November 2018](/history/isle-of-man-medical-rescheduling-2018/)
had also applied in the Isle of Man through earlier legislation. It permitted
medicinal use in limited circumstances while leaving the island to consider its
own future approach.

A legal route to prescribing does not, by itself, establish a local dispensing
service. The 2022 announcement supplied evidence of that later step, naming the
pharmacy and the date it could begin accepting prescriptions.

## A private service with a supply interval

The launch notice directed patients to private clinics registered with England's
Care Quality Commission and appropriately registered specialist practitioners.
It stated that the prescriptions were unavailable on the NHS.

Patients could take prescriptions to Karsons from opening day, but immediate
collection was not promised. Officials estimated seven to ten working days for
medication to arrive after the original prescription reached the pharmacy, with
the hope that this interval would shorten.

## A pilot to inform future provision

The licence allowed a pilot lasting at least twelve months, collecting evidence
about need and demand. Launching the route and delivering every patient's first
supply therefore should not be treated as the same event.

A later government account reported more than 650 users during the twelve-month
pilot period, aged 18 to 86. It recorded an average of 335 items dispensed per
month, mostly for chronic pain. These were measures of service use, rather than
evidence about clinical outcomes or the island's total unmet need.

## From one pharmacy to applications from others

On [11 July 2024](/history/isle-of-man-pharmacies-dispense-2024/), the government
opened applications under a continuing licensing and registration arrangement
for island pharmacies and private prescribing clinics. It retained private
payment and conditions on prescribing and oversight.

That announcement widened the route for providers to seek approval. It did not
mean every pharmacy began dispensing that day. Together, the pilot and subsequent
applications show how [Manx patient access](/country/im/) developed through service
arrangements as well as legislation.
