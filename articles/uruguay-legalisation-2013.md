The first country to legalise cannabis did it against the wishes of two thirds of its
people, with no vote to spare in one chamber, and for a reason that had more to do
with crime than with freedom.

## A contradiction since 1974

Uruguay [stopped punishing possession](/history/uruguay-personal-use-1974/) for
personal use in 1974 while keeping long sentences for growing and selling. A
Uruguayan could smoke lawfully but not buy lawfully. What users bought was pressed
cannabis from Paraguay, from the same dealers who sold pasta base, a cheap cocaine
paste. Separating those markets became the reformers' main argument.

## From home growing to a state market

By early 2012 a cross-party bill to allow home growing looked likely to pass. Then,
on [20 June 2012](/history/uruguay-strategy-for-life-2012/), President José Mujica's
government proposed something larger inside a plan against insecurity: the state
itself would produce and sell cannabis. A poll found 66 per cent opposed, and
opposition stayed above 60 per cent throughout.

Deputies merged the two ideas. The
[lower house](/history/uruguay-chamber-vote-2013/) passed the bill on 31 July 2013
with exactly the 50 votes required, the Senate on 10 December by 16 to 13, and Mujica
signed Law 19.172 before Christmas.

## The model

Citizens and residents aged 18 or over register with a state regulator and choose
one route.

| Route | Limit |
|---|---|
| Pharmacy | 40 g a month, at a state-set price |
| Home growing | Six flowering plants, 480 g a year |
| Membership club | 15 to 45 members, up to 99 plants |

Unlike [Colorado and Washington](/history/us-colorado-washington-2012/), Uruguay did
not set out to build an industry. Advertising is banned, visitors cannot buy, the
price was meant to match the street at about a dollar a gram, and any THC at the
wheel is an offence.

## Reaction and rollout

The UN's drug control board [said the law broke](/history/uruguay-incb-criticism-2013/)
the 1961 treaty. "Tell that guy to stop lying," Mujica replied of its president.

Home growers could register from August 2014 and clubs from October. Pharmacies
[began selling](/history/uruguay-pharmacy-sales-2017/) only in July 2017, in 16
pharmacies. Since then the clubs have grown most: by March 2026 some 575 of them say they
supply more than half of the legal cannabis. A 2026 government evaluation found that
pressed cannabis fell from the main source in 2014 to 6.7 per cent of the market in
2024.

In the index the law raised growing from 1 to 4 and sharing from 1 to 3; access
reached 4 once pharmacies opened. [Uruguay](/country/uy/) is not at the top of the
table because the register, the single route and the exclusion of visitors all count
as limits.
