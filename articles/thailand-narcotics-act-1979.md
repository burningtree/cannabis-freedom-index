On 28 April 1979 Thailand brought cannabis into a new five-category narcotics
system. Possession without permission could bring up to five years in prison
and a fine of up to 50,000 baht. Possession for sale carried two to fifteen
years and a 20,000–150,000-baht fine.[@1]

## One framework replaces the separate cannabis law

The Act repealed seven earlier laws, including the cannabis statute of
Buddhist year 2477, published in 1935, and the separate kratom law. Cannabis
and kratom appeared as examples in category 5, the group for drugs outside
the first four categories.[@1] The [1935 entry](/history/thailand-cannabis-act-1935/)
follows the earlier cannabis prohibition.

The law's explanatory note said the existing legislation was old and some
provisions no longer suited contemporary conditions. The replacement was
intended to make suppression and control more effective and bring domestic
law into line with Thailand's international drug agreements.[@1]

## Growing, giving and smoking were covered

Production included planting and cultivation. Distribution included selling,
exchanging and giving: an unpaid transfer was within the same definition.
For category 5 drugs, production, distribution, import, export and possession
required individual ministerial permission with committee approval.[@1]

The original law treated possession of at least ten kilograms as possession
for distribution. Manufacture, distribution, import and export carried the
same two-to-fifteen-year prison band as possession for sale. Ordinary
possession had the lower five-year ceiling.[@1] These were the original
1979 amounts and penalties; later amendments changed the framework.[@2]

Use had its own prohibition. Smoking or otherwise consuming a category 5
drug could bring up to one year in prison and a fine of up to 10,000 baht.
A person could therefore face a use offence as well as restrictions on what
they grew, held or passed to someone else.[@1]

## The framework outlives its original penalties

Parliament repeatedly amended the Act over the next four decades. The
replacement legislation of 2021 expressly repealed the 1979 Act and its
successive amendments, including the 2019 amendment through which the
[medical opening](/history/thailand-medical-2019/) had been made.[@2,3]

The [Narcotics Code of December 2021](/history/thailand-narcotics-code-2021/)
put drug control, personal-use penalties and treatment provisions into a new
framework.[@2] The [Thailand profile](/country/th/) follows the later changes.
