Before 1975, the Armenian SSR's criminal code punished making and dealing in drugs but did not make simple possession for personal use a crime. A USSR decree on strengthening the fight against drug addiction changed that, and Armenia implemented it on 29 January 1975.[@1,2]

## The union decree

The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet adopted the decree on 25 April 1974. It called for a stronger campaign against addiction across the union and became the model for republican amendments. The union text also called for treatment and prevention, but the Armenian amendment that followed concentrated on criminal liability.[@2] The exact union penalties were not copied verbatim everywhere; the republics wrote their own versions into their criminal codes.

## Armenia's amendment

The Armenian SSR's Presidium amended Articles 229 and 230 of the 1969 criminal code on 29 January 1975. Possession of drugs without intent to sell became punishable by up to five years in prison. The decree also added Article 229-1 for theft of drugs and Article 229-2 for inducing another person to use them.[@1]

Five years was a heavier penalty than the union decree's three-year term. The republican code thus did more than copy Moscow: it made the user a criminal on its own scale. The theft and inducement offences widened the code beyond simple possession, and the five-year term made Armenia's response one of the harsher republican versions.[@1]

## After 1975

The code was amended again in 1982 and 1987. The 1982 decree adjusted the drug penalties, and the 1987 decree added Article 230-1, which made repeated cultivation of ordinary cannabis or opium poppy after an administrative penalty a crime punishable by up to three years.[@1] The code remained in force until Armenia's post-Soviet criminal code replaced it.[@1] The [1987 entry](/history/armenia-soviet-cultivation-repeat-1987/) records that change.

The 1975 offence survived until the 2008 reform, when Armenia repealed the criminal offence of use and created administrative fines for small possession.[@3] The [2008 article](/history/armenia-use-decriminalised-2009/) follows that reversal, and the [Armenia profile](/country/am/) sets out the current rules.
