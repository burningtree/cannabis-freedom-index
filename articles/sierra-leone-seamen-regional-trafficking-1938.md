Prohibition turned Sierra Leonean sailors into traffickers. Once colonial bans started
in 1926, British officers complained that seamen brought cannabis to ports from Gambia
to Ghana — and by 1933 the sailors were smuggling diamba to ports from Senegal to
Nigeria and beyond.[@1]

The networks reached across the Atlantic: in 1938 one sailor was arrested with diamba
in New York City.[@1,2] The word travelled with the trade — *diamba*, the Bantu name
heard from Angola to Sierra Leone to Brazil, moved along the same sea routes as the
sailors who carried it.[@1]

The seamen's trade is the deep root of the coast's cannabis economy: a century later
Freetown's kush crisis and its wraps-counted raids run on networks the colonial state
first mapped when it tried, and failed, to keep diamba off its ships.
