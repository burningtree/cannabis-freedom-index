Eleven years after Poland made possession of any amount of a drug a crime, parliament gave prosecutors a way to let small cases go. The amendment of 1 April 2011 added Article 62a to the Act on counteracting drug addiction. It took effect on 9 December 2011.

## Article 62a

Where the drugs are of a small quantity and intended for the offender's own use, proceedings may be discontinued, even before an investigation is formally opened, if imposing a penalty would serve no purpose in view of the circumstances and the degree of social harm.

Three things about the wording matter.

- It is a "may". The prosecutor or court decides, case by case.
- It does not define a small quantity.
- Possession stays a crime under Article 62; the new article stops a prosecution and does not make the act lawful.

## The other half of the amendment

The same act pulled in the opposite direction for larger cases.

| Provision | Change |
|---|---|
| Article 62(2) | possession of a significant quantity: 1 to 10 years in prison |
| Article 56(3) | trafficking in a significant quantity: 2 to 12 years and a fine |
| Article 70a | where dependence or harmful use is suspected, a certified specialist gathers information on the accused's drug use for the court |
| Article 72 | a prosecutor may suspend proceedings while a dependent or harmful user completes treatment or an education programme |
| Article 73a | a prison sentence may be interrupted for treatment when no more than two years remain |

## How much it was used

Little, at first. Justice ministry figures for 2012, the first full year, show 2,307 cases ended under Article 62a.

| Who | Cases discontinued | Share of their cases |
|---|---|---|
| Prosecutors | 2,145 | about 11.6% |
| Courts | 162 | under 0.9% |

In all, 18,441 possession cases were recorded that year.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Poland](/country/pl/) from 9 to 11 with the [2011 amendment](/history/poland-prosecutor-discretion-2011/). Only enforcement changed, from 1 to 2: there is now a lawful way for a small case to end without a conviction, but it depends on the discretion of an official and was applied in roughly one case in nine.
