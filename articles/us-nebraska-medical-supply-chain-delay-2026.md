Nebraska's medical cannabis programme has licensed growers, but the remaining
route to patients is unfinished. At the commission's 19 October 2026 meeting,
growers were told to prepare for potentially lengthy storage before their crops
could enter a functioning regulated supply chain, Nebraska Public Media reported.

## A crop ahead of its market

Commissioners discussed a possible wait of one to two years while arrangements
for manufacturing, distribution and testing were developed. Manufacturers and
dispensaries remained unlicensed. That discussion was an estimate of possible
delay, rather than a new statutory waiting period or an announced retail date.

The practical distinction matters: authorising cultivation does not complete
the testing, processing and dispensing steps needed to put products into
patients' hands.

## The testing gap

The difficulty was already visible before the meeting. In its 14 October
report, the Nebraska Examiner described a grower nearing harvest without a
pathway to laboratory testing. The newspaper also reported that the commission
lacked statutory authority to license or oversee testing facilities.

That reporting identifies a problem extending beyond the speed of individual
business applications. A cultivation licence alone cannot resolve missing
authority or an incomplete testing system. The existence of plants is therefore
an incomplete measure of whether a medical programme provides usable access.

## What the commission expects next

The commission's published timetable anticipates consideration of its proposed
rulemaking package on 16 November. Adoption would still be followed by review
by the Attorney General and approval by the Governor. Further changes or
another hearing may be necessary after public comments are reviewed.

The agency anticipates revised rules and a new manufacturer application period
in early 2027. It says it may select up to four manufacturers. Meanwhile,
the current licensing structure, including six-month renewals, continues.
Those are stages in an approval process; the timetable does not promise
that patients will be able to buy products when applications reopen.

The October meeting is recorded as an implementation development in the
