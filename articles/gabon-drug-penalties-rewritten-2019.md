Gabon's new Penal Code, signed on 5 July 2019, retained a shorter punishment for illicit drug use while rewriting possession and supply penalties. Use carried up to a year and a fine; the general possession offence set ten years and a fine of up to 100 million CFA francs.

## Earlier law separated use from possession

The [drug provisions amended in 1993](/history/gabon-penal-code-drug-amendment-1993/) had punished illicit use with two months to a year and a fine of 100,000–500,000 francs. Unauthorised possession, cultivation and supply carried two to ten years and a fine of 250,000–50 million.

The replacement code named cannabis and its derivatives among the drugs covered. It maintained different offences for use, general possession, direct supply to a user and organising the drug trade.

## The prison and fine provisions changed together

The new use offence set a maximum of one year and a fine of up to 1.5 million francs. A court could order medical supervision or detoxification and withhold that punishment where the user needed treatment.

Evading the treatment order carried up to three years and a fine of up to two million francs. The earlier evasion offence had carried six months to two years and a much higher possible fine.

Giving or offering drugs directly for another person's consumption carried up to five years and a fine of up to ten million. General transport, possession, offering, transfer or acquisition was subject to the ten-year prison term and a fine of up to 100 million, outside the separately specified trafficking offences.

## Organising production or trafficking had longer terms

Directing a group involved in drug transport, possession, supply or trade carried thirty years and a fine equal to five times the value seized. Organising illicit production or manufacture carried twenty years and a fine of up to fifty million.

Organising import or export also carried twenty years, with a fine tied to the value seized. Organised-group cases brought higher penalties. These rules made the person's role in the operation significant alongside the conduct involved.

## The next code retained the drug rules

The [2020 replacement code](/history/gabon-drug-penalties-retained-2020/) kept these core drug provisions and renumbered them. It also retained confiscation and destruction of seized drugs.

In [May 2026, Libreville prosecutors destroyed seized cannabis and other drugs](/history/gabon-libreville-seized-drugs-destroyed-2026/) in an operation attended by Justice Minister Augustin Emane. The ministry said the aim was to prevent seized drugs returning to sale, linking the destruction requirement to enforcement in [Gabon](/country/ga/).
