The law that began federal cannabis prohibition in the United States did not prohibit
anything. It was a tax of one dollar an ounce, written that way because its authors
doubted Congress could do directly what they wanted.

## Before the Act

Most states had already acted, beginning with
[Massachusetts in 1911](/history/us-massachusetts-first-state-1911/); by 1937 at
least 46 had a marijuana law. What remained legal was medicine: cannabis extracts had
been in the pharmacopoeia since 1850.

[Harry Anslinger](/history/us-federal-bureau-of-narcotics-1930/), head of the Federal
Bureau of Narcotics from 1930, came to treat cannabis as a serious threat in 1934. He
collected some 200 police reports of crimes blamed on marijuana and, in July 1937,
published "Marijuana: Assassin of Youth".

## Why a tax

The Treasury was not sure a federal ban would survive in court. Its lawyer, Clinton
Hester, modelled the bill on the Harrison Act and the National Firearms Act, and was
candid: "The primary purpose of this legislation must be to raise revenue." The
Senate report gave the real aim, to "render extremely difficult the acquisition of
marihuana by persons who desire it for illicit uses".

## The hearings

The House committee sat for five days in April and May 1937. Hemp and birdseed
merchants won exemptions for fibre, oil and sterilised seed. The last witness was
Dr William C. Woodward of the American Medical Association, who opposed the bill.

> The term "marijuana" is a mongrel word that has crept into this country over the
> Mexican border.

He had asked the Bureau of Prisons, the Children's Bureau and the Public Health
Service for evidence of the harms described; none had any, and none had been called.
The committee was hostile. The House passed the bill on 14 June without a recorded
vote, and Roosevelt signed it on 2 August.

## What it required

Anyone handling cannabis had to register and pay a yearly tax of $1 to $24. Every
transfer needed an official order form and a transfer tax: $1 an ounce to a
registered person, $100 an ounce to anyone else. Breaking the rules meant up to five
years in prison. Doctors and pharmacists were exempt on paper.

The Act took effect on 1 October. The next day Samuel Caldwell was arrested in Denver
for selling cannabis, and within the week he had
[four years in Leavenworth](/history/us-first-tax-act-convictions-1937/).

## How it ended

Congress added mandatory minimums in [1951](/history/us-boggs-act-1951/) and
[1956](/history/us-narcotic-control-act-1956/). Then the fiction undid the law. In
[1969](/history/us-leary-1969/) the Supreme Court held, in Timothy Leary's case, that
complying with the Act meant incriminating oneself, contrary to the Fifth Amendment. Congress
replaced it with the
[Controlled Substances Act](/history/us-controlled-substances-act-1970/).

Historians are cautious about the popular explanations. Unequal enforcement is not in
doubt, but recent work finds the first laws were aimed at keeping cannabis from white
youth, not at Mexican immigrants. And the hearings exempted the hemp industry that
the Act was supposedly designed to destroy.
