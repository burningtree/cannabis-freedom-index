In May 2024 Romania made drug-driving one of its hardest traffic crimes: one to five years in
prison plus a ban on exercising rights for driving under the influence of a psychoactive
substance, two to seven years for public passenger transport, dangerous goods, a driving lesson
or a driving test.[@2] Then the judges read the law back — and asked what "under the influence"
actually means when a laboratory can find last week's joint in today's blood.[@1]

## Two things, not one

On 27 January 2025 the High Court of Cassation and Justice settled the point, bindingly from
the next day's Official Gazette: the offence needs two things, not one — the substance in a
biological sample, and proof that it was capable of affecting the ability to drive.[@1] A
positive test alone no longer convicts; neither does the court demand a number, setting no THC
threshold at all.[@1]

## The middle ground

The ruling cut both ways. Drivers gained a real defence: trace THC without impairing capacity
is not a crime, and expert evidence about the substance's effects decides the case.[@1] But the
court refused to make the prosecution prove a mistake on the road — erratic driving, a crash, a
failed manoeuvre all sit outside what must be shown.[@1] Capacity, not conduct: more than a drug
test, less than a crash, and the laboratory report now comes with a pharmacology lesson.
