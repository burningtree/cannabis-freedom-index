On 8 November 2016, the day Donald Trump was elected president, voters in four American states approved legal cannabis for adults.[@1,2,3,4] Colorado and Washington had gone first in [2012](/history/us-colorado-washington-2012/), Oregon and Alaska followed in 2014. The 2016 votes mattered more than either, because one of the four states was California. Arizona rejected a similar question the same day.[@5]

## Four votes

| State | Measure | Yes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Proposition 64 | 57.1% | law from 9 November 2016; shops from January 2018 [@1] |
| Nevada | Question 2 | 54.5% | one ounce legal from 1 January 2017; shops from 1 July 2017 [@2] |
| Massachusetts | Question 4 | 53.7% | the legislature delayed shop licensing to July 2018 [@3] |
| Maine | Question 1 | 50.3% | 381,768 to 377,773; recount abandoned in December; law from 30 January 2017, retail postponed [@4] |

Maine's margin, under 4,000 votes, made it the closest of the four.[@1,2,3,4]

## California

California had been first with medical cannabis in 1996 and had rejected legalisation at the ballot in 1972 and again in 2010.[@1] Proposition 64, the Adult Use of Marijuana Act, made possession of up to an ounce lawful and set up a taxed and licensed market, following the recommendations of the state's Blue Ribbon Commission on Marijuana Policy, whose supporters included the lieutenant governor, Gavin Newsom.[@1]

The measure did not clear old records by itself. People serving sentences for things it made legal became eligible for resentencing.[@1]

## Nevada

Nevada's vote reversed the state's own history: until 2001 it had been the only state where possessing any amount of cannabis was a felony,[@6] and its voters had rejected legalisation in 2002 and 2006.[@2] Question 2 allowed an ounce for adults over 21 and six plants at home, but only for people living more than 25 miles from a dispensary.[@2,6]

## What did not change

Federal law. Cannabis remained a Schedule I substance under the Controlled Substances Act of 1970, and everything these states licensed stayed a federal crime.

## Why the index moved

The index moved the [United States](/country/us/) from 24 to 34 with the [2016 votes](/history/us-legalisation-wave-2016/). Possession rises from 3 to 4, cultivation from 2 to 3, sharing from 2 to 4, consumption from 2 to 3 and access from 3 to 5; enforcement stays at 2, since federal law did not move. The size of the step reflects California: one magazine reckoned at the time that its vote would roughly triple the number of Americans living in states with legal cannabis. Most of the states that are legal today followed in the decade after.
