Cyprus made it a crime in January 2000 to supply or sell cannabis seeds while knowing that the recipient planned to grow illegally. The new offence carried up to eight years in prison, a fine of up to 1,500 Cyprus pounds, or both.

## The restriction reached the sale before a crop existed

The [1977 drug law](/history/cyprus-narcotics-act-1977/) already controlled unauthorised cannabis growing and supply. The 2000 amendment added a specific offence concerning seeds, making the seller’s knowledge of the buyer’s intended cultivation central.

The wording covered supplying seeds, offering them for sale and selling them. It also made involvement in another person’s prohibited conduct an offence.

For a seller, the legal issue could therefore arise before germination or harvest. Prosecutors had to connect the transaction with knowledge that the recipient intended unlawful cultivation.

## Knowing the intended use was part of the offence

The sale offence required a supply transaction and the supplier’s knowledge of the recipient’s plans. Holding seeds and supplying them for a known unlawful crop were different questions under the wording.

It applied to seeds of plants in the cannabis genus. A person buying seeds to start an unlawful crop could therefore expose a knowing supplier to a separate criminal charge.

The law also preserved the government’s power to authorise activities through regulations. That power later became important to the licensed hemp and medical-cannabis systems.

## Licensed hemp later received a separate route

In [April 2016](/history/cyprus-industrial-hemp-2016/), Cyprus introduced licences for industrial-hemp producers and suppliers. A companion amendment made the drug law’s cultivation and seed rules subject to that new agricultural law.

Qualifying hemp initially had to contain no more than 0.2% THC. Licensed activities required compliance with the agricultural rules, including seed and premises controls.

This gave farmers and suppliers a specific legal route for qualifying crops and seed. The general prohibition continued to matter for seeds knowingly supplied for unlawful cannabis growing.

## Imports were added to the seed clause in 2019

The 2019 drug-law amendment added importing cannabis seeds to the prohibited conduct, alongside changes allowing the government to regulate medical-cannabis production and trade.

The [medical regulations](/history/cyprus-medical-expanded-2019/) then supplied exemptions and licensing arrangements for the medical system. Hemp retained its separate agricultural route.

[Cyprus’s profile](/country/cy/) now distinguishes ordinary cannabis cultivation, seed supply linked to illegal growing, and licensed industrial-hemp seed trade. Those distinctions explain why an agricultural permission and a criminal seed offence can appear in the same body of legislation.
