BVI lawmakers approved a medical cannabis licensing bill on 30 June 2020, proposing a local authority to regulate cultivation, processing, distribution and sales.[@1,2] The plan still needed assent, and the Governor later called for an agreement with the UK over licensing responsibilities and international obligations.[@3]

## A new authority would oversee the industry

The published bill proposed an authority responsible for licensing and managing a medicinal cannabis system.[@2] Its remit covered growing, processing, testing, import, export, distribution and sale, putting those activities within a dedicated regulatory structure.[@2]

The proposal also provided for licensed dispensaries, standards and records, linking the supply of cannabis to authorised businesses.[@2] Its commencement clause left the start date to a later ministerial notice.[@2] Passage through the House was therefore one stage in the proposed system's development.[@1,2]

## Patients and therapeutic users had different proposed routes

The published dispensing clause provided three routes: up to one gram after proof of adult age, up to fifty grams after a declaration of therapeutic use, or an amount recommended in a medical document.[@2] Dispensaries would issue receipts recording the quantity and retain the information in a register.[@2]

Those details describe the published proposal.[@2] The official passage register records the House's approval, with no assent or gazette publication entered for the bill.[@1] A [companion record-clearing bill](/history/bvi-cannabis-expungement-bill-2020/) passed two days later.[@1]

## The Governor wanted licensing responsibilities settled

In [December 2020](/history/bvi-cannabis-bills-held-2020/), Governor Augustus Jaspert said the UK Home Office then acted as the BVI's narcotics licensing authority.[@3] He said the proposed local authority required preliminary work and a formal agreement to transfer that responsibility and protect compliance with the international drug conventions.[@3]

Jaspert supported economic diversification through a new industry, while tying assent to those arrangements.[@3] The dispute therefore involved both the planned industry and responsibility for supervising it.[@3]

## A later premier considered another route

In [January 2023](/history/bvi-medical-cannabis-way-forward-2023/), Premier Natalio Wheatley said talks with the UK had identified a way towards licensing doctors to administer medical cannabis.[@4] He suggested examining regulations under existing legislation while reconsidering whether the 2020 bill remained the preferred route.[@4]

An official July 2026 review still reported the bill awaiting assent.[@5] That unresolved step remains part of the reform history of the [British Virgin Islands](/country/vg/).[@5]
