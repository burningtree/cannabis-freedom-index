On 1 September 2020 France began punishing cannabis use with a €200 fine handed out by the police. The offence did not change. Using or possessing cannabis remained what the law of 31 December 1970 made it, a crime carrying up to a year in prison and a fine of €3,750. What changed was how it ends.

## Seven years of announcements

The fine was promised long before it arrived.

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| April 2013 | the interior minister, Manuel Valls, says he intends to make possession of a small amount a fine-only matter, with no amount or date |
| October 2014 | a penal settlement for minor offences, cannabis use among them, comes into application |
| May 2017 | the interior ministry announces the reform Emmanuel Macron promised in his campaign |
| January 2018 | the interior minister, Gérard Collomb, says the government will make the offence a fixed-fine one "without decriminalising it" |
| November 2018 | the National Assembly votes the principle of a €200 fine payable at once |
| 16 June 2020 | trials begin in Rennes, Reims, Créteil, Lille and Marseille |
| 1 September 2020 | the fine applies across the country |

## A fine inside the criminal law

The French name is the amende forfaitaire délictuelle: a fixed fine for a crime. The phrase is exact. The prosecutor keeps the power to send a case to the criminal court instead, where the person still faces a year in prison and €3,750.

It is not decriminalisation, which Collomb ruled out by name.

## The background

Figures cited from Marseille put a fifth of police activity there on the cannabis trade, with 9,095 drug cases in 2015, nine in ten of them for cannabis.

Opinion had also moved. A poll in November 2013 found 55% of the French opposed to decriminalising cannabis. One in October 2016 found them evenly split, 50% to 49%, on allowing use under conditions, and by 2021 a poll found 51% in favour of decriminalisation.

## Why the index moved

The [fine](/history/france-fixed-fine-2020/) raised [France's](/country/fr/) total from 11 to 13. Enforcement went from 2 to 3, the level between "prison possible, fines typical" and "fines or diversion only". Possession stays at 1, a crime that usually ends in a fine, because the fine is a criminal penalty and prison remains available.
