Pakistan abolished whipping as a non-hadd legal punishment in April 1996, removing the lash sentences that the prohibition order had prescribed for cannabis possession and consumption.[@1,2] The law also remitted non-hadd whipping sentences already imposed but still unexecuted.[@1] Prison terms and the underlying cannabis offences remained.[@1,2]

## Cannabis had been punishable by lashes

The [1979 prohibition order](/history/pakistan-prohibition-1979/) expressly listed herbal cannabis, resin, hashish and preparations made from them.[@2] It allowed up to thirty lashes as an alternative to two years in prison for possession, with a fine also possible.[@2] Its consumption rules likewise allowed whipping as a non-hadd punishment.[@2]

Hadd was a separate category of punishment prescribed by the Quran or Sunnah.[@1,2] The order's eighty-lash hadd rule concerned an adult Muslim drinking alcoholic liquor by mouth; its definition of liquor excluded solid intoxicants, including when liquefied.[@2] That distinction determined which lash sentences the new law abolished.[@1,2]

## A reported hashish flogging showed the punishment in use

The UN torture rapporteur recorded a report that two Irishmen convicted of smuggling hashish received five lashes in Peshawar Central Jail on 8 October 1995.[@3] His account attributed the incident to Amnesty International's citation of Pakistani media.[@3] It supplied a concrete example of cannabis punishment shortly before the repeal.[@3]

The same report traced the government's abolition proposal to November 1995 and linked it to objections that whipping violated human dignity.[@3]

## Unexecuted sentences were remitted

The President assented to the Abolition of the Punishment of Whipping Act on 3 April 1996.[@1] The Act said it came into force at once; its Gazette publication followed on 15 April.[@1]

Section 3 abolished whipping provided under any existing law, rule or regulation, except where it was prescribed as hadd.[@1] The accompanying remission rule reached sentences already awarded by a court or tribunal when the lashes had not yet been carried out.[@1] Parliament also repealed the Whipping Act of 1909.[@1]

## Cannabis offences continued under prison-based laws

The repeal removed a form of punishment while leaving the prohibition order's possession and consumption offences in place.[@1,2] The [1997 narcotics law](/history/pakistan-narcotics-act-1997/) then set quantity-based penalties for unauthorised possession and supply, including fines, prison and possible death for the largest band.[@4]

It also required provincial registration of people dependent on drugs and provided for treatment centres.[@4] Ending non-hadd whipping was one stage within [Pakistan's longer cannabis-law history](/country/pk/).[@1,4]
