On 1 January 1998 Austria got a new drug law. The Narcotic Substances Act (Suchtmittelgesetz) replaced the 1951 Narcotics Regulation and brought Austrian law into line with the European Union, which Austria had joined three years earlier.[@3] Its core idea was a sharp line between traffickers and people with drug problems — and a cannabis-specific shortcut through the bureaucracy.[@2]

## What stayed: therapy instead of punishment

The 1998 Act kept Austria's established diversion. Anyone reported for acquiring or possessing a small quantity for personal use must have the case provisionally shelved by the prosecutor for a two-year probation period, with health measures such as treatment where needed.[@1] Prosecution resumes only if the conditions are breached; a clean probation ends the case.[@1] Possession itself stayed criminal, punishable by up to six months.[@2]

## The cannabis shortcut

Section 35(4) of the new law singled cannabis out. Where a person was reported solely for acquiring or possessing cannabis in a small quantity for personal use, the prosecutor could skip the health-authority opinion on whether treatment was needed — unless a similar report had been made within the previous five years.[@1] That rule survives in today's law: qualifying cannabis cases are put on hold for one to two years and then dropped, without a mandatory health assessment for first reports.[@1]

## Quantities decide

The law sorts cases by amount. Whether a quantity counts as small or above the threshold is defined in a Ministry of Health decree, and the threshold is what separates the shelved personal-use case from trafficking charges.[@2] That mechanism, too, dates to 1998 and still structures every Austrian cannabis case.

## Why it mattered

The Act continued the approach of the 1985 amendment, which had introduced provisional shelving of small-quantity cases and treatment-based sentence deferral, rather than inventing diversion from scratch.[@2] Later amendments tightened the screws elsewhere — above-threshold trafficking drew one to ten years, up to life in the gravest cases, and growing cannabis to obtain narcotics became an explicit offence in 2008 — but the 1998 compromise still defines [Austria's](/country/at/) cannabis law: a crime on paper, a shelved case in practice.
