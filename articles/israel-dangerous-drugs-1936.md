When Israel became independent in May 1948, it did not write a new drug law. It kept the British one. The 1936 Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, a colonial tightening aimed at hashish traffickers and cafés, became the new state's drug law and remained the basis of the 1973 Dangerous Drugs Ordinance still in force today.[@1,2]

## A colonial text survives

The Provisional Council of State's Law and Administration Ordinance provided that the law in force in Palestine on 14 May 1948 continued in force, with changes arising from statehood.[@2] The 1936 ordinance therefore carried over. In 1973 the Knesset reissued it as the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance [New Version], keeping its structure, its definitions and most of its penalties. The reissue kept the ordinance's numbering and its colonial vocabulary, which is why Israeli lawyers still cite sections drafted under the Mandate.[@3]

The [1948 entry](/history/israel-law-administration-1948/) records that continuity. It is why a law drafted in Mandatory Jerusalem still supplies the twenty-year maxima used for cultivation and supply in the 2020s.

## What the 1936 law did

The 1936 ordinance raised penalties for traffickers, stated the transit offence more plainly, and added Article 14, which punished premises used for preparing or smoking hashish and the possession of pipes.[@1] The target was the visible café trade in Jaffa, Haifa, Jerusalem, Acre and Nablus, and later in Tel Aviv. The [Palestine article](/history/palestine-dangerous-drugs-1936/) tells that story in detail.

Enforcement was weak. The administration lacked money and will, and hashish policing ranked below immigration control and communal tensions. The ordinance was tougher on paper than in practice.[@1]

## What survived

The 1973 New Version kept the prohibition on growing, manufacturing and supplying without a licence, and the twenty-year maximum for supply "whether for payment or not".[@3] It also kept the Second Schedule, whose 15 g cannabis line still separates personal use from the presumption of dealing.[@3] The 2022 administrative fines changed how small possession is handled, not the underlying colonial architecture.

The [Israel profile](/country/il/) sets out the current fines, the criminal bands and the medical programme. The 1936 ordinance is the reason so much of that law still reads like a Mandate statute.
