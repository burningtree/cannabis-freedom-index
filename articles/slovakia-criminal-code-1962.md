On 1 January 1962 Czechoslovakia's new Criminal Code, No. 140/1961, came into force, and with it the drug offences that applied in Slovakia for the rest of the communist period. The [Opium Act of 1938](/history/slovakia-opium-act-1938/) had been a special law; from now on drugs were a matter for the general criminal code.

## Section 187

The central provision punished a person who, without permission, manufactured, imported, exported, procured for another or possessed narcotic substances or poisons.

| Case | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Basic offence | up to two years in prison, a corrective measure or a fine |
| Aggravated: as a member of an organised group, for substantial benefit, or causing serious injury to several people or death | two to eight years |

Section 188 added a separate offence of making, obtaining or keeping an object suitable for the unauthorised production of narcotics, with up to two years.

## The word that mattered

The code punished possession without saying for whom. A person holding a drug for their own use was therefore within it, and could be prosecuted without any proof of intent to supply. Lawful handling was possible only through authorised channels.

That wording stood for twenty-eight years. It was changed on 1 July [1990](/history/slovakia-decriminalisation-1990/), when an amendment required the possession to be for another person.

## One code, two successor states

The code was federal law and applied identically in the Czech and Slovak parts of the country. After 1993 each republic amended its inherited copy separately, and the Slovak one was tightened first. The same step is described for Czechia [here](/history/czechoslovakia-criminal-code-1962/).

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Slovakia](/country/sk/) from 20 to 12 with the [1962 code](/history/slovakia-criminal-code-1962/). Possession and cultivation fall from 2 to 1, enforcement from 3 to 2 and access from 1 to 0. Sharing stays at 1 and consumption at 2: the code reached possession by anyone, but did not make use a separate offence.
