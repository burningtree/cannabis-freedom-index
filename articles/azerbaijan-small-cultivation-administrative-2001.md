Azerbaijan did not legalise home growing in 2001. It did, however, place a small personal grow on a different legal track from larger cultivation—a distinction that remains in the law today.

## A new administrative offence

An amendment dated 12 October 2001 added Article 123-1 to the country’s 2000 Code of Administrative Offences. The translated code’s amendment notes say the change was published on 1 December.

The provision covered sowing, growing or gathering plants containing narcotic substances in an amount required for personal needs and without an aim of distribution. Its stated punishment was a fine of 10 to 15 manats. That made the conduct an administrative violation rather than the criminal cultivation offence applied to larger quantities.

The change came decades after Azerbaijan had been covered by the Soviet prohibition on unauthorised cannabis cultivation. It did not create a lawful right to grow, and it did not authorise sharing or sale.

## The distinction survives

The current Code of Administrative Offences carries the rule in Article 208. Its language now refers to cultivating—sowing, growing or gathering—a small quantity of plants containing narcotic substances. The present penalty is substantially heavier: a fine of 200 to 300 manats or, when the circumstances and the offender’s identity make a fine insufficient, administrative detention for up to two months.

The code defines “small quantity” by reference to the threshold set in separate legislation. This matters because the legal category turns on quantity, not merely on a claim that the plants were intended for personal use.

## Above the threshold

Article 237 of the consolidated Criminal Code governs cultivation in a substantial quantity. The basic offence can bring a 3,000–6,000 manat fine, one to three years’ restriction of liberty, or up to three years’ imprisonment. Group, repeat, large-scale and especially large-scale cases carry higher prison ranges, reaching ten years.

The wider narcotics law still says cultivation and production of narcotic plants are prohibited, apart from experimental samples of medicines produced under state control. The administrative rule is therefore a lower level of punishment, not permission.

## What moved in the index

The event raises Azerbaijan’s personal-cultivation segment from **0 to 2**: a small grow is prohibited but handled administratively, while larger cultivation remains criminal. The country’s overall position remains highly restrictive because even the administrative offence can lead to detention and there is no lawful recreational supply.
