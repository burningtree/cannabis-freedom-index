Burma's cannabis ban is usually dated to [1891](/history/myanmar-ban-1891/). The year appears in histories of the drug conventions.[@3] It was not the year of a law. By 1891 cannabis had been prohibited in British Burma for nearly two decades; the parliamentary exchange that year changed nothing.[@1,2]

## A ban from the 1870s

The prohibition grew out of alarm over hemp and insanity. An 1872 inquiry linked habitual hemp use to insanity, and the Government of India resolved on 17 December 1873 to prohibit ganja absolutely in British Burma.[@2] Sale and cultivation stopped from 1873–74, and [import by sea was banned in September 1874](/history/burma-hemp-drugs-prohibited-1874/).[@2] The ban was aimed at Burmans, but most consumers were Indian labourers, soldiers and police; smuggling continued.[@2] The Excise Act of 1881 put the policy on a statutory footing.[@2]

## The question that became a date

On 16 July 1891 Mark Stewart, the MP for Kirkcudbright, asked the Under-Secretary of State for India about a report in the *Allahabad Pioneer* that "the lunatic asylums of India are filled with ganja smokers".[@1] He noted that possession and sale had been prohibited "for many years past" in Lower Burma, and that the province's 1881–82 excise report called the exclusion "of immense benefit to the people". Would the government extend it to the rest of British India?[@1] John Gorst confirmed the exclusion, promised to ask the Government of India about further steps, and cited higher duty-paid ganja prices in Bengal.[@1]

## An inquiry, not a ban

The question pushed cannabis onto the imperial agenda. In 1893 the Government of India appointed the Indian Hemp Drugs Commission, which heard evidence from Burma and reported in 1894.[@2] It recommended keeping the prohibition on cultivation and on use by Burmans, while allowing licensed sales to Indians living there. The Commission thought the Burmese had no appetite for the drug and that officials supported the ban; its proposal was a limited relaxation.[@2]

## What the date hides

Modern summaries compress the story. A 2020 essay in *AJIL Unbound* states that "British Burma in 1891 prohibited cannabis due to concerns over its mental health effects".[@3] That sentence drops the 1873 resolution, the 1874 import ban and the 1881 Act, leaving a parliamentary question looking like a law. In Burma, 1891 brought no new offence or penalty: the excise reports record one cultivation prosecution in 1890–91 and 31 ganja breaches in 1891–92.[@2] The ban hardened instead of loosening. The [1939 rules](/history/myanmar-licensed-1939/) allowed licensed sales to Indians only, and the [1974](/history/myanmar-narcotics-law-1974/) and [1993](/history/myanmar-narcotics-law-1993/) drug laws turned prohibition into the modern prison regime.[@4] Today possession of even a small amount carries five to ten years, and more than 100 grams is deemed possession for sale; the [2025 amendment](/history/myanmar-narcotics-amendment-2025/) kept both rules and made life imprisonment mean incarceration until death.[@4,5] The date survives because it is easy to copy, not because it marks a legal change. The [country profile](/country/mm/) sets out what the ban means now.
