Three years after decriminalising small quantities, Trinidad and Tobago put a full medical
cannabis regime on the statute book — and then left it switched off. The Cannabis Control Act,
Act No. 10 of 2022, was assented to on 17 June 2022 and published in the Gazette on 23
June.[@1,2] But section 2 brings the Act into operation only on a date the President fixes by
proclamation, and Parliament's record still says proclamation is awaited.[@1,3]

On paper the regime is elaborate. The Act creates a Cannabis Licensing Authority and eight
licence types for medicinal, therapeutic or scientific purposes: Cultivator, Research and
Development, Laboratory, Processor (covering edibles and other derivatives), Retail
Distributor through a pharmacy or dispensary, Import, Export and Transport.[@1] Research and
laboratory licensees automatically receive import-export cover for their work.[@1] Unusually, the
Act also provides licences for religious purposes — cultivation, dispensing and import for
sacramental use.[@1]

The politics behind it were economic as much as medical. Regional experts argued a legal
cannabis sector could spur Caribbean economies, with Trinidad and Tobago named among the
islands that could benefit from cultivation, processing and medical tourism.[@4] The bill that
became the Act was introduced in 2020 on that promise: a regulated industry replacing the
illicit trade that has long crossed the Gulf of Paria.

None of it operates. With no proclamation date, no licences have issued, no dispensary has
opened, and the only lawful source of cannabis in the country remains an adult's own four
home-grown plants.[@3] Every element of the system — the Authority, the licences, the
religious-use route — exists in law but not in fact, leaving Trinidad and Tobago with
decriminalised possession and home growing beside a licensing Act that has never been turned
on.
