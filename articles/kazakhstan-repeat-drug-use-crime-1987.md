In July 1987 the Kazakh republic added a criminal offence aimed at people who kept a small amount of drugs for themselves after already being punished administratively. The same provision covered non-prescribed drug use. It was a narrowing of the gap between the administrative system and the criminal code, and it made repeat users the target.[@1]

## A second offence

The Kazakh SSR's criminal code already punished supply and cultivation. The 1987 decree added a separate crime for repeatedly buying or keeping a small personal quantity after an administrative penalty. A first administrative penalty did not make a user a criminal; a repeat did. The offence did not require a larger quantity than the first: the repeat itself was the aggravating fact. The provision also reached non-prescribed use, so a person could be prosecuted for what had been handled by inspectors and clinics.[@1]

The timing fits the union campaign of the late Soviet period. Moscow's 1987 edict made first-time small possession and use administrative offences, but it left republics room to keep a criminal response for repeats. Kazakhstan used that room.[@1]

## The 1991 reversal

The Kazakh republic changed course in June 1991. Law 694-XII removed non-prescribed drug use from the criminal offence while leaving the possession offence in place. It also ordered the closure of the Interior Ministry's treatment-and-labour institutions and the release of people held there, cleared the convictions of those sentenced for use or for escaping the institutions, and closed pending cases.[@2]

The [1991 entry](/history/kazakhstan-use-penalties-labour-camps-1991/) records that reform. It did not legalise drugs: possession and supply stayed criminal, and the state kept a separate power to order treatment alongside a sentence.[@2]

## What came next

Kazakhstan's post-Soviet codes rebuilt the rules again. The 1998 code punished personal possession only in the large-quantity band, and the current code returns even the smallest band to criminal law.[@3] The 1987 offence is a marker of how far the Soviet system was willing to go: a user who had already been fined could be turned into a criminal for the same small amount. The [Kazakhstan profile](/country/kz/) sets out the current bands and penalties.
