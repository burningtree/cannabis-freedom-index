South Vietnam's government ordered province chiefs to stop cannabis cultivation
in October 1968 and publicly condemned cannabis and opium use and trafficking.
An expanded Narcotics Bureau and closer cooperation with US officials put
growers and suppliers under greater pressure as American commanders sought to
stop drugs reaching their troops.

## Cannabis was easy to buy around Saigon

A US military survey in September 1966 found 29 fixed drug outlets around
Saigon, Cholon and Tan Son Nhut. Drivers, shopkeepers, hotel clerks and others
also supplied drugs to customers. General William Westmoreland asked the US
Embassy to act after receiving the findings.

The Army's official history described weak enforcement and poorly defined
Vietnamese drug laws. Cannabis dominated the military's early drug cases:
96 of the 100 cases investigated between July 1965 and June 1966 involved it.
For commanders, easy access outside military installations complicated efforts
to control soldiers' behaviour.

## Earlier restrictions had left stocks to be surrendered

A [Vietnamese order dated March 1954](/history/vietnam-indian-hemp-order-1954/)
had already restricted Indian-hemp imports, exports, manufacture, trade and
use, according to a contemporary Library of Congress survey. Holders had to
declare their stocks, and authorised medical and business supplies were to be
used up by the end of that year. Remaining stocks were to be surrendered.

## Local officials were told to suppress the supply

The October 1968 action brought cultivation directly into the government's
campaign. The US command's subsequent history described the Prime Minister's
circular as making province chiefs, mayors and police chiefs responsible for
eliminating cannabis and opium. Police and military instructions followed.

The enlarged Narcotics Bureau was to gather and act on information about users,
suppliers, stored drugs and fields. US commanders also called for more arrests
and education for troops. Their campaign presented cannabis use as a threat
to soldiers' ability to perform their duties in combat.

## The campaign reached fields and soldiers' baggage

In [May 1969](/history/vietnam-eradication-1969/), military aircraft began
searching the Delta for cannabis fields. Ground forces then cut and burned
the plants. Two [cannabis-detection dog teams](/history/vietnam-cannabis-detection-dogs-1969/)
arrived in August to search baggage and barracks areas.

A US health report later estimated that half a million plants had been
destroyed by 1971, mostly in the Delta. The crackdown had turned cannabis
control in southern [Vietnam](/country/vn/) into a campaign reaching from
farmers' fields to the places where soldiers lived and travelled.
