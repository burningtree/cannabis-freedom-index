In May 2024 President Cyril Ramaphosa signed the Cannabis for Private Purposes Act, the law
parliament wrote to answer the Constitutional Court. It lets adults grow, hold and use cannabis
in private and wipes old convictions, but it leaves selling a crime, and more than two years later
it has still not come into force.[@1,2]

## Six years late

In [September 2018](/history/south-africa-constitutional-court-2018/) the Constitutional Court
ruled that adults could use, possess and grow cannabis in private, and gave parliament 24 months
to fix the drug laws. If parliament failed, the court's own wording would stay in the law.[@3]
The deadline passed, and the court's wording became the rule adults lived by.

## What the Act says

An adult may use and possess cannabis, grow it in a private place, and give it to or receive it
from another adult in private, as long as no payment changes hands. Carrying it in public is
allowed; using it there is not.[@1] Cannabis comes off the drugs schedule of the
[1992 Act](/history/south-africa-drugs-act-1992/).[@1]

| Offence | Most it can bring |
|---|---|
| Dealing, or using a child to deal | 10 years in prison |
| Holding or growing more than the set limit | 5 years |
| Letting a child use cannabis | 12 months |
| Smoking in public, in a car or in front of a child | R2,000 fine |

A fine with no prison alternative does not count as a previous conviction.[@1] A child found with
cannabis is to be dealt with under child-care and treatment laws rather than the criminal
courts.[@1] Old convictions for using or possessing dagga, back to [1971](/history/south-africa-mandatory-minimums-1971/)
and including those under the laws of the former homelands, are to be expunged.[@1]

The Act sets no gram or plant limits itself: those are left to regulations.[@1]

## Still waiting

The Act starts only on a date the president proclaims.[@1] In December 2024 the Human Rights
Commission urged the government to write the regulations and set that date, saying police were
still arresting adults for private use.[@4] In [February 2026](/history/south-africa-draft-regulations-2026/)
the justice ministry published draft regulations for comment, which would cap possession at
750 g and growing at five plants. The ministry said they would take effect only after parliament
approved the limits.[@5,6] Buying, selling and the recognition of traditional growers are left to
other departments.[@5]

Until then the 2018 ruling remains the law in [South Africa](/country/za/): adults may grow and use
cannabis but have no legal place to buy it.
