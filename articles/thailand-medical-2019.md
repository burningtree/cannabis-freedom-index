On 19 February 2019 the Narcotics Act (No. 7) of the Buddhist year 2562 came into force in Thailand. It had been passed by the military-appointed National Legislative Assembly on 25 December 2018 and published in the Government Gazette the day before it took effect. It made Thailand the first country in South-East Asia to allow cannabis as medicine.

## What the amendment did

The Act amends the Narcotics Act of 1979, under which cannabis was a Type 5 narcotic. It keeps cannabis in that class, together with hemp, kratom and the opium poppy, and opens a licensed channel for it.

A Type 5 narcotic may now be planted, processed, imported, exported, distributed or possessed for treating patients, for medicine, or for research and development, with a licence from the Food and Drug Administration.

## Who may hold a licence

| Applicant | Condition |
|---|---|
| Government offices, universities, medical professionals | eligible |
| Farmers' social enterprises | eligible |
| Individuals | Thai nationals living in Thailand |
| Companies | registered in Thailand, with at least two thirds of directors, partners or shareholders Thai |

Licences run for a fixed term and can be renewed. Holders must send the regulator monthly and yearly lists of what they hold.

A patient may take a Type 5 narcotic as medicine only on a medical prescription.

## What stayed a crime

Use for any purpose other than medicine or research remained prohibited. Doing any of the licensed activities without a licence carries one to five years in prison, a fine of 20,000 to 1,500,000 baht, or both.

## The door left open

Two clauses looked ahead. The Narcotics Control Board could, within two years, designate areas by royal decree where cannabis might be grown, processed, tested, used or possessed. And the Act allowed for amendment after five years to permit uses beyond medicine and research.

Thailand did not wait five years. In [June 2022](/history/thailand-decriminalisation-2022/) cannabis was taken off the narcotics list altogether, and in 2025 the government pulled back to prescription-only supply.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Thailand](/country/th/) from 6 to 7 with the [2019 Act](/history/thailand-medical-2019/). Only access changed, from 0 to 1: a prescription route existed for patients, while possession, growing and use outside it kept the penalties of the 1979 law.
