In 1937 St Kitts and Nevis criminalised the cultivation, trade and possession of cannabis by Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, following the trend in Britain and the United States.[@1] Jamaica had shown the regional way with its Opium Law of 1913; Britain amended its Dangerous Drugs Act for cannabis imports in 1925 and for possession in 1928; America imposed its Marihuana Tax Act in 1937.[@1] The Federation's ordinance belonged to that wave.[@1]

## Preserved regulations

The 1937 Ordinance lasted nearly fifty years.[@1] Its replacement, the Drugs (Prevention and Abatement of the Misuse and Abuse of Drugs) Act 1986, repealed the Ordinance but expressly preserved the regulations made under it in 1942, 1953 and 1954 — colonial paperwork that kept working under an independence-era statute.[@1]

## The 1986 scale

The replacement law punished hard.[@1] Trafficking, including possession of more than 15 grammes, drew a fine of $400,000 or three times the street value, whichever was higher, plus five years to life in prison.[@1] Possession of less than 15 grammes drew $75,000 to $200,000 or two to seven years.[@1] Cultivation drew $100,000 to $200,000 and three to fourteen years.[@1]

## Tickets instead of cells

The commission the government appointed to rethink all this reported in January 2019, and the cabinet accepted its unanimous recommendations the next month: medicinal cannabis legalised, recreational use decriminalised for over-18s, and under 15 grammes or five plants punished only by a ticket.[@2] Before the bill could pass, the courts moved first: on 3 May 2019 High Court Judge Eddy Ventose ruled that Rastafarians and other adults could use, possess and cultivate cannabis in private, giving the government ninety days to change the laws.[@2] The Assembly complied that July, decriminalising up to 15 grammes with a $50 fine — $1,000 for public use.[@2] In February 2020 it raised the allowance to 56 grammes.[@2]

From the 1937 ordinance's total prohibition to tickets and grams: each step — the 1986 rewrite, the commission, the ruling, the amendments — peeled back a layer of a ban that had stood for eighty years, and the 1937 commencement remains the base layer under [the country's](/country/kn/) scores.[@1,2]
