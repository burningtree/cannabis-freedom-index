On 13 June 2022 Côte d'Ivoire replaced the drug law it had applied for thirty-four years. Law 2022-407 keeps cannabis illegal in every respect, but it cuts the penalty for a user from years to months and lets a court send the user to treatment.

## The law it replaced

Law 88-686 of 22 July 1988 treated the user much like a minor dealer. Possession or use carried one to five years in prison and a fine of 200,000 to 5 million CFA francs. A regional review published in 2017 recorded that the sentences were mandatory and that the law had no alternative to prison; a judge could add an order to undergo detoxification.

## What changed

| | 1988 law | 2022 law |
|---|---|---|
| Holding a drug for one's own use | 1 to 5 years, and a fine of 200,000 to 5 million francs | 1 to 3 months, and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 francs |
| Alternative to prison | none | a therapeutic injunction |
| Driving after using a drug | 1 to 5 years | 1 to 5 years |

The new law also exempts people taking part in a harm-reduction programme, on conditions to be set by decree. A paper presented by Ivorian practitioners presents the therapeutic injunction as the law's alternative to prison for users: a court can order the user into care in place of a sentence.

## What did not change

Growing cannabis without authorisation carries five to ten years in prison and a fine of 500,000 to 50 million francs under Article 3. There is no legal source of cannabis and no medical programme. How often courts use the injunction, as against the short prison term, is not recorded in the sources read.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Côte d'Ivoire](/country/ci/) from 9 to 11 with the [2022 law](/history/ivory-coast-drug-law-2022/). The gain is in enforcement, from 1 to 2: a user now faces months at most and can be diverted to treatment. Possession, cultivation, sharing and consumption stay at 1 because each remains a crime, and access stays at 0.
