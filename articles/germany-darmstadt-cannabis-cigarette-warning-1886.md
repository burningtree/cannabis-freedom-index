A police notice dated **14 December 1886** brought a warning about cannabis-labelled medicinal cigarettes before readers in Darmstadt. Published on the front page of the *Darmstädter Tagblatt* two days later, it reproduced statements from the local health council in **Karlsruhe**.

The document records an official response to the composition and advertising of a medicine. Its wording matters because a public warning and a prohibition have different legal consequences.

## Advertising and sampled cigarettes

The notice identifies **Grimault & Cie. of Paris** and its Cannabis-Indica cigarettes. It describes claims that the product treated asthma, lung and throat complaints, neuralgic pain and sleeplessness. These are the manufacturer's historical claims as reported by the authorities; the notice is not evidence that those treatments worked.

According to the reproduced warning, the firm's German-language advertising credited German doctors with identifying the remedy's medicinal value. It also discussed the difficulty of obtaining effective Indian hemp and presented Grimault's international business connections as a solution. The advertised ingredients were Indian hemp with potassium nitrate added to improve burning.

The health council reported a different result from examining cigarettes bought locally. It said they consisted almost entirely of belladonna leaves and contained only a very small proportion of Indian hemp. It characterized the belladonna material as ineffective through exposure to air. Those are the council's contemporary findings, not a modern laboratory reassessment.

## The official response

The warning called the promotional claims deceptive and disadvantageous to the public. It also warned that using the product could cause serious harm in many cases of respiratory disorder.

The item ends within a police notice signed by the **Grand Ducal Police Office in Darmstadt**, under **v. Grolman**. The signed date is **14 December 1886**; the newspaper issue is dated **16 December**. The event uses the police-notice date and identifies publication separately.

The original date of the Karlsruhe council's warning is not given. This record therefore documents its circulation through the Darmstadt police notice, rather than assigning the Karlsruhe statement an invented date.

## A warning without a stated prohibition

The reproduced text does not order a sales ban, prohibit importation, introduce a possession offence or specify a cannabis penalty. It demonstrates official scrutiny of a cannabis-labelled commercial medicine and its advertising. It cannot establish those additional legal restrictions.
