In 1940 Soviet drug law arrived in Lithuania on the Red Army's heels.[@2] A 1934 union-wide ban on sowing Indian hemp became the country's first recorded cannabis prohibition — not through any Lithuanian debate, but through annexation itself.[@1,2] Exactly which administrative act extended the ban to Lithuanian fields is unconfirmed in the sources read; the mechanism was the incorporation.[@2]

## The 1934 ban

The decree of 27 October 1934, signed by Kalinin, Molotov and Yenukidze, forbade sowing opium poppy and Indian hemp anywhere in the Soviet Union.[@1] The only exception was crops grown purely for the medical and scientific needs of the USSR, with yearly sowing quotas fixed no later than 15 August and permits issued by republic land organs.[@1] Unauthorised sowing drew criminal liability with mandatory confiscation of the crop, and each union republic had to write matching offences into its criminal code.[@1] The ban took effect in 1935, replacing a 1927 accounting measure for poppy.[@1]

## Fifteen June

The groundwork was laid in October 1939, when mutual-assistance treaties let Moscow station 20,000 soldiers in Lithuania — 30,000 in Latvia and 25,000 in Estonia — for the duration of the European war.[@2] Lithuania met the hemp ban when Moscow turned those bases into outright control.[@2] On 15 June 1940 the government, coerced by a Soviet ultimatum, agreed to admit unlimited Soviet troops; President Smetona proposed armed resistance but was overruled, and Moscow's envoy Dekanozov took charge as the Red Army occupied the state.[@2] Latvia and Estonia received their ultimata the next day.[@2] New popular-front governments staged rigged elections for new assemblies, each of which resolved by acclamation to join the Soviet Union.[@2] Moscow accepted: Lithuania was incorporated on 3 August 1940, Latvia on 5 August, Estonia on 6 August.[@2]

## What the annexation imported

With incorporation, the 1934 hemp ban became the law Lithuanian growers lived under, alongside the rest of the Soviet criminal code the decree had seeded into republic law.[@1,2] Estonia and Latvia inherited the same starting point on their own August dates, and the three [Baltic entries](/history/estonia-soviet-law-1940/) record the shared imposition.[@2] The Soviet ban stayed Lithuania's recorded first prohibition through the occupations that followed, and it is the base layer under [Lithuania's](/country/lt/) modern scores.[@1]
