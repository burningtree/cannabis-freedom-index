At midnight on 1 September 2026, Irish drug policing gained a third option. Alongside prosecution
and the adult caution, Gardaí can now send an eligible person to the health service instead. The
Health Referral Scheme is a pilot built by the Departments of Health and of Justice, Home Affairs
and Migration with the HSE and the Gardaí — and it is aimed squarely at the bottom of the market:
adults caught with a small quantity of a controlled drug for personal use.[@1]

## Who qualifies, and what happens

The bar is deliberately narrow. The person must be over 18, it must be a first drug offence —
any previous drug-use conviction or adult caution disqualifies — and at the time they must admit
possession for personal use and agree to take part.[@1] The Garda hands over details for booking
an appointment with a HSE SAOR practitioner — Support, Ask and Assess, Offer Assistance,
Refer — and the person engages in a rehabilitation and treatment programme.[@1] Refuse the
referral and the prosecution goes ahead as before.[@1]

## Still illegal, said the Commissioner

Commissioner Justin Kelly framed it as harm reduction, not tolerance: "Drug use remains illegal
and is a serious danger to the health of all users," he said, welcoming "a mechanism to steer
those taking drugs towards support services, rather than into the criminal justice
system."[@1] Only offences from 1 September qualify, and every referral follows internal Garda
policy rather than an automatic right.[@1]

## Years in the making

The scheme descends from a long official argument about what to do with personal possession short
of the courts: a government working group on alternative approaches to possession for personal
use,[@2] adult cautions from 2020, and a Citizens' Assembly that put possession for personal use
to a ballot.[@3] The pilot changes the response without touching the offence — possession stays
criminal, and from the second time on, prison is back on the table.[@1]
