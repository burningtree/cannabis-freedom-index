On 27 October 1934 the Soviet Union's central executive committee and council of people's commissars banned the sowing of Indian hemp and the opium poppy except for crops used for medical and scientific purposes. The decree took effect in 1935 and ordered the union republics to amend their criminal codes.[@1]

## The decree

The ban targeted drug-type hemp, not every fibre crop. Unauthorised growers faced criminal liability and confiscation of the crop, and the republics were told to write the offence into their codes. Ordinary fibre hemp remained outside the permit system; the decree named Indian hemp, the drug-type plant.[@1] Armenia was then part of the Transcaucasian SFSR, so the union decree applied through that structure rather than through a separate Armenian statute.[@1]

## Armenia's code

The Armenian SSR's own criminal code came later. Adopted on 26 December 1969, it punished drug production, trafficking and possession in Article 229, and the cultivation of prohibited opium poppy and drug-type hemp varieties in Article 230.[@2] The code was amended in 1975 to make possession without intent to sell a crime, and again in 1982 and 1987 to add repeat-cultivation and inducement offences.[@2]

The 1934 decree remained the template for later Soviet drug codes. Those provisions carried the Soviet ban into the republican legal order. They did not distinguish fibre hemp from drug hemp; the prohibited varieties were named in the article, and any other cannabis cultivation sat outside the licensed medical and scientific exception.[@2]

## The reversal

Armenia legalised industrial hemp in 2021. The current law allows licensed companies to grow varieties with CBD greater than THC and no more than 0.3% THC, for fibre, seed and industrial use. Licences require a minimum charter capital, nursery, greenhouse, warehouse and laboratory, and production must start within two years.[@3] Psychoactive cannabis remains prohibited, and the old distinction between drug-type and fibre hemp has become a licensing line rather than a criminal one.
