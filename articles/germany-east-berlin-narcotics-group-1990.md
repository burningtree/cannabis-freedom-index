In 1990, East Berlin's People's Police established a narcotics working group under
Major Günter Kowalschek. Historian Ned Richardson-Little describes it as the first
such GDR law-enforcement division, created as the opening of the Berlin Wall
changed access to drugs.

The development concerned East Berlin before reunification. It was not the
creation of a Germany-wide narcotics agency, nor the first time East German
customs or police had dealt with drug offences.

## Before the border opened

Cannabis had been difficult to obtain within the GDR. Richardson-Little emphasises
the restrictions on movement and the weak incentive for international sellers to
accept East German currency.

Nevertheless, authorities had intercepted hashish heading towards West Berlin.
The [1970 transit seizures](/history/germany-gdr-transit-hashish-seizures-1970/)
and [1973 narcotics law](/history/germany-gdr-narcotic-drugs-act-1973/) belonged to
that earlier encounter with a largely external market.

## A changing public discussion

After November 9, 1989, party newspapers initially continued to portray drugs as
an invading Western danger. Richardson-Little describes reports claiming dealers
were targeting East German schoolchildren and warnings that cannabis would lead
inevitably to heroin addiction.

Those claims are evidence of the contemporary alarm, rather than established
clinical conclusions. During the following months, coverage also began to address
practical questions about youth experimentation and public health.

The chapter records the interim government's admission, shortly before the March
1990 elections, that it had not been prepared for an emerging drug scene. It does
not establish a representative cannabis prevalence figure for that moment.

## A different account of supply

Kowalschek emphasised free movement and access to hard currency. In an interview
published in the *Berliner Zeitung* on April 4, 1990, he described West Germans
bringing small quantities and sharing hashish at restaurants and discos.

Richardson-Little contrasts that account with the earlier image of an organised
capitalist pusher directing every encounter. The interview describes one official's
assessment; it does not account for every supplier or prove that organised trade
was absent.

## What the working group marks

The unit represents a change in institutional attention as cannabis became more
accessible domestically. The event is dated to 1990, the precision supported by
the chapter, rather than assigning an unverified founding day.
