Opening the Berlin Wall in November 1989 changed the conditions that had kept
cannabis hard to obtain in East Germany. Ned Richardson-Little describes a shift
from warnings about a foreign threat to policing an emerging domestic issue.

In 1990, East Berlin's People's Police created a narcotics working group under Major
Günter Kowalschek. The chapter calls it the first such GDR law-enforcement division.
Kowalschek pointed to hard currency and free movement, and described West German
visitors bringing small quantities to share in restaurants and discos.

This adds the institutional response to the earlier
[1970 transit seizures](/history/germany-gdr-transit-hashish-seizures-1970/) and
[1973 law](/history/germany-gdr-narcotic-drugs-act-1973/). It concerns East Berlin
before reunification, not the creation of a Germany-wide enforcement body. No national
score changes are inferred from the chapter's account.
