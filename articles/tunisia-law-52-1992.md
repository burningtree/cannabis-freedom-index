Law 52 of 18 May 1992 made a year in prison the minimum for using or holding any illegal drug in any amount, cannabis included, and took from judges the power to give less.

## What the law says

| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| Use or possession for personal use | one to five years in prison and a fine of 1,000 to 3,000 dinars |
| A repeat offence | five years, mandatory |
| Mitigating circumstances | excluded: a judge may not go below the minimum |
| After release | up to ten years of "administrative surveillance", such as signing in with the police; no passport and no public-sector job |
| Exemption | only a first-time user who seeks treatment of their own accord before being discovered |

## What it did

Human Rights Watch studied the law's effects in 2015 and published its findings under the title "All This for a Joint". On 15 December 2015 Tunisia's prisons held 7,451 people for drug offences, 7,306 men and 145 women.

- About 70% of them, some 5,200 people, had been convicted of using or possessing cannabis, known locally as zatla.
- Drug offenders were 28% of the prison population; convictions for consumption alone were 20%.
- The prisons were officially 53% over capacity.
- Holding the cannabis users cost about 38 million dinars a year.

## How cases were made

The organisation interviewed 47 people, most between 17 and 30, in Tunis and Kasserine. All of them had been taken for a urine test after arrest, some more than once, and most said the police, not medical staff, had supervised it. Refusing was treated by the courts as a sign of guilt: two men were sentenced to a year after refusing a test when police had found only rolling papers on them, and were acquitted on appeal. Most of those interviewed described beatings. Of twenty case files reviewed, two recorded a judicial warrant for the search of a home.

## The attempts to change it

The government approved a draft replacement on 30 December 2015 that would have ended prison for a first offence. It was not put to a vote. In [April 2017](/history/tunisia-law-52-amended-2017/) parliament passed a narrower amendment restoring judges' discretion.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Tunisia](/country/tn/) from 3 to 1 with [Law 52](/history/tunisia-law-52-1992/). Enforcement falls from 1 to 0: prison became the only lawful outcome of a possession case. Possession and cultivation were already at 0 under the earlier ban.
