Mongolia adopted a replacement Criminal Code on 3 December 2015. Its commencement provision, subsequently amended in August 2016, set entry into force for 1 July 2017. Adoption and operation therefore belong to different dates.

The code is a central source for Mongolia’s cannabis penalties, but the consolidated version also contains later changes. Reading every current clause as a rule newly enacted in December 2015 would erase that amendment history.

## Possession without a selling purpose

Article 20.7 distinguishes conduct without a purpose of selling from conduct intended for sale. The unofficial English translation records a range of one to five years of travel restriction or imprisonment for the former category.

A travel restriction is a criminal sentence, even though it allows a court an alternative to prison. The provision does not establish an exemption for a small personal quantity. Nor does the availability of that sentence reveal how frequently courts choose it.

The current Mongolian text includes preparation, storage, giving to another person and acquisition without a selling purpose. Acquisition was expressly added by the [2020 amendment](/history/mongolia-drug-amendment-2020/); it should not be projected backwards without qualification.

## Sale and a place to consume

Article 20.7(2) provides two to eight years in prison for listed activities undertaken for sale. Aggravated circumstances have separate ranges, and the treatment of organised criminal groups was later changed.

Giving cannabis away is not made lawful by the absence of payment: the non-sale provision itself includes giving to someone else. That distinction makes the word “distribution” an imperfect shorthand for every case involving two people.

Article 20.8 separately addresses providing premises for drug use. Its ordinary alternatives include a fine, travel restriction or imprisonment. Possession and maintaining a place for consumption therefore cannot be reduced to one undifferentiated penalty.

## Cultivation is a separate offence

Article 20.10 covers unauthorised growing of plants supplying narcotic raw materials. It provides one to five years of travel restriction or imprisonment in the ordinary case, with a higher range for organised-group activity.

The existence of wild cannabis does not create a home-growing allowance. Authorisation and the activity performed remain relevant under the [narcotics control framework](/history/mongolia-narcotics-law-2002/).

## Which text describes the present?

The official legal-information service hosts both Mongolian legislation and an unofficial English translation. The English drug provisions omit changes recorded in the Mongolian text in 2020.

For the [current country profile](/country/mn/), the Mongolian consolidation is consequently the stronger source. The 2015 adoption belongs in the timeline, while commencement and later amendments must remain visible when explaining what the code now does.
