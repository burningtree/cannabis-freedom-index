Switzerland's proposed regulated cannabis market survived a divided public
consultation in May 2026. A National Council committee chose to revise the draft,
not abandon it or wait for every pilot study to finish.

## A project still alive

The National Council's health committee voted 16 to 8, with one abstention, to
continue work on the project implementing parliamentary initiative 20.473. The
initiative's stated aim is to regulate the cannabis market for better protection
of young people and consumers.

The vote followed the [2025 consultation](/history/switzerland-consultation-result-2025/)
on a preliminary Cannabis Products Act. Responses did not produce a clear
consensus.

## Who supported it

A majority of cantons rejected the draft in its current form. A minority of
cantons and the conference of cantonal social-services directors were more
favourable.

Representatives of cities, addiction and prevention organisations, and the hemp
sector were mostly supportive. Justice and police organisations generally took a
neutral position.

Despite those divisions, participants agreed that public health and youth
protection should be central to any new cannabis rules. The consultation also
produced demands for stronger safeguards for minors.

## The disputed points

Cantons considered implementation too complicated and asked for more funding.
Online sales also drew criticism. The committee instructed its subcommittee to
study the most contested issues and adjust the preliminary draft during 2026.

The committee said it welcomed the large number of consultation responses on the
proposed redirection of cannabis policy.

A separate proposal would have suspended the work until the results of the pilot
trials had been analysed in full. The committee rejected that option by 17 votes
to 8.

The decision did not adopt the [2025 draft](/history/switzerland-cannabis-products-act-draft-2025/)
as law. It determined the next stage of drafting: disputed provisions go back to
the subcommittee, while the larger project continues.

## What remains unchanged

Switzerland already permits limited pilot sales in participating cities and has
relaxed some possession rules. Those experiments are not a nationwide commercial
market, and the May committee decision did not broaden them.

## In the index

The vote kept reform moving but changed no person's legal rights. [Switzerland's](/country/ch/)
score therefore does not move. A future enacted market law could change several
segments; a decision to keep drafting cannot.
