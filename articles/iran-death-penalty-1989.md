On 25 October 1988 Iran approved a drug law that made the death penalty routine for cannabis trafficking. More than five kilograms of hashish, hemp or opium brought execution and the confiscation of the offender's property. The law, amended many times since, still provides the framework for Iran's drug offences.

## The five-kilogram line

The 1988 Law for Combating Illicit Drugs set out a schedule of capital offences by drug and weight. Five kilograms was the line for hashish and the other natural drugs. A repeat offender whose separate offences added up to more than five kilograms could also be executed, even if no single seizure reached the threshold. More than thirty grams of heroin, morphine or methadone carried the same penalty, as did armed smuggling. Helping another person become an addict brought death on a third conviction, or on a second if the addict was a family member, a student or a member of the security forces.

Article 9 allowed the authorities to carry out an execution in the offender's home town when the offence amounted to "corruption on earth". The Library of Congress described the law in a 1995 survey of capital drug offences; its account is one of the few detailed English-language records of the original text.

## It was not the first capital drug law

The 1988 law is sometimes described as the Islamic Republic's first drug legislation. It was not. A bill passed on 9 June 1980, the "Legal Bill to Intensify Punishments for Perpetrators of Drug Crimes and Preventive and Medical Measures", had already introduced death for a wide range of offences. These included possessing or carrying as little as five grams, growing, producing, selling, attempting to sell and providing premises for drug use.

The 1988 law replaced that approach with a more detailed schedule. It separated drugs by type and weight and attached the death penalty to the largest quantities. That structure, rather than the mere existence of a capital drug offence, is what later amendments built on.

## Cannabis at the end of the war

The law was approved while the war with Iraq was ending and Afghanistan's civil war was opening the eastern border to traffickers. The Library of Congress noted that Iran's 1,700-kilometre frontier with Afghanistan and Pakistan remained open to armed groups. Poppy cultivation had been largely suppressed in the 1980s; cannabis proved harder to eliminate.

In 1989 the Revolutionary Guard reported seizing 38 tonnes of drugs and arresting 19,000 users, smugglers and sellers. The number of people executed was not published.

## What survived

Amendments in 1997 and 2010 added offences and lowered some thresholds. In 2017 parliament raised the capital threshold for natural drugs such as cannabis from five to fifty kilograms and required an aggravating factor before a death sentence. Yet drug executions rose to 795 in 2025, 48.5% of all executions recorded that year. The five-kilogram line has moved, but the law built around it in 1988 remains in force.
