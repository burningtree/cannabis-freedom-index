Venezuela's new drug law took effect on 15 September 2010, retaining compulsory rehabilitation for consumer cases while increasing trafficking and larger-scale growing sentences. It continued a separate offence of possession outside personal use. That criminal offence included a twenty-gram cannabis quantity band.[@1,2]

## Personal use required an individual assessment

The consumer procedure covered people using drugs and those holding an individually assessed dose for themselves. Judges considered tolerance, dependence, consumption pattern, personal characteristics and forensic evidence.[@1]

Court-ordered rehabilitation remained compulsory. The law allowed treatment with or without residence in a specialised centre and provided additional measures of social reintegration, follow-up and community service.[@1]

That continued the treatment approach found in the [2005 law](/history/venezuela-possession-penalties-2005/), whose consumer article also used an individual assessment. A person's classification and purpose of possession remained central to which procedure applied.[@1,2]

## Twenty grams also appeared in a criminal offence

Possession for purposes outside lawful activities and personal use carried one to two years. The offence included quantities up to twenty grams of marijuana. It also set a five-gram ceiling for the category the statute called genetically modified marijuana.[@1]

Judges could use expert evidence to assess a personal dose. The law also rejected holdings justified as advance provision when they exceeded what could theoretically be a personal dose.[@1]

A small quantity and personal consumption were therefore separate questions within the statutory scheme. The consumer procedure and criminal possession offence had to be read together.[@1]

## Supply sentences rose sharply

The general trafficking range became fifteen to twenty-five years. Up to five kilograms of marijuana brought twelve to eighteen years. For cannabis above the possession ceiling and up to five hundred grams, the trafficking range was eight to twelve years.[@1]

The [2005 law](/history/venezuela-possession-penalties-2005/) had used lower supply bands: an ordinary eight-to-ten-year range, six to eight years for quantities up to one kilogram of marijuana, and four to six years for certain smaller distributors or people transporting drugs inside their bodies.[@2]

## Small growing cases still carried six years or more

The general offence covering plants, seeds and resin carried twelve to eighteen years. No more than ten plants or three hundred grams of seed or resin had a lower six-to-ten-year range. Directing or financing the operations carried twenty-five to thirty years.[@1]

Those distinctions continue to shape the [Venezuela profile](/country/ve/). Later [court decisions](/history/venezuela-drug-suspension-bar-struck-2017/) addressed the availability of suspended sentences within the drug-law system.[@3]
