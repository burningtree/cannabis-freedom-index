Thailand's open cannabis market lasted three years and sixteen days. It ended, as it
had begun, with a minister's signature and no vote in parliament.

## What was being undone

On [9 June 2022](/history/thailand-decriminalisation-2022/) the cannabis flower was
taken off the narcotics list. Anyone could grow it after registering on an app, shops
opened by the thousand, and a law to regulate them never passed. By 2025 more than
11,200 dispensaries had been approved.

A first restriction came in
[February 2024](/history/thailand-thirty-gram-presumption-2024/), when holding more
than 30 grams was declared intent to distribute.

## The order

On 25 June 2025 the public health minister, Somsak Thepsuthin, reclassified the flower
as a controlled herb.

- Buying or possessing it requires a prescription.
- Sales are limited to licensed medical premises.
- Advertising and recreational sales are prohibited.

Thousands of dispensaries closed rather than convert.

## Why a notice and not a law

Both the opening and the closing were done by ministerial instrument because parliament
never agreed on a cannabis act. That made the policy quick to change in either
direction, and it left the industry that grew up after 2022 with no legal protection
when the direction changed.

In [September 2026](/history/thailand-cabinet-bill-2026/) the cabinet approved a bill
to put the prescription rule into statute, with plantation registration and existing
shop licences expiring through 2028.

## The politics

The party that led decriminalisation, Bhumjaithai, now supports the controls. Its
leader, Anutin Charnvirakul, became prime minister in September 2025 promising to
balance public health and the economy.
