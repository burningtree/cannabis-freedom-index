On 29 August 1926 France banned growing hemp across French Equatorial Africa and ordered its use as a narcotic repressed, in a decree published in the Paris Journal officiel.[@1] For Middle Congo, today's Republic of the Congo, it was the first cannabis ban on record.[@2,1]

## The governor moved first

The colony's own governor had been keener than the statutes for years. Back in 1907 the governor of French Congo ordered his administration to stamp out the still fully legal plant, calling diamba selling criminal profiteering from a public poisoning that grew worse every day.[@1]

The laws that followed barely bit. French colonial drug laws of 1916 and 1918 left magistrates unsure whether the haschisch named in the legal text was the same thing as the local diamba, so prosecutions stalled on a word.[@1] A 1918 decree closed a related loophole by making clear the controls applied even to colonised subjects.[@1]

## The 1926 decree

The decree's title said what it did: prohibit hemp cultivation and repress its employment as a narcotic in French Equatorial Africa.[@1] It covered the whole federation — Middle Congo with Chad, Ubangi-Shari and Gabon — and reached the Journal officiel as a report and decree pair, the form colonial legislation took from Paris, at page 10017.[@1,2]

The wording finally matched the governor's ambitions. Where the 1916 and 1918 texts had tangled over haschisch, the 1926 decree named the crop in the ground and the use to be repressed.[@1]

## What came next

The decree stayed Congo's recorded starting point: the index still traces the country's cannabis prohibition to August 1926, with no earlier ban on record.[@2] The 1916 poisonous-substances law it superseded lingered in official citations into 2024, when an Owando cannabis-arrests report still invoked it.[@2] The same decree anchors the histories of Chad, the Central African Republic and Gabon, which inherited it from the same federation.[@2] It remains the base layer under [Congo's](/country/cg/) modern cannabis scores, now set by the 2025 drug law.
