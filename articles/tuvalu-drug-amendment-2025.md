Tuvalu's 2025 amendment changed both the penalties for cannabis offences and the framework for
licensed medical or scientific activities. Act 9 of 2025 added a schedule with minimum prison
terms for smoking, possession, cultivation and supply. The government's legislation catalogue
records commencement on 3 July 2025; the Act itself provides for commencement on publication.

## Prison terms for small amounts

The first row for smoking or possession of Indian hemp covers quantities not exceeding five
grams. It gives a minimum of twenty-four months and a maximum of five years. The next band,
not exceeding ten grams, gives three to seven years. For quantities exceeding fifty grams, the
printed range is five to twenty years.

Selling or trafficking has a similar ladder, with an additional band for quantities not
exceeding twenty grams. The amendment also changes section 8's wording to refer expressly to
trafficking.

For cultivation, the smallest listed category, not exceeding one plant, carries twelve months
to five years. Up to three plants carries two to seven years; exceeding five plants carries
four to ten years.

## Gaps in the printed table

The schedule does not provide a tidy set of consecutive bands. Its small-quantity descriptions
overlap, and it omits intermediate quantities and plant counts. A reader cannot safely fill
those gaps by extending a nearby row. The table is reported as printed; no court interpretation
resolving those drafting issues was established in this review.

The statutory minimums also do not establish how often people have been prosecuted or imprisoned
since the amendment. Post-amendment sentencing practice remains a separate research gap.

## A licensing power alongside prohibition

The replacement section 5 prohibits importing, exporting, manufacturing, cultivating, selling
or using dangerous drugs without a licence under new section 14A. That section permits the
minister to license those activities for medical and scientific purposes, with conditions on
record keeping, security and reporting.

This introduces a statutory licensing route. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that cannabis
licences have been issued, that medicines are available to patients or that recreational supply
is permitted. The framework should be read alongside the older [1948 Dangerous Drugs
Act](/history/ellice-islands-dangerous-drugs-1948/), whose cannabis restrictions the amendment
reworks.
