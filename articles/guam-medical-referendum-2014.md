Guam voters approved medical cannabis on 4 November 2014, with 56.48% voting yes. The proposal created protections for patients with qualifying illnesses and directed health officials to establish a regulated local supply. Patients’ ability to obtain cannabis would depend on the registration system, business rules and producers that followed.

## A majority chose a medical exception

The official election summary records 19,692 votes in favour and 15,175 against. The legislature had sent the proposal to voters under the name of Joaquin “KC” Concepcion II.

Its stated purpose was to alleviate symptoms caused by debilitating conditions and their treatments. The list included cancer, glaucoma, multiple sclerosis, epilepsy, HIV or AIDS, post-traumatic stress disorder and rheumatoid arthritis or similar chronic inflammatory disorders. Health officials could approve additional conditions.

The vote gave patients a route out of ordinary cannabis penalties when they met the new law’s requirements.

## Certification and registration determined protection

A qualifying patient needed a practitioner’s written certification and a registry identification card. The practitioner had to believe that the potential health benefits were likely to outweigh the risks. Certification lasted no longer than one year.

Registered patients and their caregivers were protected from arrest, prosecution and penalties for an adequate supply. The health department was to define that amount as no more than reasonably necessary for an uninterrupted three-month supply, sourced within Guam.

The law also allowed a patient with certification but no registry card to raise a defence at trial. Children needed additional explanations of risks and benefits, and written consent from a parent or guardian who controlled their dosage.

## Legal protection still needed a supply system

The health department was instructed to develop rules for licensed producers, secured growing facilities and designated distribution locations. These details would determine how a patient could actually receive cannabis.

Medical permission also carried boundaries. It did not excuse driving under the influence or possession and use in listed places such as schools, workplaces, public vehicles and parks.

## Patients later gained a way to grow their own

Four years later, governor Eddie Calvo described the struggle to get cannabis to patients when he signed the [2018 home-cultivation law](/history/guam-medical-home-cultivation-2018/). That law offered qualifying patients or designated caregivers an annual permit while no medical dispensary was operating.

It allowed six flowering plants and twelve juvenile plants under indoor security requirements. [December amendments](/history/guam-medical-grower-protections-2018/) expressly extended legal protections to compliant growers. The [Guam profile](/country/gu/) therefore follows both the medical exception voters approved and the later route patients received to produce their own supply.
