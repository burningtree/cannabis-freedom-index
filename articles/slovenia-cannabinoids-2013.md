Slovenia opened a prescription route for registered cannabinoid medicines through a drug-classification change approved on 19 June 2014.[@1] The decree was published the following day and took effect fifteen days later, on 5 July.[@2]

## Patients had pressed for access

The Human Rights Ombudsman’s report for 2013 describes a person with a chronic illness who said cannabis helped and wanted to use it without fearing the police.[@3] The Ombudsman asked the Health Ministry to explain what it was doing, while recognising that medical effectiveness needed professional assessment.[@3]

The ministry reported that the Drugs Commission had discussed cannabis in June 2013 and sought opinions from six medical boards.[@3] By its December response, only the psychiatry board had replied, opposing medical use and pointing to psychiatric risks.[@3] The report therefore records a debate still taking place during 2013.[@3]

## A citizen bill preceded the decree

Civil society submitted a [cannabis bill in December 2013](/history/slovenia-citizen-cannabis-bill-2013/) seeking medical permission.[@1] Parliament rejected the bill, and the government used the classification decree to open access to registered cannabinoid medicines instead.[@1]

NIJZ explained that doctors could prescribe these medicines for conditions where effectiveness was demonstrated, while cannabis itself remained controlled.[@1] Its guidance directed patients toward medical advice and warned against abandoning prescribed treatment because of claims that cannabis could treat every disease.[@1]

## The change used medicine regulation

The decree divided controlled substances into three groups according to health risks and use, with the schedules forming part of the instrument.[@2] It replaced the earlier classification decree and its amendments, including the 2013 amendment.[@2] The prescription change thus operated through the existing medicine and controlled-drug framework.[@1,2]

For patients, the immediate distinction was between a registered cannabinoid medicine prescribed by a doctor and cannabis obtained outside that route.[@1] The decision did not authorise growing a personal medical crop.[@1]

## Pharmacy preparations followed

NIJZ’s later report on medicine consumption describes registered medicines, specially imported medicines and compounded preparations as access routes.[@4] Its incomplete pharmacy returns for 2024 recorded 1,666 prescriptions and 2,091 compounded cannabinoid preparations.[@4] The same report described flower and standardised extracts as permitted but not yet routinely used.[@4]

The [2025 medical and scientific cannabis law](/history/slovenia-medical-law-2025/) later provided a separate domestic production framework, followed in the [Slovenia profile](/country/si/).[@5]
