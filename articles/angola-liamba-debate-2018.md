In August 2018 Voice of America asked around Luanda and found the same answer everywhere:
legalising liamba was not on any agenda — not in politics, not in parliament.[@1] The
debate existed only in society.

Supporters made the common-sense case: it made no sense to ban cannabis while beer was
freely sold.[@1] Against them stood a clinical psychologist warning of harm to thinking
and behaviour, and a jurist who said any change would need a full parliamentary debate
and an amendment of Law 3/99 — the 1999 drug law that punishes personal use with prison
and unauthorised cultivation as trafficking.[@1]

Nothing moved. No bill was tabled, no debate scheduled, and the law reads as it did.
Angola keeps the arrangement Livingstone found a century and a half earlier: prohibition on
paper, diamba in the market — now joined by police operations that destroy two-hectare
fields in Lunda-Norte and dismantle 52-tonne farms in Humpata.[@2,3]
