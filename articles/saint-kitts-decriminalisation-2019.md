In 2019 a judge told the government of Saint Kitts and Nevis that it had ninety days to change its
cannabis law. On 31 July, within the deadline, the National Assembly did: possessing a small amount
of cannabis became a matter for a ticket and a fine, not a criminal court.

## The commission and the court

The government had already been moving. On 20 February 2019 the prime minister, Timothy Harris,
announced that his cabinet had accepted the unanimous recommendations of a National Cannabis
Commission: to legalise medicinal cannabis and to decriminalise recreational use by adults over 18,
so that anyone found with under 15 grams, or growing five plants or fewer, would only get a ticket.

The court went further and faster. On 3 May 2019 a High Court judge, Eddy Ventose, ruled that
Rastafari and other adults over 18 could use, possess and grow cannabis in private, and gave the
government ninety days to bring the law into line.

## The amendment

The bill amending the Drugs (Prevention and Abatement of the Misuse and Abuse of Drugs) Act passed
its third reading on 31 July. Harris, who moved it, said it would "rehabilitate the lives of hundreds
of our young people by expunging their records" for growing and possessing cannabis. The amendments
also set out to establish a cannabis industry and to regulate use for medicinal, scientific,
religious and recreational purposes.

| | After July 2019 |
|---|---|
| Up to 15 g | A ticket and a $50 fine |
| Smoking in public | A fine of $1,000 |
| Past convictions of young people | To be expunged |
| Supply to others | Still an offence |

## What followed

In February 2020 parliament [raised the limit to 56 grams](/history/saint-kitts-56-grams-2020/)
and passed a [Cannabis Act](/history/saint-kitts-cannabis-bill-2020/) for medical and religious use,
whose licensing parts were [brought into force in 2023](/history/saint-kitts-medical-act-activated-2023/).
The same year a Rastafari rights law and a [Freedom of Conscience (Cannabis) Act](/history/saint-kitts-freedom-of-conscience-2023/)
recognised sacramental use and let adults apply for licences to grow at home.

Possession for private use by adults in
[Saint Kitts and Nevis](/country/kn/) is now treated as a minor infraction; selling cannabis outside
the licensed system remains a crime.
