Cyprus’s colonial government enacted a consolidated drug law on 6 August 1956, retaining criminal controls on cannabis prepared for smoking and a licensing system for the raw plant material and resin.[@1] A person could face prosecution for possessing or using prepared cannabis, and for allowing premises to serve as a place to smoke it.[@1] The law’s commencement was left to a later gazette notice.[@1]

## Smoking reached beyond possession

The law defined Indian hemp as dried flowering or fruiting tops of the female cannabis plant with the resin still present.[@1] It separately identified cannabis prepared for smoking, including the residue left after smoking.[@1] Selling that preparation, holding it, smoking it or frequenting premises used for smoking all appeared among the offences.[@1]

Occupiers who permitted preparation, sale or smoking on their premises were also exposed, as were people managing such premises.[@1] The prohibition extended to possessing pipes or utensils used for cannabis smoking or preparation.[@1]

## Trade required official permission

Imports and exports of raw cannabis and resin required a licence from the Director of Medical Services and had to pass through an approved port.[@1] The government could regulate cultivation, possession, sale and distribution, inspect the relevant premises and provide for seizure or destruction of unlawfully handled plants.[@1]

The general punishment allowed up to three years in prison, a fine of up to fifty pounds, or both, together with forfeiture.[@1] Courts could order forfeited material destroyed.[@1] The statute also authorised search warrants where sworn information gave reasonable grounds to suspect unlawful drugs or relevant transaction records on premises.[@1]

## Later northern law kept broad growing controls

The [1972 northern drug law](/history/northern-cyprus-drug-law-1972/) retained controls on cannabis, resin, imports and premises used for smoking or trading.[@2] Its growing offence covers any Cannabis sativa plant, with a [1977 rule](/history/northern-cyprus-young-plants-rule-1977/) stating that prosecutors need not prove flowering or fruiting tops.[@2]

Eligible possession and use defendants later gained a [supervised-treatment option in 2016](/history/northern-cyprus-drug-treatment-option-2016/), with successful completion avoiding a sentence and a conviction entry.[@3] Growing and supply convictions fall outside that route.[@3] The [Northern Cyprus profile](/country/xn/) explains those present distinctions, following a history in which cannabis users, growers and suppliers remained subject to different parts of the same drug-control system.[@2,3]
