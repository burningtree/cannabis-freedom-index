New Zealand's Misuse of Drugs Act 1975 replaced the Narcotics Act 1965 and is
still the statute that governs cannabis in the country. It created three classes
of controlled drug and put the cannabis plant, fruit and seed in Class C, the
lowest class. Cannabis preparations such as hashish, oil and THC went into Class
B, where supply carries a higher maximum.[@1,2]

## The possession maximum

Possession or use of either form carries up to three months in prison or a
NZ$500 fine.[@1] The important difference is custody. For a Class C plant or seed
offence, the court may not impose imprisonment unless there are previous
convictions or exceptional circumstances. That presumption does not apply to
Class B preparations, even though the nominal possession maximum is the same.[@1]

The Act also sets a rebuttable presumption of supply at 28 grams of cannabis
plant, 5 grams of a cannabis preparation, 250 milligrams of THC or 100 cannabis
cigarettes. Holding more than those amounts does not automatically prove
dealing, but it shifts the burden to the defendant.[@1]

## Supply and cultivation

Selling or supplying Class C cannabis carries up to eight years, and doing so to
a person under 18 carries the same maximum. Supplying Class B cannabis carries up
to fourteen years. Growing any prohibited plant, cannabis included, carries up to
seven years.[@1,3]

Those figures made the 1975 Act a prohibition statute with a relatively light
possession tier and a much heavier supply and cultivation tier. The same
structure is visible in the case law and in the sentencing bands that grew up
around cultivation.[@2]

## What survived

The Act has been amended many times, but its class system and cannabis offences
remain. The 2019 amendment told police to consider a health-based response before
prosecuting possession, and the 2020 referendum on a regulated market failed
50.7% to 48.4%. Neither changed the 1975 Act's definitions or maximums.[@1,2]

For a reader, the 1975 Act is the hinge between New Zealand's earlier narcotics
controls and the present debate. It made cannabis a criminal offence in a clear
class, set a low possession maximum and a high supply maximum, and left the
country with the structure it still argues over.[@2]
