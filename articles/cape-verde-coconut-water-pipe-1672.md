The earliest description of a coconut-based water pipe comes from the Cape Verde
islands in 1672, recorded in John Fryer's account of travels begun that year
and published in 1698.[@1,2]

The islands sat on Portugal's main shipping route into the Indian Ocean, where
traders were active above all in East Africa — Mozambique, Madagascar and the
Comoros — and India, the likely source of the pipe form.[@1] Portuguese sailors
had met coconut-based pipes in the Indian Ocean by the mid-1600s, and the form
reached the Portuguese Atlantic soon after.[@1]

The pipe outlasted the route: the calabash water-pipe tradition carries the
form across the region to this day.[@1]
