Türkiye replaced its drug-related death penalties with life imprisonment on 29 November 1990.[@1] The amendment covered aggravated drug offences that included cannabis, ending capital punishment under those clauses while retaining severe prison sentences.[@1,2] It formed part of a wider revision of the criminal code.[@1]

## Cannabis had carried life or death since 1953

The [1953 amendment](/history/turkey-capital-drug-penalties-1953/) placed cannabis alongside heroin, cocaine and morphine in a special trafficking tier.[@2] Unauthorised manufacture, import or export carried life imprisonment; specified aggravating circumstances, including organised offending and using minors, could require death.[@2]

The law also converted the life sentence to death where specified medical professionals committed the offence, or where other listed circumstances applied.[@2] Personal drug use followed a separate rule carrying three to five years in prison and a fine.[@2]

## Parliament substituted life imprisonment

The 1990 amendment replaced the death-penalty wording in the drug-trafficking clauses and the drug-related fatal-outcome clause with life imprisonment.[@1] It separately rewrote the professional and other aggravating rules so that a life sentence would be served with an initial period of isolation instead of being converted to death.[@1]

Parliament adopted the amendment on 21 November.[@1] Its commencement clause made it operative on publication, eight days later.[@1] Cannabis remained within the drug-control system, and the personal-use prison term was dealt with by a later amendment.[@1,2,4]

## Prisoners still needed relief from outstanding death sentences

Amnesty International's contemporary account reports an execution moratorium since 1984, alongside continuing death sentences and cases awaiting parliamentary approval.[@3] It also describes the April 1991 Anti-Terror Law's relief for crimes committed before 8 April: prisoners sentenced to death for drug smuggling were among those whose sentences were commuted to twenty years.[@3]

The Prime Minister's office withdrew the files of prisoners awaiting parliamentary approval of their death sentences.[@3] Amnesty reported 276 such prisoners across the offences affected by the new law.[@3]

## Personal-use sentences fell the following June

The [June 1991 reform](/history/turkey-personal-use-sentence-reduction-1991/) reduced drug use and possession for use to one to two years.[@4] It also protected a person from prosecution for use if they approached the authorities for treatment before an investigation began, while retaining hospital confinement for people considered dependent.[@4] These reforms separated the removal of capital punishment from changes to the everyday penalties facing users in [Türkiye](/country/tr/).[@1,4]
