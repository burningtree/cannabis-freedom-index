Tuvalu’s cannabis legislation began under British colonial rule. The Dangerous
Drugs Ordinance commenced on 20 October 1948, when the islands formed part of the Gilbert and
Ellice Islands colony.

## What “Indian hemp” included

The ordinance calls cannabis “Indian hemp” and defines it as either *Cannabis sativa* or
*Cannabis indica*, including any portion of the plant. Its second part also covers cannabis resin
and preparations based on that resin.

Import and export are prohibited. A separate section bars importing or exporting cannabis seeds,
alongside opium-poppy and coca seeds. Unlawfully imported or exported material is seized and
forfeited.

## The original prohibition

The cultivation rule is short and absolute: no person may cultivate Indian hemp in Tuvalu. The
offence applies whether cultivation is for private use or otherwise.

The law also makes possession and sale offences. Its wording reaches a person who has “given or
sold” cannabis to somebody else, leaving no exception for a non-commercial gift. Police may enter
suspected premises with a warrant, while a person carrying or conveying cannabis may be arrested
without one. Plants may be seized and destroyed.

For offences without a separate penalty, conviction in the High Court can bring a $2,000 fine
and ten years in prison. Summary conviction can bring up to $1,000 and five years. The property
involved is forfeited, and an attempt or incitement attracts the same punishment as the completed
offence. When a company is convicted, its managers can also be guilty unless they establish that
the act occurred without their knowledge or consent.

## From colony to country

The 2022 consolidated version is titled under the Laws of Tuvalu and says it is enacted by
Tuvalu's Parliament, but it retains the 1948 commencement date and the enactment's amendment
history. It preserves the territory’s colonial legal inheritance. A [2025 amendment](/history/tuvalu-drug-amendment-2025/) subsequently added minimum prison terms and a medical/scientific licensing power; the earlier provisions described here should be read in that historical context.

The corresponding law remains in [Kiribati](/history/gilbert-islands-dangerous-drugs-1948/), the
former Gilbert Islands. The two countries now publish separate consolidated texts of the same
original ordinance.

For [Tuvalu](/country/tv/), this is the first documented restriction in the index and changes all
six segments from the earlier unregulated state.
