France has run a medical cannabis "experiment" since 2021 and extended it again and
again without making it permanent. In July 2026 the missing piece went to the
country's highest administrative court.

## Five years of trial

France banned cannabis as a medicine in [1953](/history/france-medical-ban-1953/)
and made private use a crime in [1970](/history/france-use-criminalised-1970/). A
[trial](/history/france-medical-experiment-2021/) for up to 3,000 patients began in
March 2021.

It was meant to lead to a permanent system. Instead the trial was extended several
times. About 700 patients remain in it, covered until 31 December 2026.

## The decree

On 2 July 2026 the government sent the Conseil d'État the decree that governs how
cannabis medicines will be assessed and paid for. The finance ministry had already
approved the text.

The decree does not set prices or rates itself. It sets the procedure: how
manufacturers submit their dossiers, what evidence counts, and how the health
authority's Transparency Commission deliberates. That body cannot begin its
evaluations until the decree is published. It has had the manufacturers' dossiers
since early 2026.

## What reimbursement would look like

A draft presented to the industry ties coverage to the assessed benefit.

| Assessed benefit | Reimbursed |
|---|---|
| Major | 65% |
| Moderate | 30% |
| Minor | 10% |
| Insufficient | 0% |

Most eligible patients have a recognised long-term condition and could be covered
in full. There would be one price per category of product, fixed for three years.
The final decree may differ.

## Who would qualify

The five indications are those of the 2021 trial, and each requires that other
treatments have failed:

- neuropathic pain;
- epilepsy, in adults and children;
- painful spasticity in multiple sclerosis and similar conditions;
- symptoms linked to cancer;
- advanced palliative care.

## The timetable

The government aims to publish on 1 October 2026. If it does, the health authority
could give its opinion before the end of the year, price negotiations would follow,
and the first prescriptions under a permanent regime would be possible in 2027.

That leaves a gap. The trial's patients are covered to the end of 2026; the new
system starts, at best, some time after.

## In the index

A decree in review changes nothing yet, so [France's](/country/fr/) score does not
move. For everyone outside the trial the law is what it has been since
[2020](/history/france-fixed-fine-2020/): possession is a crime, usually settled
with a 200-euro fine.
