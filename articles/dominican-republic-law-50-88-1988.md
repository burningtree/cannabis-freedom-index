The Dominican Republic's central drug law does not ask whether a small amount of cannabis was
intended for personal use. It assigns a legal category by weight and attaches prison to every
category.

## Replacing an older framework

Law No. 50-88 was promulgated on 30 May 1988. Its preamble says the previous Law No. 168 had not
clearly classified drug offences or the penalties for sponsors, traffickers, intermediaries,
sellers and users. The new act constructed an extensive national system of definitions,
schedules, prohibitions and punishments.

“Marijuana” includes every part of a *Cannabis sativa* plant, growing or not, its seeds, extracted
resin and preparations. Mature stalks, fibre, and oil or cake made from seeds are excluded.

## Weight decides the label

Article 6 creates three cannabis categories. Up to 20 grams is simple possession. More than 20
grams but less than one pound makes the accused a distributor; more than one pound makes the
accused a trafficker. Hashish has still lower thresholds: five grams and one-quarter pound.

A 1995 amendment recorded in the consolidated text closes off a defence based on quantity: when
the purpose is sale or distribution, any amount can make the accused a distributor or seller.

The categories control punishment. Simple possession carries six months to two years in prison
and a fine. Distribution, selling or acting as an intermediary carries three to ten years.
Trafficking carries five to twenty years and a much larger fine.

## Cultivation is trafficking

The law prohibits sowing, cultivating, producing and harvesting cannabis throughout the country.
Growing marijuana without permission—or possessing more than one pound of its seed—is classified
as trafficking, with five to twenty years in prison. Illicit crops and the land used to grow them
may be confiscated.

Two paragraphs of the same Article 64 grade the penalty by the number of plants. Up to twenty
plants brings two to five years in prison and a fine of 2,000 to 10,000 pesos; more than twenty
and up to a hundred brings three to ten years and 10,000 to 50,000 pesos. Only above that does
the full trafficker's sentence apply.

This remains the foundation of the strict present-day profile for the
[Dominican Republic](/country/do/). Medical cannabis and an adult legal market have not replaced
it.

In the index, the 1988 law is recorded as a tightening within an already prohibited system. It
does not introduce a new score step because the earlier restriction is still only estimated, but
it explains the weight-based enforcement regime behind today's scores.
