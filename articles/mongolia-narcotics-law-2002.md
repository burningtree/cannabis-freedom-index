Mongolia adopted its Law on Control of Circulation of Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances on 28 November 2002. It established a framework for authorised medicines and research while prohibiting circulation outside permitted purposes.

For cannabis history, the distinction matters: this is a control law covering many substances, rather than a cannabis-only ban. Its date also does not establish when Mongolia first prohibited cannabis.

## A definition tied to international lists

Article 3 defines narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances through the international conventions of 1961 and 1971. “Circulation” encompasses a chain of activities, including preparation, storage, transport, distribution, import and use.

The definition of manufacture extends to collecting, planting and harvesting naturally occurring plants with narcotic effects. A plant growing without deliberate cultivation therefore does not automatically sit outside the control framework. The legal question concerns the substance and the activity involving it.

## Authorised use and prohibition

In the [amended text available today](https://legalinfo.mn/en/edtl/16758230735421), Article 4 recognises medical, academic and research purposes, specified laboratory work, and patients using narcotic medicines prescribed by doctors. Article 8 prohibits activities outside the authorised purposes.

These provisions cannot establish a functioning medical-cannabis programme by themselves. They concern controlled substances generally; a cannabis-specific product, prescribing arrangement or supply route needs separate documentation.

The published consolidation records later amendments, including changes to institutional responsibilities. Its present licensing arrangements should not all be described as the exact wording parliament adopted in 2002.

## Prevention and treatment

The law also reaches beyond policing. Article 11 addresses prevention and education, including school curricula and public information. Article 12 provides for voluntary treatment and court-ordered compulsory treatment in specified circumstances.

This places treatment within the same framework as prohibition. It does not make treatment an automatic substitute for prosecution, nor establish that every person using cannabis is dependent or requires compulsory care.

## The control law and the Criminal Code

The law identifies authorised purposes and prohibited circulation. The separate [Criminal Code](/history/mongolia-criminal-code-2015/) supplies the offences and sentencing ranges relevant to unauthorised possession, supply and cultivation.

Those functions should be kept distinct when reading the [Mongolia profile](/country/mn/). A prohibition on recreational consumption in the control law is not evidence that consumption alone carries the possession sentence. The criminal provision lists particular acts; the administrative treatment of use requires its own source. The 2002 framework remains important because it explains why medicinal authorisation and recreational prohibition can coexist within the same national system.
