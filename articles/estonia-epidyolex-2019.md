Epidyolex received a marketing authorisation valid throughout the European Union on 19 September 2019, bringing an authorised cannabidiol medicine into Estonia’s treatment options.[@1] The approval covered a prescription product for specified epilepsies, with Lennox–Gastaut and Dravet treatment given alongside clobazam in patients aged two and over.[@1] Estonia’s medicines agency identifies it as the authorised medicine with CBD as its main active ingredient.[@3]

## A measured medicine for epilepsy

The medicine is an oral solution containing 100 milligrams of cannabidiol per millilitre, taken using a supplied measuring syringe.[@2] Treatment must start and remain under a doctor experienced in epilepsy, with the amount adjusted to the patient’s response and tolerability.[@2]

The European Medicines Agency found that adding it to other epilepsy treatment reduced seizures in the studied patient groups.[@2] Its assessment also identified liver risks requiring restrictions and monitoring, alongside common effects including sleepiness, reduced appetite and diarrhoea.[@2] The authorisation therefore came with prescribing information and precautions for clinicians and patients, rather than an unrestricted recommendation to consume CBD.[@2]

## Other products followed an individual route

Estonia’s regulator describes a separate route for cannabinoid medicines without a marketing authorisation and for standardised cannabis prepared for medical use.[@3] A doctor can request these for an individual patient under the arrangements for unauthorised medicines, and a prescription remains necessary.[@3]

Standardised medicinal cannabis has a known cannabinoid content, which the agency distinguishes from ordinary cannabis smoked for intoxication.[@3] That distinction allows a measured medicinal product to be assessed and supplied through a medical process without opening recreational retail.[@3,4]

## Patients could not grow their supply

The agency explicitly states that cultivation to produce a narcotic substance, including for treatment, is prohibited in Estonia.[@3] The agricultural exception concerns EU-listed hemp varieties with no more than 0.3% THC; it does not create a patient home-growing entitlement.[@3]

The [1997 narcotics framework](/history/estonia-narcotics-framework-1997/) continues to govern controlled handling, with exceptions for purposes including medicine and science.[@4] Use without a prescription and small-quantity unlawful possession remain misdemeanours under that law.[@4] The [Estonia profile](/country/ee/) consequently describes limited medical access alongside the separate personal-use rules; the [2026 threshold proposal](/history/estonia-thirty-dose-proposal-2026/) concerns criminal quantities rather than the conditions of a medicine’s authorisation.[@1,4,5]
