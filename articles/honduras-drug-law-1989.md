On 13 December 1989 Honduras replaced its drug law. Decree 126-89, adopted 5 September and
published 23 November, took effect twenty days after publication — and governed cannabis
for the next thirty years.[@1]

## Growing: nine to twelve

The law prohibited cannabis cultivation outright and punished illicit growing with nine
to twelve years in prison plus a fine.[@1] Producing, planting, cultivating and gathering
plants or seeds with narcotic ingredients were all covered.[@1]

## Users: thirty days inside

Minimum quantities for immediate personal consumption, assessed medically, brought
security measures instead of prison under article 26: up to thirty days' internment and a
fine for a first case, escalating measures for repetition, and rehabilitation confinement
for dependent users.[@1] Above that minimum, possession fell under trafficking.[@1]

## A rule without a building

The internment rule had a catch the text could not fix: no state rehabilitation centre
existed, so the thirty-day rule was never applied.[@2] A 2012 draft replacement kept the
same structure — first offence thirty days, second thirty to ninety, third internment
until reintegration — and likewise left the judge to decide what counts as personal
use.[@2] In 2020 the criminal code repealed articles 16 to 30 outright, ending the
internment era without declaring personal possession lawful.[@3]
