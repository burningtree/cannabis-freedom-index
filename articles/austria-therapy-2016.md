Austria did not decriminalise cannabis in 2016. Section 27 of the Narcotic Substances Act still makes acquiring or possessing a drug a criminal offence. What an amendment in force from 1 January 2016 (Federal Law Gazette I No. 144/2015) changed is the first step after the police find someone with a small amount: the case goes to a doctor's office before it goes to a prosecutor's desk.

## The offence that stayed

| Section 27 | Conduct | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | acquiring, possessing, producing, importing or supplying a narcotic; growing cannabis to obtain one | one year in prison, or a fine of 360 daily rates |
| (2) | the same, "solely for personal use" | six months in prison, or a fine of 360 daily rates |
| (2a) | offering or supplying for payment in public | two years |

## The new route

Section 13 now tells authorities what to do when the only suspicion is an offence under section 27(1) or (2) committed "solely for one's own personal use".

- An authority that learns of it reports to the district administration in its role as health authority, "in place of a criminal complaint".
- The criminal police report to the health authority and send the prosecutor a short transfer report.
- The health authority has the person assessed by a doctor experienced in drug problems. If a health measure is needed, such as medical monitoring, treatment, counselling or psychotherapy, it works to have the person accept one.

On receiving the transfer report the prosecutor withdraws from the prosecution at once, unless something needs clearing up (section 35(9)). The case comes back to life only if, within a year, the health authority reports that the person refused the assessment or the measure, or if the person asks for it (section 38(1a)).

## What the Act already did

The idea was not new. Section 35 had already obliged the prosecutor to step back provisionally, for a probation period of one to two years, from personal-use cases. For cannabis the prosecutor does not even need a medical opinion first, unless the person has had proceedings in the previous five years. The 2016 change moved the starting point: the health authority, not the criminal file, comes first.

## Limits

The route is open only where the conduct was for personal use alone. Supplying, dealing in public and anything above the threshold quantity stay with the criminal courts. And the cooperation is not optional: refusing the doctor reopens the criminal case.

## Why the index moved

Enforcement rises from 3 to 4, because a person found with cannabis for their own use is now referred to the health authority and, if they cooperate, never faces a charge. Possession stays at 2 and consumption at 2: the conduct is still a crime with a prison term in the statute, and the outcome depends on the person complying. The total moves from 20 to 22.
