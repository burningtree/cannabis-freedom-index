The Misuse of Drugs Act was signed on 16 May 1977 and is still the basis of Irish drug law. It wrote a separate, lighter scale for cannabis into the statute itself: a person caught with cannabis for their own use could not be sent to prison until the third time.

## The cannabis scale

Section 3 makes possessing any controlled drug an offence. Section 27 then sets the penalty, and its first paragraph applies only "where the relevant controlled drug is cannabis or cannabis resin and the court is satisfied that the person was in possession of such drug for his personal use".

| Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|
| First | a fine of up to £50 |
| Second | a fine of up to £100 |
| Third or later | a fine of up to £250, or up to twelve months in prison, or both |

For any other drug, or for cannabis not held for personal use, possession carried up to twelve months on summary conviction and up to seven years on indictment.

## Growing and supplying

The lighter scale stops at possession.

| Section | Offence | Maximum on indictment |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Possession for sale or supply | 14 years and a fine of £3,000 |
| 17 | Growing opium poppy or any plant of the genus Cannabis without a licence | 14 years and a fine of £3,000 |

## Treatment

Section 28 obliges a court, before sentencing for most drug offences, to remand the person and ask for a medical report, with recommendations on treatment, before deciding what to do. The first two cannabis possession offences are excluded from that procedure, since they can only be fined.

## Definitions

The Act defines cannabis as the flowering or fruiting tops of any plant of the genus from which the resin has not been extracted, and cannabis resin as the separated resin, crude or purified.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Ireland](/country/ie/) from 9 to 11 with the [1977 Act](/history/ireland-misuse-of-drugs-1977/). Only enforcement changed, from 1 to 2, because the law itself now ruled out prison for a first or second possession offence. Possession stays at 1: it remained a crime, with a conviction and a record.
