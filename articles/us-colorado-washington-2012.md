On 6 November 2012 two American states did what no jurisdiction in the world had
done: they made it legal to grow and sell cannabis for pleasure, and wrote the rules
for a market.

## Forty years of failed votes

Legalisation had been on American ballots since
[1972](/history/us-california-legalisation-vote-1972/), when California rejected it
two to one. It lost in Oregon in 1986, in Alaska in 2004, in Nevada and Colorado in
2006 and in [California in 2010](/history/us-california-proposition-19-2010/), by
53.5 per cent to 46.5.

What had changed by 2012 was medical cannabis. Both Colorado and Washington already
had regulated medical industries, and their voters were used to seeing cannabis sold
legally.

## Two measures

| | Colorado, Amendment 64 | Washington, Initiative 502 |
|---|---|---|
| Result | 55.3% in favour | 55.7% in favour |
| Possession, age 21+ | One ounce | One ounce |
| Home growing | Six plants | Not allowed |
| Tax | 15% excise plus a retail tax | 25% excise |
| Driving | As for alcohol | 5 ng of THC per ml of blood |

Colorado's measure was an amendment to the state constitution, and let towns opt
out. Washington's was a statute whose campaign was led by a lawyer on loan from the
American Civil Liberties Union.

Possession became legal within weeks, on 9 December in Washington and 10 December in
Colorado. Colorado's governor, John Hickenlooper, said: "This will be a complicated process, but we intend to follow through."

## Washington's silence

Both laws contradicted the federal
[Controlled Substances Act](/history/us-controlled-substances-act-1970/), and the
president of the UN's drug control board said they violated the 1961 treaty.
President Obama said the matter was not a high priority. In
[August 2013](/history/us-cole-memo-2013/) the Justice Department announced that it
would not sue the two states.

The [first shops](/history/us-colorado-first-shops-2014/) opened in Colorado on
1 January 2014. Sales reached almost a billion dollars in 2015. Even so, by 2017
nearly two thirds of Colorado's municipalities had banned retail cannabis.

## What followed

Four more states voted yes in [2016](/history/us-legalisation-wave-2016/), including
California. And the federal government's decision not to act left it unable to
object when [Uruguay](/history/uruguay-legalisation-2013/) legalised a year after
the two states.

The index scores the [United States](/country/us/) as one country, so two states
moved it only a little: possession from 2 to 3, growing, sharing and consumption
from 1 to 2, and access from 2 to 3.
