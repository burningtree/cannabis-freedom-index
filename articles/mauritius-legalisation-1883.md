In 1883 the British colony of Mauritius repealed a ban on cannabis that had stood for
forty-three years. The official who argued for it made his case with a handful of
ganja, bought in a quarter of an hour.

## The ban

Mauritius had [prohibited cannabis](/history/mauritius-ban-1840/) in 1840, under
Ordinance No. 2, because of fears that it was used in robberies. In
[1848](/history/mauritius-possession-tightened-1848/) the law was extended to restrict
possession of "gandia" and other poisons.

## The demonstration

By the 1880s the colony's procureur general, Etienne Pellereau, considered the law dead.
A vote to repeal it failed in 1882. He called the prohibition "manifestly inoperative",
and set out to show it.

He sent a messenger into Port Louis, the capital. Within fifteen minutes the man was
back with a handful of the drug, which was passed round the members of the committee.

Ordinance No. 25 of 1883 followed shortly. It legalised the import, sale and possession
of cannabis.

## The backlash

Within a few years clerks at the Colonial Office in London were speculating that rising
admissions to the island's asylum were due to drink and ganja. In
[1893](/history/mauritius-reprohibition-petition-1893/) the colony's lunacy board
petitioned for imports to be banned again.

The people closest to the question disagreed. The asylum's superintendent reported that
Indian admissions had fallen since repeal. In
[1894](/history/mauritius-officials-find-no-harm-1894/) the chief medical officer wrote
that in nearly thirty years he had never met a case of lunacy caused by it. London still
minuted that Mauritius should "prohibit cannabis absolutely".

## Why it matters

Repeals of cannabis bans are rare before the twenty-first century, and this is among
the earliest on record. The response is familiar too: a campaign to restore the ban run from
far away on statistics the local doctors did not believe.

In the index the 1883 ordinance raised [Mauritius's](/country/mu/) scores. Cannabis is illegal there today.
