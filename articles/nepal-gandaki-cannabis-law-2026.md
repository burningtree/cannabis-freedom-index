Nepal's parliament has promised a cannabis law for years and not passed one. In
August 2026 a province stopped waiting, and wrote its own.

## Fifty years of prohibition

Until [1973](/history/nepal-shops-closed-1973/) cannabis was sold in licensed shops
in Kathmandu. Under pressure from the United States and the United Nations, Nepal
cancelled the licences, and the
[Narcotic Drugs (Control) Act of 1976](/history/nepal-narcotics-act-1976/) made
growing, selling and using it crimes.

Attempts to undo that have all stalled: a motion by 48 lawmakers and a
[private bill](/history/nepal-tamang-bill-2020/) in 2020, and a
[budget promise](/history/nepal-budget-medical-cannabis-2024/) in 2024.

## A bill sent back

Gandaki's provincial assembly passed its bill on 14 July 2026. The province chief,
Dilliraj Bhatta, returned it, questioning whether it conflicted with the 1976 Act
and exceeded the province's powers. It was the first time a province chief there
had sent a bill back. The assembly passed it again unchanged, and Bhatta signed
within three days, as the constitution requires.

The province's argument is that agriculture is a provincial subject. Its chief
attorney, Chiranjeevi Sharma Paudel, said the law was "introduced primarily to
benefit farmers".

## The rules

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Medicinal or industrial use only |
| THC | No more than 0.3% |
| Where | Only in zones the province designates, with barren land preferred |
| Plot | At least two ropanis, about 1,018 square metres |
| Buyer | A purchase commitment from a pharmaceutical or manufacturing company |
| Applicant | No drug conviction in the past ten years |
| Licence | Five years, after a one-year preliminary approval and a site inspection |

An inspector must be present at harvest, quantities are recorded in front of the
ward chair and police, and shipments travel sealed. Record-keeping failures bring
fines of 50,000 to 500,000 rupees. Growing without a licence or selling outside the
system is prosecuted under national narcotics law.

## What it does not do

Nobody can grow yet. The gazetted Act, section 1(2), starts on a further notice, and a
review dated 13 October 2026 had not found one. Licences also wait on regulations, zone
designations and a testing laboratory, none of which exist. And the federal ban is untouched: growing, holding
and using cannabis remain crimes under the 1976 Act everywhere in Nepal, including
Gandaki. In August the finance minister told the upper house that more study was
needed before the national rules changed.

The 0.3 per cent cap is also low: it is the level other countries use to define
hemp.

For those reasons [Nepal's](/country/np/) score does not move.
