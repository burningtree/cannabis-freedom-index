Algeria's February 1975 drug ordinance combined prison terms for users with a broad capital-punishment clause.[@1] Illicit use carried two months to one year in prison, a 500–5,000-dinar fine, or either punishment alone.[@1] Another clause allowed death sentences when an offence under the ordinance threatened the Algerian people's moral health.[@1]

## Users could be ordered into treatment

The use offence covered substances and plants classified as narcotics.[@1] Courts investigating or trying a person for that offence could require detoxification in a specialised medical establishment.[@1] The statute thus placed treatment within a criminal process that still allowed imprisonment.[@1]

Confiscation followed the drug offences.[@1] Courts had to order confiscation of the seized drugs or plants and could confiscate equipment used to make or transport them.[@1] Places used for drug consumption or manufacture could be closed.[@1]

## Supply brought longer sentences

Unlawful manufacture, preparation, import, export, storage, sale and transport of narcotics could bring ten to twenty years in prison.[@1] Attempt and participation in an agreement to commit these offences were also covered.[@1] Facilitation of another person's drug use and certain false-prescription offences had their own penalties.[@1]

The capital clause referred to offences throughout the ordinance, including the use offence, subject to its moral-health condition.[@1,2] Its wording therefore reached beyond the particular trafficking and facilitation offences later selected by the replacement health law.[@1,2]

## The ordinance reached back to earlier acts

Promulgated on 17 February and published on 21 February 1975, the ordinance said it applied to acts committed after 1 October 1974 that had not received a final judgment by publication.[@1] This made the treatment of pending cases part of the law's reach.[@1]

For people already before the courts, the relevant boundary was therefore the status of their case as well as the date of the act.[@1] The ordinance expressly tied its retrospective application to cases without a final judgment.[@1]

## A narrower clause follows in 1985

The [1985 health law](/history/algeria-health-law-drug-framework-1985/) repealed the ordinance.[@2] It kept the same basic prison range for illicit use but located its capital clause in specified trafficking and facilitation offences.[@2] It also provided non-prosecution for people who followed the prescribed treatment conditions.[@2]

That replacement changed the legal position of users in [Algeria](/country/dz/), while retaining severe penalties for people who supplied drugs.[@1,2]
