On 1 January 1938 Fiji's Dangerous Drugs Ordinance took effect, and growing,
holding or selling Indian hemp became a crime punishable by up to ten years
in prison.[@1] The colony had been circling the plant for half a century; the
ordinance finally criminalised the supply chain end to end.[@1,2]

## Ganja arrives with the indentured workers

Cannabis probably reached Fiji with the Indian indentured labourers first
shipped to the colony in 1879.[@2] The administration noticed quickly: an
1886 Gunjah Prohibition Ordinance, the colony's eleventh law that year,
already existed when a 1905 review of colonial legislation footnoted
it.[@2] Its text has not survived in any collection traced so far.[@2]

What survives is the next step, the
[import ban of 1905](/history/fiji-import-ban-1907/). Ordinance No. 21
prohibited bringing in Indian hemp "or any product or preparation
therefrom, including gunjah, bhang, chavas", plus anything the chief medical
officer judged a substitute for them.[@2] Growing and smoking the local crop,
however, stayed outside the law for another three decades.[@1,2]

## What the 1938 ordinance did

The ordinance defined Indian hemp as either cannabis plant, sativa or
indica, or any part of them.[@1] Its first part then closed every door in
turn: section 4 banned importing or exporting the drug, section 5 banned its
seeds, with anything smuggled seized outright by customs, and section 7
banned cultivating the opium poppy, Indian hemp or coca leaf anywhere in the
colony.[@1] Section 8 made it an offence to grow any of the three plants
"whether for private use or otherwise", and an offence to possess, sell, give
or to have sold them.[@1] Police could enter suspect premises on a warrant
and seize the crop with its baskets and jars, and could arrest without a
warrant anyone seen carrying it; plantations found anywhere could be seized
and destroyed.[@1]

The tariff was steep for a colony. The general penalty clause set up to ten
years in prison or a £1,000 fine in the Supreme Court, and up to twelve
months or £250 before a magistrate, with the drugs forfeited on every
conviction.[@1]

## What came next

The ordinance stayed the backbone of Fijian cannabis law for generations: a
1993 report still described only an "incipient" cannabis problem, as young
Fijians picked the drug up from foreign travellers.[@2] Industrial hemp was
finally separated from the drug in July 2022, when parliament legalised
licensed hemp at up to 1% THC.[@2] The
[Fiji profile](/country/fj/) follows the later enforcement and reform story.
