On 30 April 1928 Spain put Indian hemp, its resin and its extracts into a state import monopoly, and fined people caught holding or using them outside that system.[@1] The decree said the point was to stop these drugs being taken without a justified doctor's prescription, and to meet Spain's treaty duties.[@1]

## After the pharmacy list

[Hashish had been on the pharmacy poison list since 1860](/history/spain-hashish-controlled-1860/). A dealer then needed a signed note saying how much was being sold and what it was for. The 1928 decree went further. From that day a public service in the interior ministry had the only right to import and distribute Indian hemp, its resin and its extracts, together with opium, morphine, heroin, coca, cocaine and medicinal ether.[@1]

The service would sell only to pharmacists established in Spain, to pharmaceutical laboratories, and to teaching or research laboratories that could show they needed the drug.[@1] It could add as much as 20 percent to the purchase price. That margin paid for the service, for research into medicines that did not cause a habit, and for propaganda against abuse.[@1]

## Fines by the gram

The same fine applied to people who produced, imported or traded the restricted drugs and to people responsible only for illicit holding or use. The first time it was 50 to 100 pesetas for each gram seized. If it happened again, the fine could rise to 500 pesetas a gram, and a shop used for the offence could be closed for a time.[@1] A doctor who helped someone get the drugs for an illegal purpose faced 1,000 to 20,000 pesetas the first time, and could be suspended from practice the third time.[@1]

Fines could be paid in instalments. If they still could not be paid, the smuggling law supplied the punishment, and that could mean a loss of liberty.[@1] The decree does not set a number of days or months.

Pharmacists were not yet tied to a special official prescription form on 30 April. That form was to be required once the regulations of the decree were published. A prescription would then be valid only in the province whose medical college had issued the pad, and the doctor had to write the patient's name and address on the stub.[@1]

## Spain, the colonies and North Africa

The service covered the Spanish state, the colonies and the possessions in North Africa.[@1] In 1967 [Spain adapted its law to the Single Convention](/history/spain-narcotics-law-1967/) and limited cannabis to medical and scientific use.
