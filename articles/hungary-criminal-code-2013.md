On 1 July 2013 Hungary's new Criminal Code made consumption itself a crime again, punishable with up to two years in prison.[@2] It also narrowed the escape hatch — diversion into treatment — to offenders who had stayed clean for two years.[@1]

## A decade of widening and narrowing

Hungary had let small-quantity offenders enter treatment instead of prosecution since 1993.[@1] The 1999 tightening restricted that route; a new government reopened it to all users in 2003, requiring six months of treatment or counselling.[@1] In 2004 the Constitutional Court ordered several diversion exemptions annulled — including joint-consumption cases — while retaining the core exemption for small-quantity possession and cultivation with six consecutive months of treatment.[@3]

## The 2013 reversal

The second Orbán government's Code kept diversion but fenced it: only offenders who had not reoffended within two years could be offered treatment instead of punishment.[@1] The change hit regular and dependent users hardest, since relapse — considered a natural part of recovery by addiction science — now triggered criminal sanctions for failing treatment.[@1] Before the change, diversion was the norm rather than the exception: in 2012, 67 percent of cases went to treatment and 3,458 people were diverted from criminal prosecution.[@1]

## The direction since

Hungary never reversed course. Diversion stayed available only for first-time small-quantity offenders who admit the act and identify the seller, a route closed after two uses.[@2] In 2025 the government went further, writing zero tolerance of drugs into the constitution and launching a 'war on drugs' package that forces users into an informer role.[@4] The 2013 Code's two-year rule still defines [Hungary's](/country/hu/) cannabis law: use is a crime, and treatment is a one-repeat-customer offer.
