Rwanda offered community service as an alternative to one to two years in prison for drug use in its 2018 criminal-law rewrite. The same law sharply increased production and supply penalties, with life imprisonment specified for the highest drug category. It took effect on 27 September 2018.

## Users received a different sentencing choice

The [2012 code](/history/rwanda-penal-code-2012/) had specified one to three years and a fine for illicit use. The replacement shortened the prison range and expressly offered community service, meaning work ordered by a court as punishment.

Its drug-use clause covered consuming, injecting, inhaling and other forms of use. The community-service alternative appeared in the original 2018 text. The rewrite therefore changed the options facing someone convicted of using drugs, alongside its much harsher approach to supply.

## Production and giving drugs to someone else faced heavier penalties

Unlawful production, transformation, transport, storage, giving drugs to another person and sale fell under a separate part of the offence. The highest category carried life imprisonment and a fine of more than 20 million and up to 30 million Rwandan francs.

The next category carried twenty to twenty-five years and a fine of 15 million to 20 million francs. The lowest carried seven to ten years and a fine starting at five million francs. These were separate categories with separate penalties.

Under the earlier code, making, transforming, importing or selling drugs unlawfully had carried three to five years and a fine, with doubled penalties for international acts.

## The health minister placed cannabis in the highest category

The criminal law left the drugs within each category to a health-ministry order. In [March 2019](/history/rwanda-cannabis-category-2019/), that order expressly listed cannabis with cocaine, heroin and opium as “very severe” drugs, the category associated with the life-imprisonment tariff.

This classification connected cannabis to the heavier production and supply rules. It also made the distinction between the drug-use clause and the supply offences especially consequential for someone facing prosecution.

## Possession was made explicit the following year

The [November 2019 amendment](/history/rwanda-possession-clause-2019/) expressly included being caught with drugs in the use-and-possession clause, retaining one to two years or community service. It also kept the separate production and supply tariffs.

Later [medical licensing rules](/history/rwanda-medical-2021/) allowed authorised cannabis activities under strict conditions. A [2023 amendment](/history/rwanda-sentencing-discretion-2023/) widened judges' power to reduce sentences. For [Rwanda](/country/rw/), the history involves different rules for users, suppliers and licensed medical businesses, each with its own consequences.
