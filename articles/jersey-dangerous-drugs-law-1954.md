On 1 January 1955 Jersey's first cannabis control took effect: importing or exporting Indian hemp, as cannabis was then called, needed a licence. The rule covered the dried flowering tops of the plant and its resin, and licensed loads could move only through St Helier port or the airport.[@1,2]

## An old law, replaced late

The island's first dangerous drugs law dated from 1923 and had been concerned with opium and cocaine. It was passed by the States on 1 October 1923 and confirmed in London on 19 December that year.[@3] By 1939 the Home Office was writing that Jersey's drug legislation, meaning that 1923 law, was "much out of date".[@3]

The replacement still took another fifteen years. The Dangerous Drugs (Jersey) Law was adopted by the States on 28 January 1954 and sanctioned on 13 April, but a separate commencement act held it back until New Year's Day 1955.[@1,2] Even then, a few repeals of the 1923 law waited until 1957.[@2]

## What the law did, and what it left open

Part I of the law listed raw opium, coca leaves, Indian hemp and resin preparations. Bringing any of them in, or taking them out, without a licence was an offence.[@1] Extracts and tinctures of Indian hemp sat in Part III instead, beside medicinal opium and morphine, with the same licence rule at the border.[@1]

For what happened inside the island, the law mostly handed over a power. The Public Health Committee could make orders controlling or restricting production, possession, sale and distribution, including a full ban outside licensed hands — but the law did not itself impose one.[@1] Anyone who broke the law as it stood faced a fine of up to £1,000, ten years in prison, or both.[@1]

## The orders followed

That order-making power is why Jersey's ban looks layered today. [Growing was banned in 1965](/history/jersey-cultivation-ban-1965/), and [possession and supply followed on 1 May 1966](/history/jersey-possession-ban-1966/), eleven years after the border rule. The 1954 law itself survived until the Misuse of Drugs (Jersey) Law 1978 [replaced it in 1980](/history/jersey-misuse-of-drugs-law-1980/). The [Jersey profile](/country/je/) shows where that long sequence has ended up.
