Egypt’s 1884 commercial convention with Greece explicitly treated hashish as an exception to ordinary trade protections. It also addressed the practical enforcement problem of searching premises and shipping connected with foreign subjects.

## Signature and operation were different dates

The convention was signed at Cairo on 3 March 1884. Article XVI specified that it would operate for seven years from 20 March, with continuation provisions thereafter. This entry uses the stated operative date.

The text was reproduced in the Victoria Government Gazette in September 1884, in French and English. A later United States official publication also printed an English translation when extending the customs arrangements to American commerce.

## Hashish outside the trade protections

The convention generally provided for most-favoured-nation customs treatment and regulated import duties. Article V, however, excluded named commodities from its stipulations: tobacco and tombac, salt, natron, hashish and saltpetre.

For these goods, Egypt reserved control, with the relevant regime applicable to Greek subjects on the same terms as local subjects. This exception should not be read as an eight-percent cannabis tariff simply because that rate appeared in the convention’s general tariff provisions.

Nor did the convention itself create a complete new domestic hashish prohibition. The [existing Egyptian restriction](/history/egypt-import-ban-1879/) and the legal ability to enforce controls against foreign commerce are separate parts of the history.

## Searches and consular notification

Article V authorized immediate searches in warehouses or dwellings considered necessary by the Egyptian government. A duplicate search order was to be sent to the Greek consular authority, which could attend. That formality was expressly prevented from delaying the search.

Other articles provided general customs powers. Article XI addressed manifests and suspicious vessel stops. Article XIII allowed designated Egyptian officers to board sailing or steam vessels of less than two hundred tons within ten kilometres of shore and ascertain cargo, seize prohibited goods and establish customs infractions. Those limits should be retained when describing the maritime power; it was not an unrestricted right to search every ship anywhere.

## An arrangement extended to other foreign commerce

A separate agreement signed the same day accepted application of the Egyptian customs rules to British subjects, vessels and commerce. Britain’s acceptance appears alongside the Greek convention in the gazette.

The [United States followed through a later agreement](/history/egypt-us-customs-hashish-agreement-1885/), operative in March 1885. Together these instruments show cannabis control developing through commercial diplomacy and enforcement jurisdiction. This entry is unscored context for [Egypt](/country/eg/); it does not assign a national Greek or British possession ban to the treaty.
