On 11 January 1932 the provisional government of Getúlio Vargas issued Decree 20.930, which classed cannabis as a "toxic narcotic substance". It was the first national prohibition in a country where local bans were a century old, and it took in the whole plant: even hemp fibre for cloth and rope fell under it.

## A plant that came with the enslaved

Cannabis is not native to Brazil. It almost certainly arrived with enslaved Africans brought to the sugar plantations of the north-east from the sixteenth century, and its names show the route: fumo de Angola, diamba, liamba, maconha, all from languages of present-day Angola and Congo.

Its use spread from runaway slave communities to fishermen, dockers and labourers. Smoking circles called assembléias gathered at the end of the day in the north-east until the 1940s, and the plant had a place in some Afro-Brazilian religious practice.

| 1846 | Caxias | similar directive |
| 1866 | São Luís | similar directive |
| 1870 | Santos | similar directive |
| 1876 | Campinas | similar directive |
| 1886 | São Luís | sale, display and smoking prohibited; four days in jail for slaves |

It is unclear whether these were enforced.

## The doctors' case

The national ban owed most to medicine. In December 1915 Rodrigues Dória, a psychiatrist, law professor and former state governor, told a scientific congress in Washington that cannabis smoking was a "pernicious and degenerative vice" and a kind of revenge of the defeated: of enslaved Africans against those who had enslaved them.

His paper was the reference for almost every Brazilian study for decades. Its school called cannabis the "opium of the poor", blamed it for madness, violence and racial degeneration, and prevailed over a rival current: at the first Afro-Brazilian congress in Recife in 1934, participants described cannabis as part of an Afro-Brazilian cultural tradition.

## From classification to prison

The 1932 decree classified. A decree-law of 25 November 1938, under the Estado Novo, banned sale and use definitively, and the Penal Code of 1940 set the penalty: one to five years and a fine.

## Why the index moved

The [decree](/history/brazil-federal-ban-1932/) is the first step in [Brazil's](/country/br/) score, from the unregulated state to a total of 11. Before it a person in most of the country broke no national law by growing or smoking cannabis; after it every use of the plant was prohibited. The older municipal directives are recorded as context, because they bound single towns and may not have been applied.
