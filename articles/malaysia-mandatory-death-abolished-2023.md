On 4 July 2023, Malaysia stopped requiring judges to impose death on every person convicted of drug trafficking. The [2023 reform](/history/malaysia-mandatory-death-abolished-2023/) let a court choose imprisonment for life instead, and anyone spared death must receive at least 12 strokes of the cane.[@1] For cannabis, the change reached the sentence and stopped there: possession, growing, consumption and the presumption that 200 g means trafficking all remained.[@2]

## A sentence with no room to argue

Malaysia made death the only sentence for trafficking in [1983](/history/malaysia-mandatory-death-1983/), and the law presumed anyone holding 200 g of cannabis to be a trafficker.[@2] Executions were paused in 2018, but courts continued to sentence people to death; by 2023, 1,337 people were under sentence of death.[@3] An [amendment that took effect in 2018](/history/malaysia-sentencing-discretion-2017/) had allowed life imprisonment in limited circumstances, but the 2023 Act removed those limits.[@1]

## What the Act changed

The Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act deleted the subsections that had confined the alternative sentence and cut the whipping minimum for trafficking from 15 strokes to 12.[@1] Section 39B now provides death or imprisonment for life, with at least 12 strokes where death is not imposed.[@2] The change also applied to offences committed before 4 July 2023, so people already convicted could be sentenced under the new choice.[@1] A companion law, in force on 12 September 2023, gave the Federal Court temporary power to review death sentences that had already exhausted ordinary appeals.[@3]

## Who came off death row

The Federal Court reviewed 936 cases under its temporary jurisdiction and finished on 29 October 2024. In total it commuted 900 death sentences, including 46 in ordinary review, and the Court of Appeal commuted another 116 in 2024.[@3] By 22 January 2025, 140 people remained on death row, including 40 convicted of drug offences.[@3] The reform did not end long prison terms: in January 2025 the High Court in Kuala Lumpur sentenced three men to life imprisonment and 18 strokes for trafficking 707.05 g of cannabis and cultivating plants, with the terms running from their 2021 arrest.[@4]

## The cannabis law stayed

Cannabis is still a dangerous drug with no lawful recreational supply. Possession of 20 g brings caning, 50 g can bring life, growing a single plant brings life and at least six strokes, and consumption carries up to two years or a RM5,000 fine.[@2] The 2023 Act changed who decides the sentence for trafficking, not whether trafficking is an offence. In October 2026 a policy brief launched in Parliament by the Anti-Death Penalty Asia Network, Monash University Malaysia and Eleos Justice called for the death penalty to be abolished for drug offences; it noted that 97 people remained on death row in November 2025, 21% of them convicted of drug offences.[@5] The debate has moved beyond the mandatory sentence. The cannabis provisions of [Malaysia's](/country/my/) drug law have not moved with it.
