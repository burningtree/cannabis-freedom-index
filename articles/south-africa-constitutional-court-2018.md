South Africa was one of the first countries to ban cannabis and the one that asked
the world to do the same. In 2018 its highest court ended the ban on private use in
a single unanimous judgment, with no limit in grams.

## A century of dagga law

Restrictions began in [Natal in 1870](/history/south-africa-natal-1870/), aimed at
Indian labourers. The Orange River Colony [banned supplying it](/history/orange-river-colony-dagga-ordinance-1903/) in 1903, and the Cape [listed it as a poison](/history/cape-colony-dagga-poison-listing-1905/) in 1905.
A [national ban](/history/south-africa-national-ban-1922/) followed in 1922, and the
next year South Africa [asked the League of Nations](/history/south-africa-league-request-1923/)
to control cannabis internationally.

Apartheid made the law harsher. The [1971 Act](/history/south-africa-mandatory-minimums-1971/)
set a minimum of two years in prison for a first possession offence, and the
government called it the toughest drug law in the Western world. The
[1992 law](/history/south-africa-drugs-act-1992/) removed the minimums but kept
possession a crime.

## Three applicants

The challenge was brought by three men. Gareth Prince, Jeremy Acton, who had founded the
[Dagga Party](/history/south-africa-dagga-party-2009/) in 2009, and Jonathan Rubin
argued that the ban on adults using cannabis at home violated the constitutional
right to privacy.

On [31 March 2017](/history/south-africa-western-cape-2017/) Judge Dennis Davis of
the Western Cape High Court agreed. A declaration of that kind needs confirming by
the Constitutional Court, which heard the case that November.

## The judgment

On 18 September 2018 the court confirmed it, unanimously, in a judgment written by
Raymond Zondo. It was unconstitutional, the court held, to make it a crime for an
adult to possess, use or grow cannabis "for personal consumption in private".

| | After the ruling |
|---|---|
| Use in private | Lawful for adults |
| Possession in private | Lawful, for personal use |
| Growing in a private place | Lawful, for personal consumption |
| Quantity | No limit set |
| Use in public, or in front of children | Still an offence |
| Buying and selling | Still an offence |

The change took effect at once, as interim relief, and Parliament was given 24
months to rewrite the law.

## The law that has not arrived

Parliament missed the deadline. A bill was filed in 2020 and signed on
[28 May 2024](/history/south-africa-private-purposes-act-2024/) as the Cannabis for
Private Purposes Act, but it cannot take effect until regulations set the limits.
A [draft](/history/south-africa-draft-regulations-2026/) published in February 2026
proposes 750 grams and five plants.

So the ruling itself is still the law, and its gap is still open: a South African
may grow and smoke cannabis but has nowhere lawful to buy it.
