Algeria's May 2023 amendment created a lower prison range for growing cannabis for personal consumption: two to five years and a 200,000–500,000-dinar fine.[@1,2] The original 2004 law had imposed life imprisonment for unlawful cannabis cultivation without this personal-use exception.[@1,2]

## The purpose of a crop matters

The amended cultivation article kept life imprisonment for the general offence of knowingly growing cannabis unlawfully.[@1] It then added the separate personal-consumption penalty.[@1] A grower's purpose therefore became important to the difference between the two cultivation penalties.[@1]

The provision used personal consumption as its test.[@1] Personal cultivation remained illegal at every quantity.[@1] Even within the lower range, personal growing remained a criminal offence carrying prison and a fine.[@1]

## The personal-possession fine rises

For illicit use, buying or possession for personal consumption, the prison range stayed at two months to two years.[@1,2] The fine became 20,000–50,000 dinars, with either the fine or imprisonment available alone.[@1,2] The original law's fine had started at 5,000 dinars.[@1,2]

The amendment therefore combined a less severe cultivation rule for personal growers with a higher minimum fine for users.[@1] It also retained ten to twenty years for the basic wider production and supply offence and added a twenty-to-thirty-year range for certain people whose official or professional role facilitated it.[@1]

## Treatment protections expand for minors

The amended law retained non-prosecution for people who underwent detoxification or medical supervision after the alleged use.[@1] It also required courts to exempt minors from the ordinary use penalties when a specialist assessment established that they had completed detoxification.[@1] Further medical supervision could be ordered for up to a year.[@1]

For people outside the treatment route, the ordinary use penalties remained.[@1] The law expressly addressed those who evaded an ordered course and cases where a specialist found that detoxification was not medically needed.[@1]

## Further penalties follow in 2025

The law was promulgated on 7 May and published on 9 May 2023.[@1,3] The next major amendment, in [July 2025](/history/algeria-law-25-03-2025/), restored death penalties for specified aggravated supply offences and expanded other sentencing rules.[@1,3]

The personal-growing exception remained in the cultivation article.[@1,3] For personal growers in [Algeria](/country/dz/), that separate paragraph sets a two-to-five-year prison range and a fine.[@1]
