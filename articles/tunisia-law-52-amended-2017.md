On 25 April 2017 Tunisia's parliament amended [Law 52](/history/tunisia-law-52-1992/), the drug law that had made a year in prison mandatory for any use or possession since 1992. The vote was 133 in favour among the 140 members present.

## What changed

Before the amendment a judge faced with a person caught with any quantity of an illegal drug had to impose at least a year in prison. The amendment lets the court use its discretion when sentencing a first drug offence, so that mitigating circumstances can again be taken into account.

## What did not

- Using a drug remains a crime under Law 52.
- For a second offence, a Human Rights Watch researcher noted at the time, judges are still obliged to apply the maximum sentence of five years.
- The wider reform approved by the government at the end of 2015, which would have replaced prison with a fine for a first offence, was not adopted.

The justice minister, Ghazi Jeribi, presented the amendment as temporary, to be followed by a "comprehensive" revision of the law. Nine years later that revision has still not been passed: in June 2026 a parliamentary committee was again hearing the interior ministry on a bill to amend Law 52.

## The scale of the problem it answered

At the end of 2015, 7,451 people were in Tunisian prisons for drug offences, about 70% of them for using or possessing cannabis. Human Rights Watch's Amna Guellali called the amendment an improvement but insufficient, and described the country's drug policy as "exclusively turned towards punishing consumers", with nothing for prevention, treatment or health.

The interior ministry's own figures suggest the caseload has not shrunk. It reported about 15,253 drug cases in 2025, involving about 27,338 people.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Tunisia](/country/tn/) from 1 to 3 with the [2017 amendment](/history/tunisia-law-52-amended-2017/). Enforcement rises from 0 to 1, because a first offender can now receive something less than a year in prison. Possession stays at 0: prison remains a realistic outcome, and certain for a second offence.
