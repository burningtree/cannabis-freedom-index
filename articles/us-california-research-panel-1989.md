Herer describes California's Research Advisory Panel as calling for cannabis
relegalisation. The report itself proposes a more specific experiment.

## Home growing with limits

The commentary in the annual report for 1989 recommends cultivation at a person's
residence. It would not permit supplying others or possessing more than one ounce
outside the home. The authors opposed creating another industry resembling alcohol
or tobacco and wanted changes evaluated over time.

## Whose recommendation?

A footnote says the commentary represents members acting as individual experts, not
the policies of the agencies appointing them. Minority reports matter too: M. Douglas
Anglin wanted more public debate before changing cultivation law; chairman Edward
P. O'Brien disputed the panel's authority to recommend legislative changes.

The report is therefore evidence of a reform proposal and disagreement within an
advisory body. It is not an enacted state permission to grow.

## Reporting year and publication

We use 1989 as the reporting year printed on the document, not as a verified release
date. Contemporary reporting in August 1990 says the attorney general refused to fund
printing of the recommendations.
