In 2013 Greece stopped treating every drug user as a criminal. Law 4139/2013, the "Law on Addictive Substances", cut the penalty for personal use to at most five months in prison and kept the conviction off the ordinary record.[@2] It was the first time the law distinguished the user from the dealer — a change debated in public since September 2011.[@1]

## The law it replaced

The drug law of 1987 had stated that all aspects of drug use were punishable with imprisonment, even simple possession of cannabis for personal use.[@3] By the early 2000s, use alone carried up to five years; by 2011 it was still a crime punishable by up to a year.[@1] Prisons filled with users sentenced for cases directly related to drugs, and the system offered them little treatment.[@1]

## The proposal: use is self-harm

In 2011 a legislative committee of the Ministry of Justice proposed a different logic, announced by Minister Miltiadis Papaioannou: drug use is an act of self-harm, like alcohol or tobacco dependence, and should not be a crime at all.[@1] Supply and possession of drugs and cultivation of cannabis plants for personal use would remain punishable, but only as misdemeanours, so the authorities could focus on serious trafficking.[@1] Trafficking itself would stay a felony with objective criteria separating lighter, heavier and very serious cases — up to life imprisonment for the worst offenders.[@1] The reform also promised a real right to treatment, replacing forensic procedures that worked only on paper.[@1]

## What the 2013 law did

The final law kept use as a misdemeanour rather than decriminalising it, but the direction held. Using drugs, or obtaining or growing cannabis for one's own needs, carries no more than five months; the offence stays off the record if no further offence follows within five years, and judges may suspend the penalty for offenders entering a recognised treatment programme.[@2] No fixed quantity defines personal use — the judge decides on the substance, its quantity and purity, and the offender's needs.[@2]

| Offence | Penalty under Law 4139/2013 |
|---|---|
| Personal use, possession or cultivation | Up to 5 months, usually suspended or replaced by treatment [@2] |
| Supply by an addicted user or within a group | Up to 3 years [@2] |
| Trafficking | At least 8 years; up to life in special cases; fines to EUR 500,000 [@2] |

Dependent dealers who complete treatment can be conditionally released after serving a fifth of the sentence.[@2]

The 2013 compromise shaped everything after it: [medical use became lawful in 2017](/history/greece-medical-use-2017/), and [patients first obtained prescriptions in February 2024](/history/greece-prescriptions-2024/). The user's five months remain the baseline of [Greece's](/country/gr/) cannabis law.
