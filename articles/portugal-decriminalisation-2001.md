Portugal's reform is the most cited drug law in the world, and it was not written
with cannabis in mind. It was an answer to heroin.

## The crisis

Portugal had made drug use a crime in [1970](/history/portugal-use-criminalised-1970/).
By the late 1990s it had an estimated 50,000 to 100,000 heroin users in a population
of about ten million, and the highest HIV rate among injecting drug users in the
European Union. In 2001 and 2002 more than half of the EU's new HIV diagnoses linked
to injecting were Portuguese. Over 40 per cent of sentenced prisoners were inside
for drug offences.

A [national strategy](/history/portugal-national-strategy-1999/) adopted in 1999
treated this as a health problem. It expanded harm reduction and doubled spending on
treatment and prevention. Decriminalisation was one part of it.

## What Law 30/2000 did

From 1 July 2001, possessing any drug for personal use is no longer a crime, provided
the amount is no more than ten days' supply.

| Drug | Ten-day limit |
|---|---|
| Herbal cannabis | 25 g |
| Cocaine | 2 g |
| Heroin | 1 g |

It is still prohibited. Police confiscate the drug and refer the person to a
"commission for the dissuasion of drug addiction", one in each of the 18 districts,
made up of a social worker, a psychiatrist and a lawyer. The commission can suspend
the case, impose a fine of 25 to 150 euros, or apply sanctions such as a ban on
visiting certain places. It cannot order treatment, only encourage it. In 2013, 83
per cent of its rulings were suspensions.

## What it did not do

Growing, selling and trafficking remain crimes. Home cultivation, however small, can
be prosecuted, and since [2003](/history/portugal-seeds-law-2003/) so can possessing
seeds. There is nowhere lawful to buy cannabis. Right-wing politicians warned at the
time that Portugal would become a narco-state.

## What happened

- Recorded drug deaths fell from 76 in 2001 to 10 in 2011; they have risen since but
  stay below the 2001 level.
- New HIV diagnoses linked to injecting fell from 1,287 in 2001 to 16 in 2019.
- The share of prisoners sentenced for drugs fell from over 40 per cent to 15.7 per
  cent in 2019.
- Drug use has stayed below the EU average.

How much of this is due to decriminalisation itself is disputed. The same years
brought far more treatment, and budget cuts after the financial crisis reduced it
again.

For cannabis the effect was narrower than the law's reputation suggests. In the index
possession rose from 1 to 3, enforcement from 2 to 5 and consumption from 1 to 3.
Growing and sharing stayed at 1, and [Portugal](/country/pt/) had no legal source at
all until [medical cannabis](/history/portugal-medical-2018/) in 2018.
