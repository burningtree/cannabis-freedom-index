Boy Phiri was caught with 14.85 kg of dagga worth about E700 and fined E300,
with two thirds suspended — an effective E100.[@1]

On review the Chief Justice replaced the fine with three years in prison,
eighteen months suspended, and laid down sentencing bands that still shape
Swazi courts: personal-use amounts normally mean a fine for a first offender,
wholesale suppliers face at or near the maximum even the first time, and even
a social supplier handing out joints may earn a non-custodial sentence.[@1]

The bands came with a history lesson: the 1976 order had raised the maximum
for unlawful dagga possession from E500 and three years, showing the
legislature regarded the trade with growing seriousness.[@1]
