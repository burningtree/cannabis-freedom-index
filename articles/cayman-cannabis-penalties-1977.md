Cayman increased the punishment for cannabis possession in April 1977. Someone convicted for the first time with less than a pound faced up to seven years in prison, while a repeat conviction carried a minimum of five months. The amendment also prevented courts from using specified probation and sentencing powers to avoid or reduce those sentences.[@1,2]

## Possession already carried prison

The [1973 drug law](/history/cayman-modern-drug-law-1973/) had brought possession, use, growing and supply within a broad system of criminal offences. Its schedule named ganja, covering the cannabis plant and resin, with an exception for licensed medicinal preparations. Unauthorised possession carried imprisonment of up to five years with hard labour on a first conviction and up to ten years on a repeat conviction.[@1]

That law replaced an earlier Dangerous Drugs Law. Its stated purpose was to bring drug control into line with international standards. The 1977 amendment built a more detailed penalty structure on that foundation.[@1]

## Weight and previous convictions mattered

The new rules separated certain offences involving drugs outside a defined hard-drug group into two weight bands. Cannabis was outside that group, which named substances including cocaine, heroin and morphine.[@1]

For possession below one pound, a first conviction carried up to seven years in prison and a possible fine of CI$10,000. A repeat conviction carried five months to seven years, with a fine ranging from CI$5,000 to CI$15,000.[@1]

At one pound or more, the first-conviction range was five months to seven years; a repeat conviction meant two to ten years. The same weight structure covered listed activities including importing, producing and supplying.[@1]

## Courts lost ways to soften the sentence

The amendment expressly barred the Probation of Offenders Law and two Penal Code sections from being used to avoid or mitigate sentences within these penalty groups. The restriction made the prison terms more consequential than a list of maximum penalties alone would suggest.[@1]

The official legislation history records commencement on 12 April 1977, following the governor’s assent on 5 April.[@1,2]

## Later reform offered other paths

The current Act provides community service and attendance orders in specified cases. It also contains a sentencing-relief exception for possession of cannabis below one pound.[@2]

After voters supported reform in 2025, the [2026 consultation](/history/cayman-cannabis-reform-consultation-2026/) asked whether personal possession and use should carry administrative penalties and whether old minor convictions should be cleared. Those proposals addressed the criminal consequences accumulated under this long-running system. The [Cayman profile](/country/ky/) describes the current rules.[@3]
