In August 1969 Canada amended the [Narcotic Control Act](/history/canada-narcotic-control-act-1961/) so that possessing cannabis could be dealt with by a fine. It was a small change made under pressure of numbers: possession stayed a crime, and a fine became its usual price.

## The numbers behind it

For the whole period from 1930 to 1946 the federal police recorded 25 cannabis arrests. In 1968 there were 2,300 cases. Under the Act as passed in 1961 a court's choice was essentially between a suspended sentence and prison, and in the late 1960s nearly half of those convicted of possession were incarcerated.

## The amendment

The 1969 change added a "fine only" option. A first offence could now be punished with a fine of up to 1,000 dollars or up to six months in prison.

| | Before | After 1969 |
|---|---|---|
| Sentences open to the court | suspended sentence or prison | also a fine alone |
| Outcome for those convicted | nearly half imprisoned | a fine in about two thirds of cases, up to 1985 |

In 1972 the Criminal Code added absolute and conditional discharges, which record a finding of guilt without a conviction.

## The commission

The same year the government appointed the Commission of Inquiry into the Non-Medical Use of Drugs, chaired by Gerald Le Dain. It held public hearings attended by about 12,000 people and reported in 1972. The majority recommended ending the offence of simple possession and of growing for personal use; one commissioner would have gone further, to legal distribution. The government did not act on it.

## What the fine did not solve

Cases kept rising: 12,000 in 1972, 27,202 people found guilty of possession in 1974, and 34,535 in 1981. A fine or a discharge still left a criminal record. By the late 1990s more than half a million Canadians had one for cannabis possession, and a field experiment in 1979 found that even an absolute discharge reduced a person's chances with employers.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Canada](/country/ca/) from 11 to 13 with the [1969 amendment](/history/canada-summary-conviction-1969/). Only enforcement changed, from 2 to 3: the usual outcome of a possession charge became a fine. Possession stays at 1, a crime that leaves a record, until legalisation in [2018](/history/canada-cannabis-act-2018/).
