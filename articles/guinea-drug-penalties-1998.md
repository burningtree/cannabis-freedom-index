On the last day of 1998 Guinea rewrote its drug penalties. Personal drug possession
and use carried six months to two years, a fine, or both under the new Penal Code —
up from two months to a year in the 1989 ordinance.[@1,2]

Ordinary cultivation and trafficking carried ten to twenty years, a fine, or both,
adding an explicit fine-only alternative to the earlier combined punishment.[@1]
Prosecutors could still withhold prosecution after a user completed prescribed
treatment — the treatment route the 2016 code kept and Mainline still finds rarely
used.[@1]

The 1998 code set the shape every later Guinean law kept: short tiers for personal
quantities, ten-to-twenty for the trade, treatment on paper. The 2016 code's Article
832 still runs the same one-to-three-year personal band the 1998 text introduced.
