Puerto Rico reduced the prison exposure for personal cannabis possession on 23 June 1971 and gave eligible first offenders a route to avoid a conviction. The original controlled-substances law set one to three years for possession for one's own use, replacing the earlier cannabis penalty of five to twenty years.

## The old law treated cannabis possession as a serious felony

The [1959 narcotics law](/history/puerto-rico-narcotics-law-1959/) prohibited cannabis possession, use and transfer and allowed a substantial fine in addition to imprisonment. It also prevented doctors from prescribing cannabis.

The new law addressed personal possession in its own section. Although the offence remained a felony, meaning a serious criminal charge, it carried a much shorter prison range than the earlier cannabis prohibition.

A first conviction could also bring a fine of up to five thousand dollars. A repeat conviction under the possession rule carried two to six years and a possible fine of up to ten thousand dollars.

## A first offender could receive probation

For someone without a previous relevant drug conviction, the court could suspend proceedings without making a finding of guilt. This required the person's consent and placed them on probation under conditions set by the judge for up to three years.

If the person broke those conditions, the court could revoke probation and sentence them. If they complied, the judge could hold a hearing and dismiss the case without a conviction.

The option therefore gave the court discretion to keep an eligible person out of the ordinary conviction-and-sentence route.

## Successful completion protected the person's record

The law allowed this dismissal only once in a person's lifetime. A confidential court record remained so that judges could check eligibility in a later case.

The dismissal did not count as a conviction for legal disqualifications or repeat-offence penalties. The person could also recover fingerprints and photographs taken by the police in connection with that possession case.

These consequences made the probation option relevant beyond the immediate risk of imprisonment.

## The possession benefits began immediately

Most of the new law waited a hundred and eighty days to take effect. The personal-possession section was among the parts effective immediately on approval.

Its transitional rule also benefited people convicted after that date even if prosecutors had charged them under an earlier law.

The same statute later supplied the health secretary's powers used in the [2015 medical-cannabis initiative](/history/puerto-rico-medical-executive-order-2015/). That initiative led towards [regulated patient supply in Puerto Rico](/country/pr/), alongside a possession offence that continued to be amended after its original 1971 enactment.
