On 27 October 1934, the Soviet Union adopted a decree prohibiting the sowing of
opium poppy and what its text called Indian hemp without the required permission.
The same state was actively encouraging farmers to produce hemp fibre and seed.

Read together, the decrees show a distinction between controlled drug crops and
hemp as an agricultural commodity. Describing the October measure as a ban on
every form of cannabis farming would erase that distinction.

## Adoption and operation

The October decree was issued jointly by the Central Executive Committee and
the Council of People's Commissars. It permitted crops whose harvests served
exclusively the Soviet Union's medical and scientific needs.

Its final provisions specified operation beginning in 1935. The 1934 date on this
history entry therefore identifies adoption; it should not be read as proof that
the union-wide measure was already operative throughout that year.

## Criminal responsibility for sowing

The decree required criminal responsibility for unauthorised sowing and mandatory
confiscation of the crops. It directed the union republics to supplement their
criminal codes accordingly.

Russia's [implementing amendment](/history/russia-indian-hemp-criminal-code-1934/)
inserted Article 179-a into the RSFSR Criminal Code.
It provided up to two years' imprisonment or up to one year of corrective labour,
with confiscation, for sowing opium poppy or Indian hemp without permission.
Those are statutory alternatives, not evidence of sentences actually imposed.

The instrument concerns cultivation. It does not by itself establish a new
offence for every instance of possession, consumption or sharing.

## Hemp remained an encouraged crop

The [March decree](/history/soviet-hemp-production-incentives-1934/)
pursued increased hemp yields through material incentives.
Farmers fulfilling hemp-delivery contracts could obtain relief from specified
potato and vegetable delivery obligations for qualifying land.

It also increased access to grain at fixed state prices in return for delivered
hemp fibre, raised allowances of vegetable oil and oilcake for seed deliveries,
and improved workday credits for processing tasks. The benefits were tied to
production and delivery conditions; they did not amount to unrestricted private
cultivation or commerce.

## What the two policies establish

Industrial promotion and drug-crop restriction coexisted in Soviet policy.
Neither document supplies a modern THC threshold or a complete botanical test
for deciding which plants fell within each category.

For [Russia's timeline](/country/ru/), the October decree records a specific legal
restriction within that differentiated system. Its wording, exceptions and
implementation date support a more precise account than a claim that the Soviet
government simply stopped growing cannabis in 1934.
