Bosnia and Herzegovina adopted its state narcotics law on 28 November 2005. Publication followed in the official gazette on 7 February 2006, and Article 103 made it effective eight days later. The two dates describe different stages: parliamentary adoption and the start of the new law.[@1]

## One state framework covered drugs and crops

The law prohibited unauthorised cultivation, production, distribution and possession, and provided for confiscation. It also created a system for estimating the country’s lawful drug requirements. These controls reached beyond criminal sentencing to the administration of authorised activities.[@1]

For an individual, its unlawful-possession provision carried a BAM1,000–3,000 fine. The law also addressed how entity and Brčko legislation should be brought into line with the state framework. Its administrative provisions therefore need to be read alongside the relevant criminal law, rather than as a promise that every cannabis case would end with a fine.[@1]

## Fibre and seed had a separate permit route

Article 25 allowed hemp for fibre, seed for animal feed, further propagation and seed processing. Growers needed permission from the foreign-trade ministry and generally a production-and-purchase agreement with a registered operator, unless they themselves met the registration condition. The permission concerned specified crop uses.[@1]

In August 2022, the medicines agency explained that the industrial exception required THC no higher than 0.2% in dry plant matter. Its notice responded to people asking about CBD oils, resins, flower teas and vaping liquids sold online or in herbal shops. It said such products did not then have a lawful medical-cannabis route under the prohibited schedule.[@2]

## Medical access later needed more than rescheduling

The [December 2025 decision](/history/bosnia-medical-use-approved-2025/) removed the main scheduling obstacle to controlled medical use. That did not immediately complete the prescribing and import arrangements, as the Ministry of Civil Affairs reported at its September 2026 follow-up.[@3]

The Federation planned a new prescribing rulebook for early 2027, while Republika Srpska was finalising amendments. Work still had to settle indications, authorised prescribers, registers, doses and pharmaceutical forms. The medicines agency also needed the rules before providing the estimate required for imports.[@3]

The [country profile](/country/ba/) follows this distinction between a legal framework and an operating patient route. The hemp exception, possession sanctions and later medical decision each serve different purposes within that framework.[@1,2,3]
