Zimbabwe’s prosecutors reported on 17 July 2026 that Clever Makwarimba had received an effective four-year prison sentence for unlawful possession of 19.49 kg of cannabis. The fifty-three-year-old man from Zvishavane was sentenced by the Beitbridge Magistrates’ Court. His arrest followed a police search of his travelling bag and satchel.

## A tip led detectives to the highway

According to the National Prosecuting Authority’s bulletin, detectives received information at about 9 pm on 11 July that Makwarimba possessed illicit drugs. They went to the location identified at the 81 km mark on the Beitbridge–Masvingo Highway.

Officers found him, identified themselves and explained that they intended to search. Inside his travelling bag and satchel they discovered eight large packages of cannabis, which the bulletin described as cobs.

The reported total weight was 19.49 kg. Police arrested him, and the prosecutors’ later bulletin announced the resulting prison term.

## Possession could still bring a substantial sentence

Zimbabwe’s criminal code expressly criminalises unlawful possession and use of dangerous drugs, including cannabis. It also covers growing for a person’s own consumption within that offence.

The code provides a fine up to level ten, up to five years in prison, or both. Where addiction is established, a court may order treatment in addition to or instead of another sentence.

The prosecutors described Makwarimba’s offence as unlawful possession and his punishment as an effective four years in prison. Their account supplies a concrete example of custody following a cannabis possession prosecution.

## Licensed production had developed alongside prohibition

The [2018 medicinal and scientific production regulations](/history/zimbabwe-medical-2018/) created a separate route for licensed cannabis producers. Applicants had to meet requirements for their site, personnel, security and production activities.

Zimbabwe later introduced [industrial-hemp authorisation](/history/zimbabwe-hemp-authorised-farmers-2019/) and detailed agricultural permits. In [2023](/history/zimbabwe-hemp-one-percent-2023/), the criminal code defined industrial hemp at no more than 1% delta-9 THC by dry weight and excluded it from the cannabis-plant branch of the dangerous-drug definition.

These permissions made legal authority and the classification of the material important parts of the country’s cannabis controls.

## Prosecutors used the case to warn others

The bulletin ended with a warning against participation in the illegal drug trade. Its account connected a tip, a roadside search, the cannabis found and a custodial sentence.

For Makwarimba, the reported outcome was four years in prison. His case adds a dated account of how cannabis possession was punished while [Zimbabwe’s](/country/zw/) licensed medical and hemp industries continued to develop.
