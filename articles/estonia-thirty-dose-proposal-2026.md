In January 2026 Estonia's justice minister proposed moving the line between drug users and drug criminals: a large quantity would start at 30 doses instead of 10.[@1] Below it, possession stays a misdemeanour; above it, a crime.[@1]

## Potency broke the old line

Minister Liisa Pakosta's argument was arithmetic. As drugs grew more potent, a long-term addict's daily dose could legally qualify as a large quantity — the criminal tier swallowing the very users the misdemeanour tier was meant for.[@1]

The fix: raise the threshold so that holding less than 30 doses is a misdemeanour, not a crime.[@1] Possession of any narcotic would still be a misdemeanour with a 2,400-euro fine — leniency here means staying out of the criminal code, not going unpunished.[@1]

## Dealers face the other direction

While users would get more room, dealers would get less. The ministry wants to drop the current requirement to prove a dealer profited financially, making convictions easier.[@1] Pakosta paired the two halves explicitly: softer on users, harder on traffickers.[@1]

## What it would change

For cannabis, the proposal touches the core of the current split. Today a small quantity means a misdemeanour fine or detention, while a large quantity — enough to intoxicate ten people — carries one to ten years.[@1] Moving the line from 10 to 30 doses decides which side many cases fall on.[@1]

The proposal is still a proposal. If adopted, it would be the first threshold change since the current scheme settled — and it is traced in [Estonia's](/country/ee/) history as the moment the minister said the old line no longer measured what users actually hold.
