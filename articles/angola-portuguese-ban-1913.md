Angola's first dated ban on cannabis is a Portuguese colonial order, Portaria No. 508 of 13 May 1913. Liamba, as the plant is known there, had been smoked in the region long before; the order made it illegal.

## Why Portugal banned it

The historian Chris Duvall, who has traced the plant's history across the continent, describes the Angolan ban as aimed at the colonial troops and as a way of pushing farmers towards growing tobacco. An earlier trace of the same attitude survives from 1857, when David Livingstone recorded that cannabis use by slaves was treated as a crime in Angola.

## The law four decades on

In 1956 the United Nations published a survey of cannabis in Angola, compiled from information supplied by Portugal. It lists the 1913 order beside Legislative Decree No. 1,193 of 28 August 1940, under whose Article 90 prosecutions were then brought. The survey's own section on the legal provisions was sent back to Lisbon for checking and left out, so the wording of neither text is given.

What the survey does record is how the ban looked in practice:

- non-medical use of cannabis was illegal, and there was no licensed cultivation;
- producing cannabis needed a special government licence on the advice of the public health authorities, and none had been issued;
- in 1952 a European firm asked for permission to grow the plant and was refused;
- "severe fines" were levied on anyone found with cannabis plants, and the administrative authorities kept strict control over use of the wild plant.

Portugal reported almost no enforcement. The only seizure mentioned is two packages holding 44 grams at the port of Lobito in 1952, with one person prosecuted, and the authorities said they knew of no traffic with the Belgian Congo, Northern Rhodesia or South West Africa. Illicit growing was said to be limited to occasional plants carefully hidden. The survey does not say how far those reports reflected what happened outside the towns.

## Why the index moved

The [1913 order](/history/angola-portuguese-ban-1913/) is the only scored step in [Angola's](/country/ao/) history, from the unregulated state to prohibition. Independent Angola replaced the colonial rules with its drug law of 1999, which keeps personal use a crime with up to three months in prison. The country's present total is 11.
