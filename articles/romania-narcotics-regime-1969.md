Romania adopted a replacement narcotics law on 29 December 1969, prohibiting unauthorised production, possession and trade, alongside growing drug-containing plants for processing.[@1] Medical, veterinary, scientific and industrial activities could proceed with Health Ministry authorisation.[@1] The law directed criminal breaches to the Penal Code.[@1]

## Permission governed the whole chain

The law reached offering, selling, purchasing, distributing and transporting controlled drugs.[@1] It also prohibited administering them to another person without a medical indication and organising or allowing consumption in particular premises.[@1] Authorised operators had to keep daily records of the drugs moving through their hands.[@1]

Medical use required a doctor's or veterinarian's prescription, normally on special forms.[@1] County health authorities could authorise patients or their representatives to obtain quantities needed for long treatment.[@1] Unlawfully held substances were to be taken for confiscation and deposited with a nearby pharmacy.[@1]

## Cannabis appeared in the implementing list

The [1970 Health Ministry instructions](/history/romania-cannabis-control-instructions-1970/) expressly named Indian hemp, cannabis resin, extracts and tinctures in their controlled-substance list.[@2] They applied the authorisation system to the products in that annex, connecting the general law to particular cannabis preparations.[@2]

The instructions detailed how manufacturers applied for annual permission, including the products, quantities and manufacturing processes proposed.[@2] Manufacturing and preparing controlled products also required registration with the territorial militia.[@2] Imports and exports passed through authorised state foreign-trade enterprises and required the relevant permissions.[@2]

## Pharmacists accounted for controlled stock

The rules required daily entries in a special register validated by the county health directorate or the Bucharest authority.[@2] Manufacturers, warehouses and pharmacies therefore had to account for controlled products as stock moved through the supply chain.[@2] The instructions separately covered prescribing and medical administration, giving authorised handling its own procedures.[@2]

## The system replaced an earlier decree

The 1969 law specified commencement sixty days after publication and repealed the 1950 narcotics decree and a 1954 reporting decision.[@1,2] The implementing instructions were dated 26 February 1970 and published on 25 April; their final clause linked their commencement to the parent law.[@1,2]

Parliament's [2005 controlled-substances law](/history/romania-cannabis-medicine-controls-2006/) later repealed the 1969 law.[@3] The [Romania profile](/country/ro/) explains today's criminal penalties and the separate rules for authorised medicines and agricultural hemp.[@3]
