In 1902 the British administration of the Uganda Protectorate prohibited
producing cannabis in any form, naming bhang, ganja and charas.[@2] The ban
landed on households that grew the plant for their own use and smoked it in
water-pipes made from gourds.[@1] Who could smoke was governed by custom:
men and unmarried women took part, while married women and young fighting
men were kept out.[@1]

## Growing for the household

Hobley's survey, published the same year as the ban, found tobacco and bhang
grown for home consumption rather than for sale.[@1] The plant is known
locally as njaga or shada.[@2] Bhang sat beside tobacco in the household
economy: both were grown for home use, and while both sexes smoked tobacco,
bhang had its own users, its own rules and its own pipe.[@1] Smoking used a
hubble-bubble, a water-pipe made from a gourd.[@1] The rules differed between
peoples: in some places only men smoked and young fighting men were barred,
while elsewhere unmarried women smoked too and gave it up once
married.[@1] Not everyone grew the plant at all. Hobley noted that the Nandi
neither grew Indian hemp nor smoked it.[@1]

## A blanket ban in the same year

The prohibition covered production in any form and listed the three
preparations by name, so the household plots Hobley had just described became
illegal to cultivate, with no exception for home use or for the groups that
already abstained.[@1,2] The colony thus moved from regulated custom to a
written ban within a single year, with the administration outlawing the plant
even where custom had already restricted its use.[@2] Custom had kept bhang
from married women and young fighters; the new law kept it from everyone.[@1,2]

## Prohibition that lasted

The ban survived independence. Uganda later joined the international drug
treaties, and the 2015 Narcotics Act kept recreational growing and trade
criminal while licensing medical cultivation and export.[@2] After the
Constitutional Court nullified that Act on quorum grounds in 2023, parliament
passed a replacement, leaving the 1902 prohibition as the distant starting
point of an unbroken ban on household growing.[@2] The timeline runs through
the [2015 Act](/history/uganda-narcotics-act-2015/) and its
[2023 nullification](/history/uganda-court-nullifies-act-2023/); the
[Uganda profile](/country/ug/) follows the later medical and recreational
history.[@2]
