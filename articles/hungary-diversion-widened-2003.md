On 1 March 2003 Hungary reopened to every drug user the option it had restricted to addicts four years earlier: [diversion](/history/hungary-diversion-1993/), a six-month treatment or counselling programme in place of prosecution for possessing a small amount for personal use.

## What the reform did

The Socialist and Liberal coalition elected in 2002 abolished the [1999 rule](/history/hungary-diversion-restricted-1999/) under which only a person diagnosed as dependent could choose the programme. Access became unlimited for all offenders who possessed illegal drugs for their own use.

It also tried to go a step further. The option was extended to people who shared drugs with others without profit, for "collective use", the ordinary case of friends passing a joint.

## The Constitutional Court

That extension did not survive. In decision 54/2004 of 13 December 2004 the Constitutional Court struck it down. It held that there is "no right to intoxication" and that the term "collective use" was too unclear to stand in a criminal statute. Diversion for personal possession remained.

## How it settled

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 2008 | a joint ministerial decree sets procedural rules and the timeframe: two hours every two weeks for six months |
| 2011 | methodological guidelines for the counselling track |
| 2013 | a new criminal code limits diversion to people who have not re-offended within two years |
| 2018 | a new procedural law lets only the prosecutor suspend the case, and allows the court to charge the offender the costs, such as laboratory tests |

After 2003 diversion became the main gateway into drug services. At one Budapest provider the share of clients sent by the justice system rose from 24% to 72.6%, and more than half of them had no problem that called for treatment. The authors of that study recommended rethinking the mechanism because it drew resources away from people with real treatment needs.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Hungary](/country/hu/) from 8 to 12 with the [2003 reform](/history/hungary-diversion-widened-2003/). Enforcement returns from 1 to 3, as in 1993. It fell again, to 2, with the two-year rule of 2013, which is where the country stands.
