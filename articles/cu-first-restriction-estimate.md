Cuba's first cannabis controls date to 1919 — not to a ban but to a licensing regime. A
historical study of the period identifies marijuana among the drugs regulated by Cuba's
1919 law, with licensed import and production and criminal penalties reaching down to
possession.[@1]

## Licensed before prohibited

The 1919 rules belong to the international system's first wave: Cuba was bringing
controlled drugs inside a permit system rather than outlawing them outright.[@1] What the
law said about cannabis beyond the listing, and how the sequence ran from those controls
to later prohibition, remains provisional in the record.

## The habit arrives anyway

A 1949 survey dated the habit's arrival to about thirty years earlier — right at the law's
moment. Portuguese sailors had picked marijuana up in a Mexican port brothel and carried
it to the island; by 1949 the supply still came from Mexico while home plots spread as
wartime shipping shrank and growers chased bigger gains.[@3] Cuba's police had already put
the problem on record at the 1946 forensic-medicine congress in Havana.[@3]

## Confirmed by 1950

Whatever the gaps between 1919 and mid-century, the endpoint is firm: a United Nations
survey published in January 1951 reported that keeping and consuming hemp was prohibited
throughout the American states by 1950.[@2] The estimate window for Cuba's first
restriction runs from the 1919 law to that confirmation.[@1][@2]
