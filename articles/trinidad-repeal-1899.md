Trinidad changed its ganja regime in 1899, but the evidence does not support describing the event simply as repeal of the 1885 cultivation licence. There had been a separate prohibition in 1894, and the surviving accounts suggest a more complicated revision of the legal categories.

## The intervening prohibition

The [1885 legislation](/history/trinidad-cultivation-licence-1885/) imposed heavy duties on cultivation licences. Jamie Banks’s archival research distinguishes this restrictive licensing system from the [1894 prohibition ordinance](/history/trinidad-prohibition-1894/), which concerned cultivation, sale and possession.

Banks subsequently refers to repeal of prohibition in 1899. His discussion notes continued medical campaigning after the change, including an asylum superintendent’s claim that repeal had increased admissions attributed to cannabis. Such an assertion documents the continuing argument for restriction; it does not independently prove either medical causation or the legal scope of the repeal.

## A later parliamentary account

In December 2019, Attorney General Faris Al-Rawi gave Parliament a retrospective history of cannabis legislation. He described the 1899 measure as involving amendments and a distinction between medicinal preparations and smoking ganja, alongside the earlier ordinance.

This account complicates a simple prohibition-to-full-legalisation narrative. It suggests that changing the prohibitory regime could coexist with continued classification and regulation of cannabis products. The unrevised transcript is not sufficiently precise to reconstruct the surviving sections or determine how the older instruments operated together.

The official Digital Legislative Library lists a Ganja Ordinance, No. 10 of 1899. Its linked download, retrieved during this research, contained a single blank page when rendered and yielded no substantive text. The catalogue confirms an instrument’s identification, but that file cannot verify its provisions.

## What the record establishes

The event is retained as an easing or revision in 1899, based on Banks’s account of repeal. Its description now acknowledges both the intervening 1894 prohibition and the unresolved surviving controls. It does not establish unrestricted cultivation, retail sale or possession for every purpose.

Reading the original ordinance, amendments and commencement provisions remains necessary before assigning precise legal consequences to each product category.
