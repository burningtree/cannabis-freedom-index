Five years after it softened first-offender penalties, Gambia's drug agency admitted the
system still ran on punishment. Its five-year strategy plan for 2019–2023 described
burdensome fines, prison sentences and limited rehabilitation — and called for
alternatives for young and first-time offenders.[@1]

The plan asked for rehabilitation facilities, counselling, skills training and
alternatives such as community service, plus a review of the drug laws after the 2014
first-offender reform.[@1] It diagnosed a system dominated by punishment with little
help during and after imprisonment.[@1]

The strategy sits inside the profile as the honest counterweight to its own penalty
tables: the same agency that reported 1,219 arrests in 2024 also wrote, in 2019, that
fines and cells were not working for the young offenders who fill them.
