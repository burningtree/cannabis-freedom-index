New York revised its poison-sales legislation in 1862, two years after adopting a schedule that expressly named cannabis. The revision changed how transactions were documented and removed the schedule. It is evidence of regulation being adjusted, rather than a simple sequence in which each new law added another prohibition.

## The earlier arrangement

The [1860 law](/history/us-new-york-poison-sales-1860/) required a sales record naming the recipient and a witness, subject to an exception for a physician’s written order. Cannabis and its preparations appeared in section 3 alongside other scheduled poisonous substances.

The law also contained labelling rules, an exception for specified wholesale quantities and a provision limiting its geographical application. Those details matter when considering what the later amendment changed: the starting point was a particular sales regime, rather than a comprehensive cannabis possession ban.

## Removing the witness

Francis Tillou’s legal paper states that chapter 273 of the laws of 1862 amended the first section by removing the requirement to record a witness’s name. This reduced a particular documentary condition imposed on sellers.

The passage does not describe the complete abolition of transaction records. Treating the removal of one required name as the end of recordkeeping would extend the evidence beyond what the author actually reports.

## Repealing the schedule

Tillou also states that the 1862 amendment repealed section 3 of the 1860 act. That was the provision enumerating cannabis and the other substances to which the scheduled rules applied.

This supports recording a change in cannabis-related regulation. It does not, without further analysis of the remaining statutes and their interpretation, establish that every cannabis sale became entirely unregulated. The same paper discusses a broader poison-law framework, including obligations predating the 1860 act.

## How the change is dated

The source is a contemporary lawyer’s account, read to the Medico-Legal Society in 1870 and printed in the 1874 revised collection. It identifies the amendment’s year and chapter number, but the passage used here does not independently establish an exact calendar day. The database consequently retains year-only precision.

The event is unscored context for the [United States](/country/us/). It concerns a New York statutory revision, and supplies no basis for treating the whole country as having legalized or prohibited cannabis in 1862. The original amending act remains a useful next source to retrieve.
