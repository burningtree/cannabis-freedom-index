The oldest cannabis evidence in the batch is Zimbabwean — roughly. Pipe bowls
recovered in southern Zambia were radiocarbon-dated to 1200 CE, and sites in
Zimbabwe and Kenya yielded bowls that seem roughly contemporaneous, though dated
only in relative terms from archaeological stratigraphy.[@1]

Duvall argues cannabis likely circulated within the Kingdom of Zimbabwe (1200s–1400s)
and certainly within its successor, the Kingdom of Monomotapa (1400s–1700s), both
centred on modern Zimbabwe; pipes estimated to the 1200s were recovered from sites
that once lay within these kingdoms.[@1]

Eight centuries later the word has settled as mbanje and the custom never left: the
same plant the kingdoms smoked is seized by the hundred kilograms on the Bulawayo
Road, and the pipes of 1200 have their descendants in every charge of unlawful
possession.
