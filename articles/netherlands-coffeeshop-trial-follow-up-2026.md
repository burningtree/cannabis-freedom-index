Visitors to Dutch coffeeshops taking part in the regulated supply experiment found more choice and generally positive early experiences, researchers reported on 9 September 2026. Wider effects on cannabis use, health and public order remained unresolved. The first follow-up offers an early view of a supply system still settling into place.[@1,2]

## A legal route through the back door

The familiar [coffeeshop](/culture/traditions/the-coffeeshop/) rested on an unusual division: tightly controlled retail sales were tolerated, while commercial cultivation and supplying those shops remained prohibited. Customers could buy across the counter, but the wholesale chain feeding that counter operated outside the tolerated retail rules.[@3]

The experiment connects licensed cultivation, distribution and sales. Its [experimental phase began on 7 April 2025](/history/netherlands-regulated-supply-only-2025/), with participating shops intended to sell regulated cannabis exclusively. At the start, ministers temporarily deferred enforcement against unregulated hashish because growers needed more time to produce enough suitable supply. That shortage showed how changing a shop's legal supplier also meant changing what customers could find on its menu.[@3]

## More products, with start-up difficulties

Trimbos reported broader menus in the ten participating municipalities and better visitor assessments of flower's value for money. Municipalities, shopkeepers, growers and inspectors also described closer cooperation. Early concerns included limited hashish variety, availability and high-THC products; the illegal market continued alongside coffeeshops.[@2]

The full report describes interviews with 85 stakeholders, surveys of 865 coffeeshop visitors and 441 nearby residents or workers, visitor counts at 143 shops and analysis of 134 menus. Its first follow-up covers developments after the April 2025 start through the first quarter of 2026.[@4]

## What another measurement can reveal

Researchers compared participating municipalities with municipalities continuing under the existing policy. They found no clear differences in visits, consumption, health, liveability, safety or the illegal market at this stage. WODC said several measurements would be needed before identifying effects and drawing policy conclusions.[@1,2]

The study's design compares developments over time, but municipalities were not randomly assigned and may differ in other ways. Longer observation is needed to understand whether the changes in products and supply are accompanied by lasting changes for users and their neighbours.[@4]

The [Netherlands profile](/country/nl/) places these findings beside the country's retail rules and medical supply. The follow-up adds evidence for the continuing decision about how coffeeshops should obtain their cannabis.[@1]
