In 2016 two Brazilian families won court orders allowing them to grow cannabis for their epileptic
daughters. Medical cannabis in Brazil was opened up less by laws than by parents, growers and their
lawyers, one court case at a time.

## Parents and growers

In 2013 Cida Carvalho, a bank worker in São Paulo, read about Charlotte Figi, the American girl whose
seizures had eased with a cannabis extract. Her daughter Clárian, then ten, had Dravet syndrome, with long
convulsions and frequent emergencies. Her neurologist agreed to try, and brought a bottle of CBD oil back
illegally from a holiday in Miami; it cost about 2,500 reais. Clárian took her first drops on 26 April
2014 and went eleven days without a seizure. Afterwards a secret network of growers in Rio de Janeiro
supplied oil for free, and her father fetched it from Rio for three years.

Other families took the same risk. In the 2014 documentary *Ilegal*, Katiele and Norberto Fischer told how
they smuggled CBD to treat their five-year-old daughter Anny, knowing it made them traffickers in law.
Ribeiro describes the meeting between such parents and underground growers, who supplied the plant free,
as the moment the movement came to the boil.

## Into the courts

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 2011 | The Supreme Court [protects marches](/history/brazil-stf-marijuana-march-2011/) for legalisation |
| 2016 | First habeas corpus orders for families to grow cannabis |
| 2017 | [Abrace Esperança](/history/brazil-abrace-authorised-2017/) becomes the first association authorised to grow |
| 2019 | Associations form a [national federation](/history/brazil-fact-federation-2019/) |
| 2020 | A court lets [Apepi](/history/brazil-apepi-authorised-2020/) supply oil nationwide |
| 2022 | The Superior Court of Justice [allows home growing](/history/brazil-stj-home-growing-2022/) for three patients |

In 2016 Margarete Brito and Marcos Langenbach, whose daughter Sofia has the same syndrome as Anny,
obtained what Ribeiro calls Brazil's first habeas corpus to grow cannabis, using high-CBD seedlings donated
by growers of the Abracannabis association. Clárian's family won a similar order the same year with the
Rede Reforma lawyers' collective. Both families then founded associations, Apepi and Cultive, to share
what they produced and taught.

In the absence of regulated supply, such associations multiplied. The Rede Reforma network estimated that
more than 140,000 patients were supplied by associations or by individuals with court orders.

## Limits

Ribeiro, a neuroscientist who took part in this movement, sees its gains as real but partial. Court orders
reach only those who can go to court. A bill to regulate cultivation, number 399 of 2015, passed a special
committee by one vote and still awaited a plenary vote as he wrote, and it would favour large companies
over associations. Brazil allowed cannabis products in pharmacies [in 2019](/history/brazil-pharmacy-products-2019/),
and its first [cultivation rules](/history/brazil-cultivation-rules-2026/) took effect in 2026.
