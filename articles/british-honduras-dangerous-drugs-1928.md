On 23 August 1928 British Honduras consolidated its drug law in Ordinance No. 22, banning the cultivation, possession and sale of dangerous drugs while admitting medicinal opium and cocaine under licence.[@1] The colony that wrote those rules would become one of America's biggest cannabis suppliers — and then, with American help, burn the trade down.[@1,3]

## A consolidating ordinance

The ordinance gathered the opium and dangerous-drugs law into one text: defined drugs in its purview, prohibited growing, holding and selling them, banned importing, exporting and transhipping raw opium and other drugs, and left a licensing door open for medical use.[@1]

## Ganja in the 1950s

By the 1950s the banned plant was everywhere anyway.[@2] Growing, selling and using ganja kept increasing despite penal action, the colonial report admitted, and the penalties were stiff.[@2] Police stepped up suppression in 1954 and courts handed down heavy prison sentences, yet convictions climbed from seven in 1950 to twenty-three in 1954, with vendors targeting youths.[@2]

## Exporter, then eradication

Until the 1980s Belize ranked as the fourth-largest exporter of cannabis to the United States, behind Colombia, Mexico and Jamaica.[@3] From the mid-1980s government eradication with American support crashed production to negligible levels by 1994 — though the plant never vanished: through the first ten months of 2014 alone, Belizean authorities eradicated over 100,000 cannabis plants.[@3]

## Ten grams at home

In 2012 the government announced it was considering decriminalising small amounts.[@3] On 20 October 2017 the House of Representatives passed an amendment decriminalising possession or use of 10 grams or less on private premises, signed into law that November over the objections of the National Evangelical Association.[@3] Talk of going further never stopped: in 2017 the opposition leader said it was time to stop locking up young people for a stick of weed and prepare research toward medicinal and small-quantity legalisation, and in 2024 the Ministry of New Growth Industries was assessing full legalisation.[@3] The double standard showed the year before decriminalisation, when an American tourist caught buying 1.8 grams had her charges dismissed after the judge noted the cannabis was sold openly to her.[@3] From the 1928 ordinance through the eradication years to the ten-gram rule, prohibition bent but never broke — and the 1928 commencement remains the base layer under [Belize's](/country/bz/) scores.[@1,3]
