Greenland tightened the conditions for its low-THC cannabis exception in April 2024, requiring imports in batches for distribution through storage controlled by the authorised importer.[@1] The replacement rules took effect on 22 April and also required documentation identifying products and showing that they met the 0.2% THC ceiling.[@1]

## The earlier exception linked products to import permission

Since [January 2023](/history/greenland-local-drug-law-2023/), the drug-control regulation had excluded qualifying cannabis products from its controlled-drug definition when they contained no more than 0.2% THC and were imported by an authorised legal entity, such as a company.[@2] The Chief Medical Officer's office could grant import permission and attach conditions.[@2]

That route followed [parliament's 2019 calls](/history/greenland-low-thc-parliament-2019/) for Greenlandic access to low-THC cannabis products and oils.[@3] A local law was needed because responsibility for drug legislation had passed to Greenland with health services in 1992.[@3]

## Storage and distribution became part of the test

The April regulation retained the THC ceiling and authorised-importer requirement.[@1] It added that the goods must be imported in batches for distribution after arriving at storage or similar premises in Greenland controlled by the importer.[@1]

Applicants had to provide information making the substance identifiable as part of a clearly described product and document compliance with the THC ceiling.[@1] Permission remained restricted to legal entities, and the importer remained responsible for the products meeting that limit.[@1]

The regulation also applied its rules to import applications that had not been decided when it took effect.[@1] This brought pending applications under the replacement conditions.[@1]

## Controlled medicines followed their own permissions

Greenland's drug law separately permitted pharmacy handling and receipt of controlled medicines under prescription rules.[@4] The regulation provided permissions for patients, treatment settings and authorised institutions, while otherwise limiting controlled drugs to medical and scientific use.[@1]

Cultivation and manufacture of controlled drugs had to follow the medicines rules, including safeguards against unauthorised access and removal.[@1] Those requirements operated alongside the exception for qualifying imported low-THC products.[@1]

## The conditions continue in the current rules

The current drug-control regulation retains the authorised-import, THC and local-distribution conditions.[@5] For businesses seeking to use the exception, the route therefore involves both product documentation and permission for the organisation importing it.[@5]

The government later [emphasised customs controls and prevention in June 2026](/history/greenland-hash-prevention-response-2026/), including treatment and support for families and young people.[@6] Those policies sit alongside the low-THC route described in [Greenland's profile](/country/gl/).[@5,6]
