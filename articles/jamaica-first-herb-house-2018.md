Three years after Jamaica's 2015 ganja reform, the Caribbean got its first regulated medical
cannabis dispensary: Kaya Herbhouse, opened by entrepreneur Balram "Bali" Vaswani in Drax Hall,
St Ann, in March 2018.[@3,4]

Vaswani was among the first entrepreneurs to secure the full stack of licences under the new
regime — research, cultivation, processing and retail — and spent heavily to build a vertically
integrated business rather than just a shopfront.[@3] Before opening he conducted strain research
with the University of Technology, then grew Kaya's reputation through Cannabis Cup wins.[@2,4]
The first official harvest at Kaya Farms in Drax Hall was cut under the watchful eyes of the
Cannabis Licensing Authority's interim chief executive, and the cured, trimmed and packaged
flower went straight to the Herbhouse for the first legal sales to medical patients.[@1,2]

The Herbhouse paired a dispensary with a café and designated therapy space. Medical marijuana
cards issued internationally were accepted, an on-site doctor could write recommendations, and
visitors who signed the voluntary declaration the 2015 law provides for could buy and use
ganja on the premises.[@3] It became the template for Jamaica's "herb house" model — licensed
retailers where purchase and on-site consumption are both legal — and for the wellness-tourism
pitch of "build Jamaica, by Jamaicans, for Jamaica".[@3,4]

The road since has been hard. Legal herb houses sold at roughly five to ten times the street
price, traditional growers mostly stayed outside the licensed system, and Kaya accumulated
losses reported at US$8.8 million before merging with the ganja marketing platform NUGL in
April 2022.[@4,5] By the mid-2020s dozens of cultivators and retailers held licences, but the legal
sector still covered only a fraction of the island's ganja — a tension the 2026 fee-free
routes for small growers were designed to address.
