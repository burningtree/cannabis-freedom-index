On 22 December 2011 Sweden approved Sativex, an oromucosal spray combining THC and CBD, for spasticity in multiple sclerosis.[@1] It was the first cannabis-based medicine in the country's legal supply — and for years, nearly the only one.[@1]

## What was approved

The Medical Products Agency authorised the spray for moderate to severe MS spasticity in patients who had not responded adequately to other anti-spasticity medication.[@1] It is a cannabis-derived pharmaceutical, prescribed through the ordinary medicines route, not a herbal product.[@1]

## What it did not open

The approval changed nothing for other cannabis medicines. Anything beyond the approved prescription products still requires the patient's doctor to apply for an individual licence from the Medical Products Agency, rarely granted.[@1] Domestic cultivation for medical use stayed prohibited.[@1]

In a zero-tolerance system — where use itself has been a crime since 1988 and police can compel a drug test on suspicion — Sativex carved the single lawful channel: a pharmacy product for a defined set of patients.[@1] It remains the base layer under [Sweden's](/country/se/) limited medical access.
