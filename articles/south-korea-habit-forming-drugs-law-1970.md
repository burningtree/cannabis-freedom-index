In July 1970 South Korea criminalised its own hemp, closing a thirteen-year
loophole at American urging.[@1] The law then sat almost unused for four
years — until the day it was turned on the country's pop stars.[@1]

## The loophole America hated

The [1957 Narcotics Act](/history/south-korea-narcotics-act-1957/) named
“Indian hemp”, Cannabis sativa L.; the text has no import-only qualification.[@4]
VanVolkenburg describes Korean hemp remaining outside its reach in practice,
and from the early 1960s it sold openly
around the American bases as "happy smoke".[@1] In 1966 the United States
Forces Korea command asked Seoul to remove the loophole, and kept asking:
American military arrests for marijuana rose from eight in 1966 to 635 in
1969.[@1] Korea added hemp's active ingredient to a habit-forming drugs bill
in 1968, then let the bill sleep for two years.[@1]

Two events on 8 June 1970 woke it up. Washington announced troop cuts for
Korea, and the JoongAng Ilbo reported university students smoking marijuana
in secret clubs.[@1] The next morning every newspaper agreed marijuana had
become a problem.[@1] The Korea Times blamed the legal vacuum for friction
between the allies: the Americans had asked a province to tighten control of
marihuana and pressed the health ministry to hurry the dormant bill through;
the Korean-language press dwelt on the students instead.[@1] The bill passed
in mid-July.[@1]

## A law that slept in the camp towns

For the next four years almost every marijuana arrest reported in the press
happened in the American camp towns, not the universities — a sign, the
record suggests, that the students' guilt was exaggerated and the law's true
target the soldiers.[@1] Musicians, artists and students smoking at home
never realised the plant had become illegal.[@1] That ignorance set up the
[December 1975 roundup](/history/south-korea-entertainers-arrested-1975/),
when prosecutors suddenly enforced the law beyond the bases and more than
eighty entertainers were arrested within a month.[@2]

## What came next

The [Cannabis Control Act of 1976](/history/south-korea-cannabis-control-act-1976/)
banned the plant in all forms with prison terms, and folded into the single
Narcotics Control Act in 2000.[@3] The irony outlived the crackdown: the law
Washington had pressured Seoul to pass became the instrument for expelling
American-influenced youth culture from public life.[@1] The
[South Korea profile](/country/kr/) follows the story to medical opening.
