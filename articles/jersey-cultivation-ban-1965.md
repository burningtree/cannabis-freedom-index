In 1965 Jersey made growing cannabis a crime for the first time, and punished anyone who let their premises be used for smoking it or dealing in it.[@1] Anyone who knowingly grew a cannabis plant without a licence from the Public Health Committee faced up to ten years in prison or a £1,000 fine under the 1954 law's penalties.[@1,3] Until then the island had controlled only the drug's import and export.[@2,3]

## A law forty years out of date

Jersey's first drugs law was written in French in 1923 and aimed at opium and cocaine.[@2] A replacement drafted around 1934 was never finished, and in 1939 the Home Office in London called the 1923 law "much out of date".[@2] Guernsey moved first with a cannabis ban in its 1931 law, while Jersey waited until 1954.[@2] That year's Dangerous Drugs (Jersey) Law put "Indian hemp" — the dried flowering tops — under import and export licence, through St Helier port or the airport, but left possession, growing and sale to later orders.[@3] The 1961 Single Convention then gave the world a new vocabulary, and Jersey's 1965 amendment swapped "Indian hemp" for "cannabis" and "cannabis resin".[@1]

## What the 1965 law did

The amendment created two offences.[@1] An occupier who let premises be used for smoking cannabis or cannabis resin, or for dealing in either, committed an offence, as did anyone involved in managing such premises.[@1] And anyone who knowingly grew any plant of the genus cannabis without a committee licence committed one too.[@1] Both were punished as offences against the 1954 law: up to £1,000, up to ten years in prison, or both.[@3]

## Finished the next year, rewritten in 1980

The island's police mentioned drugs in an annual report for the first time in 1964, and cannabis by name in 1968 — the year after possession itself became an offence.[@2] That missing piece came with the [1966 order](/history/jersey-possession-ban-1966/), which banned possessing, supplying, procuring and advertising cannabis, other than extracts and tinctures, without a licence.[@2] The 1978 Misuse of Drugs (Jersey) Law then repealed the 1954 law, sorted cannabis into Class B, and added intent to supply as an offence for the first time.[@2]

From a French law about opium to a licensing regime for a plant nobody was yet growing openly: Jersey built its cannabis ban in three instalments — 1954, 1965 and 1966 — and the island's later arguments over medical use and reform play out on [Jersey's page](/country/je/).[@2]
