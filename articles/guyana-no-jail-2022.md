On the evening of Monday 7 November 2022 Guyana's National Assembly passed the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (Control) (Amendment) Bill. It removed prison as a penalty for holding 30 grams or less of cannabis. The attorney general, Anil Nandlall, who moved the second reading, was careful about what it was not: the governing party had promised this in its manifesto, he said, and had made no promise to decriminalise marijuana.

## Three years in the making

The idea crossed two governments. In July 2019 the previous coalition's cabinet approved an amendment to end custodial sentences for 30 grams or less; the prime minister of the day, Moses Nagamootoo, said that under the existing law a small quantity brought mandatory imprisonment of up to three years. That bill was not passed. The new government tabled its own in January 2021.

## Before and after

| Amount | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 15 g | could be charged as trafficking: three to five years in prison and a fine of at least G$30,000 | simple possession; mandatory counselling for a period the counsellor decides |
| Over 15 g, up to 30 g | trafficking | community service, including public work under the Extra-Mural Work Act for up to six months |
| Over 30 g | trafficking | trafficking: the automatic threshold moved from 15 g to more than 30 g |

A person who refuses or breaches a counselling or community service order can be fined G$250,000. As tabled, the bill also removed the fine and prison term for smoking or otherwise using cannabis and for being found in a place used for it.

## The debate

The government's release gives the aims as fewer repeat offenders, less crowded prisons and a lighter load on the courts. One minister, Charles Ramson Jr., put it this way: "If we are to protect young people, we cannot punish young people by putting them in prison for a joint." The opposition coalition, Nandlall said, had hardly attended the consultation meetings.

## Why the index moved

Three scores rise. Possession goes from 1 to 2: up to 30 grams is still an offence that ends in court, but the court can no longer send the person to prison. Enforcement goes from 1 to 3, because a law that had treated 15 grams as trafficking now answers twice that amount with counselling or community work. Consumption goes from 1 to 2. Growing and sharing are unchanged, and there is no lawful supply. The total moves from 9 to 17.
