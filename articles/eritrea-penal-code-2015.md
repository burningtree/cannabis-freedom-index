Eritrea's 2015 Penal Code replaced the country's transitional criminal law and set out a
quantity-based framework for controlled drugs and plants. Its smallest personal-use offence can
still mean months in prison.

## A replacement code

Article 1 repeals and replaces the Transitional Penal Code of Eritrea. The preface presents the
new code as a break from a much older penal system, while the opening provisions preserve final
decisions and explain how pending and earlier cases should move into the new framework.

Cannabis is not named in the drug-offence chapters of this document. Instead, Book V consistently
uses the general terms “controlled drug” and “controlled plant.” That distinction matters: the
code establishes offences and penalties, but the PDF alone does not show which separate schedule
places a particular plant under control.

## Personal use remains criminal

Article 396 covers possession or cultivation of a controlled plant for personal use or
consumption. Both acts are a Class 1 petty offence. The prescribed punishment is six to twelve
months' imprisonment or a fine of 20,001 to 50,000 nakfa.

The same article puts personal possession and personal cultivation together. It does not create a
small-quantity exemption, a civil fine, or a right to grow at home. Nor does it state a numerical
threshold separating personal use from the more serious cultivation offences.

## Cultivation and supply

Cultivating a controlled plant outside the personal-use provision is a Class 8 serious offence,
punishable by three to five years. Commercial quantities bring five to seven years; large
commercial quantities bring seven to ten. Trafficking in controlled plants follows the same
three penalty bands.

“Trafficking” is drawn broadly. The code includes selling, buying, giving, transferring,
transporting, sending and delivering, as well as offers to do those things. Preparing a drug for
supply by packaging it or dividing it into units also counts. Separate provisions impose still
heavier sentences when minors are supplied or recruited.

The code therefore explains the criminal structure behind the current [Eritrea profile](/country/er/),
while leaving the underlying control schedule to other law.

In the index, 2015 does not move a score. Eritrea was already recorded as prohibiting cannabis;
the event replaces and documents the penal framework rather than establishing a newly verified
change in legal freedom.
