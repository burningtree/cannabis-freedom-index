Lebanon has grown hashish in defiance of the law for a century. When it finally made
growing legal, it took five more years to appoint anyone who could issue a licence.

## A hundred years of bans

The French mandate authorities [banned hashish](/history/lebanon-hashish-ban-1926/)
in the Bekaa valley from 1 January 1926. Independent Lebanon passed its own
[law against cultivation](/history/lebanon-cultivation-law-1946/) in 1946. Neither
stopped it: large seizures of Lebanese hashish were made in Egypt in the years that
followed.

The industry grew during the civil war of 1975 to 1990. In
[1992](/history/lebanon-eradication-1992/), under pressure from the United States,
the post-war government forbade cultivation again and sent the army into the fields.

## The fields return

Poverty brought farmers back to the crop by 2001, and in 2002 production reached
37,000 acres, double the year before. It expanded again from 2011, when the war in
Syria drew the state's attention to security.

## The 2020 law

In 2018 the speaker of parliament, Nabih Berri, said a law on medical cannabis was
being prepared, following a study of the economy by McKinsey. Not every grower was
pleased: one in the Bekaa said he feared the politicians would take the income.

On [21 April 2020](/history/lebanon-medical-cultivation-2020/) parliament legalised
growing cannabis for medical and industrial use, the first Arab country to do so.
Recreational use stayed illegal.

## The missing regulator

The law required a Cannabis Regulatory Authority to manage and issue cultivation
licences. The Council of Ministers formed the authority on 17 July 2025.

That year the prime minister, Nawaf Salam, said he had high hopes for the industry
and predicted it could bring in more than a billion dollars a year and create jobs in
areas such as the Bekaa.

The appointment moves no score for [Lebanon](/country/lb/). The scheme is for licensed
producers; for a person who uses cannabis the law is what it was.
