Benin's cannabis prohibition rests on a single law: Law 97-025 of 18 July 1997 on the
control of drugs and precursors, still the statute under which the plant is illegal.[@1]

Its shape is prohibition without exceptions. Article 8 bans growing opium poppy, coca
and the cannabis plant anywhere in the country and obliges any landowner or occupier to
destroy plants that come up.[@1] Article 9 shuts the rest of the door for Table I
substances: production, trade, transport, holding, offer, acquisition, use, import,
export and transit are all prohibited.[@1] Violations draw fines of 100,000 to 500,000
francs, doubled on repeat offending.[@1]

A 2017 review found the law fixes minimum fines and prison terms judges must stay
within, offers no alternatives to prison, and contains no public-health or human-rights
protections — while noting the death penalty, abolished in 2016, no longer applies to
any drug offence.[@2] Unauthorised possession is reported to carry two to five years with
fines up to five million francs, and cross-border movement five to ten years with a fine
of three times the value seized.[@2]

Nearly three decades on, the 1997 text still governs: no licensed production, no
medical route, and a transit trade the law was written before — seizures rose from 82
kg in 2006 to 2,444 kg in 2021.
