Cannabis became a controlled plant in what is now Indonesia under Dutch rule. In 1927 the colonial government of the Dutch East Indies adopted the Verdoovende Middelen Ordonnantie, the Decree on Narcotic Drugs. The Transnational Institute's study of cannabis in Indonesia, the source for this article, links it directly to events abroad: the decree followed the inclusion of cannabis in the international opium convention of 1925.

## What it prohibited

The decree banned the cultivation, import, export, production and use of narcotic drugs, "except for medical and scientific purposes with prior government authorisation". Its main target was opium and its derivatives. But it also prohibited growing Indian hemp, and it set out restrictions on the use, possession and distribution of cannabis.

## The penalties

| Offence | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Cultivation, and certain offences by ships' captains | a fine of 1,000 guilders or six months in prison |
| Import, export, possession, preparation, use, processing | a fine of 3,000 guilders or three months in prison |

The study describes the breaches as subject to fines "and/or short-term jail sentences".

## How long it lasted

The decree outlived the colony. After independence the new government kept the colonial regulations, and the study adds that it did so "despite the unproblematic nature of cannabis in the country". The ordinance stayed the law for forty-nine years, until the republic passed [its own narcotics law in 1976](/history/indonesia-narcotics-law-1976/).
