After Réunion became a French department in 1946, new milieus made the island
more favourable to zamal use: Comorian labourers in shanty-towns around Le
Port and Saint-Denis collected cannabis from the countryside with Mauritian
vendors, and part of the crop was clandestinely exported to Mauritius.[@1]

Alongside them, continental French youth and lycée students began smoking on
the beach or outside schools, joined by dance-orchestra musicians, prostitutes
and marginal figures; most were occasional users, though some formed groups of
habitual users.[@1]

The authorities decided to deal quietly, judging the phenomenon no social
menace.[@1] The psychiatric hospital received youthful smokers only twice: in
1971 a group initiated by members of a travelling Mauritian orchestra,
harvesting zamal in the island's eastern fields, whom the psychiatrists declined
to admit and placed under surveillance until they quit; and in 1973 a harder
case, a community of 17- to 20-year-olds led by a Frenchwoman artist, briefly
hospitalised all together then sent home under surveillance while still smoking
daily.[@1] Benoist stresses that cannabis is completely overshadowed on the
island by the far worse problem of alcoholism.[@1]
