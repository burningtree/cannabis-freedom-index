In June 1985 the Standing Committee of China's National People's Congress approved joining two
United Nations treaties: the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961, as amended in 1972, and
the Convention on Psychotropic Substances of 1971. The Single Convention lists cannabis and its
resin among the drugs it controls. With that decision, cannabis became, in Chinese law, a narcotic.

## Opium gone, heroin arriving

The government's own account begins in 1949. Within three years of the founding of the People's
Republic, it says, a mass campaign wiped out the opium that had plagued China for a century.
The problem returned at the end of the 1970s, when
heroin from the Golden Triangle began to cross China's south-western border on its way to other
markets, and China became, in the government's words, a country of consumption as well as transit.

The law followed slowly. The [Criminal Law of 1979](/history/china-criminal-law-1979/) punished
making, selling and transporting drugs, but not using or holding them. A drug administration law
of 1984 put narcotic and psychotropic medicines under special control. In 1985 China also began
working with the United States on drug enforcement.

## Joining the treaties

China [acceded to the Single Convention](/history/china-single-convention-1985/) on 23 August
1985, with one reservation, on referring disputes to the International Court of Justice. The
treaty obliges its parties to limit cannabis to medical and scientific use. It said nothing new
about the plant China grew most: hemp for fibre and seed, the [*má*](/culture/language/ma/) of the
oldest Chinese texts, was not prohibited then and has not been since. The seed stayed in the
pharmacopoeia as a [medicine](/culture/traditions/hemp-seed-in-chinese-medicine/).

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1979 | Criminal Law punishes making and trafficking drugs |
| 1985 | China joins the 1961 and 1971 UN drug conventions |
| 1987 | [First national penalty for using drugs](/history/china-public-security-regulations-1987/) |
| 1989 | [China joins the 1988 convention](/history/china-1988-convention-1989/) against trafficking |
| 1990 | [A decision names cannabis](/history/china-decision-on-narcotics-1990/) and punishes possession |

## Why the index moved

The index treats 1985 as the moment cannabis came under control in [China](/country/cn/), and
records the fall here: from 98 to 16. That is a judgment about where to put the change, not a
claim that police began arresting cannabis smokers that summer. The treaty made cannabis a
controlled drug; the penalties came in the following years, from the regulations of 1987 to the
decision of 1990, which first named cannabis in a Chinese statute and made holding a small amount
an offence.

The government's white paper of 2000 shows where its attention lay. It counts registered drug
users, seven in ten on heroin, and mentions cannabis only in its seizure figures.
