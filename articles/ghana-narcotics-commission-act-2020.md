On 20 March 2020 the Parliament of Ghana passed the Narcotics Control Commission Act. For
someone caught holding drugs for their own use, it replaced a prison sentence with a fine. The
President assented on 11 May 2020, and the Gazette carried the Act the same day. Drug use stayed
an offence.

## Five years at least

Under the [1990 law](/history/ghana-pndcl-236-1990/) every drug offence in Ghana, buying and
personal use included, carried a mandatory minimum of five years in prison, and possession ten.
A 2012 amendment set [fifteen years to life](/history/ghana-act-849-2012/) for trafficking,
whatever the drug or the amount. Writing in 2017, two researchers at the Institute for Security
Studies noted that the country's prisons were overflowing.

The government first proposed a new law in 2014. The [bill of 2017](/history/ghana-ncc-bill-2017/)
treated drug use as a public health issue and promised alternatives to prison.

## What the Act changed

| | Before | Under the 2020 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Possession for personal use | Prison, at least ten years | A fine of 200 to 500 penalty units |
| Treatment | No legal basis for harm reduction | Harm reduction allowed for the first time |
| The agency | Narcotics Control Board | A commission with its own prosecutors |

The fine came to about 2,400 to 6,000 cedis. Maria-Goretti Ane of the International Drug Policy
Consortium stressed what it did not mean: "This does not mean that drug use has been legalised."
For possession, the sentence became the fine. The Act also
provided for licensing cannabis with less than 0.3 per cent THC for industrial, medical and
research purposes.

## Buying is a different offence

Section 45 makes it an offence to buy a narcotic drug or plant for personal use, and it uses the
same Second Schedule range as buying in order to sell: a fine of 2,000 to 10,000 penalty units,
or four to ten years in prison. The default-sentence column for that row is blank. A court that
convicts someone under the personal-purchase offence may also order treatment at a facility the
Commission approves. The possession fine applies to holding the drug, and the section 45 range
applies to buying it.

## The licensing clause

That last part, section 43, did not survive long. In [July 2022](/history/ghana-supreme-court-section-43-2022/)
the Supreme Court struck it down by four votes to three, holding that Parliament had not followed
the constitution's procedure for passing it, and in [May 2023](/history/ghana-supreme-court-review-2023/)
refused to reconsider. Parliament [passed the licensing provision again](/history/ghana-licences-reinstated-2023/)
in July 2023, and the first licensing round [opened in March 2026](/history/ghana-cannabis-licensing-launch-2026/).

## In practice

Prison did not disappear. In 2022 the director-general of Ghana's prisons, Isaac Egyir, said that
"the prisons still receive persons convicted and sentenced for drug use
alone": more than four per cent of the inmates at Nsawam. A fine that cannot be paid can still
end in a cell.

Six years on, the same consortium was arguing that the reform had to "move beyond law into
practice". Its 2026 review said that stigma and ongoing criminalisation still kept people from
seeking care, and called for training judges and diverting users into treatment. "Ghana's drug
policy reform will be judged not by the ambition of its law," it concluded, "but by the strength
of its implementation."
