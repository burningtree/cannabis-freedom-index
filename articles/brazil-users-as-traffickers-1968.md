On 26 December 1968, days after Brazil's military government suspended civil liberties, it issued Decree-Law 385. One of its effects lasted eight years: a person caught with a drug for their own use was to be punished exactly like the person who sold it.

## Three steps down

The dictatorship tightened drug law three times.

| Date | Measure | President |
|---|---|---|
| 4 November 1964 | Law 4.451 hardens the penalties of the 1940 Penal Code | Castello Branco |
| 26 December 1968 | Decree-Law 385 equates the user with the trafficker | Costa e Silva |
| 29 October 1971 | Law 5.726 confirms the rule | Médici |

The Penal Code they amended dated from 1940. Its Article 281 set one to five years of reclusion and a fine, originally two to ten contos de réis; the 1968 wording fixed the fine at ten to fifty times the highest minimum wage.

After 1968 the same article reached the user. There was no smaller offence for holding a drug to take oneself.

## The reversal

The rule was undone under a fourth president. The Lei dos Tóxicos of 1976, passed in the Geisel administration, separated the two figures again. Its Article 12 set three to fifteen years for people tied to the drug trade. A user faced detention of six months to two years.

That was still prison. It took thirty more years, until the law of 2006, for prison to be removed for possession for personal use.

## What changed outside the law

A campaign for legalisation gathered strength in the 1980s and 1990s, backed by artists and liberal politicians, and marches under the name Marcha da Maconha have been held in Brazil since 2002.

## Why the index moved

The [decree-law](/history/brazil-users-as-traffickers-1968/) lowered [Brazil's](/country/br/) total from 11 to 7. The enforcement score fell from 2 to 0, the bottom of the scale, because a user now faced the trafficker's sentence with nothing lighter available. The [1976 law](/history/brazil-law-6368-1976/) brought the total back to 9 by restoring a separate, shorter term for users, and the [2006 law](/history/brazil-no-prison-for-users-2006/) raised it to 14.
