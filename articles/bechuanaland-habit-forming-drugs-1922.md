Botswana's first dated restriction on cannabis is the Habit-Forming Drug Proclamation of 1922, made for the Bechuanaland Protectorate in the same year that South Africa prohibited dagga.

## What the proclamation did

A United Nations survey of 1955, compiled with the protectorate's government, sets out the law as it then stood. The proclamation applied to the cannabis plant and to cannabis, though not to the resin or to extracts and tinctures, which fell under the pharmacy law as poisons. Possession was limited to permit holders, chemists, doctors, dentists, veterinary surgeons and people holding a prescription, and use to people who had the drug from a doctor.

A narcotics offence carried a fine of up to £100 or up to two years in prison, or both, which was lighter than the £500 and three years of the sister laws in Basutoland and Swaziland. Forfeited drugs were burned in front of a police officer.

## A small problem

The survey treats cannabis in Bechuanaland as a minor matter. The climate was too dry for the plant to grow wild, the local product was thought weak, and the territory was not a real source for South Africa.

Cannabis cases were under 2% of all prosecutions. Sentences ran from a £10 fine or a month in prison to £100 or twelve months. The plant was grown in small patches screened by bush, and when the police found one it was sometimes hard to prove whose it was. The survey names the San, whom it calls Masarwa, as the main users, bartering animal skins for it.

## Why the index moved

The [1922 proclamation](/history/bechuanaland-habit-forming-drugs-1922/) is the first step in [Botswana's](/country/bw/) score, from the unregulated state to prohibition. The second came in 2025, when the country legalised cannabis under licence. The present total is 12.
