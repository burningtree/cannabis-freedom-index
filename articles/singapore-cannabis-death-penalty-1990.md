On 15 February 1990, trafficking more than 500 g of cannabis or 200 g of cannabis resin became a capital offence in Singapore. The same amendment set 20 to 30 years and 15 strokes of the cane for 330–500 g of cannabis or 130–200 g of resin. It extended a mandatory death penalty that had applied to large-scale heroin and morphine trafficking since 1975.

## A harder line after heroin

The 1973 Misuse of Drugs Act had set a maximum of 20 years or a $40,000 fine, or both, plus 10 strokes for trafficking cannabis.[@3] In December 1975, Parliament made death mandatory for trafficking more than 30 g of morphine or 15 g of diamorphine, but left cannabis outside the capital threshold.[@4]

In 1988, the number of drug abusers arrested exceeded 6,000. The Home Affairs Minister, S. Jayakumar, cited bumper heroin production in the Golden Triangle, rising heroin seizures in neighbouring countries, the growing popularity of cannabis among local users and the risk that cocaine would follow.[@4] The amendment Bill proposed death for trafficking, importing or exporting opium, cocaine, cannabis and cannabis resin above specified quantities.[@1,2]

## Where the line fell

For cannabis, the line was more than 500 g; for cannabis resin, more than 200 g. Quantities from 330 to 500 g of cannabis, or 130 to 200 g of resin, carried 20 to 30 years and 15 strokes. The death penalty also applied to importing and exporting, not only to selling or distributing.[@1]

The 1973 Act defined trafficking broadly. It covered selling, giving, administering, transporting, sending, delivering and distributing, as well as offering to do any of those things.[@3] A person proved to have more than 15 g of cannabis or 10 g of resin was presumed to be trafficking unless they could prove otherwise.[@1]

The same schedule set death for trafficking more than 1,200 g of opium or 30 g of cocaine. The lower ranges for those drugs also carried 20 to 30 years and 15 strokes.[@1]

## Beyond the death penalty

The 1989 amendment also changed how drug cases were handled. A urine specimen was to be split into two parts and tested by two different government chemists, making a second test mandatory rather than something a suspect had to request.[@1,2] The Director of CNB could place a suspected addict under supervision for up to two years, as an alternative to sending them to an approved institution. A second or later consumption offence carried at least three years in prison.[@1,2]

## What came next

The capital threshold was [extended to cannabis mixtures in 1993](/history/singapore-cannabis-mixture-1993/) and [narrowed for some couriers in 2013](/history/singapore-death-penalty-discretion-2013/). The death penalty is still in use: [Tangaraju Suppiah was executed in 2023](/history/singapore-tangaraju-execution-2023/) for abetting trafficking of slightly more than one kilogram, and [Omar bin Yacob Bamadhaj in 2026](/history/singapore-cannabis-execution-2026/) for importing a similar amount. The [country profile](/country/sg/) records the result: a zero enforcement score and a recreational status of death.
