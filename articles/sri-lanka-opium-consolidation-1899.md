Ceylon’s Opium Ordinance of 1899 retained a direct prohibition on bhang and ganja alongside a licensed opium market. Its title can obscure its relevance to cannabis: the cannabis offences appear within the same instrument, despite “bhang” having disappeared from the short title.

## Consolidation without legalisation

Ordinance No. 5 passed the Legislative Council on **18 October 1899**, received the Lieutenant-Governor’s assent on **20 October**, and appeared in the *Government Gazette* on **27 October**. The timeline uses the assent date. A separate commencement notice was not established in this research.

Section 2 and Schedule I repealed four earlier ordinances: the Opium and Bhang Ordinance of 1878, the Colombo retail measure of 1889, the municipal and local-board measure of 1893, and the [1897 ordinance](/history/sri-lanka-import-ban-1897/). The repeal saved earlier liabilities, penalties and proceedings. It was part of replacing the statutory framework, rather than evidence that bhang and ganja became freely available.

## Cannabis offences and penalties

Section 16 expressly covered possession, sale, offers for sale and permitting sale of bhang, ganja or substances containing them. On a first conviction, the court could impose a fine up to 50 rupees, imprisonment up to three months, or both. A later conviction could bring up to 100 rupees, six months’ imprisonment, or both. The imprisonment could be simple or rigorous.

Section 17 authorized police and specified local inspectors to enter places where there was reasonable suspicion that the substances were kept, possessed or sold. It provided for seizure and taking suspected persons into custody. The police court could order destruction of the cannabis substances. Section 18 allowed an informer to receive up to half of a fine actually recovered, while section 21 imposed a three-month limit for instituting prosecutions.

## Distinct treatment of medicines

The ordinance provided licences for opium possession and trade. Its section 19 medicinal exception and section 20 hospital exemption also referred to opium. Those clauses do not supply a parallel permission for bhang or ganja in the text read here.

The [1878 framework](/history/sri-lanka-bhang-medical-exception-1878/) had expressly mentioned bhang in a medicinal exception. Establishing the full transition requires reading the intervening amendments, rather than assuming the 1899 consolidation alone introduced every difference.

This record documents continued possession and sale prohibition. It does not identify a new cultivation offence or reconstruct the separate customs rules governing imports.
