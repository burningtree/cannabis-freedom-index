On 12 July 1916, in the summer of Verdun, the French parliament unanimously passed its first drug law.
It was aimed at opium, morphine and cocaine, but it named one more drug: hashish. It was the start of
cannabis prohibition in France.

## War and the "boche poisons"

The law amended an older one, of 19 July 1845, which had regulated the sale of poisons. By the 1900s
doctors were alarmed by morphine and cocaine sold freely by druggists, and the Hague Opium Convention of
1912 obliged signatories to control these drugs. The war made the case urgent. Earlier wars had left demobilised soldiers dependent on opiates, and the
historian of pharmacy François Chast writes that the slaughter of the Marne and Verdun sealed the fate of
the legislation. The bill's
rapporteur in the Senate, Dominique Delahaye, called cocaine, morphine and heroin one of the most
dangerous invasions of "our 'friends' the boches".

Hashish, Chast notes, had been popularised in France a century earlier by Napoleon's campaign in Egypt.

## What the law said

| Article | Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Breaking the rules on poisonous substances in general | 6 days to 2 months and/or a fine of 100 to 3,000 francs |
| 2 | Breaking the rules on narcotics: opium, morphine, cocaine and hashish | 3 months to 2 years and/or 1,000 to 10,000 francs |
| 2 | Using these drugs "en société", or providing a place or means for others to use them | The same |
| 3 | Obtaining them with false prescriptions, or carrying them without a legitimate reason | The same |

Courts could close the premises where an offence took place and confiscate the drugs. Using a drug alone
at home was not itself an offence. The decree of 14 September 1916 created the offence of holding ("détention") such drugs and sorted
poisons into three tables. The
narcotics went into table B, which ended with "haschich et ses préparations"; prescriptions were limited
to seven days and could not be renewed.

## Reactions

Pharmacists resented a law that treated them as suspects. The poet Antonin Artaud wrote an open letter
to the legislator of 1916, insulting him and arguing that real addicts never bought their drugs at the
pharmacy. Chast notes that the law confused patients with addicts, doctors with suppliers and
pharmacists with traffickers.

## What followed

A law of 13 July 1922 and a decree-law of 29 July 1939 hardened the system. Until 1970, using a drug
in private stayed outside the law; the law of 31 December 1970 made using any illegal drug an offence.
[France](/country/fr/) still bans recreational cannabis.
