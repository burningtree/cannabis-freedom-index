Belarus’s Soviet-era code gained a separate offence for possessing drugs without an intention to supply on 31 October 1974.[@2] The punishment was up to three years in prison or up to two years of corrective labour.[@2] A person could therefore face a criminal sentence for handling drugs for themselves, without a completed sale.[@1,2]

## The federal decree reached personal handling

The Soviet decree adopted in April covered unlawful manufacture, acquisition, storage, transport and sending of narcotic drugs without a supply purpose.[@1] It placed those acts in a separate provision from handling drugs for sale.[@1] The personal-handling offence was consequently broader than carrying a substance in a public place: acquiring it and keeping it were expressly included.[@1]

The reproduced Belarusian code identifies the republic’s amendment of 31 October as the measure that introduced article 219-5.[@2] Its annotation distinguishes that introduction from revisions in December 1982 and July 1987.[@2] This lets the history separate the earlier federal decision from the date recorded for the republic’s own criminal provision.[@1,2]

## Later small-quantity rules must not be backdated

The consolidated federal decree includes additional paragraphs on small quantities, but the separately dated 1987 amendment shows when those paragraphs were added.[@3] First acquisition or possession of a small amount without intent to sell could bring an administrative fine up to 100 roubles or one to two months of corrective labour, with twenty per cent of earnings withheld.[@3] Exceptionally, administrative arrest could last up to fifteen days.[@3]

The [Belarusian amendment of July 1987](/history/belarus-soviet-small-possession-1987/) added a separate article for repeated small possession following an administrative penalty within a year.[@2] That offence could bring up to two years in prison, corrective labour for the same period or a fine.[@2] A first administrative case therefore did not remove the possibility of a later criminal conviction.[@2,3]

## A replacement code ended this statutory structure

The [new Criminal Code took effect in January 2001](/history/belarus-replacement-criminal-code-2001/), replacing the 1960 code and its amendments.[@4] The commencement law expressly repealed that older framework, rather than merely renumbering one of its drug provisions.[@4]

The sequence matters when reading the [Belarus profile](/country/by/): the 1974 personal-handling offence and the 1987 distinction for small quantities belong to different periods of the Soviet-era code.[@1,2,3] The 2001 commencement marks the transition to a separate replacement code.[@4]
