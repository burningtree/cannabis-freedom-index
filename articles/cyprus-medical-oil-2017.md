Cyprus announced a restricted cannabis-oil programme for patients with advanced cancer in January 2017. Health Minister George Pamboridis said it would cover people for whom other treatments had proved ineffective, with applications made directly to the ministry.

## Patients needed a decision on their own case

The announcement described oil being provided free through the ministry after approval. Authorities were looking abroad for supply because a domestic medical-growing system had yet to be introduced.

The change addressed patients already facing advanced illness and limited treatment options. It brought a controlled medical exception into a system that otherwise criminalised unauthorised cannabis possession and use.

Pamboridis also announced work on wider legislation. That opened a second question alongside immediate patient access: how Cyprus would regulate the production and supply of medical cannabis.

## The doctor prescribed and the minister approved

In July, ministry press officer Margarita Kyriakou explained the process publicly. A patient submitted a request with their treating doctor’s report. The minister could approve an individual exception and notify Pharmaceutical Services.

The patient still needed a doctor’s prescription. Pharmaceutical Services then identified the chemist from which the approved oil could be obtained.

This divided responsibility between clinical prescribing, ministerial authorisation and the pharmacy supply arrangements. Each patient’s permission was tied to their own treatment.

## Ordinary possession and use remained criminal offences

The underlying drug law continued to prohibit unauthorised cannabis acquisition, possession, cultivation and supply. Its medical exceptions depended on the relevant authorisation and regulatory conditions.

That distinction was important for patients: the announced programme offered a route through the health authorities, while cannabis bought outside that route remained subject to the ordinary law.

The government’s existing power to authorise otherwise prohibited activities supplied the legal setting for individual treatment decisions. Wider production and prescribing rules would later give the medical system more detailed requirements.

## Production rules and imported oils followed

In [March 2019](/history/cyprus-medical-expanded-2019/), Cyprus published regulations for licensed medical producers, quality controls, pharmacy preparations and prescriptions. They included separate approval arrangements for patients under eighteen.

A [government purchase of imported oils](/history/cyprus-imported-medical-oils-2021/) was documented in 2021, when Panaxia secured an order and subsequently announced its first delivery to Cyprus.

[The country profile](/country/cy/) follows this progression from restricted patient approval to a broader regulatory framework and a documented supply shipment, while keeping recreational access separate from the medical route.
