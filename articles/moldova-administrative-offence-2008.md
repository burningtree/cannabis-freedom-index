On 31 May 2009, Moldova moved small drug possession and drug use out of criminal law. The new Contravention Code, adopted the previous October, classified both as contraventions — administrative wrongs punished with a fine or community work rather than prison. It was the largest single easing in Moldova's recorded history, lifting the score from 11 to 19.

## From three years to forty hours

The starting point was the Soviet one. Since the [1974 union decree](/history/moldova-soviet-possession-crime-1974/), possession without intent to sell had been a crime carrying up to three years in prison. Article 85 of the 2009 Code replaced custody with arithmetic: illegal acquisition or keeping of small quantities without the purpose of alienation, and consumption without a doctor's prescription, draw a fine of three to ten conventional units or up to forty hours of unpaid community work. The Code says so in plain terms — the conduct, the fine band and the hours are all in the article's first paragraph. A conventional unit is twenty lei, so the money range runs from sixty to two hundred lei: a parking-ticket scale of punishment for conduct that previously risked years in prison.

## A health exit written into the law

The second paragraph goes further. Anyone who voluntarily hands over the drugs they hold illegally, or who asks a medical institution for help with their illegal consumption — or agrees to ask — escapes contravention liability altogether. Treatment-seeking and surrender are not sentencing factors here; they remove the wrong itself. A later amendment reworded the paragraph in 2013, but the exit has survived. Small-scale growing got its own contravention tier nearby: illegal cultivation without the purpose of alienation draws a fine of thirty to fifty units where the act is not a crime.

## The score it bought

The 2009 reform softened possession, enforcement and consumption together, which is why the jump is eight points. Nothing since has reversed it, and the contravention model has proved stable where neighbours swung back and forth — Lithuania recriminalised small possession in 2017, while Moldova's Article 85 still reads as written.
