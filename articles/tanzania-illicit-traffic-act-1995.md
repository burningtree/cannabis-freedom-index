Tanzania replaced its colonial drug laws with a national system covering both
the mainland and Zanzibar. The new law prohibited unauthorised cannabis growing,
trade, possession and use, while setting a separate penalty for small amounts
proved to be for personal consumption.

## Colonial controls gave way to a national commission

The earlier system included a [noxious-plants prohibition](/history/tanganyika-noxious-plants-ban-1926/)
and the [Dangerous Drugs Ordinance](/history/tanganyika-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1935/).
The new law expressly repealed both, while preserving earlier actions that
remained consistent with the replacement rules.

An interministerial commission chaired by the prime minister was given the
job of defining and coordinating national policy on drug abuse and trafficking.
Its membership brought together mainland and Zanzibar officials. The law
received assent in 1995 and took effect on 1 January 1996.

## Growing and supply faced life imprisonment or a large fine

The prohibition covered growing cannabis as well as producing, possessing,
selling, purchasing, transporting, storing, consuming, importing and exporting
controlled drugs. Medical and scientific activities had to comply with the
law and any required permission.

In the original text, the main offence group carried a fine of ten million
shillings, life imprisonment, or both. A court could impose a higher fine if
it recorded its reasons. The group reached someone who knowingly allowed a
room, premises or vehicle under their control to be used for an offence,
as well as people directly handling the drugs.

## Personal use depended on proof and quantity

The separate personal-use rule applied to a small quantity intended for the
holder's own consumption and to consumption itself. Cannabis fell within the
group carrying a 200,000-shilling fine, seven years in prison, or both under
the original wording.

The commission was to define small quantities through regulations. Someone
holding drugs had to prove that they were intended for personal consumption
and not sale or distribution. That distinction could determine which penalty
the person faced.

## Later amendments changed the penalties again

The law was amended repeatedly during the following years. In
[2015](/history/tanzania-drug-control-act-2015/), a replacement for mainland
Tanzania introduced at least thirty years for unauthorised cultivation and
life imprisonment for trafficking. Its separate small-personal-use tier
provided a fine, a three-year prison term, or both for drugs outside the
specially listed group.

A further [2017 amendment](/history/tanzania-trafficking-thresholds-2017/)
split cannabis trafficking penalties at fifty kilograms and rewrote the
conditional treatment alternative. Those successive changes shaped
[Tanzania's cannabis controls](/country/tz/).
