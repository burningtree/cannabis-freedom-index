The Marshall Islands' Prohibited Drugs Act created a nationwide cannabis ban. It commenced on 20
March 1987 and still supplies the core prohibitions, although the official text is now
consolidated with later amendments.

## Cannabis throughout the statute

The act defines marijuana broadly as any part of a cannabis plant, growing or not, including its
seeds and resin and preparations made from them. Hashish and tetrahydrocannabinol are separated
into the definition of “marijuana concentrate”; both categories are prohibited drugs.

Section 903 prohibits possessing, selling or disposing of a prohibited drug. It also prohibits
knowingly planting, cultivating, producing, manufacturing or controlling one, along with
prescribing, self-administering, importing, exporting and transporting it except where the act
provides otherwise.

## Penalties by amount and record

The consolidated section 909 gives marijuana its own penalty ladder. A first offence involving
one ounce or less carries a fine of $1,000 to $5,000, six months to one year in prison, or both.
More than one ounce on a first offence—or one ounce or less after an earlier conviction—raises
the range to $5,000–$25,000 or one to five years.

A later conviction involving more than one ounce can bring five to fifteen years. Concentrates
are subject to different thresholds and much heavier maximums. Supplying any marijuana to a
person under eighteen attracts an additional fine of $25,000–$50,000 or five to fifteen years,
or both.

These are the current consolidated bands; the document identifies amendments in 1991, 1992,
2020 and 2025, so not every present detail should be attributed to the original 1987 enactment.

## A narrow medical exception

The act exempts licensed medical practitioners handling prohibited drugs for professional work,
approved research, teaching or chemical analysis. It also recognises possession obtained under a
valid prescription. The government is the sole importer and distributor for that lawful channel.
Those clauses create controlled professional authority, not proof that patients can presently
obtain cannabis medicines in practice.

The act remains the foundation of the strict [Marshall Islands profile](/country/mh/). A later
[2025 amendment](/history/marshall-islands-drug-reporting-duty-2025/) added a public reporting duty.

In the index, the 1987 ban moves the country from an unregulated score of 58 to 6 across the six
segments. It is the Marshall Islands' only recorded score-changing cannabis event.
