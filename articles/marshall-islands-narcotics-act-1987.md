The Marshall Islands’ national narcotics law took effect on 20 March 1987. It prohibited cannabis possession, cultivation and distribution while allowing specified professional and prescription exceptions. An early marijuana prosecution under the law later produced a Supreme Court ruling on the right to a lawyer.[@1,2]

## An early possession case tested defendants’ rights

George Sakaio pleaded guilty to marijuana possession without a lawyer and received two years in prison. He immediately asked to withdraw the plea, but the High Court refused. It later also refused to let the Public Defender represent him because he had already been convicted.[@2]

In [the 1989 appeal](/history/marshall-islands-sakaio-counsel-1989/), the Supreme Court reversed the conviction and sent the case back. It required police and courts to protect access to legal assistance, and judges to ensure a defendant understood a guilty plea and any decision to give up a lawyer.[@2]

## Marijuana and concentrates have different penalty bands

Later versions of the law give ordinary marijuana a quantity-based ladder. The [2020 amendment](/history/marshall-islands-drug-asset-seizure-2020/) set out a first offence involving one ounce or less at a $1,000–5,000 fine, six months to one year in prison, or both.[@3]

More than an ounce on a first offence, or an ounce or less after an earlier conviction, carries higher penalties. Concentrates such as hashish and THC have their own weight bands. Supplying marijuana to a person under eighteen also attracts an additional penalty.[@1,3]

A January 2020 newspaper report illustrates a different outcome from Sakaio’s immediate prison sentence. Gandhi Heine received a six-month suspended sentence and a $1,000 fine after pleading guilty to a marijuana import. His lawyer said it had been intended for personal use to relieve back pain.[@4]

## Medical handling is tied to professional controls

The Act recognises licensed professional possession and possession obtained under a valid medical prescription. Practitioner licences identify the drug and exact quantity and last one year. The government is the sole importer and distributor for that controlled channel.[@1]

Practitioners must keep records of drugs received, administered or dispensed, retaining them for two years. Approved research, teaching and chemical analysis also fall within the professional exceptions.[@1]

## Later amendments widened enforcement powers

The 2020 revision expressly added cash to property liable to seizure in drug transport, import or export cases. Owners have a defined period to seek recovery through the High Court.[@3]

In [October 2025](/history/marshall-islands-drug-reporting-duty-2025/), another amendment required reports of witnessed drug activity and expressly added cannabis and concentrates to the prohibited-drug definition. The [Marshall Islands profile](/country/mh/) brings those later rules together with the ordinary possession, cultivation and supply penalties.[@1,5]
