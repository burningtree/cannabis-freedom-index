The case that settled how Switzerland treats a small amount of cannabis was a dispute about a bill for 305 francs and 30 centimes. On 11 December 2015 the police in Basel checked a man's identity and took from him 0.5 grams of marijuana and 0.1 grams of hashish. Nobody claimed he had been smoking.

## The bill

The public prosecutor opened criminal proceedings and, seventeen days later, closed them under article 19b of the Narcotics Act. The cannabis was confiscated, and the man was ordered to pay 105.30 francs in costs and a 200-franc fee. The cantonal appeal court dropped the fee but kept the costs, reasoning that possession was still generally forbidden. The prosecutor appealed to the Federal Supreme Court, asking for the full amount.

## What the statute says

| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| Article 19a(1) | consuming drugs without authorisation, or committing an offence for one's own consumption, is punished with a fine |
| Article 19a(2) | in minor cases proceedings may be dropped or a warning given |
| Article 19b(1) | preparing a negligible quantity for one's own consumption is not punishable |
| Article 19b(2) | for cannabis, 10 grams is a negligible quantity |

The 10-gram figure had been in the Act since the [2013 fixed-fine reform](/history/switzerland-fine-2013/).

## The judgment

The criminal law division decided on 6 September 2017. From the start, it said, the man had been accused only of preparing to consume. Merely possessing a small amount of drugs for consumption "falls under article 19b". The cantonal court's view that such possession was generally prohibited violated federal law.

The consequence for the bill followed. Costs can be charged to a person only if they caused the proceedings by unlawful and culpable conduct. The man had done nothing unlawful, so the costs "should not have been imposed even in part". The court added that "from the very beginning no criminal offence was made out". The prosecutor's appeal was dismissed.

## What the court left open

It did not decide whether proceedings should have been opened at all, and it did not decide whether so small an amount may be confiscated, noting that the point is disputed. Consumption is a different matter: Beobachter's summary of the case law is that a person caught using cannabis, with no more than 10 grams on them, can still be given the 100-franc fixed fine.

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 3 to 4: having up to 10 grams for one's own use is not an offence, and since this judgment not a ground for a fee either. Enforcement rises from 4 to 5, because the highest court told prosecutors that there is nothing to prosecute. Consumption stays at 2, as using remains finable. The total moves from 23 to 28.
