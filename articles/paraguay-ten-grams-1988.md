Paraguay's drug law of 1988, Law 1340, contains a single article that takes the user out of the reach of punishment. Article 30 says that whoever possesses the substances the law covers "exclusively for personal consumption, will be exempted from punishment".

## How personal use is measured

The article does not leave the question open. Personal use "will be determined by the amount of substance in possession equivalent to what is considered a daily dosage", and the article then puts a ceiling on that dose.

| Substance | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| Marijuana | 10 grams |
| Cocaine, heroin and other opiates | 2 grams |

## Who decides

The daily dose in a given case is set by doctors: a forensic doctor and a specialist named by the Ministry of Social Welfare and Public Health. The accused may add a doctor of their own choosing, at their own cost.

The wording matters for how the exemption works in practice. A person is "exempted from punishment", and the amount is established by medical opinion in each case, so the exemption is applied to someone who is already in front of the authorities. The article sets no rule that the police must leave a person with a small amount alone.

## Why the index moved

Three scores rise by one point each. Possession goes from 1 to 2: up to 10 grams for one's own use is not punished, but the exemption has to be established case by case. Enforcement goes from 2 to 3 because the law itself tells the courts not to punish the user. Consumption goes from 1 to 2 for the same reason. Cultivation, sharing and access stay where they were, since article 30 speaks only of holding a drug for personal consumption. The total moves from 11 to 17. Nothing in the scored timeline changed after that until the [medical law of 2017](/history/paraguay-medical-2017/).
