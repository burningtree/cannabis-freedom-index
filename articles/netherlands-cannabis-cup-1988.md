The first official High Times Cannabis Cup is conventionally dated to 1988 in Amsterdam. The organiser's retrospective identifies Steven Hager as its founder and describes a small competition among seed companies, with Cultivator's Choice's Skunk No. 1 taking the top award.

There is a qualification to that date. Retrospective accounts from within the cannabis industry also describe an earlier gathering in 1987. The distinction should remain visible rather than being concealed behind an apparently exact founding year.

## A small panel, rather than a mass festival

The [organiser's history](https://cannabiscup.com/welcome-amsterdam-birthplace-cannabis-cup/) describes the early event as a handful of seed companies judged by a small group of experts. Ed Rosenthal was among those invited. It associates Hager's idea with growers' harvest gatherings, where participants compared the year's crops.

That format gave the competition a different starting point from the public expos later associated with its name. It was a meeting for comparison and recognition, not yet an event in which thousands of visitors cast votes.

## Why the date is disputed

[Sensi Seeds' anniversary account](https://sensiseeds.com/en/blog/40-years-sensi-seeds/) presents Ben Dronkers's recollection of a first gathering in 1987 while recognising 1988 as the widely reported official starting year. The [World of Cannabis Museum's account of Rosenthal](https://www.worldofcannabis.museum/post/guru-of-ganja) also uses 1987 and reproduces material associated with the first Cup's magazine coverage.

These are retrospective sources with close connections to the participants and industry. They establish a disagreement in the record, rather than settling the timing of every early meeting. This history entry retains 1988 for the conventionally recognised competition while acknowledging the earlier account.

## Amsterdam's social setting

The organiser identifies the ability to buy and consume cannabis in coffeeshops as a reason for choosing Amsterdam. That setting rested on [Dutch tolerance policy](/history/netherlands-toleration-1976/), not complete legalisation of the cannabis supply chain.

The competition therefore depended on a particular social environment as well as on breeders and judges. Visitors could experience public cannabis sociability that differed from what many knew under prohibition elsewhere.

## What an award represented

A trophy identified the panel's preferred entry in a competition. It was not a clinical approval or a universal measure of a plant's quality. Awards nevertheless attached names and reputations to breeders and varieties.

The [Cannabis Cup tradition entry](/culture/traditions/the-cannabis-cup/) follows the institution beyond its beginnings. The separate [Highlife Cup at Ruigoord](/history/netherlands-ruigoord-highlife-cup-2011/) documents another competition, rather than another edition of this one.
