On 15 April 1999 Nicaragua rewrote its narcotics law. Law 285, approved 6 April and
published in La Gaceta on 15 and 16 April, replaced the 1994 Law 177 with a harsher,
more detailed regime.[@1]

## The 1999 ladder

Internal trafficking drew 5 to 20 years in prison, cultivation 5 to 15, inducing others
to consume 5 to 10, and financing 10 to 25 — all with fines running into millions of
cordobas.[@1] Prevention, treatment and rehabilitation chapters sat alongside the
penalties, so the law paired punishment with a treatment promise.[@1]

## What it replaced — and what replaced it

The 1999 text rewrote a 1964 prohibition that had made marihuana a crime against public
security.[@3] It ruled for eleven years: in 2010 Law 735 on organised crime and a new
Penal Code took over, keeping the ban but sorting users by weight — under 5 g a minor
offence, above it prison in two steps.[@2]
