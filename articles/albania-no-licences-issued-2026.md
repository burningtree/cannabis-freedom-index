Nearly three years after Albania legalised growing cannabis for medical and industrial purposes, not a single licence had been issued.[@1] The framework and the cultivation zones were in place — but the agency had authorised no grower.[@1]

## A cautious law, slowly applied

The July 2023 law created a National Cannabis Control Agency and opened licensed cultivation and processing for medical and industrial use, with products destined for export.[@2] It took the government around a year just to approve the zones where cultivation would be allowed.[@1]

Albania's restraint was deliberate and regional. North Macedonia's earlier licensing boom had collapsed into scandal: media investigations linked licences to people with criminal records in Serbia and Albania, and 26 companies lost their licences between 2021 and 2024.[@1] Tirana watched and waited.[@1]

## Export yes, patients no

The design kept the legal crop away from Albanians. Cultivation of medical cannabis is permitted and its export allowed, but domestic use for medical purposes is not.[@1] Personal possession stays criminal outside a small single dose, and unlicensed growing remains a serious crime pursued by eradication campaigns.[@1]

The result is a law that exists mostly on paper: a licensing system with no licensees, for a crop the police still burn in the highlands.[@1] It remains the base layer under [Albania's](/country/al/) cannabis scores — a legal door, three years unopened.
