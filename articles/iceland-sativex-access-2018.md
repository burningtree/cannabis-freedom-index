On 22 March 2018 the Icelandic Medicines Agency answered a question it had evidently been asked
often: yes, you can buy a medicine containing cannabis in Iceland — one medicine, on one kind of
doctor's prescription. Sativex, the THC-and-CBD mouth spray, held a marketing authorisation and
could be bought at a pharmacy on a prescription from a physician specialising in neurological
diseases.[@1]

## A door, barely open

The answer drew the boundary as carefully as it opened the door. Doctors could apply for other
THC- or CBD-containing products authorised abroad, but only by justifying why the patient could
not use a product authorised in Iceland — and in practice that meant Sativex, for the
multiple-sclerosis spasticity it is approved for.[@1] There was no herbal-cannabis programme, no
home growing, no tolerated possession: the 1974 Act's bans on importing, selling, handing over,
producing and possessing cannabis stood exactly as written.[@2]

## The narrowest route in the Nordics

The confirmation mattered because it was the first lawful way to get any cannabis product in a
country whose law otherwise bans it all. Sativex remains a prescription-only exception inside a
prohibition — bought at the pharmacy, prescribed by the neurologist, and nowhere else.[@1] For
[Iceland](/country/is/), that is what medical cannabis means.
