Law 10113 was signed at the Presidency in San José on 2 March 2022 and took effect on publication. Its long title names two things: cannabis for medical and therapeutic use, and hemp for food and industry. Article 1 gives the aim as access to cannabis and its derivatives "exclusively for medicinal and therapeutic use", in order to guarantee the right to health.[@1]

## Two plants in law

The law divides cannabis at one per cent THC by dry weight. Below that it is hemp, "non-psychoactive cannabis"; at or above it, psychoactive cannabis.

| | Hemp (under 1% THC) | Psychoactive cannabis |
|---|---|---|
| Permitted purpose | food and industry | medical and therapeutic only |
| Growing | allowed with a prior authorisation and registration | needs a licence from the Ministry of Agriculture |
| Processing | allowed | needs a licence from the Ministry of Health |

## Licences

A cultivation or processing licence runs for six years and can be renewed. A person or company may hold only one of each, a limit that extends to companies in the same group, "to avoid concentration". The government may cap the number of licences, the area planted and the regions where growing is allowed. A licensed grower may sell the crop to the social security fund, to authorised laboratories, or abroad to countries where the trade is lawful.

## The patient's side

The law authorises the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social, the public health insurer, to research, produce, buy and distribute cannabis medicines, and to put products with cannabinoids on its official lists and supply them to insured people "according to the prescription" of the treating doctor. Selling medicinal products in amounts above the prescription is an administrative offence.

The activities it authorises are listed "solely and exclusively" in article 8, and growing by patients for themselves is not among them. It gave the executive six months to write the regulations.

## The line to trafficking

Article 3 leaves everything outside the authorised limits to the general drug law, [Law 8204](/history/costa-rica-law-8204-2001/). Article 32 goes further: misusing or diverting a licence, or supplying psychoactive cannabis to unauthorised third parties, is drug trafficking and carries the penalties of article 58 of that law.

## Permission and patient supply

The law creates a medical framework. It does not by itself establish that patients could obtain THC medicines in 2022. The later registration rules require prior approval for ordinary finished products, while providing separate personal-import and pharmacy-compounding routes. Operating patient supply has not been verified, so this enactment is recorded without an access-score increase.[@2]

The [Costa Rica profile](/country/cr/) distinguishes these permissions from pharmacy stock. It retains the limited medical status and records access at zero pending evidence of a working THC supply route.
