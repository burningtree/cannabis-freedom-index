Czechoslovakia's Criminal Code No. 140/1961 took effect on 1 January 1962 and carried drug offences into the communist-era code. Unauthorised production, movement, procurement and possession of narcotic substances could all trigger criminal punishment.

## From a special act to the code

The [1938 Opium Act](/history/czechoslovakia-opium-act-1938/) had introduced a broad special-law system for controlled substances. After the Second World War, drug offences moved into the general criminal code. The 1961 code largely adopted that previous criminal-law structure rather than starting from an unregulated position.

Section 187 covered a person who, without permission, manufactured, imported, exported, procured for another or possessed narcotic substances or poisons. The basic penalty could be imprisonment for up to two years, a corrective measure or a fine.

## Higher penalties and equipment

Aggravated cases carried imprisonment from two to eight years. The legal history identifies membership in an organised group, obtaining substantial benefit and causing serious injury to multiple people or death among the circumstances supporting the higher range.

Section 188 separately punished manufacturing, obtaining or possessing an object suitable for the unauthorised production of narcotic substances or poisons. Its sanctions could also include up to two years in prison, a corrective measure or a fine.

## Personal possession was included

The wording did not exempt possession for the holder's own use. That mattered because it allowed criminal enforcement without proof that the person held the substance for someone else. Legal handling remained possible only within authorised channels.

The personal-possession element survived unchanged until the political transition. Act No. 175/1990 then amended the offence with effect from 1 July [1990](/history/czechoslovakia-decriminalisation-1990/), requiring possession to be for another person and thereby removing possession solely for oneself from criminal punishment.

## What moved in the index

The 1962 code lowered the historical [Czechia](/country/cz/) scores: possession from 2 to 1, cultivation from 2 to 1, enforcement from 3 to 2 and access from 1 to 0. Sharing stayed at 1 and consumption at 2. The reductions reflect the code's broad criminal reach and the loss of the residual authorised access represented in the preceding score state, not a claim that every act of consumption was itself a separate offence.
