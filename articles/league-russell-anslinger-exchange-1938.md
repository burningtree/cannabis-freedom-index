In June 1938, Egyptian police official [Russell Pasha](/culture/people/russell-pasha/)
challenged claims that cannabis was producing a crime wave. His intervention at the
League of Nations complicated the case advanced by American narcotics chief
[Harry Anslinger](/culture/people/harry-anslinger/), who associated marijuana with
violence and mental deterioration.

Historian Haggai Ram uses the proceedings to show disagreement within the
institutions of prohibition. Their participants were not choosing between one
settled scientific position and another.

## The American argument

The United States was not a League member, but Anslinger participated in its drug
control discussions as an observer. His interventions connected cannabis with
violent crime and demanded strict international action.

The [Marihuana Tax Act](/history/us-marihuana-tax-act-1937/) had already passed in
the United States. In Geneva, his claims carried that campaign into an institution
which had been collecting evidence about cannabis since the
[1925 convention](/history/geneva-opium-convention-1925/).

## A police chief's objection

Russell represented Egypt, whose earlier advocacy had helped bring cannabis under
international control. He had served there since 1902 and directed its Central
Narcotics Intelligence Bureau.

He had already described hashish as comparatively less harmful in a
[1930 report](/history/egypt-russell-hashish-report-1930/). In the 1938 proceedings,
he said his experience did not indicate a particular crime wave attributable to
hashish. That was an experienced official's assessment, rather than proof that
cannabis could never contribute to harm.

## The dispute with Bouquet

Russell also challenged committee expert Bouquet's explanations of cannabis
use through supposedly fixed Arab or Muslim characteristics. He objected to treating
Egyptians and Tunisians as interchangeable populations.

His alternative explanation connected Egyptian consumption with modern irrigation,
parasitic disease and peasants seeking to restore lost energy. Ram treats the move
towards historical circumstances as significant, while acknowledging that the
explanation remained questionable. It should not be adopted as a demonstrated
medical account of why people used cannabis.

## Contested knowledge, continuing control

Neither official abandoned broad generalisations about the populations under
discussion. Russell's objection also did not repeal a law or establish a new
international settlement.

Ram records the committee chairman's suggestion that Bouquet redraft his text in
consultation with Russell. The historian finds no evidence that this produced the
proposed revision. The episode shows an effort to manage disagreement as well as
an argument about evidence.

The League's cannabis work ended amid the outbreak of war. The 1938 exchange is
listed as international debate, with no national score change attributed to it.
