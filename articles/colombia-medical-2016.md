On 6 July 2016 Colombia did what no Latin American country had done at that scale: Law 1787
built a licensing system for growing, processing and exporting cannabis for medical and
scientific use, from seed to shipment.[@1]

## Four licences

The framework filled in over the following years. Decree 613 of 2017 created four licence
tracks — psychoactive cultivation, non-psychoactive (hemp) cultivation, derivative
manufacturing, and seed use — issued by the justice and health ministries; Decree 811 of 2021
and later rules refined quotas, quality and exports.[@2] Exports run under Resolution 539 of
2022 through the single-window trade platform, with licences, quotas and chain-of-custody
records required to cohere.[@2]

## Protection for the small grower

Colombia wrote the little guy into the law. Manufacturers must source at least a tenth of their
quota from small or medium licensed growers, who get priority in allocation; a 2025 decree gave
micro, small and medium cultivators two exclusive years supplying the new domestic medical
flower market.[@2]

## Oils first, flower later

Patients felt the change slowly. For years the only legal products were oils, extracts and
tinctures; only in October 2025 did Decree 1138 authorise pharmacies to dispense dried flower
on prescription.[@2] The 2016 law made [Colombia](/country/co/) an exporter before it made
cannabis medicine Colombians could smoke — industry first, flower nine years later.
