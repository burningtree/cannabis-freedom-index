The Maldives' drug law began with one arrest. Two officials of the country's rehabilitation service, writing for a United Nations training institute in Japan, put it this way: "Official recognition of the problem came in 1977 when a person was arrested with 350 grams of hashish." Law No. 17/77, the Law on Drugs, was passed the same year, "in order to help the legal system deal with it, and to act as a deterrent".

## Before and after tourism

The authors say drug abuse "was not a problem before the mid 1970s". There were stories of opium use earlier in the century, limited to small numbers of people. The change coincided with the arrival of tourism in the early 1970s, when hundreds of visitors began to land daily, though the authors warn against reading that as cause and effect: Maldivians were also travelling abroad more, and drug use was rising worldwide.

## What the law covers

| Conduct (Section 2, as later amended) | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Planting, producing, importing, exporting | Life imprisonment |
| Selling, buying, giving | Life imprisonment |
| Possession with the intention to sell | Life imprisonment |
| Being an accomplice in any of these | Life imprisonment |

Sections 2 and 3 also prohibit manufacturing prohibited drugs in any form. The authors note that no illicit drug was being produced in the country; the problem was the smuggling of opiates and cannabis derivatives from the region, and their use.

## What followed

The law did not deter. By 1996 police recorded 241 cases of substance abuse. In 2002 the authorities reported that drug offences had become "the most frequent one faced by the Maldivian criminal courts", after a 200% increase. A rehabilitation centre opened in 1997 on a small island, taking people sentenced by a court to treatment.

The direction has not changed since. Amendments that took effect on 7 March 2026 allow prosecutors to seek the death penalty for trafficking or importing more than 350 grams of cannabis, the same weight that started the law in 1977, if the Supreme Court bench is unanimous. Until then the maximum was life imprisonment, defined as 25 years.

## Why the index moved

The index has a single scored event for the Maldives. It takes Law 17/77 as the start of prohibition and lowers every score to the bottom of the scale in one step. The source describes a law that punished growing, buying, giving and selling alike, and a country with no lawful source of cannabis at any point since.
