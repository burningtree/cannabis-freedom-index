Mozambique's first dated restriction on cannabis is Portaria No. 2,054 of 19 September 1914, an order of the Portuguese colonial government. It came a year after a similar order in [Angola](/history/angola-portuguese-ban-1913/), and it was still the text under which cannabis cases were prosecuted more than forty years later.

## What the order did

The order itself has not been read for this index. A United Nations survey of 1957, built on information supplied by Portugal, describes it: growing the cannabis plant and all trade in it were prohibited, and so were importing it and selling it. The plant was known locally as bange, bangue or suruma.

The penalties the survey records were set by who the offender was.

| Offender | Penalty for an offence involving the plant, cannabis or its resin |
|---|---|
| In general | a fine of up to 100 escudos, imposed by the administrative authorities |
| "Indigenous inhabitants" | correctional imprisonment of up to 120 days, or a fine of 1 escudo a day in its place |

The survey notes that the order did not name possession. It reads the bans on importing, growing, consuming and selling as prohibiting possession indirectly.

## The 1941 regulations

On 24 December 1941 the colony adopted Regulations for the Practice of Pharmacy. They state that the importation, cultivation, sale and consumption of Cannabis sativa are absolutely prohibited. Extracts and tinctures were treated as medicines: pharmacies could import and dispense them on prescription, and dealing in them without authority carried six months to a year in prison and a fine of 5,000 to 10,000 escudos.

## How it was enforced

Portugal told the United Nations little. The plant grew wild throughout the territory, and small cultivated patches were sometimes found. Control lay mainly with the administrative authorities, because their work brought them into closest contact with the African population; clandestine plots were burned, and growers faced "severe penalties, including even imprisonment". No seizures of any narcotic were reported for 1953 to 1955, and no figures for prosecutions were given. South Africa and Swaziland both reported cannabis coming in from Mozambique.

## Why the index moved

The [1914 order](/history/mozambique-portaria-2054-1914/) is the only scored step in [Mozambique's](/country/mz/) history, from the unregulated state to prohibition. The colonial rules were replaced by Law 3/97 of 1997, which punishes use with up to two years in prison. The country's present total is 11.
