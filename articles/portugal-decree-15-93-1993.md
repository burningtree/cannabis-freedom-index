Portugal’s replacement drug law kept personal cannabis use and possession criminal in 1993, with an ordinary maximum of three months in prison or a fine of thirty days.[@1] A larger personal quantity carried a higher maximum, while occasional users could have punishment waived in the ordinary tier.[@1,2]

## Treatment could replace serving a sentence

The law covered consuming drugs and growing, acquiring or holding them for personal use.[@1] Courts could suspend a dependent user’s sentence on condition that the person voluntarily accepted treatment or admission to an appropriate institution.[@1] Unjustified failure to follow those conditions could bring the ordinary consequences of breaching a suspended sentence.[@1]

The government linked its replacement law to Portugal’s ratification of the 1988 international trafficking convention, including measures against criminal profits and international trafficking.[@1] Personal use nevertheless received its own provisions, separate from the supply offences.[@1]

## A correction changed the printed quantity reference

The text published on 22 January referred to a five-day personal supply in the higher-penalty paragraph.[@1] The [20 February correction](/history/portugal-consumption-correction-1993/) changed that to three days.[@2] Above the corrected amount, the maximum was one year in prison or a fine of 120 days.[@1,2]

The law specified commencement thirty days after publication, putting its operative date on 21 February.[@1] Reading the publication together with the correction explains why the first printed quantity differs from the rule that governed commencement.[@1,2]

## Decriminalisation came eight years later

The [2001 reform](/history/portugal-decriminalisation-2001/) moved personal use, acquisition and possession into administrative proceedings and repealed the 1993 consumption offence except for cultivation.[@3] Commissions could assess dependence and circumstances, with provisional suspension required for qualifying first proceedings.[@3]

That reform also distinguished the available sanctions: dependent users received non-monetary measures, while a non-dependent person could receive a fine or a non-monetary sanction.[@3] Cannabis continued to be seized when authorities intervened under that system.[@3]

## Personal growing stayed criminal

The [2023 clarification](/history/portugal-personal-use-quantity-2023/) expressly retained the personal-growing offence, with up to three months in prison or a thirty-day fine.[@4] It also made proven exclusive personal possession administrative even above the ten-day reference amount.[@4] The [Portugal profile](/country/pt/) follows those later rules alongside the remaining cultivation and supply offences.[@3,4]
