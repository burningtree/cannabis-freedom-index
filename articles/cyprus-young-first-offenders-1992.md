Cyprus introduced a one-year prison ceiling in March 1992 for young defendants facing a first drug conviction involving exclusively personal use. The protection applied to people no older than twenty-five and required their cases to be heard through the summary procedure.

## A special limit sat inside a wider drug law

Cyprus had adopted its national [drug law in 1977](/history/cyprus-narcotics-act-1977/), covering possession, cultivation, supply and use. Its rules allowed regulated exceptions but treated unauthorised conduct as criminal.

The 1992 amendment revised several parts of that system. It expanded the wording of supply offences, added controls over equipment and substances used to produce drugs, and replaced the penalty table.

Within that broader revision, parliament created a particular protection for young people whose offending concerned only their own drug use. The practical consequence was a lower ceiling on the prison sentence a court could impose in a qualifying case.

## Eligibility depended on age and previous convictions

The rule covered a defendant aged no more than twenty-five who had no previous conviction under the drug law. Their alleged offence also had to concern exclusively personal use.

For someone who met those conditions, the case went through summary trial and the court could impose no more than one year in prison. The wording limited punishment after a conviction; it continued to treat the underlying conduct as an offence.

The personal-use condition mattered. A supply allegation brought a different question from possession or use for oneself, even where a defendant was young and had never been convicted before.

## The ordinary offences continued alongside it

The same amendment described supplying, distributing, selling or handing over controlled drugs on any terms as criminal conduct. It also extended the possession wording to buying or otherwise obtaining drugs.

Cannabis growers remained subject to a separate prohibition on cultivation and planting. A personal-use sentencing rule therefore had to be read alongside the actual offence charged and the conditions parliament attached to the protection.

This made the distinction between a user and a supplier consequential for a young person entering the criminal courts.

## Parliament doubled the ceiling in 2003

The [2003 amendment](/history/cyprus-personal-supply-thresholds-2003/) replaced the one-year rule with a two-year ceiling for qualifying personal-use offences committed before the offender turned twenty-five.

It also introduced quantity-based presumptions of supply, including thirty grams of cannabis or three plants. [Cyprus’s current profile](/country/cy/) distinguishes that later two-year protection from the much higher general penalties for cultivation and supply.
