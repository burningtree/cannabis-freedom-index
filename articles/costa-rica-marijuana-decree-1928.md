Costa Rica banned marijuana on 24 October 1928 by Executive Decree No. 5. It did so as a side effect of a scare about another drug. The sociologist Hubert Blanco Lizano, whose 1996 article in the University of Costa Rica's social science review is the source here, treats the episode as the country's first "moral panic" over drugs.

## A country without drug laws

Until then, Blanco writes, Costa Rica had prohibited the use, possession or production of no drug at all. The exceptions were the state monopoly on spirits and a 1907 decree on paperwork for opium imports that was never applied. Opium came into the country freely at the start of the century, and cocaine and morphine were used.

Cannabis was already present. Blanco cites references to marijuana use going back to 1860, and a late nineteenth-century survey of plants along the San José–Limón railway that found fields of cannabis near the Atlantic coast, planted by imported labourers.

## The heroin scandal

At the end of the 1920s heroin spread among wage workers in the south-west of San José; Blanco gives an estimate of 250 to 500 users. Importing and distributing the drug was still lawful, and late in 1928 some merchants set up a sales network in the popular districts. That produced what he calls the "public scandal", in which the press tied drug use to theft and prostitution.

## Three decrees

| Decree | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| No. 3 | 15 March 1927 | regulated the use of opium and its derivatives |
| No. 4 | 24 October 1928 | prohibited heroin and its preparations |
| No. 5 | 24 October 1928 | prohibited marijuana |

The state also set up a commission with the College of Medicine and Pharmacy. It decided to end legal private imports of drugs and to create a state monopoly, run by a narcotics board, to import and distribute the drugs that were needed. Heroin and cocaine were then seized from pharmacies and from people who distributed or used them. A further law in 1930 added penalties for trafficking.

Blanco's article gives the decree's effect in three words, "prohibited marijuana", and does not reproduce its text or its penalties.

## Why the index moved

This is the first scored step in Costa Rica's timeline. Before it the plant was unregulated; after it, it was prohibited. Possession, cultivation, sharing and consumption each fall from 10 to 1, enforcement from 10 to 2 and access from 8 to 0. The total moves from 98 to 11.
