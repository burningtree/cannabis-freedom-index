Reports in October 2013 announced that Romania had allowed medical marijuana.[@1] A contemporary account quoted the country's drug and medicines agencies denying a new legalisation and saying no cannabis medicine had been approved for the Romanian market.[@1]

## Agencies challenge the reports

Castle Craig clinic contacted the two agencies to check the reports.[@1] Drug-agency director Sorin Oprea denied a legislative change.[@1] Medicines-agency spokesperson Anca Crupariu said the manufacturer of Sativex had not sought Romanian authorisation.[@1] Romania Insider published the clarification on 10 October.[@1]

## Medical controls already existed

Romania's [2005 controlled-substances law, effective in 2006](/history/romania-cannabis-medicine-controls-2006/), already listed cannabis, resin, extracts and tinctures among substances with recognised medical interest under strict control.[@2] The law permitted their handling within its authorisation system.[@2]

For a patient, the relevant permission concerned pharmaceutical preparations made industrially or in a pharmacy.[@2] Medical use required a prescription, and patients could hold the quantity prescribed.[@2] The law also regulated the growers and businesses involved in supplying controlled medicines.[@2]

The prescription rules controlled dispensing as well.[@2] Medicines in the stricter medical schedule required special secure forms, and giving out controlled preparations without a prescription was prohibited except under the law's exemptions.[@2] Unused controlled medicines were subject to destruction procedures.[@2]

## Earlier licences had to be replaced

The legislation also recognised an older authorisation system.[@2] Licences issued under the 1969 drug law could remain valid for at most three years after the new law took effect.[@2] They then had to be exchanged under the new rules.[@2]

The 2005 law therefore addressed a regulated chain of growers, businesses, prescribers and pharmacies.[@2] For patients, access depended on a pharmaceutical preparation reaching that chain and a doctor prescribing it under the controlled-medicine rules.[@2]

## A later proposal seeks clearer protection

In May 2023, deputies proposed exempting medically prescribed cannabis possession from the penalties for personal use.[@3] Their bill also requested a special medical-cannabis law and proposed administrative fines for possession of up to three grams for recreational personal use.[@3]

The Senate rejected the proposal that September and sent it to the Chamber of Deputies.[@4] Its lower-house committees produced a rejection report in December.[@4]

The [Romanian proposal](/history/romania-three-gram-proposal-2023/) connected patient protection with a broader effort to change how the criminal law treats people holding cannabis for themselves.[@3]
