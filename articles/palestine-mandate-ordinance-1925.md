Palestine got its first full drug law in 1925. The Dangerous Drugs Ordinance arrived as the local confirmation of that year's Geneva Opium Convention, modelling itself on the metropolitan laws — Britain's 1920 Dangerous Drugs Act and the 1925 Act.[@1]

## Manufacture, import, export, possession

The ordinance outlawed the manufacture, import, export and possession of the convention drugs: opiates, coca, cocaine and their derivatives — and finally, Indian hemp, mentioned for the first time in a Palestinian statute.[@1] It turned the military government's 1919 hashish warning, the first anti-hashish reference in the country, into a codified ban.[@1]

## Amended three times, enforced never

The law was amended in 1928, 1932 and 1936, each time on the Opium Advisory Committee's recommendations and each time raising trafficker penalties — the 1936 round adding the plainly stated transit offence and the Article 14 assault on smoking premises, covered in its own article.[@1] Yet the whole edifice stayed a dead letter: Palestine lacked the enforcement infrastructure, the funds and the will, hashish policing ranked below immigration control and communal tensions, and the demoralised police found smuggling "an annoyance and a burden".[@1]

## The corridor

Through it all the geography dictated the trade: hashish moved south from Lebanon and Syria through Palestine to Egypt along routes no mandate boundary could close.[@1] The [country page](/country/ps/) follows the ordinance from confirmation to the Mandate's end.
