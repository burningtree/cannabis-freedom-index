In 1546, François Rabelais published the *Third Book* of his Pantagruel stories in Paris. Near its end, the narrative celebrates an extraordinary plant called Pantagruelion. Martin Booth identifies it with hemp in *Cannabis: A History*, placing a familiar agricultural material within the history of European literature.

The Bibliothèque nationale de France identifies the first edition as the work of printer Chrétien Wechel. The episode belongs to Renaissance fiction, long before the modern division between industrial hemp and controlled cannabis products.

## A plant for a voyage

Pantagruel prepares ships for a journey and takes supplies of the herb. The description emphasises its tall stalk, leaves, seed and valuable fibres. The following chapter describes soaking the stems and separating fibre from the woody material.

Those details explain why the passage interests historians of hemp. Its central material is the stalk and what can be made from it, rather than an account of smoking hashish.

## Useful fibre, dangerous rope

The praise ranges across ordinary work: textiles, bags, writing materials, fishing and navigation. Rabelais also gives the plant a darker application in a joke about the hangman's rope. A useful material could support both travel and punishment.

The episode enlarges practical craftsmanship into extravagant comedy. Its imaginative scale should not turn the narrator's every botanical or medicinal assertion into an observed fact.

## A literary source, not a prohibition record

Booth's chapter places Rabelais among European writers, herbalists and travellers who knew different uses of cannabis. The comparison is useful because the same plant could appear as fibre, a remedy or an unfamiliar intoxicant, depending on who described it.

A literary narrator, a medical writer and a traveller do not supply the same kind of evidence. Rabelais’s familiarity with hemp does not prove that he consumed it for intoxication, and the extravagant praise should not be mistaken for an account of drug-taking.

## What the episode preserves

Pantagruelion gives cannabis history an example of hemp as a material worth celebrating in its own right. The subject is connected to the older meaning of [hemp](/culture/language/hemp/), encompassing rope, cloth and useful fibre.

Its appearance in 1546 is a literary publication, not a change in French cannabis law. No score change follows from it.
