Ceylon’s Opium and Bhang Ordinance of 1897 prohibited more than importing cannabis. The original text also created offences for possessing and selling bhang or ganja and removed cannabis from important parts of the earlier licensed trade.

## The instrument and its date

Ordinance No. 9 of 1897 was passed by the Legislative Council on 12 November, assented to on 15 November and published in the government gazette on 26 November. This entry uses the assent date. A separate commencement date was not established from the material inspected.

The ordinance’s title coupled an increase in opium import duty with prohibition of bhang and ganja imports. Its operative provisions went further than that title alone suggests.

## What the law covered

Section 2 defined bhang through dried hemp leaves, whether from male or female, cultivated or uncultivated plants, and included substances containing it. Ganja was defined through dried flowering tops of cultivated female hemp plants bearing resin under the conditions described in the statute; substances containing ganja were included too.

Section 6 added bhang, ganja and substances containing either to Schedule C of the 1869 customs ordinance. The text and its marginal heading identify this as an import prohibition. It should not be generalized into a ban on every hemp material without checking the statutory definitions.

## Possession, sale and enforcement

Section 7 penalized possession, sale, offering for sale and allowing sale of either drug. For a first conviction, the maximum was fifty rupees, three months of simple or rigorous imprisonment, or both. Subsequent convictions could bring up to one hundred rupees, six months, or both.

Under section 8, police officers or municipal inspectors could enter and inspect shops or places where there was reasonable cause to suspect prohibited possession or sale. They could seize the drugs and their containers and take suspected possessors or sellers into custody. Section 10 allowed a court to award an informer up to half of a fine actually recovered.

## Ending parts of the licensed market

The repeal schedule deleted bhang references from specified provisions of the 1878, 1889 and 1893 licensing ordinances. This connects the prohibition directly to the earlier [Colombo licences](/history/sri-lanka-colombo-bhang-licences-1893/) and [municipal licensing framework](/history/sri-lanka-municipal-bhang-licensing-1893/).

A description that places the entire transition from licensing to prohibition in 1905 overlooks these 1897 offences.
