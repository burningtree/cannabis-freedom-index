In August 2021 two Namibian cannabis users asked the Windhoek High Court to declare
the ban on adult possession and use unconstitutional. Brian Jaftha — president of
Ganja Users of Namibia and of the Rastafari United Front — and GUN secretary general
Borro Ndungula sued the government, the prosecutor general, the police chief and two
ministers, through lawyer Kadhila Amoomo.[@1]

## The claim

The two men said they were themselves in the dock — Jaftha facing charges in two
matters, Ndungula in eight — and argued the criminalisation of cannabis "is
irrational and unjustified and unjust", violating dignity, equality, freedom from
discrimination, privacy, administrative justice, and culture and religion.[@1] The
ban, they claimed, owed its existence to "outdated, unfounded and false convictions"
about cannabis, "motivated in part by a now defunct racist political agenda" against
the Rastafari religion — and could not be scientifically, morally or rationally
justified.[@1] Cannabis, they alleged, had no rational basis for being classed with
nicotine, alcohol or opiates.[@1]

## The leader goes to jail first

While the case crawled, Jaftha was sentenced to two years in prison in October 2023
for cannabis possession, about five years after his arrest.[@2] He said the plant
material was never tested and the investigating officer had died and could not be
cross-examined; Amoomo — who had not represented him in the criminal trial — said his
firm would handle the constitutional case for free, calling Jaftha "just the latest
victim" of a law subjecting many Namibians to prosecution.[@2] The challengers' demands
grew to include removing cannabis from the prohibited schedules, amnesty for
prisoners serving cannabis sentences, and expunging cannabis records.[@2]

## Dismissed as premature

The government's answer was process, not substance: the justice minister had approved
a Law Reform and Development Commission review of the cannabis laws, so the claim was
premature and "not ripe for judicial adjudication".[@3] In July 2026 Judge Claudia
Claasen agreed and upheld the state's special plea. Cannabis policy — legalise or
keep the ban — is primarily for the executive and legislature, she held; the dispute
spans harm, values, addiction, cost and policing, and courts should not interfere
while the government is reviewing the legislation.[@3] She did not decide whether the
ban is constitutional.

A parallel attack fared no better. Juvani Swartbooi, sentenced to 12 months for
dealing in 2021, challenged the rule that holding more than 115 g presumes dealing;
Deputy Judge President Hannelie Prinsloo dismissed that bid in 2026 too.[@4]

[Namibia's profile](/country/na/) reflects the law the challengers failed to move:
the [1971 Act](/history/namibia-south-african-act-1971/) intact, the [115 g
presumption](/history/namibia-dealing-presumption-upheld-2026/) intact, and the
[2021 challenge](/history/namibia-constitutional-challenge-2021/) ended, for now, on
prematurity.
