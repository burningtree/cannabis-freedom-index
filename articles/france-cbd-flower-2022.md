On 29 December 2022, France's Conseil d'État annulled a blanket prohibition on selling unprocessed flowers and leaves from low-THC cannabis varieties. The ruling concerned a specific part of a ministerial order, rather than general legalisation of cannabis.

Its central question was whether public health and enforcement concerns justified banning those materials altogether. The court required the restriction to be supported by evidence and proportionate to the risk.

## A distinction inside the order

The order allowed qualifying flowers and leaves to supply extracts while prohibiting their sale in unprocessed form to consumers. The legal dispute was therefore about different treatment of material from the same permitted varieties, not a proposal to abandon narcotics control.

The judgment dealt with the prohibition challenged by the applicants. Annulment removed that provision; it did not replace the whole regulatory system with unrestricted commercial access. Cultivation conditions and other product rules remained separate questions.

## Public health as a justification

In its published explanation, the court distinguished CBD from THC and assessed the evidence presented by the parties. It concluded that the risks shown for the low-THC materials did not justify a general and absolute marketing ban.

That is a legal conclusion about the justification for a restriction. It should not be expanded into a promise that every CBD product is harmless, clinically effective or suitable for every consumer. The evidentiary question in the case was narrower than those claims.

## Could police tell the difference?

The government also relied on the resemblance between low-THC flowers and illicit cannabis. The court found that quick, inexpensive THC testing could distinguish varieties, and rejected appearance alone as sufficient reason for prohibiting their marketing.

The ruling separated a difficulty of recognition from a justification for banning the object being recognised. Sellers and consumers were not required to accept an absolute prohibition merely because lawful and unlawful material could look alike.

## The scope of the decision

The official judgment records annulment of the challenged prohibition and the rejection of other claims. This is why the timeline's shorthand “legalises CBD flower” needs the fuller explanation: the mechanism was judicial review of an administrative restriction.

The decision belongs to [France's](/country/fr/) history of differentiating cannabis products by their properties and legal uses. It did not authorise a recreational market in high-THC cannabis. Its significance was to require the government to justify the boundary it had drawn around low-THC flowers and leaves.
