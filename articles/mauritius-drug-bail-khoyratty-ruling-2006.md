On 22 March 2006 the Privy Council threw out the State's appeal and restored
judges' power over bail in drug cases.[@1]

Abdool Rachid Khoyratty had been charged with possessing 3 grams of heroin for
the purpose of selling under the Dangerous Drugs Act 2000 — and police objected
to bail under a constitutional and statutory bar meant to take the decision out
of judges' hands.[@1]

The Board held the bar unconstitutional: Parliament, acting in its constituent
capacity, had altered the basic democratic form of the State — notably the
separation of powers — without the special deep-entrenchment procedure of a
referendum and unanimous Assembly vote.[@1] Section 5(3A) of the Constitution
and section 32 of the Dangerous Drugs Act fell with it.[@1]

The ruling restored judicial consideration of bail rather than requiring release
in every case — and it reached cannabis offences within the wider drug-case
framework.[@1]
