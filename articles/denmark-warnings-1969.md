Denmark tightened its drug law in 1969 and, in the same breath, decided not to use it against people who smoked cannabis. The arrangement lasted more than thirty years. This account follows the Transnational Institute's country report on Denmark, which draws on the work of the Danish researchers Esben Houborg, Vibeke Asmussen Frank and Kim Møller.

## Two laws

| Law | Use |
|---|---|
| Euphoriant Substances Act, [1955](/history/denmark-euphoriants-act-1955/) | minor offences, punishable with up to two years in prison |
| Penal Code, section 191, 1969 | organised sale and possession of larger amounts |

Neither text distinguishes cannabis from other illegal drugs.

## The condition

Section 191 was passed at a time of public and political concern about young people's cannabis use, in the belief that tighter law would cut the supply reaching them. It was aimed at suppliers. The political leaders of the day did not favour legalising cannabis, the report says, but they were worried that stricter sentences would further criminalise users. So the section was approved "on condition that law enforcement would distinguish between cannabis and other drugs, as well as between users and sellers".

## What followed

In practice, for over thirty years, the police and prosecutors stayed "relatively lenient towards cannabis users, and to some degree also towards sellers". The report states the result plainly: possession of cannabis for one's own use, up to 10 grams, "was not penalised before the early 2000s". The usual answer was a warning. The street market in the Freetown Christiania, founded in 1971, was not regularly policed apart from occasional raids.

The law itself never changed in the user's favour. Possession, production, import and sale all remained illegal throughout. The leniency lived in enforcement practice, which is why a later government could end it without repealing anything, as it did in [2004](/history/denmark-fines-2004/).

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 1 to 3, because holding a small amount for one's own use stopped being punished. Enforcement rises from 2 to 5, the biggest part of the change, since the policy was a decision about what the police and prosecutors would do. Cultivation, sharing and consumption stay at 1, and there was no lawful access. The total moves from 11 to 22.
