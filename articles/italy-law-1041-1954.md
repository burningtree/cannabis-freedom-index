Italy's Law 1041 put unauthorised possession of listed narcotics into the same
penalty range as purchase, sale and several other dealings. Dated 22 October
1954, it was published on 12 November and took effect on 27 November.

Its importance lies in the breadth of Article 6. Holding drugs no longer had
to be described in that provision as possession for sale: unauthorised possession
itself fell within an offence carrying years in prison. The law nevertheless
maintained a regulated system for production and medicinal distribution.

## One provision, several kinds of conduct

Article 6 prescribed three to eight years' imprisonment and a fine of 300,000
to four million lire for unauthorised purchase, sale, transfer, import, export,
transit, procurement for others, use or possession of listed substances.
It contained no exception for possession intended for personal use.

This is the basis for describing the law as treating users and dealers alike.
More precisely, those forms of conduct shared a statutory penalty range.
The text does not establish that every defendant received the same sentence,
and it provided other penalties for other offences, including unlawful commerce
by authorised operators.

## Cannabis within a listed system

Article 3 instructed the health authority to compile and publish a narcotics
list, taking international conventions into account and consulting the superior
health council. Article 6 linked its controls to that list.

Cannabis also appeared expressly in the law's administrative machinery.
Article 8 required authorised producers to report on materials and outputs;
for Indian hemp, as for raw opium and coca materials, reports had to identify
the content of active narcotic principles. The law governed an authorised
supply chain alongside its criminal prohibitions.

## Medical access and coercive intervention

Article 6 exempted pharmacies from its authorisation requirement for specified
medicinal transactions. Article 16 imposed prescription, identification and
recordkeeping requirements on pharmacists. That exception therefore did not
remove medicinal distribution from control.

Article 21 also provided for confinement following medical assessment where
habitual drug abuse caused serious mental disturbance and specified danger or
public-scandal conditions. This continued the coercive approach visible in the
[1934 decree](/history/italy-narcotics-decree-1934/), within a differently worded
legal framework.

## The framework's later replacement

The [1975 reform](/history/italy-modest-quantity-1975/) replaced most of Law
1041. In [Order 224/1976](/history/italy-confinement-case-returned-1976/), the
Constitutional Court recorded the repeal, including the exception concerning
the central narcotics office, and returned a confinement case for reconsideration.

For [Italy's history](/country/it/), the 1954 law marks the breadth of a criminal
possession rule. Its text establishes the penalty and regulatory structure;
actual patterns of cannabis prosecution require separate evidence.
