On 1 December 2018, a regional drug-prosecution guideline took effect for Curaçao,
Sint Maarten and the Caribbean Netherlands. Its cannabis table combined seizure
with the possibility of dismissal for small quantities, while retaining
exceptions and a separate approach to cultivation.

It replaced a guideline dated 1 July 2015. The 2018 document therefore records
a particular version of prosecution policy, rather than proving the first
introduction of leniency in these jurisdictions.

## A shared instruction

The prosecutor-general addressed the chief prosecutors of Curaçao,
[Sint Maarten](/history/sint-maarten-prosecution-guideline-2018/)
and Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba. The document identified itself as an
instruction under the statutory framework for the public prosecution service.

Its tables concerned the demands prosecutors should make. They should be
distinguished from the underlying offences and from the sentences courts
ultimately impose. The general discussion described quantity as a guide that
could be supplemented by evidence about role, duration of dealing and other
circumstances.

## The cannabis table

In the 0–25 g band, the table called for seizure and dismissal, which could
be conditional. The next band, 25–50 g, specified a fine demand up to NAF 500,
with a corresponding US-dollar figure for the jurisdictions using that currency.
From 50 g, it referred to half the demand in the cocaine/heroin table.

These were enforcement bands within a prohibition framework. They did not
create a lawful retail market or a legal allowance to keep cannabis without
the risk of seizure.

## Circumstances could override the table

The small-quantity responses had express exceptions. These included another
offence, repeat offending, indications of dealing and cases involving minors.
The footnote also named special settings, including carnival processions,
workplaces, government buildings and detention facilities.

The document's broader provisions allowed increased demands for repeat offences.
Consequently, weight alone did not settle how prosecutors should handle a case.

## Cultivation remained separate

A dedicated passage stated that cannabis cultivation was prohibited and that
grow cases required individual assessment. It identified plant numbers,
location, professionalism and involvement as relevant considerations.

The personal-quantity table therefore supplied no equivalent small-plant
allowance. Nor should it be assumed that a kilogram or gram category could
simply be converted into a permitted number of plants.
