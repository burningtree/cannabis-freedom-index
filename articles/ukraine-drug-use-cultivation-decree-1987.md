A small amount of cannabis could lead to a fine, corrective labour or, exceptionally, fifteen days’ administrative arrest under Soviet Ukraine’s decree issued on 21 August 1987. Repeating small personal possession or drug use without a prescription within a year after an administrative penalty could then bring up to two years in prison.[@1]

## A second offence could become a crime

The decree followed a June Soviet Union decision and described its purpose as stopping the spread of drug addiction and substance misuse. It amended both the republic’s criminal code and its administrative offences code, creating a sequence of penalties for people caught again.[@1]

Under the administrative rule, a fine could reach 100 roubles. Corrective labour could last one to two months, with twenty per cent of earnings deducted. Administrative arrest was reserved for exceptional cases where those penalties were considered insufficient.[@1]

After an administrative penalty, repetition within a year exposed a person to a criminal charge. The court could impose up to two years in prison, corrective labour for the same period or a fine of up to 300 roubles. The rule covered small acquisition or possession without intent to sell, as well as use without a doctor’s prescription.[@1]

## Growing depended on the variety

The law divided cannabis plants into two groups. Growing expressly prohibited Indian, southern Manchurian, southern Chuy, southern Arkhon and southern Krasnodar hemp carried up to five years in prison directly. Repetition or specified previous drug crimes could raise that to eight years.[@1]

For other hemp, unlawful sowing or growing brought a warning or a fine of twenty to 100 roubles. Repeating it within a year after an administrative penalty could bring up to three years in prison or two years of corrective labour. Certain previous drug crimes exposed growers to up to eight years.[@1]

The distinction extended the system that [Ukraine had written into its criminal code in 1974](/history/ukraine-personal-possession-prison-1974/). That earlier decree already punished personal possession and named several prohibited hemp varieties.[@2]

## Surrender and medical help offered exemptions

Voluntarily surrendering drugs could exempt a person from specified acquisition, possession, transport and sending offences. Voluntarily seeking medical help for non-medical drug use could also remove liability for use and for specified handling of the drugs consumed. The administrative rule contained related exemptions.[@1]

The health rules also allowed compulsory hospitalisation for up to ten days to examine someone suspected of non-medical drug use who refused an examination. Treatment and punishment sat alongside each other in the decree.[@1]

Later laws changed the boundaries again, including Ukraine’s [2009 rewrite of small-grow penalties](/history/ukraine-ten-plants-2009/). The [country profile](/country/ua/) follows the later possession, growing and medical-access rules.
