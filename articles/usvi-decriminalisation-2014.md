The US Virgin Islands replaced the ordinary criminal penalty for possessing an ounce or less of cannabis with a $100 civil fine on 19 December 2014. Legislators overrode Governor John de Jongh’s veto to make the change law. An unpaid fine could still lead to a separate offence carrying jail time.[@1]

## Legislators wanted police to focus on serious crime

The legislature had passed the measure in September. Its stated reasons included changing public attitudes and freeing criminal-justice resources for more serious offences, against the background of the territory’s high murder rate. The veto override brought the measure into law in December.[@1]

The same year, voters had [backed consideration of medical-cannabis legislation](/history/usvi-medical-referendum-2014/). That referendum asked about licensing patients, caregivers, growers and distribution centres, and received 56.5% support. Its result later appeared in the preamble to the medical-cannabis bill.[@2]

## A civil ticket still had consequences

Possession of an ounce or less became a civil infraction. Cannabis remained a controlled substance, and possessing more than an ounce could still bring up to a year in prison, a fine of up to $5,000, or both.[@1]

The smaller-amount rule also contained an enforcement trap: failing to pay the fine within 180 days was a misdemeanour punishable by up to three months in jail. The reform reduced the ordinary penalty for a small possession case while retaining a route to imprisonment for nonpayment.[@1]

## Cannabis smell remained a reason for police scrutiny

In [a 2016 vehicle-stop case](/history/usvi-cannabis-smell-stop-2016/), Judge Robert Molloy considered whether decriminalisation prevented officers from stopping a vehicle after smelling cannabis. He concluded that a trained officer could still rely on a smell sufficiently connected to the vehicle.[@1]

But the driver, Victor Cannergeiter, won his challenge to the particular stop. Prosecutors had relied on a general statement about smelling marijuana without enough evidence about the officer’s experience or the smell’s connection to his truck. The court suppressed the evidence obtained after the stop.[@1]

## Legal possession came in a later law

The [2023 Cannabis Use Act](/history/usvi-cannabis-use-act-2023/) set an adult possession allowance of two ounces and higher limits for qualified patients. It also exempted conduct complying with its rules from the older controlled-drug chapter.[@3]

The [US Virgin Islands profile](/country/vi/) follows those later possession rights and the separate process of establishing licensed sales.
