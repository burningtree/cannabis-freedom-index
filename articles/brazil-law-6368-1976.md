On 21 October 1976 Brazil stopped treating its drug users as traffickers. Law 6.368, the
Tóxicos law of the Geisel government and the dictatorship's first drug-law revision in
eight years, split the two figures the regime had fused since 1968: trafficking drew
three to fifteen years of reclusão, while possession for personal use drew six months
to two years of detenção.[@1]

## When the user became the dealer

The fusion dated to 26 December 1968. Amid the hard-line turn under Artur da Costa e
Silva, Decree-Law 385
equated the user with the trafficker in criminal punishment.[@1] The equation was
reaffirmed on 29 October 1971 by Law 5.726 under Emílio Médici, at the height of the
regime's repression — the years when hippie cannabis use was spreading through the
cities.[@1]

## The 1976 separation

The 1976 law undid the equation. Its article 12 typed the trafficker and set three to
fifteen years of reclusão for those tied to the drug market, while users faced a
lighter detention of six months to two years.[@1] Prison remained the answer for both — detenção is still custody, served in a less severe
regime than reclusão — but the law recognised for the first time in the dictatorship
era that the figures were different: a user was no longer a dealer by definition.
The split lasted thirty years, through democratisation, until 2006 removed custody
for users altogether and the Supreme Court [decriminalised possession in
2024](/history/brazil-supreme-court-2024/).

The distinction survived the dictatorship. In 2006 Law 11.343 ended prison and
reclusão for consuming, storing or possessing a small quantity for personal use,
replacing them with warnings, community service and education
measures.[@1]
[Users lost the prison sentence in 2006.](/history/brazil-no-prison-for-users-2006/)

## What moved in the index

The [1976 law](/history/brazil-law-6368-1976/) put [Brazil's](/country/br/) possession,
cultivation, sharing and consumption at 1 and access at 0, with enforcement at 0, the
baseline from which the later easing — 2006, the 2011 march ruling and the 2024
decriminalisation — departed.
