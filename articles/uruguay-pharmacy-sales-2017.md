Uruguay's pharmacies began selling non-medical cannabis on 19 July 2017. The
opening completed the most public-facing part of the system promised by the
country's [2013 legalisation law](/history/uruguay-legalisation-2013/), giving
registered residents a state-regulated alternative to growing at home or joining
a cannabis club.

## The delayed third route

Law 19.172 had provided three ways to obtain cannabis: home cultivation,
membership clubs and pharmacy sales. Home growing and clubs began operating in
2014, but implementation of commercial distribution took several more years.

The presidency announced that 16 pharmacies had joined the system and satisfied
the requirements of the Institute for the Regulation and Control of Cannabis,
known as IRCCA. Those shops were the starting network for sales of state-licensed
psychoactive cannabis for non-medical use.

## Registered residents only

Pharmacy access was not open tourism. Buyers had to be Uruguayan citizens or
permanent residents, at least 18 years old, and enrolled in the national registry.
Their identity and registration were checked at the point of sale using a
fingerprint scanner.

The system limited purchases to 40 grams a month. At launch, the pharmacies sold
two standardised varieties, Alfa 1 and Beta 1, both relatively low in THC. About
5,000 people were registered when commercial distribution began.

This was a regulated supply channel rather than conventional commercial
legalisation. The state controlled production and the rules of sale, while access
depended on residence, registration and purchase limits. Foreign visitors could
not buy through it.

## Why the opening mattered

Before pharmacy sales, legalisation existed but most adults who did not want to
grow or join a club still lacked the retail route written into the law. Opening
the pharmacies made legal cannabis directly obtainable through ordinary shops,
even though only a small number participated at first.

It arrived roughly three and a half years after President José Mujica signed the
legalisation law in December 2013.

## In the index

Pharmacy sales raised [Uruguay's](/country/uy/) access score from 2 to 4. The
other five scores did not move because possession, home growing, enforcement,
sharing and consumption had already been changed by the 2013 law and its earlier
implementation. This event records the moment regulated retail access actually
began.
