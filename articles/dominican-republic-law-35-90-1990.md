On 7 June 1990 the Dominican Republic hardened a drug law barely two years old. Law 35-90
amended Law 50-88 of 1988 on several fronts at once — procedure, borders, chemicals and
guns — while leaving the user, possession and cultivation bands exactly where they
were.[@1]

## Stay in custody

Its most human provision worked against the accused: a court's habeas corpus ruling in a
drug case was suspended while under appeal, so a person ordered released stayed in
custody until the appeal was heard.[@1] Long pre-trial detention, already common, gained a
statutory anchor.

## Ports and precursors

Controlled substances could enter the country only through the ports of Haina and Santo
Domingo and Las Américas airport.[@1] A list of precursor chemicals was added to the
controlled regime, tightening the net around manufacture.[@1] And the head of the drug
directorate gained the power to assign firearms to its members — the enforcement arm
arming itself in law.[@1]

## What did not change

Simple possession stayed at six months to two years, distributors at three to ten,
traffickers at five to twenty, and growing without authorisation stayed
trafficking.[@1] The 1990 reform was about the machinery around the bands, not the bands
themselves — which is why, unusually for a tightening, it moved no score.
