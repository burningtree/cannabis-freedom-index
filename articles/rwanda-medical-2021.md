Rwanda treats cannabis as one of the most serious drugs in its penal law, and at the
same time invites investors to grow it behind two fences for export.

## The penalty side

[Law 68/2018](/history/rwanda-penal-law-2018/) classes cannabis as a very severe
narcotic drug. Producing, transporting, storing or selling it carries twenty years to
life in prison and a fine of 20 to 30 million francs. Using it illegally brings three
to five years and a fine of 500,000 to five million francs.

## The business side

In October 2020 the cabinet approved a policy that opened the way to producing
cannabis commercially, for medical use only. On 25 June 2021 the ministers of health
and justice signed Ministerial Order 003/MoH/2021 on cannabis and cannabis products.

It allows investors to grow, process, import and export cannabis for medical or
research purposes, at licensed sites only. Licences for cultivation, processing,
research, import and export last five years. Cannabis products may be made up only on
the prescription of a specialist doctor.

## Behind the fence

The order's security rules are the most detailed part:

- a security plan approved by the national police;
- double fencing with a patrol lane between the layers;
- a licensed security company on guard day and night;
- lighting, cameras, watchtowers and an intrusion alarm;
- staff in work clothes without pockets;
- transport only by authorised companies, tracked throughout.

The police may add measures and audit sites, working with the army.

## The contradiction

The order says plainly that recreational use remains illegal. The 2012 narcotics law
under which the scheme operates still lists cannabis as an illegal narcotic. The
development board expects export income and jobs, and a special export levy is
planned.

A study of 1959 to 1960 found cannabis use in Rwanda confined to the Twa minority. A
health minister first proposed medical use in 2010.

The order changes no score for [Rwanda](/country/rw/): nothing in it reaches a person
living there.
