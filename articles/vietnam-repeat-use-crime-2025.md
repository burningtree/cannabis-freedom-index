For sixteen years, using a drug in Vietnam was not a crime in itself. It had been [taken out of the criminal code in 2009](/history/vietnam-use-decriminalised-2009/), and the 2015 code punished only possession and what follows from it. On 25 June 2025 the National Assembly passed Law No. 86/2025/QH15, which amends the Criminal Code and adds a new Article 256a: "illegal use of drugs". It applies to acts committed from midnight on 1 July 2025.[@1]

## Who counts as a user

The definition comes from the 2021 Law on Drug Prevention and Control. An illegal drug user is a person who uses drugs without authorisation and tests positive. No drug needs to be found on them.[@1]

## The four situations

Article 256a applies only to the defined rehabilitation-related situations. Its two-year post-management category also requires current illicit-user management; the conditions must both hold.[@2]

| Situation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| During rehabilitation or treatment with substitute drugs | two to three years in prison |
| During the period of management that follows detoxification | two to three years |
| Within two years after post-rehabilitation management ends AND currently under illicit-user management | two to three years |
| Within two years after quitting voluntary detoxification or substitute treatment | two to three years |
| Committing the offence again | three to five years |

Ordinary monitoring after a first detection lasts one year. Further use during that monitoring can lead to addiction assessment and rehabilitation measures. Using again during subsequent rehabilitation or substitution treatment can then meet the criminal offence. The ordinary monitoring period alone does not establish that offence.[@2]

## The knock-on rule

A person with an unexpunged conviction under Article 256a who then possesses, transports or appropriates drugs, without intent to sell or produce them, is prosecuted under the ordinary possession, transport and appropriation offences in Articles 249, 250 and 252.[@1]

## Ordinary monitoring has a different role

The State Audit Office explains that a person detected using drugs who has not undergone the qualifying rehabilitation measures does not thereby commit this use offence. Its account distinguishes administrative management of a user from management after rehabilitation.[@2]

## Why the index moved

The history records a consumption-score drop because drug use can again lead to prison in the defined rehabilitation-related situations. Ordinary possession and sale have their own criminal rules; this change concerns the separate use offence. The [Vietnam profile](/country/vn/) sets those offences alongside the administrative and rehabilitation procedures.[@2]
