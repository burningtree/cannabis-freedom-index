In 1915 the Texas border city of El Paso banned the sale and possession of
marijuana — the first American measure to draw on Mexican beliefs that the drug
caused madness and violence.[@1][@2]

## A fight, a law, a pattern

Bonnie and Whitebread date the ordinance to 1914 and say a fight involving a user
was said to have set it off.[@2] Whatever the spark, the pattern caught on: between
1915 and 1930 practically every state west of the Mississippi passed an
anti-marijuana law.[@1]

## From border town to federal law

El Paso's ordinance became the template. State bans multiplied through the 1920s,
the federal Marihuana Tax Act followed in 1937, and the plant stayed in the
strictest federal category into the 2020s.[@1]
