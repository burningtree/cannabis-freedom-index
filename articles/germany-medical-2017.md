On 19 January 2017 the Bundestag adopted the government's bill amending narcotics law without a single vote against. A competing motion from the Left Party was rejected by the majority. The index dates the change to 10 March 2017.

## From permits to prescriptions

Before the law, cannabis could be used as a medicine in Germany only under an exception permit from the Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices, and patients generally paid for it themselves. According to the Institute about 1,000 patients held such a permit.

The law replaced the permit with an ordinary prescription. Dried cannabis flowers and extracts of controlled quality can be dispensed in pharmacies, and insured patients gained a claim to dronabinol or nabilone as well. The statutory health insurers pay.

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who prescribes | The treating doctor; no restriction to specialists |
| Who pays | Statutory health insurance, for a serious illness with a realistic prospect of benefit |
| Approval | The first prescription generally needs the insurer's approval; three days in palliative cases |
| Supply | Cultivation in Germany under a state cannabis agency run by the Institute |

## What parliament changed

Two changes were made during the deliberations. Doctors decide for themselves whether cannabis is appropriate, "even if other treatment options exist": patients no longer had to have exhausted every other therapy first. And an accompanying survey of the medical effects was kept, but with patient data collected anonymously.

The German Hemp Association asked at the hearing that patients be allowed to grow their own. The Bundestag's account lists this as a demand, not as something adopted.

## What the survey found

The Institute's five-year observational study later showed what the prescriptions were for: chronic pain in 76.4% of cases, spasticity in 9.6%, anorexia or wasting in 5.1% and nausea or vomiting in 2.2%. Since 1 April 2024 the rules sit in a separate Medical Cannabis Act.

## Why the index moved

Only access changed, from 0 to 1, taking the total from 20 to 21. Until 2017 Germany had no lawful source of cannabis except for the roughly 1,000 permit holders. Afterwards any doctor could prescribe it and a pharmacy could supply it. For everyone without a prescription nothing changed: possession, growing and supply stayed offences until the Cannabis Act of 2024.
