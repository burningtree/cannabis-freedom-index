In Sri Lanka the only legal cannabis is the cannabis the police have already seized.[@3]

## From licence to ban

The Dutch criminalised narcotic trafficking on the island in 1675. Under the British,
an [1867 ordinance](/history/sri-lanka-licensing-1867/) limited sales of opium and
bhang to licensed dealers, the excise approach used in India. Imports of bhang and
ganja were [banned in 1897](/history/sri-lanka-import-ban-1897/), and the
[Indian Hemp Ordinance](/history/sri-lanka-indian-hemp-ordinance-1905/) of 1905 moved
from licensing to prohibition; its 1907 version set a fine of 100 rupees and up to six
months in prison. The
[Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance](/history/sri-lanka-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1935/)
of 1935, much amended, is still the basis of the law.

## The exception

The Ayurveda Act of 1961, amended in 1962, allowed Ayurvedic physicians to obtain
opium and cannabis for their medicines: it brought registered practitioners within the
Ordinance's practitioner definitions and set up Board-granted certificates issued by the
Government Agent.[@1,2] A 1984 amendment exempted lawful extracts and
tinctures of the hemp plant from the criminal provisions.[@1]

The result is a closed loop:

- about 16,000 Ayurvedic practitioners are the only people allowed to sell cannabis;[@3]
- their only legal source is the Ayurvedic Drugs Corporation, under the health ministry;[@3]
- the corporation gets most of its cannabis from police raids on illegal shipments.[@3]

By the time it reaches the practitioners it is often old and of poor quality.[@3]

## Attempts to fix the supply

Ministers of indigenous medicine sought limited legalisation in 2008 and proposed
amending the Act in 2013. In 2017 the government announced a 400-hectare plantation
near Ingiriya to supply practitioners, with the possibility of exporting remedies to
the United States. In [August 2025](/history/sri-lanka-export-cultivation-2025/) seven
foreign investors received licences to grow for export, the first legal commercial
cultivation in the country.

## For everyone else

Possession of less than five kilograms is treated as a minor offence, with a fine or
a short jail term.

In the index the Act raised access for [Sri Lanka](/country/lk/) from 0 to 1, since it
opened one narrow legal route to cannabis.
