On 9 October 1990 Italy consolidated its drug rules into a single text, Presidential Decree 309. The decree is still the country's drug law. In its first version it did something its predecessor had avoided for fifteen years: it punished a person simply for holding a drug to use.

## What it replaced

Since 1975 Italian law had told users and dealers apart. A person found with a "modest quantity" for personal use was not punishable, though the law never said how much that was.

The 1990 text closed that door. Mere possession for personal use became punishable, and a fixed "average daily dose" marked the line: above it a crime, below it administrative sanctions.

## An argument, then a vote

The decree opened a heated public debate, and its subject was exactly this: the treatment of the simple consumer.

It was settled at the ballot box. A referendum in 1993 struck out the criminal penalty. Since then possession for personal use has not been a crime in Italy. It remains an administrative offence.

## What a user faces now

| Sanction under Article 75 | |
|---|---|
| Suspension of passport | one month to one year |
| Suspension of driving licence, or a bar on obtaining one | one month to one year |
| Suspension of firearms licence | one month to one year |
| Treatment and rehabilitation programme | where the conditions are met |

There is no fine. The sanctions can be applied singly or together. They once ran for one to three months for soft drugs and two to four for hard ones.

## Where personal use ends

The line between a user and a dealer is drawn by the active ingredient. Possession counts as personal when the amount does not exceed the average single dose multiplied by a set figure, and the ministry's table speaks of the quantity of active principle, not the weight of the plant.

It is not the only test. A judge decides case by case, weighing every circumstance of the act, whether the drug was for the person's own use or meant for sale. One plant can hold many times the table's limit, and all cultivation of the plants in the first table is forbidden.

## Why the index moved

The [1990 decree](/history/italy-criminalisation-1990/) lowered [Italy's](/country/it/) total from 21 to 11: possession fell from 3 to 1, enforcement from 4 to 2 and consumption from 2 to 1, because holding a drug for oneself went from unpunished to criminal above a small dose. The [referendum of 1993](/history/italy-referendum-1993/) reversed it exactly, back to 21. The decree itself survived, and it is the text every later change has amended.
