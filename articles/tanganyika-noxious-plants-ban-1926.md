In June 1926 the government of Tanganyika made it a crime to grow, hold, use or consume cannabis, with up to a year in prison or a 2,000-shilling fine.[@1] The ban went beyond a customs notice two years earlier that had already stopped cannabis imports.[@1] Together they made the plant illegal in the territory's trade and in its gardens.[@1]

## The customs notice came first

Tanganyika's customs law listed Cannabis sativa among prohibited imports in a 1924 government notice.[@1] The entry called it hemp or true hemp and, in Swahili, bhang or bangi.[@1] The notice dealt only with imports; the 1926 ordinance created the domestic offence.[@1]

## What the ordinance banned

The ordinance's main section covered two kinds of conduct.[@1] It prohibited planting, growing or cultivating any plant named in its schedule, and it prohibited consuming, using or possessing such a plant, any substance obtained from it, or any compound or mixture containing it.[@1] The schedule named Cannabis Sativa, known as true hemp and in Swahili as bhang or bhangi.[@1] A conviction brought a fine of up to 2,000 shillings or up to twelve months' imprisonment, and the court could order the plants or substances destroyed.[@1]

## Search, arrest and a changing list

A magistrate or administrative officer could issue a warrant to enter and search any place by day or night and to seize any plant or substance connected with an offence.[@1] A police officer could arrest without a warrant anyone found committing an offence.[@1] The governor could add to or change the schedule by order published in the gazette, so the list of banned plants was not fixed in the statute.[@1]

## The ban outlasted the colonial state

The 1935 Dangerous Drugs Ordinance consolidated Tanganyika's drug law and raised the maximum penalty for cannabis offences to ten years or a 20,000-shilling fine in the High Court.[@1] The 1926 ordinance stayed in force alongside it.[@2] The 1995 drug law repealed both colonial statutes and created a separate tier for small personal use; the 2015 Act then set at least thirty years for unlawful cultivation.[@2,3] The [country profile](/country/tz/) records the current penalties.
