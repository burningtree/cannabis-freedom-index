On 1 December 1939 a joint regulation of the Anglo-French condominium made dangerous-drug law for the New Hebrides, and it still anchors Vanuatu's cannabis prohibition today.[@1] The rule came from two colonial powers governing together, and it outlived them both.[@1]

## A condominium's drug law

Joint Regulation 12 of 1939 was amended repeatedly after independence — in 1952 and through to 2005 — and the 2006 consolidation prints the result.[@1] Cannabis meant the whole plant, with cannabis resin defined separately beside it.[@1] Growing any cannabis plant was banned, and occupiers who let their premises be used for smoking cannabis or dealing in it faced punishment too.[@1] The 1939 wording itself was never read for this article, so how the original treated cannabis in its own terms stays unconfirmed; what survives is the consolidated text and its commencement date.[@1]

## Enforcement reaches the islands

The old law still bites on the outer islands, where growers never fully adjusted.[@4] In 2017 police stepped up arrests on remote islands whose residents, in the press phrase, "did not adapt" to the law they were suddenly being held to.[@4] A Malekula case that year showed the courts' answer: eight first offenders from Bankir village, whose conduct dated back to 2009 and included single small plants, each received fifty hours of community work and six months' probation under 2013 sentencing guidance — with a warning to one defendant that his church belief did not permit him to keep growing.[@2] The next year some sixty young people from Malekula were ferried under police escort from Epi Island to Port Vila for cultivation, distribution and consumption.[@4]

## An export industry beside the ban

While village growers faced the courts, the government built a legal industry alongside the prohibition.[@4,3] In September 2018 the Council of Ministers backed medical-cannabis and industrial-hemp production, and a 2021 licensing law in force from January 2022 opened both for export, defining hemp at no more than 0.3 percent THC.[@4,3] The two tracks run side by side: in January 2026 the agriculture director general restated that all cannabis and hemp activity needed strict licences, secure handling and export, with no local sales.[@5] The 1939 regulation thus frames both the village courtroom and the export licence — the base layer under [Vanuatu's](/country/vu/) modern scores.[@1]
