In 1973 Oregon stopped treating a person with a little marijuana as a criminal. Possession of up to one ounce became a violation, punished with a fine of $100. No American state had done that before, and within five years ten more had followed.

## A commission the president did not want to hear

The ground had been prepared by a federal body. President Nixon appointed a commission to study marijuana, chaired by Shafer, and told him of his strong opposition to legalisation, advising him to keep the commission in line.

Its report, 1,184 pages long, concluded in March 1972 that cannabis was a relatively benign drug whose dangers had been exaggerated, and it endorsed decriminalisation. Nixon dismissed its findings. The states did not.

The federal law of 1970 had already given them a model, reducing simple possession from a felony. By 1973 only two states still classed it as one.

## Eleven states in six years

| Year | States |
|---|---|
| 1973 | Oregon |
| 1975 | Alaska, Maine, Colorado, California, Ohio |
| 1976 | Minnesota |
| 1977 | Mississippi, New York, North Carolina |
| 1978 | Nebraska |

In 1975 congressional hearings on decriminalising cannabis were held for the first time. That same year the Supreme Court of Alaska went further than any legislature, ruling that the state constitution's right to privacy protected an adult's use at home.

## What happened in Oregon

Researchers surveyed Oregonians one and two years after the change, asking those who did not use marijuana why not.

| Reason given | Share |
|---|---|
| Not interested | 53% |
| Health dangers | 23% |
| Legal or law enforcement reasons | 4% |
| Not available | 2% |

One in twenty-five named the law.

## The end of the wave

Nebraska in 1978 was the last. No further state decriminalised for about two decades, as a parents' movement and then the Reagan administration turned policy the other way, and in 1986 Congress brought back mandatory federal sentences.

## Why the index moved

The [Oregon law](/history/us-oregon-decriminalisation-1973/) stands in the index for the whole wave it began. It raised the [United States](/country/us/) total from 11 to 16: possession went from 1 to 2 and enforcement from 2 to 3. The country is scored as one place, so eleven states covering about a third of Americans moved the national figure part of the way towards decriminalisation, not all of it.
