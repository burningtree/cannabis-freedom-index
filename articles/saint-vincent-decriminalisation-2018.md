The Governor-General of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines assented to Act No. 10 of 2019 on 12 August 2019. It did not take effect then. Its first section says it comes into operation on a date set by proclamation, and that date was 3 March 2020. The Act amends the Drugs (Prevention of Misuse) Act, the country's drug law; cannabis had been banned there since [1937](/history/saint-vincent-dangerous-drugs-1937/).

## Still unlawful, no longer arrestable

The new section 7A begins by restating the ban: it "shall not be lawful for a person to have 56 grammes or less of cannabis or cannabis resin in his possession". What changes is the consequence.

| Subject | Rule |
|---|---|
| Amount | 56 grams or less of cannabis or resin |
| Maximum penalty | a fine of $500 on summary conviction |
| Arrest | the person "shall not be liable to be arrested or detained" |
| Record | a contravention "shall not form part of the criminal record of the offender" |

The Act also raises from fifteen to fifty-six the gram figure in section 7(4)(e) of the principal Act.

## The notice

A police officer who believes someone has committed the offence must offer, by written notice, "the opportunity to discharge the liability to conviction". The measure the officer imposes is not money. It is the provision of educational material about cannabis, and, for anyone under eighteen or anyone the officer reasonably believes may be dependent, a referral for counselling or rehabilitative care. If the person accepts, no proceedings are brought. If not, the notice becomes a complaint and a magistrate may issue a summons.

## Smoking in public

Section 7C makes it an offence to smoke or use cannabis in a public place, also with a maximum fine of $500 and also without arrest. Here the route out is a fixed penalty paid to the magistrates' court within a month. "Smoking" includes vaping.

The definition of a public place carries two exceptions: "a privately-occupied residence not used for commercial purposes", and "a usual place of worship of members of the Rastafarian faith, or other faith". The owner or manager of a public place may tell a smoker to stop and may remove them.

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 1 to 3: up to 56 grams is still an offence, but it ends with a leaflet or a referral and leaves no record. Enforcement rises from 2 to 4, because the Act takes arrest and detention away from the police for this offence. Consumption rises from 1 to 3, since use at home or in a place of worship falls outside the public-smoking offence. Cultivation and sharing are not mentioned in the Act and stay at 1. The total moves from 11 to 22.
