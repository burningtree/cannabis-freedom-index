On 17 December 1873, the Government of India set out a policy of discouraging ganja and bhang consumption through controls and taxation. The resolution followed a provincial inquiry and acknowledged the practical difficulty of imposing a uniform arrangement across different territories.

## A dated policy response

The Indian Hemp Drugs Commission’s 1894 report identifies Finance Department Resolution No. 3773 and reproduces its reasoning. This gives a precise date for the formal response, distinct from the [earlier inquiry](/history/india-cannabis-insanity-inquiry-1872/), which began in 1871.

The source consulted is the later official report’s reproduction. The original Finance Department file has not been independently inspected. That provenance permits a dated account of the policy while leaving open comparison with the original administrative record.

## Restrictions and duties

The resolution asked local governments and administrations, where possible, to discourage consumption by restricting cultivation, preparation and retail. It also supported duties as high as could be levied without encouraging illicit practices.

This was a statement of administrative objectives. The quoted passage does not itself provide one tax rate, one licensing form or one cannabis offence applicable throughout British India. Those details depended on territorial legislation and implementation.

## Provinces did not agree

The resolution acknowledged that most local governments did not favour changing existing arrangements. It identified British Burma and the Central Provinces as exceptions and treated the prospect of reproducing Bengal’s controls elsewhere as difficult.

The document also expressed official beliefs about cannabis and insanity. Those are historical claims within the argument for intervention, rather than medical conclusions this article adopts. The commission’s later report criticized the reliability of much earlier evidence, making attribution especially important.

## Policy and implementation

A government can announce a restrictive policy before or without enacting a single uniform prohibition. This distinction helps explain why later provincial licences, duties and bans need their own dated records rather than being treated as automatic consequences of the resolution.

The event is therefore recorded as administrative context for [India](/country/in/), without a national score change. It supplies a documented policy direction between the initial inquiry and later regulatory measures. It also corrects an earlier timeline impression that the inquiry and the formal response were both completed in 1872. The individual provincial instruments remain necessary evidence for establishing what became enforceable in each place.
