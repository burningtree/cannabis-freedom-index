Sierra Leone passed a flagship drug law in 2008 and forgot to switch it on. The
National Drugs Control Act created an enforcement agency and powers to destroy
cannabis crops — but left its commencement date blank.[@2]

The fix came on 18 September 2008, when the Amendment Act let the president bring the
law into force on a date fixed by published legal order.[@1] The urgency was real: that
July a cocaine-laden plane had landed in the country, forcing the law's hurried
passage.[@2]

The pattern — tough law, improvised start — still shapes enforcement. The 2008 Act's
five-year possession floor and fifteen-year pharmacy-law cultivation terms stand, while
courts and task forces work around them: refusing bail in kush cases, handing down
twenty-five-year sentences for grams, and raiding Freetown's selling spots wrap by
wrap.
