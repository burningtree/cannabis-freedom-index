Hong Kong’s 1992 drug reform gave courts power to impose heavier possession penalties while removing rules that made trafficking easier to presume. Cannabis remained controlled, but holding more than a set quantity would no longer automatically place a defendant under a legal presumption of intending to supply it.[@1,2]

## Court challenges forced a review

The reform followed the territory’s Bill of Rights Ordinance. Introducing the bill in February, the security secretary said the Court of Appeal had ruled the previous September that some drug-law presumptions were inconsistent with those rights.[@2]

The government reviewed the law to preserve its ability to prosecute drug offences while meeting those requirements. Its proposed changes addressed assumptions about manufacture, possession, knowledge and premises used for drug-taking.[@2] The legislation revised the system retained under [the replacement drug ordinance of 1968](/history/hong-kong-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1968/).

## Lawmakers wanted stronger possession penalties

The government proposed up to seven years and HK$1 million for possession on indictment, the more serious prosecution route. Legislators supported that increase and also raised the maximum fine in summary cases from HK$10,000 to HK$100,000, retaining their three-year prison ceiling.[@1]

Professor Felice Lieh Mak explained the committee’s concern that someone holding a large quantity might escape a trafficking conviction if prosecutors lacked evidence of intended supply. The increased possession penalties gave courts greater sentencing power in those cases.[@1]

## Quantity alone no longer supplied the presumption

The bill removed the presumption that possessing more than a specified amount meant possession for trafficking. It also removed the separate trafficking-intent possession offence, bringing that conduct within the definition of trafficking instead.[@2]

This kept possession for supply criminal while changing how a prosecution had to establish its case. Other changes narrowed possession-related presumptions and removed presumptions concerning manufacture and drug-taking premises.[@2]

## The reform began two days after passage

Lawmakers passed the amended bill on 24 June. A later official table records its commencement as 26 June 1992.[@1,3]

The higher ceilings left room for courts to assess individual sentences. In [Phung Hai’s 2000 appeal](/history/hong-kong-personal-cannabis-sentence-2000/), a six-month sentence for possessing 1.18 g of cannabis was cut to two months. He had two earlier drug convictions, but the prosecution did not defend the original sentence.[@4]

The [Hong Kong profile](/country/hk/) follows the later law and sentencing practice, with possession and trafficking still treated as separate offences.
