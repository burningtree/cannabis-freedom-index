On 18 June 2003 Peru wrote a number into its drug law: possession for one's own immediate use
is not punishable up to 8 grams of marijuana or 2 grams of its derivatives. Law 28002,
published the day before, rewrote Article 299 of the Penal Code around that limit — and
excluded anyone holding two or more types of drugs.[@1] Under the constitution the law took
effect the day after publication.[@2]

## What 8 grams means

The limit is a ceiling for non-punishment, not a right. Below it, immediate personal
possession is not punished; above it, or mixed with other drugs, the trafficking rules
apply.[@1] The exemption also dies where the circumstances point to supply rather than
immediate use.

## The hundred-plant line

The same law introduced Article 296-A on cannabis cultivation: even where the crop did not
exceed one hundred plants, growing drew two to six years.[@1] Above that line the general
trafficking penalty of eight to fifteen years applies. Personal use may be excused in the
pocket, but never in the ground.

## Still the line today

The 8 g rule survived every later reform. The 1991 code's framework, the 2017 medical law and
the 2023 patient-treatment exemption all sit on top of it: ordinary users get 8 grams,
registered patients get what their treatment needs.[@1]
