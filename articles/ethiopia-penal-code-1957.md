Ethiopia's new penal code made unauthorised drug supply punishable with prison
and a fine, and extended the same punishment to people who knowingly provided
a place for others to use drugs. Approved in 1957, the code took effect on
5 May 1958.

## A new code replaced the emperor's earlier law

Emperor Haile Selassie approved the code after parliament passed it. Its
opening proclamation said Ethiopia's progress required replacing the penal
code of 1930, and gave the new rules a later starting date.

The drug offence appeared among crimes against public health. It grouped
poisons, drugs and narcotic substances together, treating their unauthorised
production and circulation as a danger the criminal law should control.
Ethiopia had already [joined the amended international opium convention](/history/ethiopia-opium-convention-1947/)
in 1947, placing cannabis within its international drug-control commitments.

## Supply included receiving and storing drugs

The offence reached well beyond a completed sale. Producing, transforming,
importing, exporting, transporting, acquiring, receiving and storing the
substances without lawful authority were all covered. Offering them for
sale, distributing them or obtaining them for someone else also fell within
the rule.

The ordinary punishment combined at least three months of simple
imprisonment with a fine of up to 20,000 Ethiopian dollars. The wording
therefore reached the movement and keeping of drugs as well as the person
who finally passed them to a customer.

## A private gathering could expose the host

Someone who knowingly made premises available for taking drugs could receive
the same punishment. The code expressly included private premises, bringing
the person supplying a place into the reach of the drug offence alongside
the person supplying the substance.

More serious cases could bring up to five years of rigorous imprisonment,
the code's heavier form of custody, and a fine of up to 30,000 dollars.
These included organised or professional trafficking. The heavier punishment
also covered knowingly supplying drugs or access to premises, for gain or
an improper motive, to young people, people with mental disabilities or
people dependent on drugs.

## Later laws returned to the host and the user

The [1999 drug law](/history/ethiopia-drug-control-law-1999/) set seven to
fifteen years for allowing illicit narcotic drug use on one's premises.
The [replacement criminal code](/history/ethiopia-criminal-code-2005/)
then expressly separated trafficking from possession, growing and supply
for private use. Those later distinctions became part of
[Ethiopia's cannabis law](/country/et/) long after the emperor's code began
punishing the supply chain and the host together.
