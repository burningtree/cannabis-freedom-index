Jack Herer made USDA Bulletin 404 part of his argument for hemp's industrial revival.
The original study supports a narrower conclusion: hemp hurds could make usable printing
paper, with practical obstacles still to solve.

The bulletin's Table II compares the land needed to sustain a pulp mill: 10,000 acres
of annually grown hemp against 40,500 acres of pulpwood land under its assumptions.
That is a sustained-supply calculation, rather than a universal substitution ratio.
The authors also identified disadvantages in raw-material storage and digester capacity.

Herer extends the report into a forecast of large-scale replacement of wood pulp. The
forecast belongs to his advocacy; it is not a result established by the trials. This
entry provides industrial context for the [United States](/country/us/) and does not
move a personal-freedom score.
