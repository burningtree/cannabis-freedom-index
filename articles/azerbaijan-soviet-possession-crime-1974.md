On 8 April 1975 Soviet Azerbaijan added a new article to its criminal code. From then on, buying, keeping, carrying or sending narcotics without intent to sell was a crime. The republic was following a union decree of 25 April 1974 on strengthening the fight against drug addiction.[@1,2]

## A code that punished supply, not use

The [1961 criminal code](/history/azerbaijan-ssr-criminal-code-1961/) dealt with drugs mainly through two articles. Article 226 punished making, buying, keeping, carrying or selling narcotics with intent to sell, with up to ten years in prison and confiscation for aggravated cases. Article 227 punished planting or growing opium poppy, Indian, South Manchurian or South Chui hemp, or other prohibited drug plants, with up to five years; a repeat brought three to eight.[@1]

Simple possession without intent to sell had no article of its own. The code's drug offences were built around supply and cultivation, not around what a person kept for themselves.[@1]

## The 1975 amendment

The new Article 226-1 covered making, buying, keeping, carrying or sending narcotics without intent to sell. It carried up to three years in prison or up to two years of corrective labour, and a repeat brought up to five years.[@1] The article set no quantity threshold. A small amount for personal use therefore fell within the offence, even if a court might treat it more lightly than a commercial quantity.

The timing mattered. The union decree of 1974 called for a stronger campaign against addiction, and the republican amendment turned that instruction into a criminal offence that police and courts could use against users themselves.[@1,2]

## The 1987 reversal

A USSR edict of 22 June 1987 changed the rule. A first-time purchase or keeping of a small amount without intent to sell became an administrative offence, punished with a fine of up to 100 roubles, one to two months of corrective labour, or up to fifteen days' arrest. A repeat within a year could still bring up to two years in prison. The same edict made drug use without a doctor's prescription an administrative offence.[@2]

The [1987 entry](/history/azerbaijan-soviet-possession-relaxed-1987/) records that change. For a first-time small amount, a person could again be dealt with outside prison, though the offence stayed on the books for repeats.

## What remained

Azerbaijan's post-Soviet Criminal Code kept the possession offence. Article 234.1 now punishes possession without intent to sell above a substantial amount, set at 5 g of dried cannabis; below that line the administrative code applies.[@3] The 1975 article is the reason possession was criminal in the later Soviet years, and the quantity line is its descendant. The [Azerbaijan profile](/country/az/) sets out the current tiers.
