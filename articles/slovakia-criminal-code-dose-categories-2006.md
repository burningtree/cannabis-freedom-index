On 1 January 2006 Slovakia counted drugs in doses. The new Criminal Code defined possession for personal use as no more than three usual single doses, punished with up to three years in prison, and a larger personal-use category of no more than ten doses, punished with up to five.[@1]

## Doses, not allowances

The categories were a novelty with a catch: they sorted offenders inside criminal law rather than permitting anything. Three doses or ten, possession stayed a crime — the numbers only decided which prison range applied.[@1] The rules covered all controlled drugs generally, cannabis included. Slovakia drew no line between a misdemeanour and a crime the way its Czech neighbour did; every band led to a criminal court.[@1]

The scheme lasted eighteen years. Courts worked with "usual single doses" — an elastic measure that depended on expert opinion about what a dose was — while the lowest cannabis quantities still carried years of threatened prison.[@1]

## Grams replace doses

In August 2024 Act 40/2024 split the old drug offence into four and replaced usual doses with fixed amounts.[@2] Possessing cannabis in a negligible amount, up to 2 g (or up to 200 mg of THC above 2 g), now carries up to one year; growing up to two plants for one's own use carries up to six months, with larger tiers above.[@2] The milder text applied immediately, including to earlier acts.[@2]

The reform cut the threatened sentences but kept the structure: even the negligible band remains a criminal offence, with courts able to withhold punishment while ordering treatment.[@2] From doses to grams, [Slovakia](/country/sk/) still counts cannabis in a courtroom.
