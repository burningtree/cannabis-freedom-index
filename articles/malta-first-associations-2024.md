At the end of January 2024 a non-profit association called KDD Society, in Ħ'Attard, began
handing out cannabis to its members. Its licence, granted three months earlier, had been the first
of its kind in the European Union, and its opening came more than two years after
[Malta legalised](/history/malta-legalisation-2021/) the drug in December 2021.

## No shops, only clubs

The 2021 law let adults carry up to 7 grams, keep up to 50 grams at home and grow four plants per
household. It allowed no shops. Anyone who did not grow their own could get cannabis legally only
from a Cannabis Harm Reduction Association: a club run without profit, licensed by a new regulator,
the Authority for the Responsible Use of Cannabis (ARUC). It was Malta's version of the
[cannabis social club](/culture/traditions/cannabis-social-clubs/).

| An association may | |
|---|---|
| Have | Up to 500 members, each belonging to one association only |
| Give each member | At most 7 g a day and 50 g a month, and up to 20 seeds a month |
| Sell | Only cannabis it has grown itself and the regulator has tested |

## The licences

The regulator granted the first two operating licences in October 2023, to KDD Society and to Ta'
Zelli in Ħaż-Żebbuġ, and four more associations received licences in principle. Its chair, Leonid
McKay, said the authority would not set prices but expected them to rival the illicit market.
The parliamentary secretary for reforms, Rebecca Buttigieg, called it a historic moment, and added
that drug trafficking would never be tolerated. The minister who had steered the law through
parliament, Owen Bonnici, explained why it set no limit on potency: "you will be creating a new
market for the black market."

Before an association could hand anything out, its harvest had to pass the regulator's laboratory
tests for potency, microbial contamination and purity. KDD Society's did, and on 30 January 2024
MaltaToday reported it fully operational.

## A slow start

The approach was deliberately cautious. ARUC describes its aim as "discouraging and delaying
cannabis use", and its chair said that anyone whose goals did not match the authority's was not
welcome to work with it. In 2025 parliament [tightened the rules](/history/malta-cannabis-amendments-2025/)
for associations, keeping them 250 metres from schools and youth centres.

By October 2026 the regulator's register listed 24 associations with an operating permit, from
Birkirkara to Għajnsielem. They remain the only legal source in [Malta](/country/mt/) for
anyone who does not grow at home, and they are closed to tourists.
