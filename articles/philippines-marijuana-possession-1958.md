On 13 June 1958 the Philippines wrote marijuana into its criminal code. Republic Act 2060 is two sections long. It amends a single article of the Revised Penal Code, Article 190, and takes effect on approval.

## What Article 190 had covered

Article 190 punished the "possession, preparation and use of prohibited drugs, and maintenance of opium dens".  Marijuana had been regulated since [1953](/history/philippines-narcotic-drugs-law-1953/) under a separate tax and registration statute, which punished unregistered possession but said nothing about use.

## What the amendment added

The new text lists as prohibited drugs opium, cocaine, alpha and beta eucaine and Indian hemp, with their derivatives and preparations, and any other drug with a narcotic action. It then defines the newcomer:

- Indian hemp is "otherwise known as marihuana", and also as hashish, bhang and other names;
- it covers the dried or fresh flowering or fruiting tops of the female cannabis plant from which the resin has not been extracted, in any form.

Anyone who, "unless lawfully authorized", possesses, prepares, administers or uses a prohibited drug faces a penalty running from the middle range of arresto mayor to the lowest range of prisión correccional, and a fine of 300 to 10,000 pesos. The same penalty applies to anyone who keeps a "dive or resort" where a prohibited drug is used.

The amendment does not deal with importing or selling; those stayed under other provisions.

## What it changed

Two things were new. Use of marijuana became an offence in its own right, where the 1953 law had reached only possession and trade. And marijuana was now named in the criminal code itself, beside opium and cocaine.

## Why the index moved

The index moved the [Philippines](/country/ph/) from 7 to 4 with the [1958 amendment](/history/philippines-marijuana-possession-1958/). Only consumption changed, from 3 to 0, because using the drug now carried imprisonment by name. The other segments had already fallen in 1953.
