Greece raised its industrial-hemp threshold in May 2026, bringing it from 0.2% to
0.3% THC. In the same article, it shut dried hemp flower out of the consumer
market.

## The threshold rises

Article 43 of Law 5302/2026 amended the drug law's definition provisions. Raw
harvested products from approved *Cannabis sativa L.* varieties containing no more
than 0.3% THC are excluded from the controlled substances covered by those
provisions.

The previous threshold was 0.2%. The article also retains routes for regulated
foods, cosmetics and food supplements containing THC, subject to ministerial rules
and maximum limits.

The cultivation rule has an enforcement margin. If an approved industrial-hemp
crop tests above 0.3% but no higher than 0.6%, the prosecutor is notified and the
crop is destroyed at the grower's expense. Above 0.6%, criminal proceedings also
begin under the drug law.

## Flower is carved back out

The law then creates a specific exception to the hemp exclusion. Dried flower at
or below 0.3% THC is not treated as an exempt raw harvested product when it is
intended for retail sale or distribution to consumers.

That distinction is written into a new paragraph 3A added to Article 1 of Greece's
drug law, Law 4139/2013.

For that flower, the prohibition is comprehensive. It covers retail sale,
circulation and supply to the public, as well as purchase and use by consumers. It
applies to processed and unprocessed flower throughout Greece.

The text therefore separates agricultural hemp from a dried flower offered as a
consumer product. Raising the crop threshold did not legalise low-THC flower on
shop shelves.

## What remains possible

The ban does not cover importing, storing, wholesaling or moving dried flower when
it is intended exclusively for industrial processing. The statute gives cosmetics
as an example.

Two months later Greece adopted a separate ban on plant products with cannabinoids
added for smoking or vaping. That later measure is covered in the [July 2026
entry](/history/greece-sprayed-products-ban-2026/).

## In the index

The index scores ordinary cannabis law, while the May measure concerns hemp flower
within the low-THC threshold. [Greece's](/country/gr/) six scores therefore do not
move, even though lawful consumer access to this particular product was tightened.
