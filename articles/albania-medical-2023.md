Albania’s parliament adopted a cannabis production law on 21 July 2023, creating separate routes for medical and industrial activity.[@1] The national cannabis agency describes the resulting products as exclusively for export, with general cannabis use still outside the legal framework.[@4]

## Medical licences lasted fifteen years

The medical licence could cover cultivation and production, domestic transport and export of cannabis plants and their products.[@2] It lasted fifteen years, with renewal available on request, and could not simply be transferred to another operator.[@2]

A medical production unit had to cover between five and ten hectares, while the national cultivation ceiling was 200 hectares.[@2] An operator holding more than one unit could have up to four adjacent units.[@2] The agency would review licence conditions every three years, and the health minister’s approval formed part of issuance.[@2]

## Industry followed a separate permit

Industrial cannabis had its own definition: varieties containing no more than 0.8% THC.[@1] Its permit lasted five years, with renewal possible, and required at least one hectare.[@3] That was a distinct route from the medical licence, with its own application and ministerial approval procedure.[@1]

Permitted industrial activities could include importing seeds or seedlings, propagation, cultivation, processing, transport and export.[@3] Operators had to use recognised registered varieties and comply with security, marking, traceability and record requirements.[@3] Imports of planting material also required approval by the competent seed authority.[@1]

## Production did not provide patient access

The law regulated an authorised production chain rather than giving residents a place to buy cannabis.[@4] The agency’s explanation explicitly separated the medical and industrial purposes from general use and described both as export-oriented.[@4]

The [Albania profile](/country/al/) therefore distinguishes permission for authorised production from recreational possession, home growing and consumer supply.[@1,4] Medical licence requirements concerned businesses, controlled premises and qualified personnel, rather than individual patients growing at home.[@1]

## Implementation continued after adoption

An [April 2026 review](/history/albania-no-licences-issued-2026/) reported that no licence had yet been issued, nearly three years after parliament adopted the law.[@5] That was a dated implementation finding, rather than the law’s commencement rule, which provided for entry into force fifteen days after official publication.[@1,5]

In July 2026, the cabinet ordered a [national register](/history/albania-cannabis-register-2026/) linking licence and permit information with traceability and other state systems.[@6] In October it adopted [detailed inspection rules](/history/albania-cannabis-inspections-2026/) for the authorised chain, covering activities from import and growing to export and destruction.[@7]
