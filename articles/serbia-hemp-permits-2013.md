Serbia’s detailed hemp-growing rules took effect on 1 August 2013, requiring an annual agriculture permit and registered varieties containing less than 0.3% THC. Growers had to apply before sowing and provide documents covering their land, seed and eligibility.

## The 2010 law had defined the permitted crop purposes

The [controlled-substances law](/history/serbia-controlled-substances-act-2010/) allowed licensed hemp for fibre, animal-feed seed, propagation and specified seed activities. It prohibited cannabis varieties capable of containing more than 0.3% THC and placed the permit system with the agriculture ministry.

Agriculture Minister Goran Knežević signed the implementing rules in July 2013. They explained which varieties could be grown, who could obtain permission and what paperwork a grower had to keep.

The rules connected crop permission with the existing agricultural seed and business-registration systems.

## Growers had to apply before putting seed in the ground

An application had to reach the agriculture ministry before sowing, with 30 April as the annual deadline. It identified the applicant, intended use, field, seed variety and quantity, and planned sowing and harvest dates.

Only varieties entered in the agricultural variety register and containing less than 0.3% THC qualified. Seed had to be produced, processed, packaged, declared and labelled under the seed legislation.

Businesses producing seed for further propagation also needed registration as seed producers. This added a specific condition for growers supplying future crops.

## Land and seed records formed part of the permit check

Applicants had to show their right to use the land through ownership or a lease. Depending on the arrangement, the documents also demonstrated the grower’s business registration or a production-and-purchase contract with a registered business.

The rules required proof concerning drug convictions for the individual or responsible person. An identity document, a seed-quality declaration and the seed purchase invoice formed part of the supporting paperwork.

The applicant therefore had to document the crop’s origin and purpose before receiving a permit. The controls reached both the field and the supply of planting material.

## Permission covered one production year

A hemp permit lasted for one production year, with copies sent to the health and interior ministries. Growers had to retain it for a year after issue, together with original seed invoices, declarations, packaging and labels.

Later government consultations accepted objections to reducing the THC ceiling, citing the impact on varieties already cultivated in Serbia. These licensed fibre-and-seed crops remain a distinct route within [Serbia’s cannabis rules](/country/rs/), alongside the criminal penalties for unauthorised psychoactive cannabis cultivation.
