From 2 January 1990, ganja or any herbal cannabis carried six months to three years in prison up to 5 kilograms, and three to fifteen years above that weight.[@1] Cannabis resin above 2 kilograms, and tetrahydrocannabinol above 10 grams, carried death or life imprisonment.[@1]

## Licensed ganja, then a single narcotics act

[Ganja had been grown under licence and sold in approved shops](/history/bangladesh-ban-1989/), a holdover from colonial excise law. Cultivation was prohibited in 1987 and sales in 1989. The Narcotics Control Act, dated 1 February 1990, is deemed to have been in force since 2 January 1990.[@1] It repealed the Opium Acts of 1857 and 1878, the Excise Act of 1909, the Dangerous Drugs Act of 1930 and the Opium Smoking Act of 1932, and it also repealed a narcotics ordinance of 1989.[@1] Work already done under that ordinance was treated as done under the new act.[@1]

## What people could no longer do

For a narcotic other than alcohol, the act prohibited cultivation, production, processing, carrying, transport, import, export, supply, buying, selling, keeping, storing, displaying and use.[@1] The same ban covered a plant or substance used to produce a narcotic.[@1] A licence could still cover production and trade when the drug was needed to prepare a medicine approved under an act, or for scientific research, and a pass could cover carrying or transport.[@1]

## The prison terms

The penalty table applies to those dealings other than cultivation. A fine is added to each of these bands. Someone convicted again, where the offence is not already death or life imprisonment, gets twice the maximum.[@1]

| Substance | Amount | Prison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ganja or any herbal cannabis | Up to 5 kilograms | 6 months to 3 years |
| Ganja or any herbal cannabis | Above 5 kilograms | 3 to 15 years |
| A cannabis plant of any species | Up to 25 plants | 6 months to 3 years |
| A cannabis plant of any species | More than 25 plants | 3 to 15 years |
| Cannabis resin, with opium | Up to 2 kilograms | 2 to 10 years |
| Cannabis resin, with opium | Above 2 kilograms | Death or life imprisonment |
| Tetrahydrocannabinol, with pethidine and morphine | Up to 10 grams | 2 to 10 years |
| Tetrahydrocannabinol, with pethidine and morphine | Above 10 grams | Death or life imprisonment |

## Replaced in 2018

[The 2018 narcotics act](/history/bangladesh-narcotics-control-act-2018/) replaced this one. Herbal cannabis became a Class B drug, with six months to five years up to 5 kilograms, and hashish was classed with the Class A drugs.
