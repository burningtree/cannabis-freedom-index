In 2012, Dutch coffeeshop policy acquired two additional conditions: a private-club requirement and a residence requirement. Enforcement began in the southern provinces, limiting access to residents of the Netherlands rather than everyone visiting from abroad.

The policy is often remembered as a tourist ban or “weed pass”. The [official evaluation](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2013D27815) distinguishes the two criteria, which had different purposes and did not remain together unchanged.

## Membership and residence

The private-club criterion required shops to restrict entry and sales to members and keep a verifiable membership list. The residence criterion limited eligibility to adult residents of the Netherlands. Residence was about living in the country, rather than holding Dutch nationality.

This distinction mattered for both foreign residents and Dutch citizens living elsewhere. A nationality-based description would misstate the boundary the rule drew. The requirement concerned access to tolerated retail premises, not a new legal right to consume or supply cannabis.

## A regional implementation

The WODC study examined the period from May to November 2012 in Limburg, North Brabant and Zeeland. It compared selected southern municipalities with municipalities where the conditions were not being enforced during that period.

This design reflects the policy's uneven implementation. The national criterion and the experience of a particular town were not necessarily the same thing. A visitor could not infer conditions in every city from reports about one southern border municipality.

## Changes before the end of the year

The [minister's November parliamentary letter](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-24077-293.html) set out a revised approach retaining the residence criterion while dropping the private-club requirement. Membership registration and exclusion of non-residents should therefore not be treated as one permanent, inseparable system.

The government's [toleration-policy explanation](https://www.government.nl/topics/drugs/toleration-policy-regarding-soft-drugs-and-coffee-shops) describes residence checks and municipal decisions about enforcement. The rule's existence did not ensure uniform practical exclusion in every city.

## What the restriction changed

For the [coffeeshop institution](/culture/traditions/the-coffeeshop/), the new criteria challenged the familiar image of an accessible place for international visitors. They sought to control who could enter the retail setting, rather than resolve the separate problem of its supply.

The later [regulated supply experiment](/history/netherlands-supply-experiment-2023/) addressed that other problem. Keeping access restrictions and supply reform separate helps explain how Dutch cannabis policy could become more controlled in one respect while experimenting with legal production in another.
