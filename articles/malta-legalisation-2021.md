The first country in the European Union to legalise cannabis was its smallest.
Malta's law of December 2021 had no shops in it; Germany later arrived at the same
design.

## From prison to a fine

Malta's [1939 ordinance](/history/malta-dangerous-drugs-1939/) banned possessing
and growing cannabis, and growing later carried a mandatory prison sentence, even
for one plant. In 2014, after the government went back on a promise to decriminalise,
cannabis seedlings appeared in roundabouts as a protest.

The [2015 reform](/history/malta-depenalisation-2015/) turned possession of up to
3.5 g into a fine of 50 to 100 euros, decided by a commissioner instead of a
criminal court. [Medical cannabis](/history/malta-medical-2018/) followed in 2018.

## The law

Prime Minister Robert Abela [announced](/history/malta-legalisation-announced-2021/)
the plan in February 2021. Parliament passed it on 14 December and President George
Vella signed it on 18 December.

| | Rule for adults of 18 or over |
|---|---|
| Carrying | Up to 7 g |
| Carrying 7 to 28 g | A fine of 50 to 100 euros, no criminal record |
| At home | Up to 50 g of dried cannabis |
| Growing | Four plants per household |
| Smoking in public | Still prohibited |

A new body, the Authority on the Responsible Use of Cannabis, oversees the system.

## Associations instead of shops

Nobody may sell cannabis for profit. The law's answer is the non-profit
association: up to 500 members, growing for themselves, each member receiving at
most 7 g a day and 50 g a month. Associations may not operate near schools or youth
clubs.

[Germany](/history/germany-cannabis-act-2024/) reached the same design in 2024,
after abandoning its plan for licensed shops as incompatible with European law.

## Two years without a source

The associations took time. The [first ones opened](/history/malta-first-associations-2024/)
in January 2024, so for two years the only lawful cannabis in Malta was what people
grew at home.

In [2025](/history/malta-cannabis-amendments-2025/) parliament tightened the rules
unanimously: associations must keep 250 metres from schools and face fines of up to
10,000 euros for admitting minors, and a 235-euro fine now covers smoke that bothers
the neighbours, even from inside a home.

In the index the 2021 law raised growing from 1 to 4, enforcement from 4 to 6 and
possession from 3 to 4. Access for [Malta](/country/mt/) stayed at 1 until the
associations opened, and is 4 today.
