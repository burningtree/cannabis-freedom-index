Venezuela's 1984 drug law gave consumers and people holding an immediate personal dose a treatment procedure separate from the criminal possession offence. Judges could order rehabilitation, detoxification or supervision. Growing and supplying drugs remained serious crimes carrying ten to twenty years in prison.[@1]

## Judges assessed an immediate personal dose

The consumer rule covered someone who used drugs and held a dose for immediate personal consumption. It instructed judges to assess the amount with forensic reports. The article supplied no fixed cannabis gram ceiling.[@1]

The separate possession offence applied to holdings for purposes outside personal consumption and the other specified lawful or trafficking activities. That distinction meant the purpose of holding cannabis mattered to the route through the law.[@1]

## Treatment could involve confinement

The security measures included admission to a rehabilitation or specialised therapy centre, detoxification, social readaptation and supervision. A foreign consumer who was not resident could be expelled from the country.[@1]

Admission meant living in an establishment for treatment. Detoxification could take place with or without admission. For an occasional consumer, supervision meant referral to specialists to guide their conduct and prevent further use.[@1]

People whose cases entered this procedure therefore remained subject to state intervention. The change concerned how they were dealt with: treatment and supervision occupied a separate part of the law from prison sentences for drug offences.[@1]

## Growing and supply kept long prison terms

The law set ten to twenty years for trafficking, supplying and producing drugs. Sowing, growing and related dealings in drug plants or seeds had the same range. Possession outside the personal-use route and other listed categories carried six to ten years.[@1]

The law also repealed the older 1934 drug statute and the drug provisions of the Penal Code. Those included the [1964 rules](/history/venezuela-cannabis-prison-code-1964/) expressly naming marijuana.[@1]

## Missing facilities undermined the treatment route

The law required the state to create orientation and rehabilitation centres within a year. When lawmakers prepared the [1993 reform](/history/venezuela-personal-dose-1993/), their explanatory memorandum said the necessary facilities and professional posts had not been supplied. They reported that this had led to consumers being criminalised in practice.[@1,2]

The later reform added twenty-gram cannabis wording and reduced the separate possession sentence. Its own account shows why the treatment route must be traced back to 1984, together with the failure to provide what judges needed to use it. These stages lead into the later law described in the [Venezuela profile](/country/ve/).[@2]
