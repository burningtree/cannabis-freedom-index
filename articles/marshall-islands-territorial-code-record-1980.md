The Marshall Islands had cannabis restrictions before its national narcotics law of 1987. The territorial code published in 1980 records a comparatively small penalty for the smallest marijuana offences: a fine of no more than $50 for possessing an ounce or less, with the same rule for giving away that amount without payment.[@1,2]

## The rules reached the Marshall Islands

The drug chapter belonged to the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands. The code’s geographical clause expressly covered the Marshall Islands alongside the Caroline Islands and the Mariana Islands other than Guam. Its rules therefore form part of the islands’ earlier legal history.[@2]

The republic’s Constitution, effective from 1 May 1979, continued existing laws subject to the Constitution until they were repealed, revoked or amended. Rights, obligations and liabilities under those laws also continued. Self-government came with a rule for carrying earlier law forward.[@3]

## An ounce separated fines from possible prison

The controlled-substances chapter expressly listed marijuana and THC in Schedule I, its most restricted schedule. For marijuana possession, however, it set separate penalties according to quantity.[@1]

An ounce or less brought only the maximum $50 fine. More than an ounce but less than 2.2 pounds could bring up to three months in prison, a $500 fine, or both. At 2.2 pounds or more, possession could bring up to a year and $1,000, while creating a presumption of trafficking that the defendant could challenge.[@1]

## A small gift received the possession penalty

The same chapter generally prohibited unauthorised manufacture and delivery. Marijuana trafficking could bring up to five years and $5,000. But someone giving away no more than an ounce without payment faced the small-possession fine instead.[@1]

First-time possession defendants also had a possible route through probation. With their consent, a court could postpone a finding of guilt. Successful completion brought dismissal, and the person could then ask for the arrest and case records to be cleared, apart from a confidential court record used to check eligibility.[@1]

## National legislation followed

The [national narcotics law took effect in March 1987](/history/marshall-islands-narcotics-act-1987/). The later consolidated law retains professional and prescription exceptions while prohibiting ordinary cannabis possession, growing and distribution. Its marijuana penalties distinguish quantity and previous convictions.[@4]

The [Marshall Islands profile](/country/mh/) describes that later system. The earlier territorial code supplies a different historical reference point: small possession and a small unpaid gift were both offences carrying a fine alone.[@1,4]
