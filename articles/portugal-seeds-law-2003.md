Portugal is known for the law that stopped treating drug users as criminals. Two years
later it passed a smaller one that made a cannabis seed a controlled substance.

## The gap it closed

The 1961 Single Convention defines cannabis as the flowering tops of the plant. Seeds
are left out, and in many countries that ban growing they can be bought and sold
freely. Portugal's 1993 drug law followed the convention, so seeds were outside it.

## The law

Law 47/2003, of 22 August 2003, was the tenth amendment to the 1993 law. It added an
entry to Table I-C, the table that holds cannabis:

> cannabis seeds not intended for sowing

The same law added the stimulant PMMA to another table. It took effect the day after
publication.

The wording follows the hemp rules. Seed sold for sowing means certified seed of
approved industrial varieties, which farmers may buy. Everything else is now in the
drug table.

## What it means in practice

Possessing cannabis seeds is an offence in Portugal, except certified hemp seed, and
ordering them from abroad is unlawful for residents as well. A grower therefore
commits an offence before planting anything.

## How it sits with decriminalisation

The [2001 reform](/history/portugal-decriminalisation-2001/) covered using and holding
a drug for one's own use, up to ten days' supply. It never extended to producing it.
Home cultivation, however small, can still be prosecuted, and the seeds law pushes the
line one step further back in [Portugal](/country/pt/).
