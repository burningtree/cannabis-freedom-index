Oman’s 1999 drug law set a one-year prison minimum for personal drug holdings and growing. It also allowed courts to order treatment for proved dependence and protected people who sought treatment before being caught using or prosecuted.[@1]

## Personal holdings already carried prison

The [1974 penal code](/history/oman-personal-drug-penalties-code-1974/) had punished holding or buying narcotic drugs for personal use with three months to two years and a fine. Supplying someone else, including without payment, carried three to ten years.[@2]

The new law took effect on 15 March 1999. Its personal-use rule prescribed at least one year in prison and a fine of five hundred to three thousand rials. It covered possession, buying, importing, producing and growing listed plants for personal use outside the authorised circumstances.[@1]

Cannabis and cannabis resin appeared in the drug schedules. Male and female cannabis plants were expressly listed among prohibited crops, with stalk fibres and seeds treated against germination exempted.[@1]

## Treatment could replace the personal-use sentence

A court could send someone proved dependent to a treatment centre instead of imposing the personal-use penalty. It could also order attendance at specialist psychological and social treatment under a professional’s programme.[@1]

Residential treatment lasted between six months and one year. Release required a court decision on a committee’s recommendation. The law limited repeat placements, and unsuccessful treatment or breaches could return the case to the court for sentencing.[@1]

## Seeking help before prosecution had its own protection

The law protected people who approached the authorities for treatment voluntarily or at the request of a spouse or specified close relatives. The protection excluded people already caught using or prosecuted for that offence.[@1]

Treatment information was confidential. The law separately punished disclosure of information obtained through work or its use for private benefit.[@1]

## Supply offences carried much heavier penalties

Specified import, production and growing offences could bring death or life imprisonment, with mandatory death in listed aggravated cases. A separate trading offence began at ten years. Unpaid supply for consumption carried five to ten years.[@1]

The [2015 amendments](/history/oman-death-penalty-widened-2015/) made smuggling for trade a mandatory-death case and expressly set the personal-use range at one to three years. In [2026 the replacement law](/history/oman-narcotics-law-2026/) retained treatment alternatives while raising personal-use fines and supply sentences. Those later rules are described in the [Oman profile](/country/om/).[@3,4]
