On 27 January 1938, Czechoslovakia enacted its Opium Act, Law No. 29/1938. The measure implemented three international drug-control agreements and created the country's first comprehensive modern framework for narcotic substances, including Indian hemp.

## International rules become domestic law

The act implemented the International Opium Conventions of 1912 and 1925 and the 1931 convention limiting manufacture and regulating distribution of narcotic drugs. Earlier Czechoslovak law had retained rules inherited from Austria-Hungary that mainly addressed poisons, medicines and particular forms of dangerous sale rather than a comprehensive drug-control system.

The government's historical review describes the Opium Act as a major legislative step. It divided narcotic substances into four groups, defined key terms and introduced special authorisations, official inspections in pharmacies and dedicated registers.

## A broad criminal offence

Without authorisation, intentionally manufacturing, preparing, processing, possessing, holding for sale, circulating, offering, importing, exporting or transiting a controlled substance was an offence. The ordinary penalty was three months to three years in custody plus a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 crowns.

Large-scale or commercial offending, or conduct causing serious injury, death or wider danger to life or health, could bring one to five years and a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 crowns. The law also punished making, obtaining, keeping or transferring equipment clearly intended for unauthorised manufacture or processing.

## Cannabis under control

The cannabis history identifies Indian hemp as subject to the new regime. Manufacture, trade and possession therefore moved from the unregulated baseline into a permit system. Authorised medical handling remained possible through the controlled pharmacy framework; this was prohibition with exceptions, not an absolute ban on every use.

The act's basic approach endured as drug offences later moved into general criminal codes. The [1962 code](/history/czechoslovakia-criminal-code-1962/) continued broad criminal controls under the communist state.

## What moved in the index

The first national prohibition produced the timeline's largest fall for [Czechia](/country/cz/): possession and cultivation dropped from 10 to 2, enforcement from 10 to 3, sharing from 10 to 1, consumption from 10 to 2 and access from 8 to 1. The remaining access point reflects authorised medical channels under the controlled system.
