Guatemala’s congressional legislation and constitutional committee opposed a bill that would permit private adult cannabis use, five home plants and licensed commercial supply. Prensa Libre reported the rejection in November 2016, with sponsor Álvaro Velásquez promising to keep seeking support.

## The bill offered household growing and commercial licences

The [proposal brought before Congress in April](/history/guatemala-adult-cannabis-bill-2016/) would let adults use cannabis privately and grow up to five plants at home for personal or family consumption.

Businesses would need health-ministry licences for commercial production, distribution and sales. The proposed rules also required identification of products by variety and THC strength and provided for quality controls.

A 10% sales tax would support hospitals and police education programmes in equal shares. Household growing for domestic consumption would be exempt, giving the proposal separate rules for family use and commercial supply.

## The committee invoked health duties and treaty commitments

The committee, led by Oliverio García Rodas, cited constitutional responsibilities concerning health and action against drug dependence. It also invoked Guatemala’s international drug-treaty commitments.

Velásquez criticised the committee’s legal approach and pointed to reform discussions elsewhere. He said he would continue trying to advance the proposal.

The dispute concerned whether lawmakers should replace criminal restrictions with a regulated cannabis system. For users and growers, the bill offered defined permissions covering activities that existing drug offences punished with prison and fines.

## Existing possession and cultivation penalties continued

Under the [1992 law](/history/guatemala-ley-contra-la-narcoactividad-1992/), acquiring or possessing drugs for personal consumption carried four months to two years in prison and a fine.

Personal use depended on a reasonable amount for immediate consumption and circumstances supporting that purpose. The law specified no fixed cannabis allowance in grams.

Unauthorised cultivation carried five to twenty years plus a fine, while trafficking carried twelve to twenty years plus a fine. The proposed cannabis exceptions would have altered those risks for adults acting within the new rules.

## Velásquez returned to medical access

In December, Prensa Libre described a renewed proposal focused on medicinal use, pharmacy supply, medical supervision and scientific research. It also envisaged a regulator overseeing cannabis activities.

A medical cannabis bill received an [adverse health-committee opinion in 2017](/history/guatemala-medical-bill-rejected-2017/). The sequence shows reformers pursuing different supply systems while [Guatemala’s existing drug offences](/country/gt/) remained in place.
