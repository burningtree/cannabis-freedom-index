In Nordally v Attorney General, reported at 1986 MR 204, the Supreme Court
held that the Dangerous Drugs Act's statutory exclusion of bail was
inconsistent with the Constitution.[@1]

It protected the judiciary's role in deciding bail and other matters
incidental to criminal proceedings against executive interference.[@1]

This was a procedural safeguard within the drug-control system, not an
exemption from cannabis offences or an automatic entitlement to
release.[@1]

The ruling is documented retrospectively in the Privy Council's 2006
Khoyratty judgment, which has its own
article.(/history/mauritius-drug-bail-khoyratty-ruling-2006/)
