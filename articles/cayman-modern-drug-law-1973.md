Cayman's replacement drug law prohibited unauthorised possession,
consumption, cultivation and supply of cannabis, which it listed as
ganja.[@1]

Possession or growing carried imprisonment of up to five years with hard
labour on a first conviction, rising to ten years on a repeat conviction,
with additional fines possible.[@1]

The law replaced an earlier Dangerous Drugs Law and described its purpose
as bringing drug control into line with international standards; the
official legislation history dates commencement to 12 December 1973.[@1,2]
