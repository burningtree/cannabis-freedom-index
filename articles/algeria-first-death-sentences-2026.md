Algeria has not executed anyone since 1993. On 5 October 2026 a court in Algiers
sentenced nine people to death for drug trafficking, the first use of a law passed
fifteen months earlier.

## How the law got here

Hashish has been prohibited in Algeria since
[French decrees of 1916](/history/algeria-french-decrees-1916/). The country's own
[drug law of 2004](/history/algeria-law-04-18-2004/) banned growing, trading and
possessing cannabis, and let a user who accepted treatment avoid prosecution.

Two amendments then raised the stakes. In
[2023](/history/algeria-law-23-05-2023/) personal use was set at two months to two
years in prison, and trafficking by an organised gang at life. On
[1 July 2025](/history/algeria-law-25-03-2025/) Law 25-03 added the death penalty
for aggravated cases:

- synthetic-drug offences already punishable by life;
- trafficking that causes death or serious harm to public health;
- trafficking by a transnational organised group;
- trafficking aimed at national security;
- use or threat of a firearm.

## The case

The Algiers criminal court convicted nine people of importing, transporting and
storing drugs for sale as an organised group: 113 kg of cocaine and 225,030 tablets
of pregabalin. Three were in court. Six were tried in their absence. The charges
also included smuggling, identity fraud and money laundering.

No cannabis was involved.

## The signal

Two weeks earlier, on 22 September, the justice minister, Lotfi Boudjemaa, had said
in Oran that the death penalty would be "activated" against traffickers, citing the
scale of hard-drug seizures. He added that sentences would be carried out only after
every appeal was exhausted.

Days before the verdict the national assembly extended the death penalty to arson
and to child abduction with serious violence. A petition signed by several dozen
citizens asked that the moratorium on executions be kept.

## What it means for cannabis

Directly, nothing changed: the sentences concern cocaine and a prescription drug,
and the law was already in force. But the aggravating circumstances are not tied to
a substance: trafficking by a transnational organised group qualifies whatever the
drug.

[Algeria](/country/dz/) already scores close to zero on the index, and the entry
moves no score. It marks the point at which a penalty that existed on paper began
to be imposed.
