In March 2019 Ethiopia nearly got its first legal cannabis grow — and then the internet
found out. Africana Cannabis Holding, a venture of Ethiopian and Canadian investors,
proposed a farm and factory in Dembecha, in the Amhara region, to grow cannabis for
medicine and export.[@1]

When the local administration posted news of a meeting with residents, protests spread
online.[@1] The health minister, Amir Aman, said growing cannabis for medical purposes
had neither been recognised nor approved, and that such requests had been and would be
denied.[@1] The head of the Investment Commission said no permit to grow cannabis in
Ethiopia would be issued.[@2]

The refusal drew the line the code keeps: no THC threshold, no licence, no fibre
exemption — all forms, including CBD and hemp, count as illicit.[@2] The outcry ended
the only proposed legal grow, leaving Shashemene's unlicensed gardens as the country's
real cannabis sector.
