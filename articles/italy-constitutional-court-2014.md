On 12 February 2014 Italy's Constitutional Court struck down the drug law that had treated
cannabis like heroin for eight years. It did not rule on whether the law was too harsh. It ruled
that Parliament had passed it the wrong way.

## A drug law inside an Olympic decree

At the end of 2005 the government issued an emergency decree, number 272, on security and
funding for the coming Winter Olympics. One article of it dealt with drugs, and only to
keep addicts who reoffended in treatment programmes. When Parliament converted the decree into
law in February 2006, it added twenty-three further articles. Together they rewrote the
country's drug law, the 1990 consolidated text, and merged the separate schedules for "light"
and "heavy" drugs into one. This was the [Fini-Giovanardi law](/history/italy-fini-giovanardi-2006/).

| Producing or selling cannabis | Prison | Fine |
|---|---|---|
| Before 2006 | 2 to 6 years | €5,164 to €77,468 |
| Under the 2006 law | 6 to 20 years | €26,000 to €260,000 |

## The case

The question came from the Court of Cassation, whose third criminal section was hearing an appeal
in a drug case. In June 2013 it asked the Constitutional Court whether the 2006 changes were
valid. A law converting a decree, it argued, may only contain what belongs to the decree's
subject and purpose, under article 77 of the Constitution. These amendments had no such link.

The court agreed. A conversion law, it held, cannot be opened to any content at all; otherwise
the fast procedure meant for emergencies could be used for other ends, bypassing the ordinary
debate in Parliament. Adding unrelated articles was an abuse of the power to convert.

## What came back

Because the 2006 provisions had never validly replaced the old ones, the court said, the
earlier text of article 73 and its schedules applied again. That was the
[1990 law](/history/italy-criminalisation-1990/) as changed by the
[1993 referendum](/history/italy-referendum-1993/): possession for personal use an
administrative offence, and cannabis once more in a lighter class than heroin or cocaine. Il
Post wrote that the struck-down law had contributed decisively to the overcrowding of Italian
prisons.

The court left it to ordinary judges to make sure that no defendant was worse off as a result.
At the end of May 2014 the [Court of Cassation ruled](/history/italy-resentencing-ruling-2014/)
that people imprisoned under the annulled provisions had the right to be resentenced. Grazia Zuffa, of the Italian group Forum Droghe,
wrote that this could affect about 10,000 prisoners held for cannabis offences.

## Afterwards

The ruling restored a distinction; it legalised nothing. Producing and selling cannabis remained
crimes in [Italy](/country/it/), and a later attempt to change that by referendum was
[blocked by the same court](/history/italy-referendum-blocked-2022/) in 2022.
