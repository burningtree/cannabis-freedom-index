Sierra Leone's 2008 drug law gave officers powers to enter land without a warrant and destroy unlawfully cultivated or wild cannabis, subject to evidence and urgency safeguards.[@1] It created a national enforcement agency and replaced the colonial Dangerous Drugs Act.[@1]

## A cocaine flight prompted urgent legislation

A South American aircraft carrying cocaine landed in Sierra Leone on 13 July 2008.[@2] According to the Library of Congress's contemporary account, the incident exposed outdated drug laws and helped prompt parliament to act under a presidential certificate of urgency.[@2]

The bill was drafted with United Nations assistance and won overwhelming parliamentary support.[@2] Its effects extended beyond the cocaine trade: the resulting law specifically addressed cannabis crops, as well as possession, use and other drug offences.[@1]

Cannabis already faced overlapping controls.[@3] The [2001 pharmacy law](/history/sierra-leone-pharmacy-drugs-act-2001/) prohibited smoking and unauthorised possession, and required written permission to cultivate narcotic-producing plants.[@3]

## Officers could destroy crops before a court case

The crop power covered cannabis growing wild as well as plants being cultivated unlawfully.[@1] An authorised officer could enter the land without a warrant, uproot the plants and destroy them.[@1]

Before doing so, the officer had to reasonably believe that samples and other evidence had been collected to establish the identity and location of the unlawful crop.[@1] Immediate action also had to appear necessary to prevent harvesting, removal, concealment or use, or danger to someone's life or safety, before normal seizure and disposal could occur.[@1]

For growers, those rules meant the crop could be lost during the investigation, before any trial had decided their guilt.[@1]

## Courts had sentencing discretion

The Act set at least five years in prison for unlawful possession and use of a drug.[@1] It also allowed a shorter term or a fine when the circumstances would make that punishment harsh or unconscionable, and provided treatment alternatives for minors.[@1]

Those exceptions mattered alongside the prison terms: the published law gave judges choices that a summary describing every possession sentence as mandatory would miss.[@1]

## The start date needed another law

The original Act left its commencement date blank.[@1] A [September amendment](/history/sierra-leone-drugs-commencement-amendment-2008/) then let the president bring it into operation through a legal order.[@4]

The law repealed the [colonial Act](/history/sierra-leone-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1926/), but preserved earlier regulations and pending proceedings.[@1] In [Sierra Leone](/country/sl/), the transition carried parts of the old controls into a new national enforcement system.[@1]
