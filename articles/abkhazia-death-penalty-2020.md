Abkhazia’s highest drug-supply and import penalty tiers took effect on 1 March 2020, allowing life imprisonment or death for the most serious cases. An amendment later that March limited those penalties to cases without mitigating circumstances, a restriction the Supreme Court reinforced in 2025.[@1,3]

## Deputies argued for harsher punishment

Deputy Dmitry Dbar promoted the legislation as a response to overdose deaths and drug couriers arriving in Abkhazia. Reporting on the debate described a moratorium on executions dating from 1993, making the proposed capital drug penalties a significant departure in the drug-law debate.[@2]

The changes followed existing prison offences. The [personal-possession rule introduced in 2012](/history/abkhazia-personal-possession-code-2012/) prescribed one to three years in its ordinary tier, while supply already carried longer terms and fines.[@1]

## The highest tier covered several circumstances

The supply offence’s highest tier covered organised-group activity, especially large quantities, use of an official position and offences involving a child under fourteen. The import offence had its own highest tier for organised groups, especially large quantities and use of an official position.[@1]

The code expressly notes that these penalty tiers commenced on 1 March 2020. The [later March amendment](/history/abkhazia-capital-drug-mitigation-2020/) recast the punishment as fifteen years with a fine, with life or death available where mitigating circumstances were absent.[@1]

That qualification mattered at sentencing: the presence of mitigation changed which punishments the court could impose.[@1]

## Judges had to consider mitigation

In its [October 2025 guidance](/history/abkhazia-drug-court-guidance-2025/), the Supreme Court expressly told judges that they could impose neither life imprisonment nor death where mitigating circumstances existed. It also explained further sentencing reductions for specified cooperation circumstances.[@3]

Separate general rules excluded women, people who committed the offence while under eighteen and men aged sixty-five by sentencing from the death penalty. The code also allowed presidential commutation of a death sentence.[@1]

## Prosecutors later questioned the results

At a March 2023 coordination meeting, prosecutor’s-office official Daur Amichba argued that harsher drug penalties had not significantly reduced cases or the number of users. He proposed a [central drug-control body](/history/abkhazia-prosecutor-drug-body-proposal-2023/) and said enforcement mostly reached consumers and small dealers.[@4]

His criticism became part of the continuing debate over enforcement. The [Abkhazia profile](/country/xa/) separates personal possession, growing and use from the most serious supply and import cases.
