On October 27, 1970, East Germany's *Neues Deutschland* published Günter Böhme's article under a headline historian Ned Richardson-Little translates as “Hashers don't read Das Kapital.” The party newspaper used cannabis to draw a boundary between disciplined socialist politics and a Western counterculture it portrayed as distracted and self-destructive.

The archive's accessible heading, date, byline and preview confirm the item. The complete newspaper text requires an archive subscription; the interpretation here follows Richardson-Little's examined chapter rather than claiming a new full-text reading of the original.

## A dispute within the Left

Some West German radicals regarded shared cannabis smoking as an expression of solidarity and a rejection of conventional social life. Hashish pipes became visible symbols within parts of the extra-parliamentary opposition during the student revolt.

For East Germany's ruling Socialist Unity Party, that claim inverted the proper relationship between politics and pleasure. Intoxication diverted people from building socialism. The disagreement complicates any assumption that cannabis acceptance followed automatically from left-wing political commitments.

## Words borrowed across the divide

Richardson-Little notes that the phrase came from the West German magazine *konkret*. Its reuse by the East German press shows an argument crossing the border even as it served opposing political settings.

The chapter does not establish the original *konkret* item's date, and it has not been independently examined here. October 27 dates Böhme's newspaper article, not the invention of the phrase or the first German use of [Hasch](/culture/traditions/hasch-in-the-gdr/).

## A wider propaganda vocabulary

Earlier GDR reporting had repeated claims of cannabis-induced madness and later adopted the idea that hashish led inevitably to harder drugs. Richardson-Little traces similarities with Western prohibitionist narratives, despite their different ideological explanations.

This article belongs to that history of public persuasion. The newspaper's portrayal of drug users is not treated as a clinical finding or a representative description of West German youth.

## A domestic image of a foreign market

Cannabis was hard to obtain inside East Germany, yet [transit seizures](/history/germany-gdr-transit-hashish-seizures-1970/) were increasing as suppliers sought customers in West Berlin. The public symbolism and the border problem developed together.
