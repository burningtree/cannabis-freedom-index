Gibraltar announced on 8 September 2023 that GHA doctors had completed their cannabis training and that a supply agreement with Cantourage UK had been reached, subject to final details.[@1] The announcement concerned prescribing products for patients with specified conditions through the public health service.[@1]

## Trained doctors were central to access

The government said training had been completed before the supply announcement.[@1] Cantourage UK co-founder Joshua Cuby described a collaboration involving doctors, nurses, a medical director and international growers, intended to help eligible patients whose conventional therapies had failed.[@1]

Public-health minister John Cortes said work to make the products available had been under way for some time.[@1] The agreement followed the [2019 prescribing rules](/history/gibraltar-medicinal-cannabis-regulations-2019/), which created a defined role for trained GHA cannabis practitioners.[@2]

## The rules limited conditions and products

The regulations identified four groups of conditions: treatment-resistant multiple-sclerosis spasticity, severe refractory epilepsy, severe life-altering pain, and intractable nausea or vomiting associated with chemotherapy.[@2] Products needed GHA approval, and the rules prohibited administering them by smoking.[@2]

The regulations also distinguished a specified cannabis-extract mouth spray from the wider category of approved cannabis-based medicinal products.[@2] Access therefore depended on the practitioner, the patient’s condition and the approved product, with controlled-drug handling requirements around the supply.[@2]

## The industry law awaited commencement

Parliament had already approved a [medicinal cannabis industry law in 2021](/history/gibraltar-medicinal-cannabis-act-2021/).[@3] It described licences for production, import, export, marketing and supply, and provided for commencement by ministerial notice.[@3]

The official version remains marked Not In Force.[@3] The 2023 government announcement instead concerned the prescribing arrangement with trained GHA doctors and an identified supplier.[@1] The [Gibraltar profile](/country/gi/) follows the dispensing regulations alongside that announcement when describing patient access.[@1,2]

## Patients also faced the driving rules

The traffic law separately made exceeding a specified controlled-drug limit an offence for drivers and people in charge of vehicles on roads or other public places.[@4] It allowed a defence for lawfully prescribed or supplied medicine taken in accordance with directions, including advice about the interval before driving.[@4]

That defence depended on those conditions being met, while driving when unfit through drugs remained a separate offence.[@4] Medical prescribing and the ability to drive therefore required separate attention under Gibraltar’s rules.[@2,4]
