The United States' attempt to decide whether cannabis generally belongs in
Schedule III paused on 29 September 2026. The interruption concerned the hearing
record, not the narrower medical-cannabis change already in force.

## Two separate tracks

In [April 2026](/history/us-medical-schedule-iii-2026/), the federal government
moved FDA-approved cannabis products and cannabis covered by qualifying state
medical licences from Schedule I to Schedule III. Unlicensed cannabis and products
in state adult-use systems remained in Schedule I.

A separate administrative hearing began on 29 June to consider whether marijuana
more broadly should move to Schedule III. The DEA's public docket contains the
hearing transcripts and the judge's procedural orders. After the hearing, the
chief administrative law judge was to make a recommendation; that recommendation
would not itself be the final agency decision.

## The new report

On 23 September, the Government Accountability Office issued a report about
federal drug scheduling. Parties opposing rescheduling asked the judge to reopen
the evidentiary record so the report could be added.

Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius stayed the post-hearing proceedings
on 29 September while that request was considered. He gave the federal government
until 13 October to respond on whether the record should reopen. The order delayed
the next stage rather than deciding the scheduling question.

## What the pause did not do

The stay did not reverse the April medical order. Qualifying state-licensed
medical cannabis and FDA-approved products remain in Schedule III under that
order. Cannabis outside those categories remains in Schedule I while the broader
proceeding is unresolved.

Nor did the hearing propose national adult-use legalisation. Scheduling under the
Controlled Substances Act and the legality of state markets are connected but
different questions. A move to Schedule III would loosen federal control without
by itself creating a nationwide licensed recreational market.

## In the index

Nothing moved in the [United States](/country/us/) score. The September order was
a procedural delay in a pending federal case, and it left both the April medical
change and the state-by-state adult-use landscape in place.
