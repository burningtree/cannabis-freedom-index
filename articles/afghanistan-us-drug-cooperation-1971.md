Afghanistan's foreign minister told his American counterpart in October
1971 that hashish cultivation was already prohibited, but the government
struggled to enforce the prohibition. He promised cooperation as the
United States pressed for a wider effort against drugs.

## The talks began with drought and economic pressure

Musa Shafiq met Secretary of State William Rogers in New York on
13 October. Afghanistan was suffering a severe drought, and Shafiq
described damage across the economy and a water table at its lowest
level in living memory.

He also sought help rescheduling debt. Afghanistan wanted to preserve
its credit and independence while avoiding deeper financial dependence
on the neighbouring Soviet Union. Drug cooperation was therefore part
of a broader conversation about assistance and the country's position
between larger powers.

## Foreign visitors brought the drug trade into the discussion

Rogers introduced drugs by asking whether hippies continued to pose
a problem. Shafiq said foreign visitors had caused little concern in
earlier years, but that growing numbers of young travellers were
overstaying and engaging in practices the authorities considered harmful.

Rogers warned that a country's reputation could be shaped by its
attitude to drugs. He pointed to Turkey's poppy cultivation and said
successful American-backed programmes there might encourage buyers to
look towards Afghanistan instead.

The concern connected visitors' demand inside the country with a larger
international market. Washington wanted cooperation before changes in
one source country shifted the business to another.

## Prohibition met geography and farmers' incomes

Shafiq said hashish and opium cultivation were prohibited under Islamic
law because they were intoxicants. He acknowledged that enforcement
had been uneven and described particular difficulties in remote mountain
areas where drugs were grown.

Ambassador Malikyar raised the income problem: poppy provided cash in poor,
isolated communities, and those farmers needed another way to earn.
The discussion therefore connected enforcement with replacing a rural
livelihood.

## A stronger commitment followed the next year

By December 1972, Shafiq had become prime minister. An American
memorandum recorded his parliamentary promise of stronger measures
against trafficking and a proposed programme against illicit opium
cultivation with foreign help.

The [rising smuggler arrests](/history/afghanistan-smuggling-crackdown-1972/)
and [renewed prohibition reported in 1973](/history/afghanistan-ban-1973/)
followed this earlier diplomatic exchange. It shows how foreign demand,
enforcement difficulties and the need for outside support had already
become intertwined in [Afghanistan's cannabis history](/country/af/).
