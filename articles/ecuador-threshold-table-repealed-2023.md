On his second day in office, 24 November 2023, President Daniel Noboa signed a decree ordering the end of the [personal-use table of 2013](/history/ecuador-threshold-table-2013/), which had treated up to 10 g of cannabis as an amount for a person's own use. He announced it with a video of himself tearing up a sheet of paper and the caption "Today the drug table goes". It had been a campaign promise.

## The decree

The decree has two operative articles. The first tells the Interior Ministry to repeal Resolution 001-CONSEP-CD-2013 and its amendments. The second tells the Interior and Health ministries to run coordinated programmes of information, prevention and control, and to offer treatment and rehabilitation to occasional, habitual and problem users.

The Presidency said the table "encouraged micro-trafficking" and was "a harmful element for Ecuadorian society".

## What the lawyers said

Article 364 of the constitution was not changed: it still says that addictions are a public health problem and that users may in no case be criminalised. Expreso collected the objections the next day.

- Ismael Quintana, a constitutional lawyer, said the decree breached Constitutional Court ruling 7-17-CN/19 of April 2019, which according to the paper had upheld the table.
- Carlos Salazar said the table stayed in force until the ministry formally repealed the 2013 resolution, and that changes to the criminal code were for the National Assembly.
- A prosecutor, unnamed, said the trafficking article of the criminal code is hard to apply without the table as a reference, since the code itself says the amounts for personal use are set by regulation.

## What replaced it

No new amounts were set. Extra reported in 2025 that the National Court of Justice had told judges and prosecutors to weigh each case as a whole, looking at psychosocial reports, the person's circumstances and the substance, and that the Interior Ministry's guide gives criteria but no quantities. The [court guidance](/history/ecuador-table-repealed-court-guidance-2023/) and the [2025 Solidarity Law](/history/ecuador-solidarity-law-2025/), which speaks of "parameters" where the old rule spoke of grams, have their own entries.

## Why the index moved

The three scores that rose in 2013 fall back by one point each. Possession goes from 3 to 2, enforcement from 4 to 3 and consumption from 3 to 2, because a person stopped with a few grams no longer has a published amount to rely on and has to persuade a prosecutor and a judge that it was for their own use. Access stays at 1, since the [medical law of 2019](/history/ecuador-medical-2019/) was not touched. The total moves from 23 to 18.
