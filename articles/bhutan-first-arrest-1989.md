Bhutan's first recorded arrest for drug abuse came remarkably late. On 29 June 1989 police in Gelephu arrested a man for using cannabis, a plant that grew freely and was better known locally as animal feed.

## A plant before a drug

Cannabis grows prolifically in [Bhutan](/country/bt/). It had traditional practical uses: farmers fed it to pigs to fatten them, and its fibre was sometimes used for textiles. The Dzongkha name recorded for cannabis fibre is *kenam*.

According to the historical account collected by Wikipedia, awareness of the plant as an intoxicant was limited. Some officials later connected wider knowledge of drug use with the arrival of television in 1999. The International Narcotics Control Board reported in 2002 that Bhutan had no history of cannabis abuse, while also noting growing government concern about youth drug use.

## The Gelephu arrest

The Bhutanese dates the first drug-abuse arrest to 29 June 1989. Police caught a man using marijuana in Gelephu, a town near the Indian border. The source does not name him or describe a court case, sentence or the amount involved.

That makes the event important but narrow. It documents the first known use of criminal enforcement, not necessarily the first cannabis use in the country and not the enactment of the law under which the arrest occurred.

## Enforcement grows

The later figures show the distance between that single case and the enforcement system that followed. The Bhutanese reported 817 drug-related arrests from 1989 through 2005. Wikipedia records cannabis seizures rising from 4 kilograms in 2010 to 75 kilograms in 2011. Those numbers cover different measures and should not be treated as a continuous statistical series.

Modern Bhutanese law still prohibits cannabis, even though the plant grows wild. Small-scale users can be directed into treatment and counselling under the current framework, while trafficking attracts serious prison terms.

## Why the index did not move

The [1989 arrest](/history/bhutan-first-arrest-1989/) is evidence that prohibition was being enforced, but the sources read do not establish that the law or its penalties changed that day. The Cannabis Freedom Index therefore records it for context and leaves every Bhutan score unchanged.
