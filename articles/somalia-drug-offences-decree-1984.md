In May 1984 Somalia's military government rewrote the price of the drug trade. Law 25
set ten years in prison and a 50,000-shilling fine for unlawful manufacture,
production, import, dealing or supplying another person — a flat decade regardless of
drug or quantity.[@1]

The decree reached into the places of use as well. Hosting a venue for drug consumption
carried five years and a fine; entering it to consume carried three years and a
fine.[@1] Drug cases went to the National Security Court and its regional branches, with
transitional rules for pending cases and earlier offences kept under their earlier
penalties.[@1]

The 1984 text still anchors the profile's supply and premises offences four decades
later — a statutory baseline from a state that no longer exists in the same form, cited
because nothing has verifiably replaced it.
