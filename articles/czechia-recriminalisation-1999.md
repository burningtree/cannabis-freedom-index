On 1 January 1999, Czechia restored criminal liability for possessing drugs for personal use. The amendment ended a period that began in 1990, when possession for oneself had been removed from the criminal offence.

## The 1990 distinction

The post-communist [1990 amendment](/history/czechoslovakia-decriminalisation-1990/) changed the offence so that punishable possession had to be carried out for another person. Production, import, export and supplying others remained criminal, but possession solely for the holder's own use was outside that offence.

The government later described the reversal as one of the significant drug-policy developments of the 1990s. Czech society had become more directly confronted with drug distribution and use, and the proposal to criminalise personal possession prompted a major debate.

## “Greater than small”

The amendment added section 187a to the criminal code. It made possessing a narcotic or psychotropic substance for personal use a crime when the amount was “greater than small.” Possession below that line remained an administrative offence, while consumption itself was not made a separate crime.

The statute did not define the phrase numerically. The Adiktologie legal history explains that police and prosecutors had indicative instructions, but courts were not bound by them and had to consider the circumstances of individual cases. The Czech section of the drug-liberalisation history describes judicial practice as generally using a multiple of an ordinary single dose.

## One rule for different drugs

The government review notes that the amended law did not distinguish among drugs according to health or social risk. It also records the later PAD study's conclusion that criminalising personal possession did not fulfil its proponents' expectations, while the worst predictions of opponents were not confirmed either.

More explicit cannabis thresholds arrived with the [2010 criminal code and regulation](/history/czechia-thresholds-2010/).

## What moved in the index

Recriminalisation lowered [Czechia's](/country/cz/) possession score from 3 to 2, enforcement from 5 to 4 and consumption from 4 to 3. Cultivation stayed at 1, sharing at 1 and access at 0. Small possession remained administrative, so the scores did not return to the lowest possible level, but the possibility of prosecution above an undefined line materially reduced freedom.
