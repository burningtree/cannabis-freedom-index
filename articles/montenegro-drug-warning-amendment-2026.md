Montenegro’s amended drug law took effect on 7 August 2026, strengthening early warnings about new psychoactive substances, European information-sharing and restrictions on advertising drugs.[@1,2] An individual whose advertising encourages prohibited drug activities can face the existing €100–€2,000 misdemeanour fine band.[@1] The law also expressly defines industrial hemp as cannabis with no more than 0.2% THC that is outside the controlled-drug list.[@1]

## Warning systems were tied to European reporting

The law describes an early-warning system that detects new psychoactive substances and exchanges information about their production, sale, use and risks with European authorities.[@1] Its national information network collects and analyses indicators aligned with the EU Drugs Agency, so the country can compare its findings with European data.[@1]

Public bodies, institutions and participating organisations must supply information about use, treatment, rehabilitation and harm reduction.[@1] The reporting provisions require information to be supplied without personal data identifying people with drug dependence.[@1]

## Advertising became a wider offence

The revised prohibition covers promotion and advertising of drugs and new psychoactive substances, including visible displays, names, signs and other visual presentation.[@1] It also covers advertising whose form, name or purpose directly or indirectly encourages production, possession, use or circulation.[@1]

That latter advertising offence was included among the acts for which an individual may receive a €100–€2,000 fine.[@1] The same fine band continues to cover unlawful personal possession, connecting the new advertising restriction to the [2011 drug law’s](/history/montenegro-drug-abuse-law-2011/) existing misdemeanour system.[@1]

## Hemp and scientific work had separate conditions

Food and industrial hemp growing remains subject to a permit and a contract for purchase of the whole crop.[@1] A scientific legal entity has a separate land-registration exception, while controlled-drug research and specialist laboratory work can receive a Health Ministry authorisation for one year and quantities needed for the work.[@1]

The [September reporting rules](/history/montenegro-drug-data-rules-2026/) then specified annual submissions using EU agency forms, covering treatment, deaths, offences, seizures, potency and prices.[@3,4] They also permit more frequent reporting on request and require incomplete submissions to be corrected.[@3] The [Montenegro profile](/country/me/) combines these operating rules with the separate penalties for personal possession, unlawful growing and supply.[@1]
