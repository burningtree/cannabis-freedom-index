Iraq raised the maximum prison term for personal-use drug possession and growing to fifteen years in 1979, while allowing treatment instead for some people with dependence caused by a medical condition.[@1] The same law removed permission for supervised cannabis crops grown exclusively for fibre and seeds.[@1]

## The personal-use maximum rose again

The [1968 drug amendment](/history/iraq-drug-death-penalties-1968/) had set three to ten years for personal-use possession or cultivation.[@3] The 1979 law retained the three-year minimum but extended the maximum to fifteen years, alongside a fine.[@1]

It kept special penalties for members of the armed forces and people working with or for them.[@1] Drug offences in that group could bring life imprisonment or at least ten years, with death possible when committed while confronting the enemy.[@1]

The changes preserved a severe criminal response while introducing a separate route for a defined group of people needing treatment.[@1]

## Courts could send some people to a health facility

A court could replace the personal-use punishment with six months in a designated health facility when dependence had arisen from a medical condition.[@1] Earlier release required medical evidence of recovery and a court decision following a request from the prosecutor or the person detained.[@1]

The law's stated reasons referred to treatment and rehabilitation ideas in the amended international drug convention.[@1] The practical decision still rested with the court, and the medical circumstances specified in the law determined eligibility.[@1]

This linked the sentencing decision with a health assessment and a facility authorised for treatment.[@1]

## The industrial crop exception disappeared

The [1965 law](/history/iraq-narcotics-law-1965/) had allowed cannabis cultivated exclusively for fibre and seeds, with special agriculture-ministry permission and direct supervision.[@2] The 1979 amendment deleted that exception.[@1]

It also gave the police narcotics office a larger role in the licensing system, including consultation over manufacturing permissions and notification of import authorisations.[@1]

For growers, the reform therefore removed a specific permitted industrial purpose while keeping crop control within the drug-enforcement system.[@1,2]

## A broader treatment route came much later

The [2017 drug law](/history/iraq-narcotics-law-2017/) replaced the old legislation and reduced the ordinary personal-use prison range to one to three years.[@4] It also protected people who voluntarily presented themselves to a specialist hospital for treatment, subject to its conditions.[@4]

Treatment in [Iraq](/country/iq/) had moved from the narrow medical exception of 1979 towards a wider set of hospital, outpatient and prosecution rules.[@1,4]
