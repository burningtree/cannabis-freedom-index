When Poland legalised medical cannabis in 2017, every gram had to be imported — from Germany,
Canada, the Netherlands. Five years later parliament voted to let Poland grow its own, but only
just: the amendment of 24 March 2022, in force on 7 May, allowed cultivation of non-fibre
cannabis solely by research institutes supervised by the agriculture minister, each plant tracked,
each permit from the Chief Pharmaceutical Inspector running at most five years.[@1]

## The 0.3% line

The same act redrew the boundary of what counts as hemp at all. Until 7 May 2022 fibre hemp
meant plants at no more than 0.2% THC; from that day it meant no more than 0.3% of THC and THCA
together in the flowering or fruiting tops.[@2] Anything above the line stayed non-fibre
cannabis, and nobody but the institutes could grow it.[@1][@2]

## A permit, but no harvest

The opening proved narrow on purpose. In December 2023 the Warsaw Institute of Biotechnology of
the Agricultural and Food Industry received Poland's first cultivation permit — and as of early
2026 domestic production had still not materially entered the supply chain.[@3] The market the
law was meant to supply kept growing without it: 105,000 patients in 2025, nearly five and a half
tonnes of flower dispensed, every gram imported, mostly compounded by pharmacies into magistral
preparations from foreign raw material.[@3]

The 2022 act put [Poland](/country/pl/) in the odd position of a country that grows its medicine
on paper while buying it abroad — a placeholder reform, waiting for someone to use it.
