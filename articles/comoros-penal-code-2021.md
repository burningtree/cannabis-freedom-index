The Comoros replaced their penal code with a law adopted on 29 December 2020 and promulgated by presidential decree on 16 February 2021. Chapter 6 of the new code is headed "Trafficking in narcotics". Despite the heading, it reaches well beyond traffickers: the same article that punishes transporting and selling also punishes holding, acquiring and using.

## The scale

| Article | Conduct | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| 205 | directing or organising a group for the growing, production, import, transport, holding, supply, acquisition or use of narcotics | life imprisonment and a fine of 30 to 100 million Comorian francs |
| 206 | illicit production or manufacture | 10 to 20 years; 15 to 25 in an organised gang |
| 207 | import or export | 15 to 25 years; 20 to 30 in an organised gang |
| 208 | illicit transport, holding, offer, supply, acquisition or use | 10 to 15 years and a fine of 10 to 20 million francs |
| 210 | supplying or offering narcotics to a person for that person's own consumption | 5 to 10 years; 7 to 10 if to minors or in schools or government premises |

## The user in article 208

Article 208 lists six acts in one sentence: "le transport, la détention, l'offre, la cession, l'acquisition ou l'emploi illicite de stupéfiants". The last word, emploi, is use. It carries the same ten to fifteen years as transport. The code sets no smaller penalty for a small amount and offers no treatment alternative in this chapter.

The result is an oddity in the scale. A dealer who sells to someone for that person's own consumption falls under article 210 and faces five to ten years. The buyer, who acquires and uses, falls under article 208 and faces ten to fifteen.

## Around the edges

Article 211 punishes anyone who makes it easier for others to use "high-risk drugs", for payment or not, with 5 to 10 years; it names the owners and managers of hotels, bars, restaurants, clubs and dance halls who tolerate use on their premises. Article 213 punishes incitement to use, even without effect, with 5 to 10 years. Article 214 doubles the maximum penalties of the chapter when violence or weapons were used.

The articles quoted here speak of narcotics in general and do not name cannabis.

## Why the index moved

Every score that was still above zero falls to zero. Possession, cultivation, sharing and consumption each go from 1 to 0, because the least serious act in the chapter, holding or using, now carries at least ten years. Enforcement goes from 1 to 0 for the same reason. The total moves from 9 to 0.
