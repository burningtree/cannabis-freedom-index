Two years after it [ruled out prison for possessing up to 70 grams](/history/georgia-no-prison-2015/), Georgia's Constitutional Court turned to the act of using cannabis itself. Article 273 of the Criminal Code made consuming marijuana a crime when it was repeated. Givi Shanidze challenged it in complaint No. 732, and the First Chamber gave judgment on 30 November 2017.

## A different right

The 2015 case had been argued as cruel punishment. This one was argued under article 16 of the Constitution, the right to the free development of one's personality. The court held that choosing one's recreation, including consuming marijuana, falls within the protected sphere of personal autonomy.

## The state's arguments, and the court's answers

| Argument for the ban | Court's finding |
|---|---|
| Public order | Parliament offered no verified evidence that being under the influence raises the risk of crime |
| Gateway to other drugs | no scientific evidence that marijuana creates a need for other narcotics |
| Distribution | with individual consumption "there is not even theoretical chance of distribution" |
| The user's own health | a legitimate aim, but criminal liability to stop a person harming themselves is a "form of paternalism" that "is not compatible with the free society" |

The court also objected to how broadly the article was drawn. It punished use "without any exceptions, regardless of place and situation of consumption", with no regard to who the person was or what danger actually arose. It was declared incompatible with article 16.

## The growing case, four months earlier

On 14 July 2017 the same chamber had ruled on three men who grew cannabis, with 63.73, 150.72 and 265.49 grams. It called imprisonment "purposeless and therefore, unjustified" for conduct that endangers only the person's own health, and held prison disproportionate for the first two amounts. For 265.49 grams it accepted that the risk of distribution was high and that prison was a permissible kind of punishment, but found the term of six to twelve years out of proportion.

## Why the index moved

Three scores rise by one point each. Consumption goes from 1 to 2: using cannabis is no longer a crime, though the administrative penalty for it stood for another eight months. Possession goes from 2 to 3 and enforcement from 2 to 3, reflecting that by the end of 2017 the court had removed prison for possession, criminal liability for use and prison for small-scale growing. Cultivation itself stays at 1, since growing remained an offence. The total moves from 14 to 19. The administrative penalty for use fell in [July 2018](/history/georgia-constitutional-court-2018/).
