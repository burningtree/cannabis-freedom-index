Finland made using drugs a crime by twelve votes, against the wishes of its own
government, and has not changed its mind since.

## The ban and the vote

Cannabis has been [prohibited in Finland](/history/finland-ban-1966/) since 1966.
Whether the user should be punished was a separate question. In 1971 parliament voted
92 to 80 to make personal use illegal, an outcome that went against the government's
position. The matter was close enough that the Grand Committee's stance had been
settled by drawing lots after a tied vote. The new rule took effect in 1972.

Few countries had gone that far. Most punished possession and left use itself alone.

## Fines instead of trials

In [2001](/history/finland-summary-fines-2001/) the procedure was changed to lighten
the courts' load. Police now issue a summary fine for most personal-use offences, and
a case goes to court only if the accused wants it to. Possession of up to 10 g of
hashish or 15 g of marijuana counts as personal use and brings 10 to 20 day-fines.

## A narrow medical door

Finland [authorised Sativex](/history/finland-sativex-2012/) in 2012. In 2014, 223
medical users were permitted to buy cannabis products, from 27 pharmacies licensed to
sell them.

## Petitions

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2019 | A [citizens' initiative](/history/finland-citizens-initiative-2019/) to decriminalise use closed with 59,609 signatures, above the 50,000 needed |
| 2021 | The Green League became [the first party in parliament](/history/finland-greens-back-legalisation-2021/) to back legalisation |
| 2026 | Parliament [rejected](/history/finland-legalisation-initiative-rejected-2026/) an initiative to legalise by 145 votes to 18 |

The margin had grown from twelve votes to 127.
