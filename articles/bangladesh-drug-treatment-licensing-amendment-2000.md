Bangladesh's 2000 narcotics amendment gave courts an express power to send drug users for treatment instead of, or alongside, the prescribed punishment.[@1] It also expanded the licensing provisions for authorised pharmaceutical use, treatment and scientific research to include cultivation.[@1] The same amendment introduced supervised drug deliveries as a way to identify people involved in trafficking.[@1,2]

## A decade after the cannabis ban

The [1990 narcotics law](/history/bangladesh-narcotics-control-act-1990/) prohibited growing, trading, possessing and using narcotics other than alcohol, subject to its authorised exceptions.[@1] It specifically listed ganja, cannabis plants, cannabis resin and tetrahydrocannabinol in its penalty table.[@1]

The later amendment revised several parts of that system, including the rules for lawful activities and the consequences of unlawful drug use.[@1] The official historical text identifies these changes as additions made by the 2000 amending law.[@1]

## Licences and permits governed authorised activities

Where a controlled drug or plant was needed for authorised pharmaceutical use, treatment or scientific research, the revised exception allowed cultivation under a licence.[@1] Applying and using the drug required a permit, while carrying and transporting it required a pass.[@1] The Act also retained prescribing controls, including written authorisation for doctors prescribing drugs in its two stricter classes.[@1]

The treatment-centre provisions were revised too.[@1] The government could establish counselling, treatment and rehabilitation centres, while private operators could establish and run them under licence.[@1] Government hospitals, including prison hospitals, could be designated as addiction-treatment centres by a Gazette notification.[@1]

## Courts gained a treatment alternative for users

The amendment added separate use penalties by drug class: two to seven years for Class A, one to five years for Class B, and six months to two years for Class C, each with a fine.[@1] A court could consider the seriousness of the offence and order treatment in addition to, or instead of, that punishment.[@1]

Alongside those treatment provisions, investigators gained controlled delivery: allowing a suspicious drug consignment to move under official supervision to identify the people behind it.[@1,2] Government written approval was required, with authorisation lasting no more than three months at a time.[@1] The [2002 investigation-deadline amendment](/history/bangladesh-drug-investigation-deadlines-2002/) later added time limits and officer accountability to this enforcement system in [Bangladesh](/country/bd/).[@1]
