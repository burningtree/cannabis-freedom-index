For three years in the 1970s cannabis was legal in the Comoros. It is one of the very
few cases in the twentieth century of a country lifting its ban outright, decades
before Uruguay.

## What is recorded

The Comoros, an island state between Madagascar and the African coast, became
independent from France in 1975. Under the revolutionary president Ali Soilih the ban
on cannabis was lifted. By the account the index follows, he did it to win the support
of young people.

Cannabis stayed legal from January 1975 to May 1978.

## What is not recorded

Almost everything else. The sources read for this article give no text of the measure,
no account of how cannabis was sold or grown during those years, and no figures. They
do not say whether anything replaced the ban or whether it was simply left unenforced
by decree.

That thinness is itself worth noting. The episode appears in timelines of cannabis law
as a legalisation, but it has left no statute that later reformers could point to and
no study of what happened.

## How it ended

Soilih was overthrown and killed in 1978. The new government reversed his decrees and
[banned cannabis again](/history/comoros-ban-restored-1978/).

## In the index

The index records 1975 as a rise in the [Comoros'](/country/km/) scores and 1978 as
their fall, on the strength of the dates alone. Both entries should be read with that
caution: the dates are documented, the content of the freedom is not.
