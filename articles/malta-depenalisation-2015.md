The first Act of the Maltese parliament in 2015 carries its policy in its title: the Drug Dependence (Treatment not Imprisonment) Act. President Marie-Louise Coleiro Preca assented to it on 20 February 2015, and it came into force on a date set by ministerial order; the index uses 15 April 2015. The Act did not repeal the drug laws. Article 3 says they "shall continue to apply except in respect of any matter explicitly provided for in this Act".

## Small possession leaves the criminal courts

Article 4 takes simple possession away from the magistrates and gives it to a Commissioner for Justice, the tribunal that hears minor offences.

| Substance | Limit | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Cannabis or cannabis resin | 3.5 grams | €50 to €100 |
| Any other drug | 2 grams, or two pills | €75 to €125 |

The limit applies only where nothing suggests the drug is for anyone but the person holding it. The drug itself is confiscated and destroyed.

## Second offences

A second case within two years goes to the same Commissioner and carries the same penalties. For other drugs the Commissioner must then send a resident to the new Drug Offenders Rehabilitation Board. Cannabis is treated differently: the person is referred only if the Commissioner finds reason to believe they are abusing, or likely to abuse, drugs "other than cannabis or cannabis resin". Failing to appear before the Board is a criminal matter, with a fine of €100 to €500 or three months in prison.

## One plant

Article 7 deals with home growing in a single sentence. A person found guilty of cultivating "not exceeding one plant", where the court is satisfied it was for personal use, "shall not be liable to a mandatory term of imprisonment". Growing stayed an offence tried in court. What changed is that the judge was no longer forced to send the grower to prison, and could use probation or a suspended sentence.

## A first medical clause

Article 10 lets specialists in neurology, oncology, anaesthetics, psychiatry and other listed fields prescribe "medicinal preparations of the plant cannabis licensed under the Medicines Act", if there is no viable alternative.

## Why the index moved

The total rose from 9 to 21, the largest step in Malta's series before legalisation in 2021. Possession rose from 1 to 3, because up to 3.5 grams became a fixed penalty of at most €100 with no criminal court involved, and consumption from 1 to 2. Cultivation rose from 0, the score for a mandatory prison term, to 1, since a single plant could now end in probation. Enforcement rose from 2 to 4.
