Brunei has controlled cannabis under the same statute since the summer of 1978. The Misuse of Drugs Act, Chapter 27 — an Act to control dangerous or otherwise harmful drugs — commenced on 1 July 1978, and its text, consolidated in the 2001 revised edition that Brunei communicated to the United Nations, still carries the original prohibitions.[@1]

## Five core offences from day one

The Act's opening sections criminalise the whole supply chain: importing or exporting a controlled drug without authorisation, possessing one, and smoking, administering or otherwise consuming one.[@1] Paraphernalia follows — pipes, syringes, utensils intended for smoking, administering or consuming a controlled drug — and so does cultivation: section 8 makes it an offence to cultivate any plant of the genus cannabis, alongside the opium poppy and the coca plant.[@1] Owners and occupiers who let premises be used for consumption, trafficking or manufacture are liable too.[@1]

## Cannabis by definition, Class B by schedule

The Act defines cannabis as any part of any plant of the genus cannabis from which the resin has not been extracted, and cannabis resin as the separated resin, crude or purified.[@1] In the 2001 consolidation both sit in Part II of the First Schedule — the Class B list — alongside codeine and amphetamines, while cannabinol counts as a Class A drug only when it is not contained in cannabis or cannabis resin.[@1] The class structure itself dates to 1990s amendments, so the "Class B" label describes the consolidated schedule rather than the Act's first day.[@1]

## Presumptions and the death threshold

Quantity does the accusing. Anyone proved or presumed to hold more than 15 grammes of cannabis or 10 grammes of resin is presumed to be trafficking until the contrary is proved.[@1] The Second Schedule then scales punishment: unauthorised trafficking in 330 to 500 grammes of cannabis draws 20 to 30 years' imprisonment with 15 strokes, and more than 500 grammes draws the death penalty; resin thresholds run lower at 130 to 200 grammes and above 200.[@1] The [country page](/country/bn/) continues from this 1978 foundation.
