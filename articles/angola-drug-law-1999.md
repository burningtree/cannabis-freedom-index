Angola's replacement drug law took effect on 4 November 1999, shortening the ordinary personal-use sentence to three months while raising ordinary trafficking penalties to eight to twelve years. People growing or holding cannabis for themselves remained subject to prison, with a higher maximum of one year above three days' supply.

## Colonial penalties gave way to a mixed law

The [Portuguese drug law extended to Angola in 1970](/history/angola-portuguese-drug-law-1970/) had prescribed up to two years and a fine for personal possession or non-profit supply. Other unauthorised dealings carried two to eight years and a fine.

Parliament presented the replacement as a response to growing concern about drug production, trafficking and consumption, particularly among young people. Its preamble called the existing legislation inadequate and stressed both prevention at home and cooperation against traffickers abroad. The new law repealed several colonial texts, including the 1970 law.

## Growing for oneself received a shorter sentence

The personal-use offence covered consumption, acquisition, possession and cultivation. All could bring up to three months in prison. When the quantity exceeded average individual consumption for three days, the maximum rose to one year.

The controlled list included cannabis leaves and flowering or fruiting tops, resin and oil. The three-day rule described personal needs; the law left ministers to specify daily-dose quantities separately.

Selling or giving cannabis without authority fell within the broader trafficking offence. Courts could impose two to eight years when the circumstances made the offence substantially less serious. Another shorter sentence applied to dealing solely to obtain drugs for one's own use, provided the quantity stayed within five days' supply.

## Treatment could accompany a suspended sentence

People seeking help voluntarily were promised anonymity, and the health ministry was required to provide care for those who came forward. The law also protected confidentiality about treatment.

A court could suspend a dependent user's sentence on condition of voluntary treatment or admission to an appropriate institution. The person had to demonstrate compliance as the court directed. A [companion licensing law](/history/angola-controlled-drug-licensing-1999/) governed authorised cultivation, manufacture and pharmaceutical supply.

## The trafficking sentence remained consequential

In [October 2023](/history/angola-cannabis-sentence-pardon-2023/), the Supreme Court upheld a five-year sentence in a case involving 52 kilograms of cannabis, accepting special mitigation below the ordinary eight-year minimum. It also applied a quarter pardon under the 2022 amnesty law.

The case showed how quantity, the purpose of possession and sentencing mitigation shaped the outcome under [Angola's drug law](/country/ao/).
