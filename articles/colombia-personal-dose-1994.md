On 5 May 1994, Colombia's Constitutional Court struck down the punishment attached to possessing or consuming a personal dose of drugs. It preserved the statutory definition—including a limit of 20 grams of marijuana—but rejected arrest, fines and compulsory treatment imposed simply for personal use.

## A dose defined and punished

Law 30 of 1986 defined a personal dose as the amount someone carried or kept for their own consumption. For marijuana the ceiling was 20 grams, and for hashish it was 5 grams. Any amount intended for distribution or sale fell outside the definition.

Yet the same law punished conduct within that dose. A first incident could bring up to 30 days' arrest and a fine; a later incident could bring a longer arrest. The law also provided for compulsory treatment where a medical assessment found dependence.

The [1986 history entry](/history/colombia-law-30-1986/) records that contradiction: the statute drew a boundary around personal possession, then penalised the person inside it.

## Autonomy under the constitution

In Judgment C-221/94, the court connected personal consumption to the constitutional right to the free development of personality. It reasoned that recognising autonomy means allowing a person to choose a way of life, provided the choice does not interfere with the autonomy of others.

The court held that criminalising drug consumption was incompatible with that protection. The state could educate people about risks, but could not substitute punishment for an adult's autonomous decision where only that person was affected.

## What survived the judgment

The court declared Articles 51 and 87 of Law 30 unconstitutional. It left the definition of a personal dose in Article 2(j) intact. Trafficking also remained criminal, and the definition expressly excluded drugs carried for sale or distribution regardless of quantity.

The ruling therefore protected possession and use, not a legal supply system. Later political and judicial disputes continued over public consumption, police confiscation and the boundary between a personal dose and trafficking.

## What moved in the index

The ruling raised [Colombia's](/country/co/) possession score from 1 to 4, enforcement from 2 to 4 and consumption from 1 to 5. The index records smaller cultivation and sharing increases from 1 to 2; the court itself did not expressly legalise either. Access remained 0 because the judgment created no lawful market.
