Romania published longer drug-trafficking sentences on 2 March 2023, raising the ordinary range for supplying risk drugs such as cannabis plant or resin to three to ten years.[@1] The amendment took effect on 5 March, while personal-use possession remained under a separate offence.[@2]

## Giving and selling were covered

The supply offence covered offering, selling, distributing, delivering, transporting and other unauthorised circulation of drugs.[@1] It also covered growing or producing for that supply chain.[@1] For risk drugs, the new range was three to ten years with disqualification from exercising certain rights.[@1]

Where the substances were classified as high-risk, the trafficking range became five to fifteen years.[@1] Those categories depended on the drug schedules, so the legal classification mattered alongside what the person had done.[@2]

## Crossing the border carried a higher range

Unauthorised import or export of risk drugs became punishable by five to fifteen years.[@1] The corresponding range for high-risk drugs became ten to twenty years.[@1] The amendment therefore maintained a separate, higher range for crossing the border than for ordinary domestic risk-drug supply.[@1]

Specified drug offences causing a victim’s death carried fifteen to twenty-five years and disqualification from certain rights.[@1] The amendment also allowed equipment used in illegal manufacture to be used for training law-enforcement staff or in drug-risk awareness campaigns.[@1]

## Personal use retained its separate rules

Buying, possessing or growing risk drugs for personal consumption carried three months to two years or a fine under Article 4.[@2] Providing publicly accessible premises for illegal drug use was separately punishable by two to seven years.[@2]

The [Romania profile](/country/ro/) distinguishes those personal-use, supply, border and premises offences.[@2] A person’s assertion that cannabis was for personal use therefore concerns a different offence from the supply and import provisions.[@2]

## Assistance remained part of the response

The 2023 amendment also addressed assessment of drug users for integrated assistance.[@1] A [2024 change](/history/romania-drug-assistance-evaluation-2024/) revised that procedure, making assessment mandatory for minors and allowing prosecutors to order it for other personal-use cases when needed.[@3]

Under the current law, complying with an assistance programme can permit waiver or deferral of punishment for an eligible personal-use defendant.[@2] Separately, the [cabinet’s February 2026 proposal](/history/romania-controlled-preparations-proposal-2026/) addressed the rules for controlled medicines and pharmacists, continuing the distinction between authorised pharmaceutical handling and illegal circulation.[@4]
