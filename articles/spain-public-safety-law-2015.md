On 1 July 2015 Spain's new public safety law came into force. Organic Law 4/2015 of 30 March, which its critics call the gag law, replaced the law of [1992](/history/spain-corcuera-law-1992/). For cannabis it kept the same structure, a fine for use or possession in public, and made the fine larger and harder to escape.

## Four drug infractions

Article 36 lists the serious infractions. Four concern drugs.

| Point | Conduct |
|---|---|
| 16 | Consuming or holding drugs in public places, streets, establishments or public transport, even without intent to traffic; leaving the instruments used there |
| 17 | Driving people to a place so that they can obtain drugs, where it is not a crime |
| 18 | Planting or growing drugs "in places visible to the public", where it is not a crime |
| 19 | Tolerating drug use or dealing in a public establishment |

Point 18 is new. A plant on a balcony or in a garden that can be seen from the street became finable, even where the growing itself would not be prosecuted.

## The fine

| Grade | Fine for a serious infraction |
|---|---|
| Minimum | €601 to €10,400 |
| Medium | €10,401 to €20,200 |
| Maximum | €20,201 to €30,000 |

Under the 1992 law the range for a serious infraction had begun at 50,001 pesetas, about €300. The floor doubled.

## The way out that closed

The 1992 law let the sanction be suspended if the offender completed a detoxification treatment. Under the 2015 law that option was removed for adults, so a person fined for a joint in the street has to pay.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Spain](/country/es/) from 33 to 31 with the [2015 law](/history/spain-public-safety-law-2015/). Enforcement falls from 5 to 4, for the higher floor and the loss of the treatment alternative. Possession and consumption stay at 3: private use is still untouched, and the public fine was already counted in 1992.
