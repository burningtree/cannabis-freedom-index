People caught with up to 25 grams of cannabis in Sint Maarten could have it confiscated and their case dismissed under prosecution guidance effective from 1 December 2018.[@1] Dismissal could come with conditions, and the policy excluded several groups and circumstances, including minors and indications of dealing.[@1]

## Small quantities could end in confiscation

The [Opium Ordinance](/history/sint-maarten-opium-ordinance-1964/) prohibited cannabis possession and use outside its exemptions.[@2] Prosecutors' guidance then set out how to handle offences, with a separate cannabis table alongside rules for other drugs.[@1]

The first band, 0–25 grams, directed confiscation and a possible conditional dismissal.[@1] In the next band, 25–50 grams, prosecutors were to seek a fine of up to NAf 500.[@1] These were instructions for prosecution decisions and requests to the court; the table described what prosecutors should seek.[@1]

The guidance covered Curaçao and the Caribbean Netherlands as well as Sint Maarten, replacing an earlier version from July 2015.[@1] The December date therefore marks a revision of a shared prosecution policy.[@1]

## Age and circumstances changed the response

A small amount alone did not settle the case.[@1] The dismissal policy had exceptions for repeat offending, another offence, indications of dealing and minors.[@1] Location also mattered: the listed exceptions included carnival parades, workplaces, government buildings and detention facilities.[@1]

The guidance's discussion of young people called for prosecution even for very small amounts so that assistance or treatment could be pursued.[@1] An adult carrying a small quantity and a minor with the same amount could therefore face different prosecution decisions under the policy.[@1]

## Growing received its own assessment

For quantities above the 25–50 gram band, the cannabis sentencing requests were half those in the cocaine and heroin table.[@1] Qualifying resident defendants could receive a request for a wholly or partly suspended sentence, with three years of probation.[@1]

Cultivation required a separate assessment.[@1] Prosecutors were told to consider the number of plants, the location, professional equipment and the defendant's involvement when deciding what sentence to seek.[@1]

## Legalisation became a separate government project

In [September 2022](/history/sint-maarten-cannabis-study-call-2022/), health minister Omar Ottley invited proposals for research into a regulated cannabis industry.[@3] A [government committee followed in 2024](/history/sint-maarten-cannabis-committee-2024/), tasked with studying the effects of criminalisation and preparing rules for growing, distributing and consuming cannabis.[@4]

That work placed the future of cannabis policy alongside the prosecution rules affecting people in [Sint Maarten](/country/sx/).[@1,4]
