On 14 April 2025 Uganda's replacement narcotics law took force, ending almost two
years without a valid drug statute.[@4] From that day, possessing cannabis can bring up
to twenty years in prison with no lighter tier for a small amount, using it up to ten
years, growing it without a licence up to five, and trafficking it — which the law says
includes giving it away — up to life.[@1] Only licensed medical growing, decided case by
case by the health minister, sits outside the ban.[@1,2]

## A law passed without enough MPs in the room

The gap began with khat, not cannabis. In May 2023 the Constitutional Court annulled
the 2015 Narcotic Drugs Act after the Wakiso Miraa Growers and Dealers Association
showed parliament had passed it without a quorum.[@2] The judges stressed that the
ruling did not legalise cannabis.[@2] The government reintroduced the bill within weeks
and pushed it through three sittings; parliament passed the 94-clause replacement on
22 August 2023, and the president assented on 2 February 2024.[@2,3] Section 1 left the
start date to the internal affairs minister, who fixed it by statutory instrument at
14 April 2025.[@1,4]

## What the law says

The Act punishes the user as well as the seller. Smoking, sniffing or chewing a
narcotic, sitting in a den, or keeping a pipe each carry up to ten years.[@1] A table
of the maximum prison terms:

| Offence | Longest prison term |
|---|---|
| Possessing a narcotic drug | 20 years |
| Smoking, using, keeping a den or a pipe | 10 years |
| Growing a prohibited plant without a licence | 5 years, life for a repeat offence |
| Trafficking, including giving cannabis away | Life |

Fines run alongside: up to fifty thousand currency points — a billion shillings — or
three times the drug's market value if that is more, for possession and
trafficking.[@1] Courts can also order property forfeited, and a pharmacist who
prescribes outside the rules faces the same billion-shilling scale.[@1,3]

## Licences for medicine only, and a fight over khat

Parliament meant the licensing door to open only a crack. All licences sit with the
health ministry, and ministers say cannabis and khat may be grown and used strictly
for medical purposes and research.[@2,3] Attempts to save khat failed on the floor:
one MP's bid to drop it from the banned list was voted down, and another's plea to
align with East African neighbours, where cross-border truck drivers chew it openly,
went nowhere.[@3] Since commencement the government has policed the line it drew. It
licensed one company to grow, process and export medicinal cannabis in Nakasongola
and Luweero under police supervision, and in October 2026 ministers warned licence
holders that diverting crops to the street would bring action under the law.[@4]
