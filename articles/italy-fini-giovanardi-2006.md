From 2006 to 2014, Italy applied a common basic trafficking penalty range to cannabis and heroin. A decree-law of 30 December 2005, converted into Law 49 on 21 February 2006 and known by the names of its sponsors, Fini and Giovanardi, abolished the distinction between soft and hard drugs. The amendments took effect on 28 February 2006. In February 2014 the Constitutional Court struck it down.

## What the law did

The change was made by amending the drug decree of 1990. It unified trafficking penalties. Personal-use possession remained an administrative offence.

Before it, the decree's Article 73 had two tracks: one paragraph for hard drugs and a later one, with milder sentences, for soft ones. After it there was one.

A new paragraph covered importing, buying, receiving and holding a drug, acts that a dealer and a consumer both commit. Criminal liability depended on indications of a non-exclusively-personal purpose, assessed through quantity, packaging and other circumstances.

The administrative sanctions for personal use no longer distinguish either. Suspensions of a passport or driving licence, which once lasted one to three months for soft drugs and two to four for hard ones, now run from one month to a year for both.

## How it ended

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 30 December 2005 | Decree-Law 272 |
| 21 February 2006 | converted into Law 49 |
| 28 February 2006 | drug-law amendments took effect |
| 12 February 2014 | declared unconstitutional |

The Constitutional Court removed the amendments to Articles 73, 13 and 14 of the decree. The effect, in the court's own description, was the revival of the original provisions: the two tracks came back, and with them the milder sentences for cannabis.

## The distinction afterwards

The restored paragraph for soft drugs became the centre of the next argument. A referendum proposed in 2021 sought, among other things, to remove prison for producing and selling soft drugs under that paragraph, and to strike cultivation from the list of crimes. Its organisers filed 630,000 signatures. The Constitutional Court declared it inadmissible on 16 February 2022.

## Why the index moved

The [2006 law](/history/italy-fini-giovanardi-2006/) lowered [Italy's](/country/it/) total from 21 to 17. Possession fell from 3 to 2 and enforcement from 4 to 3: personal use stayed an administrative matter, as the 1993 referendum had left it, but criminal possession now carried the same basic range across the merged drug table; a quantity threshold alone did not establish dealing. The [2014 ruling](/history/italy-constitutional-court-2014/) restored the earlier scores.
