On February 21, 1908, John Ainsworth, provincial commissioner at Kisumu, wrote to the Crown Advocate in Mombasa seeking legal action against cannabis cultivation. Neil Carrier cites the letter as evidence of colonial concern about the plant before Kenya's 1914 restrictions.

The letter was an appeal for legislation, not an ordinance in its own right. The event records an official argument for control, rather than a ban taking effect that day.

## A local concern before the treaty controls

Carrier describes officials in western Kenya and on the coast expressing concern about production, imports and consumption. Ainsworth's letter linked the substance with harm to consumers and crime.

Those claims explain the commissioner's request. They do not independently establish that cannabis caused the offences he had in mind, or that his view represented everyone living in the region.

The chapter cites the letter in Kenya National Archives file AG 32/38, concerning the Abuse of Opiates Prevention Ordinance. That reference identifies a route back to the original document; the letter itself has not been independently inspected for this article.

## Cannabis and an opium framework

The [1914 ordinance](/history/kenya-ban-1914/) followed the [1912 Hague Opium Convention](/history/hague-opium-convention-1912/). The convention did not itself require cannabis control.

That distinction is central to Carrier's example. Local colonial officials had already sought restrictions on cannabis, and they used an emerging drug-control framework to pursue them. The Kenyan history cannot be explained simply as compliance with a cannabis obligation created at The Hague.

## An argument that became a legal question

The [1925 Geneva convention](/history/geneva-opium-convention-1925/) later introduced international cannabis controls. Ainsworth's request preceded that development by seventeen years.

It shows how officials could classify a locally available substance as a problem before a global treaty system addressed it. It also separates the history of advocacy for prohibition from the date at which a particular legal restriction began.
