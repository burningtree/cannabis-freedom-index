On 1 January 2013 possessing a small amount of cannabis for personal use stopped being a crime in Croatia. It became a misdemeanour instead — still punished, but no longer a criminal conviction.[@1]

## What it replaced

Since April 1996, unauthorised possession of drugs had been a criminal offence called abuse of narcotics, carrying a fine or up to a year in prison.[@2] Croatia's Supreme Court scholars were already debating decriminalisation: possession without intent to sell, they argued, did not belong in criminal law.[@2] The new Criminal Code that took effect in 2013 settled it by taking small-amount possession out of criminal law altogether.[@1]

## What the misdemeanour means

Personal-use possession now brings a fine of EUR 663.61 to 2,654.46, or up to 90 days' detention.[@3] The law sets no fixed gram threshold: courts assess personal use case by case.[@1] The statute also allows treatment orders instead of, or alongside, penalties — addicted users can be ordered into mandatory treatment for three months to a year.[@1]

The category change mattered more than the money. A misdemeanour is handled as an administrative violation, like a serious traffic offence, instead of landing the holder with a criminal record.[@1] Growing, selling and trafficking stayed firmly criminal: even a few home-grown plants count as production, punished with six months to five years.[@1]

## The line that held

Every later attempt to move further has failed. Legalisation bills, including one proposing nine plants per adult, were tabled without success, and the government rejected the latest after a debate in November 2022.[@1] Medical cannabis has been nominally legal since 2015, yet pharmacies ran out in 2018 and a 2025 market survey found no medicine on sale.[@4] The 2013 compromise — a fine instead of a file — remains [Croatia's](/country/hr/) cannabis law.
