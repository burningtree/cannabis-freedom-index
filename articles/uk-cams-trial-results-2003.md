The CAMS trial is a useful example of why the choice of outcome measure mattered in
UK debates about medical cannabis.

Published on 8 November 2003, it enrolled 667 patients and treated 630 at 33 UK centres.
Participants received oral cannabis extract, THC or placebo for fifteen weeks. The
study did not find a treatment effect on its primary measure, the Ashworth spasticity
scale. It did find effects on patient-reported spasticity and pain; the authors also
noted some unmasking of participants in the active-treatment groups.

Suzanne Taylor describes how patient organisations questioned the weight given to
clinical measurements compared with lived improvements. The published abstract supports
a distinction between the primary endpoint and patient-rated outcomes, rather than a
simple claim that the trial proved efficacy or showed no benefit at all.
