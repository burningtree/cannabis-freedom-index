Aruba's prosecutor-general brought a drug-prosecution guideline into operation
on 11 February 2021. For a small amount of soft drugs, it specified dismissal
of the case and surrender of the drugs, while retaining exceptions and a scale
of prosecution demands for larger quantities.

The document matters because it explains how an offence could remain in law
while attracting a limited enforcement response. It does not establish a right
to possess, buy or grow cannabis.

## A five-gram band

The soft-drug table, organised under Article 4 of the Narcotic Drugs Ordinance,
lists dismissal and surrender in its 0–5 g possession/courier band. The general
instructions also provide for seizure and destruction of drugs found by the
authorities.

The table therefore describes how prosecutors should handle specified cases.
The presence of a small-quantity band does not make the substance lawful or
provide a legal place to obtain it.

## The exceptions matter

A footnote removes the ordinary response where possession accompanies another
offence, involves repeat offending or concerns a minor. It also identifies
special circumstances, including possession during a carnival procession,
at work or in a government building.

The guideline separately explains that a minor with a user quantity may face
a prosecution-related intervention intended to support prevention and assistance.
Age and circumstances therefore affect the response even within the smallest
quantity band.

## Demands depend on role and quantity

For the table's 5–250 g band, the possession/courier demand is up to one month
in prison, potentially partly suspended, with three years' probation. The same
band gives higher demands for intermediaries and organisers: up to two and
four months respectively.

Above that range, the soft-drug table refers to half the demand used for hard
drugs. These are prosecutorial demands, rather than sentences a court must
automatically impose. The document's general principles also consider the
person's role, repeat offending and individual circumstances.

## A policy within the Kingdom

The guideline says its preparation took account of policies elsewhere in the
Kingdom, especially the
[2018 regional guideline](/history/curacao-prosecution-guideline-2018/).
That background does not make the quantity bands identical across jurisdictions.

For [Aruba's history](/country/aw/), the verified event is the commencement of
this particular instruction. The earlier policy has not been reconstructed
here, so the entry does not claim that February 2021 was the first occasion
on which a small cannabis case could be dismissed.
