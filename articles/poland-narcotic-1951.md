On 8 January 1951 Communist Poland replaced the drug law of [1923](/history/poland-narcotics-act-1923/) with the Act on pharmaceutical and narcotic agents and sanitary articles. Most of it concerns the pharmacy trade. A few articles deal with narcotics, and for the first time the list includes the cannabis plant's herb, not only its resin.

## The list

Article 5 names twenty narcotics. Among them are raw and medicinal opium, opium for smoking, coca leaves, cocaine, heroin and morphine, and two entries for cannabis:

- hashish, carried over from 1923;
- the herb of Indian hemp, *Herba Cannabis Indicae*, with its preparations and mixtures.

The health minister could add substances by regulation.

## The rules

Making, processing, importing, exporting, storing or selling a narcotic was allowed only for medical, scientific or industrial purposes and under a permit. Pharmacies could handle narcotics for medical purposes without one.

| Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Making, processing, importing, exporting, transporting, storing or selling without a permit (Article 29) | up to five years in prison, a fine, or both |
| Using a narcotic without a doctor's prescription in the company of another person (Article 30) | up to one year of detention, a fine, or both |

The substances and the tools of the offence could be forfeited. Supervising officials could enter premises, take samples and seize narcotics held unlawfully.

## What it left alone

The Act has no provision on cultivation, so hemp and poppy could still be grown freely. It does not punish possession for one's own use as such: the offence is storing without a permit, in a list of trade activities. And use alone, at home, was not an offence, only use in company.

That shape lasted until [1985](/history/poland-drug-prevention-act-1985/), when Poland passed a separate law on preventing drug addiction.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Poland](/country/pl/) from 43 to 31 with the [1951 Act](/history/poland-narcotic-1951/). The herb was now a narcotic by name and the ceiling for unlicensed handling was five years. Possession, cultivation and enforcement each fall by one point, consumption falls from 6 to 2, and the last point for access goes. Cultivation, at 4, stays comparatively high because growing the plant remained lawful.
