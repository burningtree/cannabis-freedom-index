In 1798 an English sailor at Delagoa Bay watched the locals smoke bang through a cow's
horn — one of the earliest eyewitness accounts of cannabis use in southern
Africa.[@1] White's journal, with its plate of the scene, fixed the plant and its
water-pipe at the bay more than a century before prohibition.[@1]

The account matters because it predates every law: the 1914 colonial ban, the 1997
drug act, the 2024 court battles. Smoking was already established, already piped,
already social — the smoker's cough the travellers suspected after a pipe was put on
lives in the atlas's [tradition](/culture/traditions/the-smokers-cough/).[@1]

What White called "bang" the coast still smokes as suruma and mbanje: the word changed,
the horn became glass, the custom never left.
