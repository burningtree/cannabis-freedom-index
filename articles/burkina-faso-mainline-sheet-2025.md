Burkina Faso's drug law promises treatment as an alternative to prison. In practice,
Mainline's August 2025 country sheet found, users go to jail.[@1]

Police and courts repress users severely, and imprisonment of drug users is routine.[@1]
Judges can order treatment in place of or alongside punishment, but the report describes
missing protocols, missing facilities and unclear responsibilities — the alternative
exists in the code and almost nowhere else.[@1]

The numbers show an enforcement-heavy system: in 2020, 420 people were arrested for
drugs with 13,800 kg of cannabis seized; in 2023 more than 300 tons of drugs were seized
and 377 people were arrested for drug-related offences.[@1] The sheet, built on research
and civil-society interviews, also notes the scarcity of specialised public-health
centres and the commonness of prison sentences on top of missing care.[@1]

The law keeps moving without fixing this: parliament adopted a replacement Penal Code on
20 October 2026 whose drug articles have not been reviewed, leaving the treatment gap
exactly where Mainline found it.
