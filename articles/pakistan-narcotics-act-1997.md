Pakistan's 1997 narcotics law placed cannabis possession and supply within three quantity bands: up to 100 grams, more than 100 grams to one kilogram, and more than one kilogram.[@1,2] The first allowed a fine instead of prison, while the largest could bring death, life imprisonment or a term of up to fourteen years, plus a fine.[@1,2]

## A permanent law followed the earlier ordinances

The [1995 ordinance](/history/pakistan-narcotics-ordinance-1995/) had already prescribed quantity-based sentences and death or life for drugs above its higher limits.[@3] For cannabis, its lowest fine-or-prison band stopped at ten grams; the middle band extended to one kilogram and carried a three-year prison minimum.[@3]

The 1997 Act widened the lowest band to 100 grams and gave the middle band a seven-year ceiling without that minimum.[@1,2] It received presidential assent on 7 July and came into force at once; publication followed on 11 July.[@1]

## The weight of the drug shaped the sentence

The new law covered cannabis resin, including charas and hashish oil, the flowering or fruiting tops, and mixtures or drinks made from those forms.[@1,2] Seeds and leaves unaccompanied by the tops were excluded from the definition of cannabis.[@1,2]

Unauthorised production, possession, sale, purchase, distribution and delivery on any terms were prohibited.[@1,2] Up to 100 grams could mean two years, a fine, or both; the middle band carried up to seven years and a fine.[@1,2] Above ten kilograms, the large-quantity rule required at least life imprisonment.[@1,2]

## Growing and treatment had their own rules

Growing cannabis or gathering part of a plant was separately prohibited, with licensing possible for medical, scientific or industrial purposes.[@1,2] The original cultivation penalty allowed up to seven years, a fine, or both.[@1,2]

Provincial governments were required to register people dependent on drugs, who were to carry registration cards.[@1,2] The federal government was to pay for first-time compulsory detoxification, while provinces were to establish treatment and rehabilitation centres.[@1,2]

## Later amendments separated the cannabis products

The Act replaced older opium and dangerous-drug laws and the preceding 1997 ordinance, while preserving specified earlier proceedings and liabilities.[@1,2] It also retained provincial and special laws imposing additional or greater restrictions.[@1,2]

The [2022 federal amendment](/history/pakistan-cannabis-penalty-tables-2022/) replaced the shared weight bands with separate tables for bhang, charas and hashish oil.[@4] That later law explains today's federal prison ranges in [Pakistan](/country/pk/); the 1997 figures belong to the earlier system.[@4]
