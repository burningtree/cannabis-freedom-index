Pakistan's drug law is a single statute, the Control of Narcotic Substances Act of 1997. It replaced three laws inherited from British India, the Opium Acts of 1857 and 1878 and the Dangerous Drugs Act of 1930, and it is still in force.

## What it defines

The Act treats cannabis in all its forms as one narcotic. Its definition covers charas, the separated resin; the flowering or fruiting tops of the plant, by whatever name, including bhang, siddhi and ganja; and any mixture or drink made from them.

Section 4 forbids cultivating any cannabis plant, letting land for it, or gathering any part of the plant. Section 6 forbids producing, possessing, selling, buying, delivering or transporting a narcotic. Both allow one exception: a government licence for medical, scientific or industrial purposes.

## A sentence for every weight

Section 9 sets the punishment by a table: the drug, the quantity, the term. In the consolidated text it reads as follows for cannabis.

| | Quantity | Prison |
|---|---|---|
| Bhang | up to 999 g | 6 months to 3 years |
| | 1 to 10 kg | 3 to 7 years |
| | 10 to 20 kg | 7 to 14 years |
| | 20 kg or more | 14 years to life |
| Charas | up to 499 g | 10 months to 5 years |
| | 500 to 999 g | 5 to 9 years |
| | 1 to 5 kg | 9 to 14 years |
| | 5 to 10 kg | 14 to 20 years |
| | 10 kg or more | 20 years to life |

Every row has a minimum as well as a maximum, and a fine. The smallest amount of charas a person can be caught with carries at least ten months.

Growing is punished separately. Since an amendment of 2022 the term is one to seven years, with a fine.

## Users

The Act has a chapter on addicts. Each province is to register them, the addict must carry a registration card and show it on demand, and the federal government pays for a first compulsory detoxification. Provinces are to set up treatment centres.

## Why the index did not move

The [Act](/history/pakistan-narcotics-act-1997/) is recorded as context. The step that set [Pakistan's](/country/pk/) scores is the prohibition order of 1979, which closed the licensed shops; the 1997 Act put that prohibition into a single code with fixed terms. A caution on the table above: the copy read is published by a provincial judicial academy and marks the table as substituted by a Sindh act of 2021, so the bhang rows in particular should be checked against the federal gazette.
