In 1961, the year the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs was signed, Canada replaced its old opium and narcotics statute with the Narcotic Control Act.[@3] The Act implemented the convention, and it stayed Canada's drug law until 1996.[@2,3] Cannabis was one of its "narcotics", next to heroin and cocaine.[@1]

## The offences

| Section | Offence | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Possession | 7 years |
| 4 | Trafficking, or possession for the purpose of trafficking | life |
| 5 | Importing or exporting | life, with a minimum of 7 years |
| 6 | Growing opium poppy or marijuana without a federal licence | 7 years |

"Traffic" was defined widely: to manufacture, sell, give, administer, transport, send, deliver or distribute, or to offer to do any of them. Giving a drug to another person therefore counted as trafficking.[@1]

Possession was lawful only for licensed dealers, pharmacists and practitioners who needed the drug for their profession.[@1]

## Harsh at the top, looser at the bottom

Importing carried a minimum of seven years whatever the amount.[@1] For simple possession the Act moved the other way. The earlier law had required at least six months in prison for possession. The 1961 Act set a ceiling and no floor, so a judge could again impose a fine or probation.[@3]

## A law ahead of its problem

Cannabis had been on Canada's schedule since [1923](/history/canada-ban-1923/), but there was very little of it. The first police seizure came only in 1937. Between 1946 and 1961 cannabis accounted for about 2% of all drug arrests in the country.[@1]

That changed within a few years of the Act. Cases rose to 2,300 in 1968 and to 12,000 in 1972, and the people charged were increasingly middle-class young people, which led first to a [change in procedure in 1969](/history/canada-summary-conviction-1969/) and then to a royal commission.[@2]

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Canada](/country/ca/) from 9 to 11 with the [1961 Act](/history/canada-narcotic-control-act-1961/). Only enforcement changed, from 1 to 2, because prison was no longer mandatory for possession. Every other segment stays where the earlier law had put it.
