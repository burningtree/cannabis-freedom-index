In 1988 Costa Rica did something almost no country in the region had tried: it took
drug use out of the criminal law. Law 7093, the first version of the country's
present drug law, made the use of drugs an administrative matter punished with
day-fines — while production and trafficking stayed crimes.[@1]

## Use without prison

The 1988 formula drew a line the later laws kept: the user paid a fine, the
trafficker went to prison.[@1] Costa Rica never re-criminalised consumption itself;
the 2001 rewrite and every later reform argued about trafficking bands, plant counts
and supply routes instead.[@1]

## The line that held

Personal possession carries no criminal penalty to this day, home growing is tolerated
case by case where trafficking cannot be proved, and even the failed 2024
recreational bill had to be struck down as unconstitutional rather than enforced
against users.[@1]
