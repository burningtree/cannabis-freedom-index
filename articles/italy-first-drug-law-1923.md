Italy's Law 396, dated 18 February 1923, established criminal controls over
unauthorised narcotics supply. It was published on 5 March and took effect on
20 March. The original text helps explain what the law punished, while a later
regulation establishes where cannabis appeared in the framework.

Together, the two instruments document a system of controlled supply, medical
distribution and penalties. Calling the February law a blanket ban on possessing
hashish loses distinctions that matter to understanding its reach.

## Holding drugs for supply

Article 1 addressed selling or administering specified narcotics to the public
without the medicinal-trade status described in the provision. It also covered
holding those substances for sale or administration. The ordinary penalty was
two to six months' imprisonment and a fine of 1,000 to 4,000 lire.

That purpose attached to possession is significant. The provision did not make
every instance of possession for personal use the same offence as holding a
stock for supply. Other articles regulated manufacturers and intermediaries,
prescriptions and the conduct of authorised medicinal sellers.

## Gatherings were separately punished

The law also reached some circumstances of consumption. Article 8 punished
people responsible for premises used for gatherings to take narcotics, including
where the substances were provided free. It allowed closure of the premises
and confiscation of their furnishings.

Article 10 imposed fines on participants in those gatherings, with detention
possible for repeat offending. A description limited to commercial trafficking
would therefore miss part of the law. These were specified offences connected
with gatherings, rather than an undifferentiated personal-possession rule.

## Cannabis entered the express list

The February law named cocaine and morphine and used a wider category of
stupefying poisonous substances. Article 12 provided for a ministerial list.

The [implementing regulation](/history/italy-indian-hemp-schedule-1923/), approved
by Royal Decree 2534, expressly included Indian hemp and its hydroalcoholic
extract in Table A. Its authorisation rules covered importing, exporting,
manufacturing, selling and holding scheduled substances for sale or
administration. Public pharmacies were exempt from that particular authorisation
requirement, which should not be read as exemption from the entire framework.

## Two instruments, several dates

The decree was dated 9 November, published on 8 December and took effect on
23 December 1923. Its transitional provisions required declarations of existing
stocks, another feature of the administrative controls accompanying criminal
penalties.

For [Italy's history](/country/it/), the original documents establish both the
early law's supply focus and the regulation's explicit cannabis listing. They
do not establish how consistently authorities enforced those rules, or justify
assigning every cannabis activity the same legal status from February onward.
