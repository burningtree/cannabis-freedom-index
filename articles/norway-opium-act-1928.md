Norway's first drug law with a penalty dates from 1928. The index takes it as the country's first restriction on cannabis, although the drug was almost unknown in Norway for another forty years.

## From 1913 to 1928

Norway passed an Opium Act in 1913, the year after the Hague Convention. According to the Institute of Public Health's historical overview, it covered opium, opiates and cocaine and contained no penal provision.

In 1925 the Geneva Convention brought cannabis under international control and required the countries that ratified it to introduce penalties. Three years later the Storting replaced the 1913 law with a new Opium Act. It applied to opium and to other narcotic substances considered liable to misuse, with the King given power to name them, and it set a punishment: a fine or up to six months in prison. The overview does not say that the 1928 text named cannabis.

## The law catches up with the drug

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1964 | The Medicines Act replaces the Opium Act; narcotics offences carry up to two years |
| 1965 | The first seizure of cannabis in Norway, and the first conviction under the Medicines Act, of a foreign student |
| 1968 | Section 162 of the Penal Code sets up to six years for serious offences; use itself is banned in the Medicines Act |
| 1972 | The maximum rises to ten years |
| 1984 | The maximum rises to 21 years, the most severe punishment in Norwegian law |

In 1966, the overview notes, groups of young people had begun smoking hashish in the park beside the royal palace in Oslo, and the Penal Law Council was asked to consider harsher penalties. The steep rise in maximum sentences came in the two decades that followed.

## Why the index moved

The [1928 Act](/history/norway-opium-act-1928/) is the first step in [Norway's](/country/no/) score, from the unregulated state to prohibition. The index places the change there because it is the first law that could punish, and that reached beyond the opiates, while recognising that cannabis prosecutions began only in 1965. The second scored step came in 2022, when the Supreme Court ruled that people dependent on drugs should not be punished for small amounts. The country's present total is 17.
