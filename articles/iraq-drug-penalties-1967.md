People possessing or growing cannabis for personal use without permission faced one to five years in prison and a fine after Iraq changed its drug law in 1967.[@1] Giving drugs away and growing for trade brought hard labour and larger fines, with especially severe punishment for officials involved in drug control.[@1]

## The earlier law had allowed a fine alone

The [1965 narcotics law](/history/iraq-narcotics-law-1965/) had brought cultivation and drug controls together.[@2] Its general penalty for many violations allowed a fine, six months to five years in prison, or both.[@2]

The 1967 amendment replaced that general approach with separate groups of offences.[@1] Personal-use possession or growing now required imprisonment and a fine of 100–500 dinars.[@1]

The distinction between personal use and supply mattered because the punishment changed according to the purpose and the conduct involved.[@1]

## A gift counted as supply

The supply offences included selling, delivering drugs to someone else, giving them away without payment and acting as an intermediary.[@1] Holding or buying drugs for trade and cultivating cannabis for that purpose were covered too.[@1]

Those offences carried a term of hard labour and a fine of 500–2,000 dinars.[@1] Customs staff and officials charged with controlling drugs faced lifelong hard labour and a larger fine if they committed the covered offences.[@1]

For cannabis users, passing the drug to another person could therefore move a case into the supply rules even when no money changed hands.[@1]

## The rules reached places where people gathered

Allowing other people to use drugs on one's premises was punishable even without payment.[@1] Being knowingly present where drugs were being used could also be an offence, subject to specified family exceptions.[@1]

The amendment separately punished encouraging someone under eighteen to use drugs.[@1] It also required confiscation of seized drugs and equipment, and destruction of illegally grown plants.[@1]

People who knew about unlawful crops had to report them or risk a fine.[@1] The law thus reached growers, suppliers and people around the place where drugs were used.[@1]

## Another increase followed within two years

The [1968 amendment](/history/iraq-drug-death-penalties-1968/) raised personal-use possession and growing to three to ten years and increased supply penalties.[@3] It introduced death or lifelong hard labour for specified repeat offenders, trafficking leaders and officials.[@3]

In [Iraq](/country/iq/), the 1967 separation between personal use and supply became the basis for another round of harsher punishment.[@1,3]
