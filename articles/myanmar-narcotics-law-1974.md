On 20 February 1974 Burma's Revolutionary Council enacted the Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs Law, a sweeping prohibition that covered cannabis along with opium, heroin and other drugs. Possession, cultivation and transport carried five to ten years in prison. Manufacture, import, export and sale carried ten years to an unlimited term, a fine or death. The law was passed at the peak of a heroin epidemic and stayed on the books, as amended, into the 1990s.[@1,2]

## From opium to heroin

Before the 1970s, opium and to some extent cannabis were the main drugs of abuse in Burma, according to a 1985 article by U Khant in the UN Bulletin on Narcotics.[@2] Heroin spread rapidly among young people in the early 1970s and reached epidemic proportions, bringing serious social and health problems. The authorities responded with the 1974 law.[@2]

The law treated cannabis as one of the narcotic and dangerous drugs whose production, possession and sale were prohibited for the whole population. Its definition covered any product of the poppy, coca leaf and cannabis plants, as well as substances the government declared to be narcotics.[@1]

## What the law banned

Section 3 banned cultivating the cannabis plant for producing drugs. Later chapters banned production, manufacture, possession, transport, import, export, external dealing, transfer and sale.[@1] Possession was allowed only with government permission.[@1]

Cultivation, production, possession, transport and transfer carried five to ten years and a fine of up to 10,000 kyats. Manufacture, import, export and sale carried ten years to an unlimited term, a fine of up to 50,000 kyats, or death.[@1] The law also allowed the confiscation of drugs, equipment, vehicles, land and buildings.[@1]

## Treatment and registration

Addicts had to register with the government, which could direct their curative treatment. Failure to follow its directions carried six months to three years.[@1] The law exempted government work in medicine, research or other purposes, and authorised institutions and officers on duty.[@1]

## Amendments and after

The law was amended in 1983 and 1988. A 1994 Library of Congress summary describes the amended text and the same division between five-to-ten-year offences and the heavier penalties for manufacture, import, export and sale.[@3] Myanmar's [1993 narcotics law](/history/myanmar-narcotics-law-1993/) is a later entry in its history, and the [country profile](/country/mm/) sets out the present rules.
