In September 2021 a third of a million Italians signed for a cannabis referendum in
three days. Five months later the Constitutional Court ruled that they could
not vote.

## A referendum country

Italy has changed its drug law by referendum before. In
[1993](/history/italy-referendum-1993/) voters repealed the criminal penalties for
possessing drugs for personal use that had been introduced three years earlier.
Possession became an administrative matter, punished by suspending a driving licence
or passport. Growing stayed a crime, with two to six years in prison.

In [December 2019](/history/italy-home-growing-2019/) the Court of Cassation ruled
that growing is not a crime when it is minimal, domestic and rudimentary and involves
few plants. It did not say how many.

## The signatures

The 2021 campaign collected signatures online, using the state's digital identity. It
gathered 330,000 in 72 hours and more than 630,000 in all, above the
half million a referendum needs.

The question would have legalised growing cannabis for personal use and eased the
penalties for related offences, so that people selling small amounts no longer risked
prison.

## The ruling

On 16 February 2022 the Constitutional Court declared the question inadmissible. Its
president, the former prime minister Giuliano Amato, said the referendum would have
been enough to make Italy "violate multiple international obligations". The same day
the court blocked a referendum on assisted suicide.

Benedetto Della Vedova of the +Europa party said the ruling deprived Italy of a public
debate. Matteo Salvini of the League, who opposed the proposal, argued that it would
encourage the use of soft and hard drugs alike.

## Since then

No vote was held. In September 2024 the Chamber of Deputies approved a security decree
restricting hemp flower products, and in 2025 the
[ban on trading hemp flowers](/history/italy-hemp-flower-ban-2025/) became law.

The ruling moves no score for [Italy](/country/it/): the law stayed as the courts had
left it in 2019.
