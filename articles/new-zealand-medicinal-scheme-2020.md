On 1 April 2020 New Zealand's Medicinal Cannabis Scheme opened: doctors could prescribe
products meeting a national quality standard, dispensed by pharmacies, for any
condition.[@1] Medicinal law had changed the year before; the scheme made it real.[@1]

## Medicine while the country voted

The scheme arrived in the same year New Zealanders voted on full legalisation. The
referendum failed narrowly — 50.7% against proposed legislation, 48.4% in
favour — so recreational cannabis stayed illegal while the medical channel
operated.[@1] Any doctor can prescribe, but products are unsubsidised and costly, and
the market stays small beside the illegal one.

## The science advice around it

The Chief Science Advisor's office framed the scheme as one half of a health-centred
approach: the Misuse of Drugs Act affirms discretion to press charges, taking that
health-centred line, and police have been required since August 2019 to consider a
health response before prosecuting possession.[@1] Its evidence review noted the equity
stakes — Maori have borne the brunt of biased enforcement — and that most local
illicit cannabis runs high-THC, low-CBD.[@1]

[New Zealand's profile](/country/nz/) reflects the split the scheme created: legal
prescription medicines from pharmacies under the [2020
scheme](/history/new-zealand-medicinal-scheme-2020/), against the failed
[referendum](/history/new-zealand-referendum-2020/) for everything else.
