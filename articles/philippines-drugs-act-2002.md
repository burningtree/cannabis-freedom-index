The Philippines' new drug law took effect on 4 July 2002, making possession of less than 300 grams of marijuana punishable by twelve years and one day to twenty years in prison, plus a large fine.[@1,2] Unauthorised selling, giving away or growing cannabis carried life imprisonment to death regardless of quantity.[@1]

## Quantity had previously allowed much shorter sentences

Under the [1993 amendment](/history/philippines-death-penalty-1993/), the highest band for specified marijuana offences began at 750 grams; the corresponding resin or oil threshold was fifty grams.[@4] The Supreme Court's [1994 Simon ruling](/history/philippines-simon-quantity-ruling-1994/) placed marijuana below 250 grams in a much lower prison range and applied the favourable change to earlier offences.[@3]

The 2002 Act replaced that system with separate provisions for possession, supply, cultivation and use.[@1] It repealed the 1972 drug law and created the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency, with the Dangerous Drugs Board responsible for policy and strategy.[@1]

## Possession began with more than twelve years

For marijuana below 300 grams, the fine accompanying the twelve-to-twenty-year prison range was ₱300,000–400,000.[@1] At 300 grams but below 500 grams, the prison range rose to twenty years and one day to life, plus ₱400,000–500,000.[@1]

At least 500 grams brought life imprisonment to death and a fine of ₱500,000–10 million.[@1] Resin and resin oil had lower thresholds: below five grams, five to below ten grams, and at least ten grams.[@1] A personal-use claim did not create a smaller possession band in this provision.[@1]

## Sharing and growing had no small-amount tier

The supply offence covered selling, delivering and giving a dangerous drug to another person.[@1] Its life-to-death range applied regardless of quantity, ending the small-sale reductions available under the earlier quantity scheme.[@1,3]

The same severe range applied to planting or cultivating marijuana regardless of quantity.[@1] The Act provided for regulated cultivation by medical laboratories and research centres for experiments, research and developing medicines.[@1]

## Use and punishment followed different tracks

A first confirmed use offence called for at least six months of rehabilitation in a government centre; a second carried six years and one day to twelve years and a fine.[@1] When the person also possessed drugs covered by the possession offence, that provision applied instead.[@1]

The [2006 abolition](/history/philippines-death-penalty-abolished-2006/) removed death sentences, substituting the applicable prison penalty.[@5] It left the long custodial ranges that remain central to [the Philippines' cannabis restrictions](/country/ph/).[@1,5]
