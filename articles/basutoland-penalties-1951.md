Proclamation No. 58 of 1951 amended the Basutoland drug proclamation so that
any narcotics offence drew a fine of up to £500 or, in default, up to three
years in prison with or without hard labour — or prison without the option of a
fine, or both.[@1]

The 1955 United Nations survey that records the amendment adds the practice:
no statutory distinction separated smoker from trafficker, but traffickers and
cultivators got somewhat more severe sentences, and courts used the quantity
found to decide which the offender was.[@1]

The same survey fixes the vocabulary: cannabis was known locally as dagga or
matekoane.[@1]
