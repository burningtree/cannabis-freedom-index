Law 23.737 was passed by Argentina's Congress on 21 September 1989 and published on 11 October. It is still the country's drug law. It answered the Supreme Court's [1986 ruling](/history/argentina-bazterrica-1986/), which had protected possession for personal use, by writing that offence back into the statute with a lighter sentence and a way out through treatment.

## The penalties

| Conduct | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Growing, producing or trading without authorisation (Article 5) | 4 to 15 years in prison and a fine |
| Supplying free of charge | 3 to 12 years |
| Growing a small amount for personal use (Article 5) | 1 month to 2 years |
| Possession (Article 14) | 1 to 6 years and a fine |
| Possession for personal use (Article 14, second paragraph) | 1 month to 2 years |
| Using a drug ostentatiously in public (Article 12) | 2 to 6 years |

## Treatment in place of prison

The law treats the user as a patient under supervision, on pain of the sentence returning.

- A dependent person convicted of possession for personal use may have the sentence suspended and be ordered into treatment; if it succeeds, the penalty is lifted (Article 17).
- Before trial, proceedings may be suspended for treatment, ending in dismissal (Article 18).
- A first-time or experimental user may, once, be given an educational programme of at least three months in place of the penalty (Article 21).
- After three years of recovery a judge may have the entry removed from the criminal record (Article 22).

## Twenty years in the courts

The Supreme Court upheld the personal-use offence in 1990, in the Montalvo case. In 2009, in [Arriola](/history/argentina-arriola-2009/), it unanimously declared the second paragraph of Article 14 unconstitutional as applied to adults holding small amounts in private. The paragraph was never repealed, so the text of the law and the practice of the courts have differed since.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Argentina](/country/ar/) from 22 to 11 with [Law 23.737](/history/argentina-law-23737-1989/): possession and consumption from 3 to 1, and enforcement from 4 to 2. Enforcement did not fall further because the sentence for a user was short and could be replaced by treatment or a course.
