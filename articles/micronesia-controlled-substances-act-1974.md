In the last weeks of July 1974 the Congress of Micronesia passed Public Law 5-110, the Trust Territory Controlled Substances Act, in a special session that ran from 23 July to 9 August.[@3] Marihuana meant all parts of the cannabis plant, its seeds and the resin from any part, and it went into Schedule I beside tetrahydrocannabinol.[@1] For the first time the whole Trust Territory — from Palau to the Marshalls to Chuuk — shared one marijuana law.[@1]

## A director decides

The Act copied the American model: the health director would recommend, on current medical knowledge, which substances belonged in which schedule, and the Congress would list them.[@1] Sections 301 and 302 set up that pipeline, letting the director's judgment decide what the territory's drug law covered.[@2]

## Tested the same week

The scheme met its test almost immediately.[@2] Prosecutors charged Bermudes with possessing and selling marijuana under section 303, and the defence attacked the whole edifice: marijuana was no narcotic, personal use sat inside a protected zone of privacy, and letting an administrator choose the regulated drugs was an unlawful delegation of Congress's power.[@2] Decided 23 July 1974 — as the special session that passed the Act was opening — the court split the difference.[@2,3] The dismissal is tracked separately as [the Bermudes charges case](/history/cnmi-bermudes-marijuana-charges-1974/).[@2] The territory could prohibit the possession, use and sale of the drug, but the delegation failed: the director's power to decide which drugs counted violated equal protection, and marijuana could not properly be classed a narcotic anyway.[@2] The judges added a pointed aside — the penalties for marijuana ran harsher than those for opium and heroin, and nothing in current knowledge justified it.[@2]

## Adopted twice over

The Act outlived the territory.[@4] The Federated States adopted the old Trust Territory Code as its own criminal code in 1980, carrying the marijuana penalties with it: up to $50 for an ounce or less, three months or $500 to a kilo, a year or $1,000 above it, and a presumption of trafficking — up to five years or $5,000 — for kilo quantities.[@4] Chuuk, which never wrote its own drug law, simply used the national code.[@4] That 1980 adoption is why the 1974 Act still anchors the recorded first prohibitions of [Micronesia](/history/micronesia-controlled-substances-act-1974/), [Palau](/history/palau-controlled-substances-act-1974/) and the [Northern Marianas](/history/cnmi-controlled-substances-act-1974/) alike.[@1,4]
