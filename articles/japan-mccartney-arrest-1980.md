Paul McCartney arrived in Japan for an eleven-concert Wings tour on 16 January 1980. Customs officers instead found 219 grams of cannabis in his luggage, beginning ten days in detention and cancelling the entire tour.

## Arrival at Narita

McCartney travelled with his wife Linda and their four children. Japan had refused him a visa in 1976, but admitted him for the short 1980 visit. At Narita International Airport, customs officers found the cannabis in his luggage. The source reports a street value of 600,000 yen.

Narcotics officers arrested and handcuffed him, questioned him for an hour and held him for further questioning. In detention he became prisoner number 22.

## Eleven concerts disappear

Wings had scheduled eleven dates from 21 January to 2 February in Tokyo, Nagoya and Osaka. The promoters had reportedly sold almost 100,000 tickets, so the arrest immediately placed a large tour and substantial ticket revenue at risk.

Japanese television and radio stations stopped playing Wings' music. The other members of the band left the country on 21 January. None of the concerts took place.

## Ten days, then deportation

McCartney remained imprisoned until 25 January. Japanese authorities then released him without charge and deported him to England. The outcome avoided a trial, but the detention, cancellation and expulsion demonstrated the practical consequences of Japan's strict cannabis enforcement even for one of the world's best-known musicians.

The episode did not create a celebrity exception. It is remembered precisely because fame did not prevent arrest, prolonged detention or removal from the country.

## The law later became stricter

At the time, Japan punished possession but did not separately criminalise consumption. That distinction survived for decades. A reform that took effect in December 2024 [made cannabis use itself a crime](/history/japan-use-criminalised-2024/) and raised the maximum penalty for possession.

## Why the index did not move

The [McCartney arrest](/history/japan-mccartney-arrest-1980/) was an enforcement episode under existing law, not a statutory change. It therefore moves none of [Japan's](/country/jp/) six scores. Its place in the timeline is evidential: it makes the severity and international reach of the established regime unusually visible.
