The first dated restriction on cannabis in Guinea's record was written in Paris. On 30 December 1916 a decree extended a French law on poisonous substances to French West Africa, the federation of colonies that included what is now Guinea.

## A law made for France

The law was the Act of 12 July 1916 on the importation, exportation, trade in, possession and use of poisonous substances. It was passed during the First World War for metropolitan France, and the regulations made under it there list Indian hemp, its resin and their preparations among the narcotics of Schedule B.

Three further decrees carried it into the colonies.

| Date | Decree |
|---|---|
| 30 December 1916 | extends the Act to French West Africa |
| 5 March 1918 | on the application of the Act there |
| 26 January 1926 | regulations on the trade in, possession and use of poisonous substances in French West Africa |
| 31 May 1952 | amends one article of the 1926 regulations |

What these texts said about cannabis in the colony is not confirmed. They are known from a filing France made to the United Nations in 1953, which cites them by title and date and reproduces only the 1952 amendment, a change of one word.

## After independence

Guinea's rules today are in its Penal Code. Article 377 covers the serious offences, from illicit production and international trafficking to financing and laundering, and further articles deal with supplying a drug to someone for their personal use, with driving under the influence, and with optional penalties against foreigners and assets. A decree of 2011 created a general secretariat in the presidency charged with special services against drugs and organised crime.

A review of the region's laws in 2017 could find no stated penalty for possession or use. The index's own record of the 2016 Penal Code gives one to three years for personal use.

## Why the index moved

The [1916 decree](/history/french-west-africa-poisonous-substances-act-1916-gn/) is the first step in [Guinea's](/country/gn/) score, from the unregulated state to prohibition. The index dates the change to the extension of the French Act because that is the earliest text that reached the territory, while noting that the colonial wording has not been read. The country's present total is 9.
