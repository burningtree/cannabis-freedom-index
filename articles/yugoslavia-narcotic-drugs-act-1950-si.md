Slovenia came under a federal ban on growing Indian hemp through Yugoslavia’s 1950 narcotics law.[@1] Specified scientific institutions could grow it on their own experimental fields, under conditions and quantities fixed by the public-health authority.[@1] The law placed narcotic cultivation, manufacture and trade within the state economic plan.[@1]

## Scientific institutions had a narrow exception

Under the federal law, Indian hemp appeared among controlled raw drugs, while its extracts, tinctures and resins were listed as preparations.[@1] Manufacturing and preparing narcotic drugs was reserved to appointed public undertakings for medical and scientific purposes.[@1] Retail supply ran through pharmacies, dispensaries and specified veterinary outlets, with registers recording drugs received and supplied.[@1]

Unlawful production for gain and unlawful trade could bring corrective labour or up to two years in prison.[@1] Import and export offences had a separate three-year maximum, with higher penalties for specified aggravated cases.[@1] Drugs involved in those offences had to be confiscated.[@1]

The decree was dated 23 January 1950; the act specified commencement on publication in the federal official gazette.[@1]

## The later law kept possession separate from supply

A study of Slovenian parliamentary debate traces the next major federal framework to the [1964 Narcotic Drugs Act](/history/slovenia-yugoslav-narcotics-act-1964/).[@2] It describes continued restrictions on cannabis cultivation and distribution, with possession treated as a minor offence and use itself outside a separate offence.[@2]

The authors emphasise the difficulty of distinguishing personal activity from production or possession for resale.[@2] Their account also explains that giving another person cannabis to consume falls under the modern offence of facilitating drug use, so the personal-possession category does not settle the law on sharing.[@2]

## Medical and scientific cultivation gained distinct routes

Slovenia’s [2025 cannabis Act](/history/slovenia-medical-law-2025/) created separate authorisations for medical and scientific production and trade.[@3] Scientific permission comes from the Health Ministry and covers a defined research project or teaching process.[@3]

Researchers can seek permission to work with any cannabis variety, but the law requires secured premises, traceability and arrangements for destroying waste.[@3] The [2026 research rules](/history/slovenia-research-permit-rules-2026/) supplied detailed application requirements under that Act.[@4] The [Slovenia profile](/country/si/) places those licensed routes alongside the rules affecting personal possession, growing and supply.[@2,3]
