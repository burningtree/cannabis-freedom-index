Luxembourg’s medical cannabis law took effect on 5 August 2018, giving specially trained doctors a route to prescribe approved cannabis products.[@4,2] Patients would obtain treatment through hospital pharmacies, under a programme that [began dispensing in February 2019](/history/luxembourg-medical-dispensing-starts-2019/).[@1,3]

## Patients needed a qualifying illness

The law covered serious, advanced or terminal illness, or illness whose symptoms substantially and persistently harmed quality of life and could be relieved by cannabis.[@2] Regulations identified severe chronic pain, chemotherapy-related nausea or vomiting and symptomatic muscle spasticity associated with multiple sclerosis.[@1]

Eligibility also depended on a connection to Luxembourg: residence, Luxembourg nationality or coverage by Luxembourg health insurance could qualify a patient.[@1] The medical route therefore depended on both clinical criteria and a qualifying personal connection.[@1]

## Doctors trained before prescribing

Doctors authorised to practise in Luxembourg needed special training before prescribing cannabis.[@2] The law required teaching on pharmacology, available forms, indications, adverse effects and the scientific and practical basis for prescribing.[@2]

The programme evaluation describes training delivered in January and February 2019, before the first patients entered the pilot.[@1] The treatment route brought doctors and hospital pharmacists into the same controlled process for prescribing and dispensing.[@1]

## Products came through hospital pharmacies

The law’s medical definition included standardised dried flowering tops, extracts, tinctures and oils approved by the health authority.[@2] Dispensing was reserved to hospital pharmacies.[@2] The evaluation describes a centrally organised programme with a working group and scientific committee overseeing its development.[@1]

Patients therefore obtained specified medicinal products through a prescription, with the law assigning responsibility for their quality and supply to the medical system.[@1,2] The [Luxembourg profile](/country/lu/) follows that route separately from the later permission to grow cannabis at home.[@2]

## The programme later changed its flower supply

In November 2024, the Health Ministry announced a reduction in the flower allowance from 100 to 60 grams per twenty-eight days.[@3] It also announced the end of replenishment of THC-dominant flower from January 2025, while continuing CBD-dominant and balanced flower.[@3]

THC-dominant treatment remained available as oil, alongside balanced and CBD-dominant oils.[@3] The ministry cited dosing, administration and adverse-effect concerns in explaining the [2025 changes](/history/luxembourg-medical-flower-restrictions-2025/).[@3]
