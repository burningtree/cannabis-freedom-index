For more than sixty years, anyone in [Tunisia](/country/tn/) could buy cannabis in a tobacco shop. It
came in a five-gram box of chopped flower heads called takrouri, prepared by the state. A decree of
23 April 1953, published in the *Journal Officiel Tunisien* five days later, ended that trade.

## A state product

The geographer Chris Duvall writes that cannabis was smoked openly in mid-nineteenth-century Tunisia,
then still under Ottoman rule, and that growing it was [banned in 1875](/history/tunisia-cultivation-ban-1875/).
Three years after France made Tunisia a protectorate, a [decree of 1884](/history/tunisia-hemp-decree-1884/)
set up a tobacco monopoly that let farmers grow only under licence and only in certain areas.
[Takrouri joined it in 1890](/history/tunisia-takrouri-monopoly-1890/). The aim, Duvall writes, was less
revenue than control: to limit the land used for the crop and to stop adulteration.

The system was simple. The monopoly issued permits, fixed the area to be planted each year and bought
the whole harvest; the Tunis tobacco factory chopped and packed it. From 1902 there was a single product,
the five-gram box, and in the 1910s about five tonnes were sold a year. The Transnational Institute
calls this regime "controlled toleration": it kept smuggling down and use within moderate limits.

## The 1953 decree

The decree, which France sent to the United Nations, turned the monopoly product into contraband.

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Growing, possessing, buying, selling, carrying, importing or exporting hemp or takrouri | Prohibited |
| Consuming it | Prohibited |
| Medical and veterinary use | Allowed only until pharmacy stocks ran out, by 1 January 1955 at the latest |
| Hemp for textiles | Grown only under licence, with the usable parts destroyed |
| Unlicensed growing | Fine of 1,000 francs per 100 plants, 5,000 to 50,000 francs |
| Possessing unlicensed hemp flowers | 2,000 francs per kilogram, 5,000 to 70,000 francs |

Pedlars who sold takrouri door to door also lost their means of transport, on top of the penalties
of a 1939 decree on toxic substances.

## Independence and after

Tunisia became independent in 1956 and kept the ban. It joined the
[Single Convention](/history/tunisia-single-convention-1964/) in 1964. Its
[Law 52 of 1992](/history/tunisia-law-52-1992/) made at least a year in prison mandatory for using or
possessing any amount, and only in [2017](/history/tunisia-law-52-amended-2017/) were judges allowed to
weigh mitigating circumstances.
