The Somali Republic placed narcotics possession, cultivation and supply under health-ministry authorisation in 1970, covering the north now administered as Somaliland.[@1] Unauthorised possession carried two to seven years in prison and a fine, while cultivation carried six months to two years and a fine.[@1]

## Control moved through the health ministry

The law created a system involving a central narcotics bureau, a special police detachment and regional and district health officials.[@1] The health secretary was responsible for drawing up the controlled-substance list, taking international conventions and the bureau's advice into account.[@1]

A [September decree](/history/somaliland-cannabis-schedule-1970/) then expressly included cannabis, with hashish as a synonym, and Indian hemp.[@2] That list connected the licensing and punishment rules to named substances.[@1,2]

The earlier [penal code](/history/somaliland-penal-code-1964/) had already punished drug trading and possession for sale.[@3] The new law expressly addressed acquisition and possession within a wider authorisation system.[@1]

## Growing and passing drugs on needed permission

Cultivating, harvesting or processing plants that yielded controlled drugs required special health-ministry permission, with conditions set after consultation with the agriculture ministry.[@1] Breaching those requirements brought the lower cultivation penalty.[@1]

Importing, exporting, producing, buying, selling or holding the listed drugs required registration and authorisation.[@1] Transfers had to go to authorised recipients even when no payment changed hands.[@1]

The law also required delivery and transport documents, making control of the supply chain part of the licensing system.[@1]

## Prescriptions and dependency were treated differently

State medical establishments and pharmacies were entered into the authorised register, and pharmacies could dispense medicinal preparations on prescription.[@1] They had to verify the adult patient's identity, supply the prescribed quantity and keep records.[@1]

Doctors could be punished for prescribing to encourage drug abuse without therapeutic need or in excessive quantities.[@1] The law also required health professionals to report dependency and permitted compulsory detoxification after medical assessment where habitual drug abuse made someone dangerous to themselves or others.[@1]

## Heavier penalties followed under the same state

The [1984 decree](/history/somaliland-drug-offences-decree-1984/) imposed ten years for unlawful drug manufacture, import, dealing and supply, alongside new penalties for consumption venues.[@4]

Somaliland later adopted its own constitution, retaining earlier laws compatible with sharia and fundamental rights until replacement.[@5] The 1970 law is therefore an important part of the inherited legal background to cannabis control in [Somaliland](/country/xs/).[@1,5]
