Curaçao’s Advisory Council asked the government to strengthen its proposed medical-cannabis industry before taking the bill to Parliament. In July 2019, its concerns included the economic case, licensing procedures and how a new cannabis bureau would pay growers and recover its money from buyers.[@1]

## Growing still needed a change in the law

The inherited drug ordinance prohibited growing plants of the genus Cannabis. Its later consolidated text also controlled possession and use, while providing medical exceptions for specified activities and lawfully obtained treatment supplies.[@2]

Small personal quantities were handled through a separate [prosecution policy](/history/curacao-prosecution-guideline-2018/). That guidance offered confiscation and possible dismissal in its lowest cannabis band. It required prosecutors to assess grow cases individually, considering their scale and circumstances. For minors, even user quantities were to be prosecuted to support assistance services.[@3]

## A bureau would stand between growers and buyers

The proposed system required licensed growers to sell their cannabis plants exclusively to the new bureau. The council asked how harvest purchases would be financed and what guarantees would ensure that buyers repaid the bureau. It also sought clearer application procedures and stronger support for the economic assumptions.[@1]

The council concluded that the government should address its observations before submitting the proposal. Its signed opinion is dated 16 July 2019.[@1]

## Investors anticipated a new industry

By September, local reporting said a [bill for the cannabis office](/history/curacao-medical-cannabis-bill-2019/) had reached Parliament. The office was intended to maintain contact with growers and sellers.[@4]

In October, ministry official Alvin Daal reported interest from nine companies. He described plans covering cultivation, processing, medicine manufacturing and research, with local investors collaborating with overseas businesses. The jobs and revenues he discussed were forecasts attached to those plans.[@5]

## Approval remained a political question

In April 2023, [opposition lawmakers renewed their call for approval](/history/curacao-medical-cannabis-opposition-2023/) after two parliamentary discussions and a change of government. The reporting identified former minister Suzanne Camelia-Römer as the original political advocate.[@6]

The [replacement ordinance in 2024](/history/curacao-opium-ordinance-2024/) retained the cultivation ban and medical exceptions, and created a route for future exemptions requiring Parliament’s prior approval. The [Curaçao profile](/country/cw/) sets out those rules and the separate prosecution guidance for small quantities.[@7]
