Lithuania’s medical reform, effective from May 2019, opened a route for registered cannabis-based medicines. Patients would receive pharmaceutical products whose safety and effectiveness had been demonstrated, under rules separate from the prohibition on recreational possession.[@1,4]

## A parliamentary route to medicines

Parliament [approved the reform on 11 October 2018](/history/lithuania-medical-vote-2018/), with ninety members voting in favour and three abstaining.[@1] Conservative politicians Mykolas Majauskas and Irena Degutienė had promoted the proposal, and the vote sent it to the president ahead of a planned 1 May start.[@1]

Majauskas presented the change as a way for seriously ill patients to obtain medicines registered in the European Union, for treatment or symptom relief.[@1] That placed the medicine and its authorisation at the centre of access: a diagnosis alone would not create an open market in cannabis.[@1]

## What the medicines register shows

The state register lists Epidyolex, a prescription oral solution containing cannabidiol at 100 mg per millilitre.[@2] Its authorisation date is 19 September 2019, through the central European procedure.[@2]

The register distinguishes being authorised from being supplied: one 100 ml bottle presentation is marked as supplied, while the three-bottle presentation is not.[@2] It also marks this cannabidiol medicine as not controlled.[@2] A patient’s actual route therefore depends on the particular product and package available through the medicines system.[@2]

## Manufacturers need separate permissions

The medicines agency provides a licensing route for manufacturing medicinal products containing Schedule I substances.[@3] Applicants need both a pharmaceutical manufacturing licence and a licence for the controlled-substance activity.[@3]

Those requirements regulate the businesses making medicines; they do not give individual patients a cultivation permit.[@3] The agency’s licensing guidance keeps manufacturing, wholesale and other controlled activities within defined authorisations.[@3]

## Recreational reform followed another path

The drug-control department records that [from January 2017](/history/lithuania-criminalisation-2017/), small unauthorised possession was handled as a criminal misdemeanour, replacing overlapping administrative and criminal liability.[@4] The medical route developed alongside that criminal law.[@1,4]

In [April 2025](/history/lithuania-cannabis-committee-2025/), a parliamentary committee returned to the stalled proposal for administrative treatment of small cannabis possession.[@5] Lithuania’s [country profile](/country/lt/) tracks that debate separately from the registered-medicine route patients can use.[@2,5]
