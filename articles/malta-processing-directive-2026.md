Malta's January 2026 association directive sets out how licensed non-profit associations may make and distribute solid cannabis resin.[@1] Members' daily and monthly allowances are calculated using the product's THC concentration, so a gram of strong resin can consume more of the allowance than a gram of flower.[@1]

## A grower's permit does not cover processing

The regulator had [announced solventless resin permissions in May 2025](/history/malta-solventless-resin-permission-2025/).[@3] Version 4.0 of the directive, dated 16 January 2026, gives the operational requirements in its processing standard.[@1] An association's existing cultivation-and-distribution permit is insufficient: it must obtain a specific processing authorisation from ARUC.[@1]

An applicant must identify the responsible person, describe each proposed product and method, and show the equipment, staff, processing area and estimated quantities.[@1] ARUC can refuse if the proposed process or premises do not meet the reform's harm-reduction principles and compliance requirements.[@1] This places the decision on a defined activity at the association, rather than treating every licensed grower as a resin producer.[@1]

## Solid products made without solvents

Only solventless methods are allowed, with purified water the stated exception to the ban on using liquids, gases, solvents or chemicals to assist processing.[@1] Distillates, oils and tinctures are prohibited, and the finished product must be solid.[@1] The directive also controls the flower used as input and requires testing before the processed product is released.[@1]

Different batches may be mixed only where they are the same strain.[@1] The association must maintain the processing and batch records required by the standard, so its product remains connected to the regulated cultivation and testing system.[@1]

## Strength changes the amount a member receives

The association limits remain seven grams a day and fifty grams a month, expressed as dried-flower quantities.[@1,2] A gram of processed product at up to 33% THC counts as one gram of flower; at 34–66% it counts as two; above 66% it counts as three.[@1] Mixed flower-and-resin supplies must stay within the combined allowance.[@1]

Those calculations govern distribution by associations to their registered members under the [2021 reform](/history/malta-legalisation-2021/).[@1,2] The directive gives associations a route to approved resin while retaining a controlled membership system and product checks.[@1,2] The [Malta profile](/country/mt/) separates that route from household growing and the pharmaceutical products available to medical patients.[@2]
