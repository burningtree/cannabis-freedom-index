In 1890 Ottoman Tunisia folded cannabis into its state tobacco monopoly. Takruri — Tunisian smoking cannabis — came under the same state control as tobacco, in one of only two cannabis monopolies anywhere in the world; the other was Morocco's.[@1] The 1890 law set prices for two products: flower heads, and mixed herbal material.[@1]

## Licensed farming, state sales

Cannabis had been smoked openly in mid-century Tunis, but in 1875 its cultivation was banned.[@1] The tobacco monopoly followed in 1884: farming legal only with a licence and only in certain districts, with the state controlling sales too — meant to limit the land and labour spent on the crop and to stop prepared products being adulterated.[@1] When takruri joined the monopoly in 1890, farming restrictions stayed loose, because Tunisia then relied on imports from Constantine across the border in Algeria; only over the following decades did the protectorate grow its own.[@1]

## The five-gram box

From 1902 the monopoly sold a single product: trimmed and chopped flower heads in five-gram boxes — the most common preparation across the Maghreb, and Tunisia's standard package for decades.[@1] It sold steadily: in the 1910s about five thousand kilograms a year passed over the monopoly counters, and the system lasted into the 1950s.[@1] These were serious revenues — in neighbouring Morocco the tobacco and kif monopolies were a primary source of government income, accounting for over a third of one northern region's takings in 1884.[@1]

## Monopoly instead of prohibition

Tunisia's answer to cannabis was management, not bans. The monopolies generated revenue, but their main purpose was shaping colonial agriculture and limiting consumption — the same administrative strategy several southern European countries used for tobacco, though only Morocco and Tunisia applied it to cannabis.[@1] Where Egypt's 1879 ban had driven a vibrant hashish economy underground, Tunisia and Morocco kept legal but strictly controlled internal markets.[@1] Morocco, by contrast, lurched between monopoly and executive prohibition — its sultan [banned kif outright in 1888](/history/morocco-hassan-kif-ban-1888/) before his son restored the monopolies.[@1] Tunisia held its course until the [1953 ban](/history/tunisia-takrouri-ban-1953/) finally closed the monopoly era.[@1] The [country page](/country/tn/) follows the arc from licensed boxes to prohibition.
