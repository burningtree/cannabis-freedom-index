In 1939 Burma allowed ganja to be sold legally, but only to Indians. The rules created a government-controlled trade for one community while the earlier prohibition remained for everyone else. The scheme operated until the Japanese occupation and was not revived after the war.[@1,2]

## A trade for one community

Burma had prohibited hemp cultivation throughout the country before 1939.[@1] Demand continued. A 1965 United Nations article on drug use in Asia described government ganja shops in at least Rangoon and Mandalay selling cannabis primarily to the Indian population of Burma.[@2] The 1939 rules were issued "to legalize the sale of ganja to Indians only", as a later UN report put it.[@1]

That wording defined the limit. Indians could buy from the government system; everyone else remained outside it. The rules did not make ganja legal for the population as a whole. For Indian consumers the shops created a lawful supply that had not existed under the ban; for other users, the criminal law stayed as it was.[@1]

## Licensed growing for government shops

The scheme also reached back to the growers. Cultivation, formerly prohibited throughout the country, was permitted from 1939 under a system of licence, as part of the plan to sell ganja to Indians in Burma. The crop grown was supplied to the government under contract.[@1]

The state controlled both ends of the trade. The farmer grew for it, and the consumer bought through its shops.[@1,2]

## War, prosecutions and after

Japan occupied Burma from 1942 to 1945.[@1] After the re-occupation, the sale of ganja to Indians was not reintroduced, although the 1939 rules remained in force.[@1] In 1946 the government made no sales and issued no cultivation licences for the purpose of supplying its ganja trade.[@1] Illicit cultivation continued in the hills of the Pegu Yomas, which the report called the main source of supply for Indian users.[@1] The excise staff brought 216 prosecutions over hemp drugs that year and 208 succeeded.[@1]

The scheme ended with its legal channel unused and illicit growers supplying Indian users. In 1974 [Burma banned cannabis again](/history/myanmar-narcotics-law-1974/) with a law that prohibited cultivation, possession and sale.[@3] The [Myanmar profile](/country/mm/) sets out the present rules.
