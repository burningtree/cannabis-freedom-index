In June 2022 Thailand went from prohibition to Asia's freest cannabis regime in a
single day. It did so by deleting a line from a list, and never passed the law
that was meant to follow.[@2]

## From prison to policy

Cannabis had been banned in Thailand since the
[Cannabis Act of 1935](/history/thailand-cannabis-act-1935/) and again under the
[Narcotics Act of 1979](/history/thailand-narcotics-act-1979/). The first opening
was [medical](/history/thailand-medical-2019/): Thailand became the first country in
South-East Asia to allow it.[@1]

The push beyond that came from one party. Anutin Charnvirakul, leader of Bhumjaithai,
campaigned for legalisation and became health minister in 2019.[@1]

## 9 June 2022

On that day the cannabis flower was removed from the narcotics list. Possessing and
growing the plant stopped being offences, and about 4,200 prisoners were released.[@1,2]
The government had announced a giveaway of a million plants to households.[@1]
Extracts with more than 0.2 per cent THC stayed controlled: from 9 June only cannabis
extract above that line counted as a narcotic, while seeds, plants and inflorescence
could be grown, sold and bought freely with no licences and no plant limit.[@2]

What replaced the ban was thin:

- Growers registered through an app; there was no limit on plants.[@1]
- Sales to anyone under 20, or to pregnant or breastfeeding women, were barred.[@1]
- Smoking in public could be punished as a nuisance, with a fine of up to 25,000
  baht or three months in prison.[@1]
- Foreign-owned companies were excluded from the trade.[@1]

A cannabis law to regulate the new market was sent to parliament and was still
pending a year later.[@1]

## The boom

[Dispensaries](/history/thailand-dispensaries-open-2022/) had begun trading even
before the delisting, and they multiplied. By 2025 more than 11,200 had been
approved.[@1]

## The retreat

The correction came in steps. In
[February 2024](/history/thailand-thirty-gram-presumption-2024/) holding more than
30 grams was declared intent to distribute. On
[25 June 2025](/history/thailand-prescription-only-2025/) the health minister
reclassified the flower as a controlled herb: buying it now needs a prescription,
and advertising and recreational sales are prohibited. In
[September 2026](/history/thailand-cabinet-bill-2026/) the cabinet approved a bill to
write that into law.

By then Anutin was prime minister, promising to "strike a balance" between public
health and the economy.

## In the index

Possession went from 1 to 8 and growing from 0 to 7, with access at 6, and for three
years [Thailand](/country/th/) had one of the highest scores in the index. After the
2025 rule it stands at 2 for possession and 3 for access.
