The Netherlands never legalised cannabis. In 1976 it decided not to enforce part of
its own law, and built the world's best-known cannabis market on that decision.

## Two commissions

The Netherlands had [banned possession](/history/netherlands-possession-ban-1953/) in
1953. A 1969 survey of about 1,000 drug users found addiction rare and imprisonment
the most harmful consequence of use.

Two commissions drew the conclusion. The
[Hulsman Commission](/history/netherlands-hulsman-commission-1971/) argued in 1971
that pushing all drug users into one criminal subculture exposed cannabis smokers to
harder drugs. The government's own
[Baan Commission](/history/netherlands-baan-commission-1972/) proposed in 1972 to
sort drugs into "acceptable" and "unacceptable" risks. It rejected legal supply as
contrary to the Single Convention.

The government wanted to go further. A
[1974 memorandum](/history/netherlands-memorandum-1974/) said cannabis use "should be
removed as soon as possible from the domain of criminal justice", but that this
"would bring us into conflict with our treaty obligations".

## The 1976 law

The revised Opium Act made the distinction official. Cannabis went on a list of its
own, possession of up to 30 g became a minor offence, and the maximum sentence for
trafficking in other drugs rose from four years to twelve.

The real change was in the guidelines for prosecutors adopted the same year. Dutch
law lets prosecutors decline a case in the public interest. Under the guidelines they
stopped pursuing small possession and took what the UN's control board described as
"a relatively tolerant attitude towards small-scale dealing of cannabis conducted in
cafes". In [1980](/history/netherlands-dealers-not-prosecuted-1980/) the conditions
were made public. Selling remained a crime that was not prosecuted.

## The rules today

| | Tolerated |
|---|---|
| Possession | Up to 5 g |
| Growing | Up to 5 plants, which police still seize |
| Coffeeshop sale | 5 g per person, adults only |
| Coffeeshop conditions | No hard drugs, no minors, no advertising, no nuisance |

The limit was 30 g until [1996](/history/netherlands-five-grams-1996/). Municipalities
decide whether to allow coffeeshops at all, and their number fell from about 1,500 to
750 in the years before 2005.

## The back door

The policy has one flaw it has never resolved. A coffeeshop may sell cannabis, but
nobody may lawfully grow or deliver it: "the front door is open, but the backdoor is
illegal". Only since [2023](/history/netherlands-supply-experiment-2023/) has an
experiment let shops in some cities buy from licensed growers.

In the index the 1976 change lifted possession from 1 to 4, enforcement from 2 to 6
and access from 0 to 5. The [Netherlands](/country/nl/) scores lower on possession
today, since the tolerated amount was cut to 5 g.
