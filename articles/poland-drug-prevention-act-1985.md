For twelve years, in a country under communist rule, having drugs was not a crime. A
person could hold any quantity; the police could take it away and do nothing more.

## What came before

Poland's [first drug law](/history/poland-narcotics-act-1923/), of 1923, named hashish
beside opium, morphine and cocaine and punished making, storing and trading them. The
[1951 act](/history/poland-narcotic-1951/) added the herb of Indian hemp, kept up to
five years for storing or trading without a permit, and punished using a narcotic in
the company of another person. Neither said anything about growing the plant. Poppy
and hemp were ordinary crops.

## Why a new law

That was the problem. In the mid-1970s users learned to make "kompot", a home-made
opiate, from poppy straw, which could be had without restriction. Poland became the
first country in Europe whose opiates came from home production. Patients grew
younger, and between 1980 and 1984 the number of people convicted under the old rules
nearly doubled.

## What the act did

The Sejm passed the Act on preventing drug addiction on 31 January 1985; it took
effect on 1 March.

| | Rule from 1985 |
|---|---|
| Possession | No penalty, in any quantity; confiscation only |
| Treatment | Voluntary and free |
| Growing hemp | Only for the textile industry, with a permit; otherwise up to two years |
| Supplying for gain | One to ten years |

The model was preventive and therapeutic: the law punished those who profited from
another person's habit and treated the user as a patient. The criminologist Krzysztof
Krajewski has stressed that leaving possession out was a deliberate choice, not an
oversight.

For cannabis the act cut both ways. Holding it carried no penalty, but for the first
time growing it without a permit was a crime, and collecting its herb or resin was
allowed only for science.

## How it ended

The police complained that a dealer who could not be caught in the act of selling
could not be touched. Liberal critics made the opposite point: there was no legal way
to obtain what it was legal to hold. In [1997](/history/poland-drug-law-1997/) a new
act made possession an offence but left a small amount for personal use unpunished,
and in [December 2000](/history/poland-criminalisation-2000/) that exception was
deleted.

In the index the 1985 act raised possession from 3 to 4 and use from 2 to 3 for
[Poland](/country/pl/), and cut growing from 4 to 1.
