Tanzania introduced a thirty-year prison term for cannabis trafficking involving
fifty kilograms or less, while keeping quantities above that in the life-sentence
tier. The same amendment broadened the trafficking definition and rewrote the
conditions under which a person convicted of drug use could receive treatment.

## The earlier law already carried life for trafficking

The [2015 drug law](/history/tanzania-drug-control-act-2015/) had set life
imprisonment for trafficking and at least thirty years for unauthorised growing
of prohibited plants. It also contained a separate provision for small amounts
intended for personal consumption, with a fine, three years in prison, or both
for drugs outside its specially listed group.

The government [published an amendment bill](/history/tanzania-amendment-bill-2017/)
in October 2017. President John Magufuli assented to the resulting law in
November, and it was published on 1 December.

## Fifty kilograms divided two trafficking penalties

Cannabis and khat received a specific weight boundary. Trafficking more than
fifty kilograms remained within the life-imprisonment rule. At fifty kilograms
or below, the new trafficking offence carried thirty years.

Cases in that lower-quantity tier were assigned to subordinate courts, below
the High Court. The amendment added possession and production to the definition
of trafficking, which already included giving and storing drugs alongside
buying, selling, transporting and distributing them.

That broader wording made how a person held or supplied drugs important to
the offence as well as the weight involved. The separate personal-consumption
provision remained in the law, with the accused responsible for proving that
small quantities were intended for their own use.

## Treatment required consent and a medical assessment

For a person convicted of a drug-use offence, a court could direct treatment
instead of immediately imposing prison or another sentence. It had to be
satisfied that the person was dependent, that the dependence had motivated
the offence, that treatment was possible within a reasonable time, and that
this course served the interests of justice.

The person had to consent. A written medical report had to address the
dependence, available treatment and prospects of success. Violence, weapons,
organised crime, quantities beyond personal use and a role in directing
others were among the circumstances that ruled against this route.

## The amendment combined different changes

The law separated lower-weight trafficking from the life-sentence tier while
expressly bringing more conduct within trafficking. It also made medical
evidence central to the treatment decision.

Together with the earlier [national drug law](/history/tanzania-illicit-traffic-act-1995/),
these changes explain how [Tanzania's cannabis rules](/country/tz/) developed
through successive definitions, penalties and exceptions.
