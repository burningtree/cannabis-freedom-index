Governor Albert Bryan Jr. signed the US Virgin Islands’ medical cannabis law on 19 January 2019. Sponsored by Positive T.A. Nelson, the programme provided for certified patients, licensed businesses and a route for some residents to grow their own medicine.[@1,3]

## Voters had asked legislators to act

A [November 2014 referendum](/history/usvi-medical-referendum-2014/) asked voters whether the legislature should consider licensing and regulating medical cannabis patients, caregivers, cultivators and distribution centres. It received 56.5% support, a result cited in the later bill’s preamble.[@2]

That preamble described protecting patients, practitioners and caregivers from local arrest and prosecution, alongside regulating the businesses that would grow, process, package and distribute cannabis. The proposal joined patient protection to a system intended to control the supply of medicine.[@2]

## Patients needed certification and registration

The programme required a qualifying condition and written certification from a healthcare practitioner. Patients applied to the Office of Cannabis Regulation for registration cards; the scheme also provided for caregivers to assist them.[@3]

Residents could possess four ounces of cannabis, while nonresident patients had a three-ounce allowance. Patients whose cards authorised cultivation could grow twelve plants in a secure location and keep cannabis harvested from those plants. Either the patient or their designated caregiver could be registered to cultivate for that patient.[@3]

## A regulated supply chain needed businesses

The medical programme assigned the regulator responsibility for licensing growers, manufacturers, testing facilities and dispensaries. The law’s design therefore brought patient registration together with cultivation, production and retail oversight.[@3]

The [broader law signed in 2023](/history/usvi-cannabis-use-act-2023/) replaced the earlier medical chapter with a combined system for medical, adult and sacramental use. It retained higher patient possession limits and expressly allowed qualified residents six flowering and six immature plants for personal medical use, with the landowner’s permission.[@4]

## Commercial cultivation took longer to begin

In September 2026, regulator Joanne Moorehead reported a certificate to operate for a Tier 1 grower on St. Croix. She said the laboratory was operational and waiting for plants to test, while officials worked with applicants on St. Thomas.[@5]

That [commercial-growing milestone](/history/usvi-first-commercial-grow-2026/) followed years of establishing the licensing and testing system. The [US Virgin Islands profile](/country/vi/) brings the patient-growing rules and the later retail status together.
