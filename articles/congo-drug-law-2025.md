The Republic of the Congo enacted a new national drug-control law in August 2025. It preserves
criminal penalties for personal cannabis while spelling out a lower range for a small amount
than for trafficking conduct.

## A new national framework

Law No. 30-2025 covers illicit production, possession, manufacture, transport, trafficking and
use of narcotics, psychotropic substances and precursors. It was signed at Brazzaville on 22
August and published in the official journal on 25 September 2025.

The law uses schedules based on international drug-control conventions. Article 7 prohibits
cultivating plants classified as narcotics throughout the country and requires the owner,
operator or occupier of land to destroy controlled plants that grow there. Its final article
repeals earlier provisions that conflict with the new act, without naming a predecessor law.

## Personal-use amounts

Article 52 prohibits use of a controlled drug outside a medical prescription. A drug held for
illicit use is seized and confiscated by judicial decision even if the holder is not prosecuted.

Article 53 applies when an illegally bought, possessed or cultivated amount is small enough to be
considered for personal consumption. Cannabis derivatives other than cannabis oil carry one to
six months' imprisonment and a fine of 50,000 to 250,000 CFA francs, or either penalty. Cannabis
oil falls under the high-risk category: two months to one year and a fine of 100,000 to 500,000
francs, or either alone.

The article sets no numerical definition of “small.” It also permits a waiver of the sentence or
its execution when the person has not reached the age of criminal majority, while repeat cases
may receive stricter measures.

## Supply and cultivation

For high-risk drugs, sale, delivery, transport, purchase and possession carry two to ten years.
Giving or offering such a drug to another person for personal consumption carries one to two
years. Unlawful cultivation or production in the high-risk category carries five to ten years.
The law provides different bands for drugs placed in its lower-risk schedule.

The result is the framework described on the current [Republic of the Congo profile](/country/cg/):
a small amount remains criminal, cultivation remains banned, and sharing is separately punished.

In the index, the 2025 law does not move a score because the country was already recorded as
prohibited. It replaces contrary rules and clarifies penalties, but the text does not establish
that legal freedom changed on its commencement.
