A 2025 amendment to the Marshall Islands' principal drug statute added an unusually broad public
reporting rule. It does not legalise or reclassify cannabis; it requires witnesses to notify the
police about drug activity.

## A new section in an old act

The Prohibited Drugs (Prohibition and Control) Act began in 1987 and has since been amended
several times. Its current official consolidation identifies Public Law 2025-37 as the source of
new section 912A, headed “Reporting and Obligations.”

The consolidated document does not give the amendment's exact enactment day, so the history entry
records only 2025. It also does not reproduce a separate statement of legislative purpose. What
can be established directly is the duty the amendment inserted.

## Everyone who witnesses activity

Section 912A says any person who has witnessed activities involving drugs is obligated to report
them to national or local police. The language does not limit the duty to professionals, public
officials, serious trafficking, or a specified list of controlled substances.

Failure to comply is an offence punishable by a $1,000 fine. The section contains no prison term
for the reporting offence and does not define a separate penalty according to the quantity or
kind of drug witnessed.

## Protection for the report

The same section says a person who reports information in good faith is not liable to criminal or
civil penalties for doing so. It also guarantees the confidentiality of the information. The text
does not describe the procedure police must use to receive a report or set out exceptions for
family relationships, medical confidentiality or self-incrimination.

The duty sits beside the statute's longstanding cannabis prohibitions and penalties. Those remain
described in the article on the [1987 Prohibited Drugs Act](/history/marshall-islands-narcotics-act-1987/)
and on the [Marshall Islands profile](/country/mh/).

In the index, the reporting amendment is a context event and moves no score. It expands an
enforcement obligation around the prohibited market, but it does not alter the statutory penalty
used to score possession, cultivation, sharing, consumption or access.
