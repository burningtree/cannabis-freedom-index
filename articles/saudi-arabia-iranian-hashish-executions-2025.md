On 1 January 2025 Saudi Arabia announced that it had executed six Iranian nationals in Dammam, on the Gulf coast, for bringing hashish into the kingdom.[@1] The interior ministry said they had "clandestinely introduced" the drug, and Saudi reports said the Supreme Court had upheld the verdicts; the statement did not say when the executions had taken place.[@1,2]

## The charge

The six had been convicted of smuggling cannabis into Saudi Arabia, an offence that can carry death under the kingdom's [2005 narcotics law](/history/saudi-arabia-anti-narcotics-law-2005/).[@1] A specialised court found them guilty; the verdicts were upheld on appeal and reviewed by the Supreme Court, and a royal decree authorised the executions.[@2] Drug executions had [resumed in 2022](/history/saudi-arabia-drug-executions-resume-2022/) after an informal moratorium, and the six were part of the surge that followed.[@1]

## A protest in a wary rapprochement

Iran's foreign ministry summoned the Saudi ambassador in Tehran and called the executions "unacceptable", saying they violated international legal standards.[@2] The protest came as the two countries were repairing relations after the 2016 rupture; a China-brokered agreement had restored diplomatic ties in March 2023.[@1] The announcement came after a year in which Saudi Arabia executed 117 people for drug trafficking, according to an AFP tally of official figures, following a public anti-drug campaign launched in 2023.[@1]

## What came next

The numbers rose. Saudi Arabia executed [356 people in 2025](/history/saudi-arabia-execution-record-2025/), and 68% of them were for non-lethal drug offences, Human Rights Watch said.[@3,4] By 22 June 2026 Amnesty had counted 96 executions, 61 of them for drug offences, including seven Ethiopians put to death for "smuggling hashish".[@3] Another five Ethiopians were executed on 27 July, after court sessions that Human Rights Watch said lasted between three and twenty minutes and in which they had no lawyer.[@4] In March 2026 a UN working group published an opinion finding that the death penalty for drug offences was incompatible with international human rights law in the cases of three Egyptians, two of whom had been executed in 2025, and urged Saudi Arabia to restore a moratorium.[@5]

## Who was affected

Foreign nationals bore most of the cost. They made up 75% of people executed for drugs in 2024 and 78% in 2025, Amnesty said.[@3] The UN working group described the same pattern in the Egyptian cases: no lawyer at every stage, confessions under torture, and detention on discriminatory grounds because the men were foreign.[@5] Saudi Arabia said the men had been convicted and their appeals exhausted.[@2]

## In the index

No law changed in January 2025, so the entry moves no score. Saudi Arabia remains at the bottom of the [Cannabis Freedom Index](/country/sa/), and the death penalty for hashish smuggling is still written into its law. The six follow the [2022 resumption of drug executions](/history/saudi-arabia-drug-executions-resume-2022/) and precede the [2026 Ethiopian executions](/history/saudi-arabia-ethiopian-hashish-executions-2026/).
