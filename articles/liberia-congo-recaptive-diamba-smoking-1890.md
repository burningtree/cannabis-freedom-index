In 1890 the Swiss naturalist Johann Büttikofer reported that the "Congo-negroes"
of Liberia nearly all smoked hemp, a habit they had brought with them from their
old home.[@1,2]

They farmed diamba and smoked it in calabash-based water pipes of the kind made
across Atlantic Central Africa.[@1] Harry Johnston still found hemp growing and
smoking in Liberia in 1906 only among Americo-Liberians of Congo origin, and by
the 1920s diamba had a coastal distribution from Sierra Leone to the Liberian
grasslands.[@1]

The episode is one of the coast's clearest records of a plant travelling with
freed people: the United States Navy resettled several hundred "Congoes" near
Monrovia in the 1820s and 1830s, and their descendants kept the pipe
lit.[@1]
