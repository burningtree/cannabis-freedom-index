Berar tightened the financial conditions of licensed cannabis cultivation in 1884. A flat eight-rupee licence charge was replaced by a charge of ten rupees for each acre or part of an acre. The change shows how a government could restrict cultivation through the terms of a licence while continuing to permit a crop.

## From a flat charge to acreage

Under the [1875 system](/history/india-berar-cultivation-licences-1875/), the required stamp paper cost eight rupees regardless of cultivated area. The revised arrangement connected the charge to acreage and counted a fraction of an acre as a chargeable unit.

That altered the structure of the cost, rather than merely increasing the price of an otherwise identical licence. Describing the revision only as an increase from eight to ten rupees would miss the more consequential change in its basis.

## A date with two presentations

The contemporary memorandum to the Indian Hemp Drugs Commission identifies 1884 as the year of modification. The later Buldana district gazetteer describes the fee increase under the administrative year 1884–1885.

The database uses 1884 and explains the difference in its description. The sources consulted do not establish an exact commencement day. An administrative-year account should not be converted into a precise calendar date without the underlying order.

## Accounts for vendors

The memorandum reports that, when the changes were introduced, ganja and bhang vendors also became obliged to keep accounts of receipts and disbursements. The revision therefore concerned supervision of the trade as well as the terms on which cultivation was allowed.

The evidence does not establish that these accounting duties amounted to a prohibition on all sales or possession. Growing and vending were different activities within the same regional system, and their conditions need to be described separately.

## What happened to cultivation

Officials attributed a later contraction in cultivation to the revised rules. That is an administrative explanation of an observed trend, not an independent demonstration that the charge alone caused the decline. Other influences would require separate evidence.

The event is regional context in the history of [India](/country/in/). Its significance lies in a documented regulatory mechanism: changing the cost and reporting requirements of an existing licensed market. It does not justify a nationwide score change or establish a complete cannabis ban in Berar in 1884.
