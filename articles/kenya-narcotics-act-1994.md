Kenya's replacement drug law began operating on 26 August 1994 and prescribed ten years' imprisonment for cannabis possession when the defendant satisfied the court it was solely for personal consumption.[@1,2] Other cannabis possession carried twenty years.[@1] Trafficking exposed a defendant to life imprisonment plus a fine linked to the drug's market value.[@1]

## A national law replaced the colonial ordinance

The [earlier drug law](/history/kenya-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1933/) had prohibited cannabis cultivation and nonmedical possession, with different maximum penalties depending on the court.[@3] The new Act repealed it and introduced a broader system covering narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, property forfeiture and rehabilitation.[@1]

Kenya had already formed a specialist [Anti-Narcotics Unit in 1983](/history/kenya-anti-narcotics-unit-1983/).[@4] A later police briefing identifies enforcement of the 1994 Act as the unit's mandate and lists cannabis and hashish among the drugs it covers.[@4]

## Personal consumption had to be established in court

Possessing cannabis was an offence unless a statutory exception applied.[@1] The lighter cannabis tier depended on satisfying the court that the drug was intended solely for the defendant's own consumption; otherwise, the twenty-year tier applied.[@1]

The Act preserved exceptions for lawful possession by specified professionals and licensed people, and for drugs lawfully supplied for medical purposes.[@1] It also empowered the Pharmacy and Poisons Board to license activities such as import, export and manufacture.[@1]

Trafficking carried a fine of one million shillings or three times the drug's market value, whichever was greater, in addition to life imprisonment.[@1] Its statutory definition included supplying, giving, selling, storing and transporting drugs.[@1]

## Using and growing cannabis had separate penalties

Smoking, inhaling or otherwise using a narcotic drug could bring a fine of 250,000 shillings, up to ten years in prison, or both.[@1] The same provision reached certain smoking utensils, allowing premises to be used for drugs, and being at a drug-use location without a lawful and reasonable excuse.[@1]

Cultivation could bring up to twenty years, a fine of 250,000 shillings or three times the plants' value, whichever was greater, or both.[@1] The Act also provided for forfeiture of land in specified cases involving the owner or the owner's involvement in cultivation.[@1]

## Rehabilitation accompanied the criminal penalties

The Act provided for rehabilitation centres and allowed a court to commit a convicted drug-dependent person to one for treatment in addition to another sentence.[@1] These provisions placed treatment within the same system as imprisonment and property forfeiture.[@1]

The personal-consumption tier later became the subject of the [2022 amendment](/history/kenya-amendment-act-2022/), another stage in [Kenya's cannabis-law history](/country/ke/).
