In 1995 a presidential ordinance prescribed death or life imprisonment for anyone found with more than a kilogram of cannabis in Pakistan. The same scale let a court punish up to ten grams with two years or a fine, and ten grams to a kilogram with three to seven years and a fine.[@1] The top band also required a fine of at least one million rupees.[@1]

## A ban inherited from 1979

Cannabis had been illegal since 1979, when General Zia-ul-Haq's [prohibition order](/history/pakistan-prohibition-1979/) made possession punishable by up to two years or thirty lashes and ended the licensed sale of charas and bhang.[@4] The 1995 ordinance was issued while the National Assembly was out of session; the same communication to the United Nations included a separate ordinance creating the Anti-Narcotics Force.[@1] Its preamble said the government needed to consolidate the narcotics laws and take immediate action.[@1]

## The kilogram that carried death

The middle band ran from ten grams to one kilogram, with a minimum of three years, a maximum of seven and a fine.[@1] Above one kilogram the ordinance prescribed death or life imprisonment and a fine of at least one million rupees.[@1] Cannabis was not one of the drugs with a lower ceiling of its own, so a person with just over a kilogram of charas faced the same top sentence as a heroin courier carrying the same weight.[@1]

## An ordinance without a start date

The ordinance was made under Article 89 of the constitution, which lets the president legislate when the National Assembly is not sitting. Its first section left commencement to a notification in the official Gazette.[@1] No such notification has been found, and the 1997 Act's repeal clause names a later 1997 ordinance, not this one.[@1,2] The 1995 text is the clearest statement of the sentencing model that followed, but it does not prove the date on which that model began to apply.

## The permanent law kept the top sentence

The Control of Narcotic Substances Act 1997 put the rules in a permanent statute and came into force at once. It kept death above a kilogram, but widened the lowest band to 100 grams and set the middle band at up to seven years without a minimum.[@2] The [2022 federal amendment](/history/pakistan-cannabis-penalty-tables-2022/) replaced the shared weight scale with separate tables for bhang, charas and hashish oil and removed death from the cannabis rows; a [2023 amendment](/history/pakistan-drug-death-penalty-abolished-2023/) removed it from the remaining federal drug rows.[@3] The heaviest federal cannabis sentence is now life imprisonment, and the 1995 ordinance's death sentence survives only as history. Recreational possession and sale remain illegal across [Pakistan](/country/pk/).
