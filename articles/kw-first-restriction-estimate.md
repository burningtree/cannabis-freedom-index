Kuwait’s drug-control law took effect on 25 July 1960, expressly covering Indian hemp and every preparation or derivative sold under another name. Possession, purchase, sale and transfer were prohibited outside the law’s authorised routes. Violations could bring up to three years in prison and a fine of 3,000 rupees.[@1]

## The schedule named the plant

The law issued under Abdullah Al-Salem Al-Sabah attached a list of controlled drugs. Its fifth entry named Cannabis indica and its preparations and derivatives. That made cannabis part of the same control system as the other scheduled narcotics.[@1]

The prohibition covered people acting as intermediaries as well as those buying, selling, possessing or handing over drugs. A separate provision required special Health Department permission for imports and exports.[@1]

## Pharmacies and patients had controlled routes

Eligible permit holders included licensed pharmacies, laboratories, government departments, scientific institutions, hospitals and doctors. Each import or export required an application identifying the substance, quantity and reason; the department could refuse it or reduce the requested quantity.[@1]

Patients could possess quantities prescribed by licensed doctors for medical reasons. They were prohibited from passing those drugs to another person. Doctors were to prescribe only for proper treatment, while pharmacists had to follow dispensing, storage and recordkeeping requirements.[@1]

## Records followed a drug through the pharmacy

The law required special registers for controlled drugs received and dispensed. Pharmacy records identified the patient, prescriber, substance, quantity and dispensing date, and records and prescriptions had to be retained for five years.[@1]

Controlled drugs were to be stored securely under the responsible pharmacist’s control. The rules also required warnings on containers and packages. These requirements put the handling of scheduled drugs within a supervised medical and institutional system.[@1]

A [December amendment](/history/kuwait-drug-permit-fees-1960/) authorised the head of the Health Department to set fees for the law’s administrative procedures.[@2]

## Later laws changed the system

A [replacement schedule in 1979](/history/kuwait-cannabis-schedule-1979/) explicitly listed cannabis resin, powder, oil, extract and tincture alongside Indian hemp and preparations.[@3] Kuwait then [adopted a replacement drug law in 1983](/history/kuwait-drug-law-1983/), which provided for repeal of the 1960 law.[@4]

The [Kuwait profile](/country/kw/) follows the later penalty system. The 1960 law shows how cannabis control already linked ordinary possession restrictions to permits, prescriptions and supervised handling.
