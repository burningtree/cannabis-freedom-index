The Comoros' three years of legal cannabis ended the way they began, with a change of
government.

## The reversal

Ali Soilih, who had [lifted the ban](/history/comoros-legalisation-1975/) as president,
was overthrown and killed in 1978. The government that replaced him reversed his
decrees. Cannabis, legal since January 1975, was prohibited again in May 1978.

## A reform tied to one man

The legalisation had been one measure among a revolutionary president's decrees, and it
did not outlive him. Nothing in the sources read suggests it had been debated, voted on
or built into any institution that might have defended it.

Reforms made that way are easy to undo. Compare the changes that have lasted: the
Netherlands' [1976 policy](/history/netherlands-toleration-1976/) rested on two
commissions and a revised law, and Uruguay's
[2013 law](/history/uruguay-legalisation-2013/) on a parliamentary vote and a
regulator. Both survived changes of government that were hostile to them.

## Since then

Cannabis has stayed illegal. As described in the source read, which dates from 2019,
growing, selling and possessing it for recreational use are all offences in the
[Comoros](/country/km/).

## In the index

The 1978 entry returns the Comoros' scores to where they stood before 1975. As with the
legalisation itself, the dates are documented and the detail is not: no text of the
law that restored the ban was found.
