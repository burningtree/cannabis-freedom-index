Cuba made specified aggravated drug offences punishable by fifteen to thirty years in prison or death in March 1999. Cannabis cultivation, supplying drugs and holding them for trafficking came within the rewritten offence. The highest range applied when specified aggravating circumstances were present.[@1]

## Personal possession kept its own sentence

By then, simple cannabis possession already had a separate penalty: six months to two years in prison, a fine of 200–500 quotas, or both. The [fine option came from the 1994 amendment](/history/cuba-cannabis-possession-fine-1994/). Law 87 rewrote the main trafficking offence while that separate possession rule continued.[@1,2]

For someone found with cannabis, the purpose of holding it therefore mattered. The supply offence expressly covered possession with the purpose of trafficking. The simple-possession offence covered holdings without the required authorisation or medical prescription.[@1,2]

## Growers could lose their land

The ordinary range for trafficking, production, transport and supplying drugs to another person was four to ten years. The same range applied to growing the plant the law called Cannabis Indica, known as marihuana, and knowingly possessing its seeds or parts.[@1]

For growers, the consequences extended beyond prison. Owners faced confiscation of the land. Those who held a right to use or occupy it could lose that right. Courts could also confiscate other property from people convicted under the trafficking article.[@1]

Relatively large quantities raised the prison range to eight to twenty years. The law placed its capital cases in a further, separately defined group.[@1]

## International trafficking could carry death

The highest range covered officials exploiting their position or state resources, involvement in international trafficking, and offences using someone under sixteen. It allowed fifteen to thirty years or death.[@1]

The amendment also reached people who knew about preparations for these offences or their execution and failed to report them: they faced two to five years. Preparatory acts were punishable under the code's general rules.[@1]

The National Assembly adopted the law in February 1999. Its publication on 15 March brought it into operation under its final commencement rule.[@1]

## The replacement code retained capital drug offences

The [2022 code](/history/cuba-penal-code-2022/) kept cannabis possession in a separate offence with the same prison and fine options. Cultivation and supply retained an ordinary four-to-ten-year range, while the aggravated range became ten to thirty years, life imprisonment or death, with a broader list of aggravating circumstances.[@3]

Those distinctions between possession, growing and aggravated supply remain central to the [Cuba profile](/country/cu/).[@3]
