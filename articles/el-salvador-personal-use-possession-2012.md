On 16 November 2012 El Salvador's Constitutional Chamber did what the drug law's
numbers could not: it said an adult's decision to affect their own health by using
drugs is not a penally relevant fact. Possession for one's own use, the court held,
sits outside criminal punishment — and article 34's two weight bands must be read
that way.[@1]

## Weight is a factor, not a rule

Article 34 punished possessing less than 2 g with one to three years and 2 g or more
with three to six.[@2] The chamber refused to let the scale decide: weight is one
factor among others, not a rule by itself.[@1] Only a proved purpose — trafficking,
cultivation, processing or promoting use — puts the holder back inside the prison
bands, even below 2 g.[@1]

## What the ruling did not touch

Supply and cultivation stayed crimes: growing without authorisation kept five to
fifteen years under article 31, and buying, selling, storing or transporting seeds or
plants ten to fifteen.[@2] The court carved out the user, not the market.

## The debate that went nowhere

A cross-party legalisation debate opened in 2013, but the president opposed it,
parliament never acted, and the 2012 ruling remains the whole of El Salvador's
leniency.[@3]
