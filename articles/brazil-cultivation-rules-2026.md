For six years Brazilian pharmacies sold cannabis medicines made entirely from
imported plants. On 4 August 2026 it became legal, for the first time, to grow
cannabis in Brazil.

## Why everything was imported

Brazil [banned cannabis](/history/brazil-federal-ban-1932/) nationwide in 1932, and
a 1938 decree set five years in prison for growing it. Prison for users ended in
[2006](/history/brazil-no-prison-for-users-2006/), and in
[2024](/history/brazil-supreme-court-2024/) the Supreme Court ruled that possessing
up to 40 g or six female plants for personal use is not a crime.

Medicine took a separate path. In
[2019](/history/brazil-pharmacy-products-2019/) the health regulator, Anvisa,
allowed cannabis-based products to be sold in pharmacies on prescription, but left
cultivation prohibited. Some growers operated under individual court orders.

## The resolution

A ruling of the Superior Court of Justice required the regulator to act. Anvisa's
board approved the text on 28 January 2026; it is dated 30 January as RDC
1.013/2026 and took effect on 4 August.

- **What may be grown:** cannabis with no more than 0.3 per cent THC, measured in
  the dried flowering tops.
- **For what:** medicinal, pharmaceutical and research purposes only.
- **By whom:** establishments holding a Special Authorisation from Anvisa that
  expressly covers cultivation.
- **Controls:** every plant is identified by lot, variety and growth stage, every
  lot is tested for THC, and plants above the limit must be destroyed and reported
  within 48 hours.
- **Trade:** transport only between authorised holders, in sealed and numbered
  packaging. Exporting plants or seeds is prohibited.

Growers already operating under a court order have until 5 August 2027 to comply.
The resolution was one of a set of five; according to the index's own entry, the
package also lets patient associations grow for their members under a supervised
trial scheme.

## What it leaves out

The 0.3 per cent limit is the important restriction. Plants above it stay
prohibited unless specifically authorised. Nothing in the resolution concerns people growing at home.

## In the index

Licensed production is described on the index but not scored, and the rules reach
no ordinary user, so [Brazil's](/country/br/) score does not move. What changes is
the supply behind the pharmacy counter, which no longer has to cross a border.
