A new Faroese party announced in July 2018 that it wanted people to be able to grow and use cannabis, with medical access a particular priority.[@1] Its founder, Hallur Hjalgrímsson Djurhuus, sought the signatures needed to take that campaign into an election.[@1]

## One issue brought the party together

The group called itself Framtakið fyri rættinum at velja kannabis, translated by the public broadcaster as the campaign for the right to choose cannabis.[@1] Djurhuus argued for personal choice over growing and using the plant and for free access for people who needed it medically.[@1]

The party proposed staying neutral on other political questions.[@1] Members could hold different positions on subjects unrelated to cannabis, while supporting legalisation and research into its effects.[@1]

At its launch, Djurhuus said he had collected about 250 of the 800 signatures required to contest an election.[@1]

## Medicines were available, but flower was excluded

The campaign entered a debate in which cannabis treatment already existed in a narrower form.[@3] In March 2018, the public broadcaster reported that Faroese GPs could prescribe eleven cannabis-containing medicinal products, following guidance shared with Denmark.[@3]

Cannabis flowers and buds available under Denmark's new trial scheme were still excluded from Faroese approval at that point.[@3] The Central Party had proposed opening a similar trial in the islands.[@3]

The new campaign's call for people to grow and use cannabis therefore went beyond the existing prescription options.[@1,3]

## The founders submitted their signatures

By February 2019, the founders had handed 800 signatures to the Ministry of the Interior.[@2] Djurhuus described relief after months of collecting support and repeated the group's objective of legal access and personal cultivation.[@2]

The broadcaster also named Arnfinn á Birtuni as a co-founder and reported Hjalmar Zachariassen's candidacy.[@2]

## Medical reform followed its own route

In April 2019, MP Jenis á Rana returned to parliament with a proposal for access to Denmark's medical trial, after an earlier rejection.[@4] Health minister Sirið Stenberg offered to discuss adding cannabis products to the medicines list through an administrative route.[@4]

The Chief Medical Officer confirms that the [Faroese medical trial](/history/faroes-medical-cannabis-trial-2019/) began that April.[@5] The campaign and the prescribing debate were distinct parts of the widening argument over cannabis in the [Faroe Islands](/country/fo/).[@1,4]
