The US Virgin Islands’ cannabis regulator told lawmakers in August 2026 that licensed sales needed an operational product-testing laboratory. By September, the laboratory was ready and a St. Croix commercial grower had permission to operate. An October update still reported no dispensary open for sales.[@1,4,5]

## Conditional licences came before operating shops

The territory’s [2023 cannabis law](/history/usvi-cannabis-use-act-2023/) had created a combined system for medical, adult and sacramental use. Its licences covered cultivation, manufacturing, dispensaries and testing, with additional routes for micro-cultivation and consumption premises.[@6]

The implementing rules distinguished a conditional licence from permission to purchase or sell cannabis. The conditional award reserved an applicant’s place while they satisfied the requirements for a licence.[@2]

In [January 2026](/history/usvi-conditional-dispensaries-2026/), the regulator published conditional dispensary approvals. Those awards were one step in assembling a market whose growers, manufacturers, testing facilities and shops each needed authorisation.[@3,6]

## The laboratory was an August bottleneck

Executive Director Joanne Moorehead told a budget hearing that cultivation could begin within weeks and at least two dispensaries might open before year-end. Her office had conditionally approved fifteen cultivation licences, ten dispensary licences and ten manufacturing licences, alongside research licences and micro-cultivation permits.[@1]

She said commercial sales needed a laboratory capable of verifying potency and screening for contaminants. A vendor had been selected, but finding a suitable facility had delayed progress. Stakeholders were then pointing to October for readiness.[@1]

## September brought permission to grow

At the Cannabis Advisory Board’s September meeting, Moorehead reported a certificate to operate for a Tier 1 commercial cultivator on St. Croix. She described this as the beginning of official cultivation operations on the island.[@4]

She also said the laboratory was operational and waiting for plants. Officials were working with St. Thomas applicants on their operating certificates. The update moved the story from conditional paperwork and a missing laboratory to a grower authorised to produce a crop.[@4]

## Harvests and retail opening were the next steps

An October market update reported that no dispensary had opened for sales.[@5] The [first-grow milestone](/history/usvi-first-commercial-grow-2026/) and the [US Virgin Islands profile](/country/vi/) track this stage of the industry: legal allowances, approved businesses and the work needed to supply customers.
