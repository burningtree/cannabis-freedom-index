Sint Maarten's health minister Omar Ottley invited local and international organisations to propose studies on cannabis legalisation in September 2022.[@1] The government wanted evidence about economic, social and medical effects before deciding how a regulated industry should work and benefit local people.[@1]

## A minister asked for evidence

Ottley's announcement connected legalisation to decisions about the kind of industry Sint Maarten might develop.[@1] It invited organisations with relevant experience to submit proposals by 5 December 2022, bringing local and international expertise into the planned research.[@1]

The announcement asked for help studying potential benefits and developing a route towards regulation.[@1] Economic opportunity, social consequences and medical uses were all part of the question put to prospective researchers.[@1] The minister presented research as a step towards making informed decisions about legalisation.[@1]

## Users already faced a quantity-based policy

The call came against a background of [prosecution guidance](/history/sint-maarten-prosecution-guideline-2018/) allowing confiscation and possible conditional dismissal in the 0–25 gram cannabis band.[@2] That policy excluded minors, repeat offending, indications of dealing and several other circumstances.[@2]

Growing cannabis received an individual sentencing assessment under the same guidance, taking account of plant numbers, location, professional equipment and the person's role.[@2] The research call broadened the government's discussion from responding to offences to designing a regulated industry.[@1,2]

## A committee took on the next stage

In [February 2024](/history/sint-maarten-cannabis-committee-2024/), the government formally created a cannabis legalisation committee involving the health, justice and economic-affairs ministries.[@3] Its mandate covered medical, scientific, religious and recreational uses.[@3]

The committee was to review research, examine the effects of criminalisation and prepare recommendations for ministers.[@3] It was also asked to consider conditions for cultivation, processing, testing, distribution and consumption, including cannabis concentrates.[@3]

The regulation linked this work to people continuing to be punished for possession and cultivation, alongside the government's search for revenue.[@3] Those concerns gave the research a direct connection to both criminal justice and economic policy.[@3]

## Ministers continued to debate the choices

By [August 2026](/history/sint-maarten-pm-cannabis-caution-2026/), prime minister Luc Mercelina was urging parliament to settle the policy choice and build supervisory capacity before rushing legislation.[@4] He raised youth protection, health services, treaty obligations and coordination across the French border.[@4]

Health minister Claret Connor then [sought regional advice in October](/history/sint-maarten-health-review-cannabis-2026/), asking the Pan American Health Organization for help assessing cannabis policy and Caribbean experience.[@5] Research and public-health capacity continued to shape the reform debate in [Sint Maarten](/country/sx/).[@4,5]
