An Austrian pharmaceutical notice records authorization for **Indian-hemp cigarettes made by Ubald von Trnkóczy**, a pharmacist in **Laibach**, under an order dated **22 March 1907**. The authorization allowed general distribution in pharmacies subject to an express medical-prescription condition.

Laibach is present-day **Ljubljana, Slovenia**. At the time it belonged to the Austrian half of Austria-Hungary. The event is recorded under Austria as the governing historical jurisdiction, while the article identifies the modern location.

## What the contemporary notice says

The notice appears in the *Pharmazeutische Post* of **21 April 1907**, volume 40, issue 16. Its heading lists pharmaceutical preparations admitted to general distribution in pharmacies. It then separates preparations without a prescription restriction from those restricted to medical prescription.

Trnkóczy's preparation appears in the second group. The wording identifies the pharmacist, his location and the product as **Indische Hanfzigaretten**. The cited administrative reference is **Z. 2010 ex 1907**.

This verifies an authorization for a named preparation from a named manufacturer. It does not establish that any manufacturer could sell any cannabis cigarette under the same conditions. Nor does general pharmacy distribution mean unrestricted over-the-counter sale: the subsection expressly preserves the prescription restriction.

## Keeping the dates and page reference precise

The database date is the order date reproduced in the notice, **22 March 1907**. The source itself was published a month later. The full administrative file, any separately stated commencement and the precise day pharmacies began supplying the product have not been established.

The original scan places the notice on printed page **312**, the tenth page of the digitized issue. A modern study points to page 313. Reading the scan resolves that locator discrepancy without requiring a change to the reported order date.

## Pharmaceutical permission and fiscal rules

A separate **1905** newspaper report had already distinguished domestic hemp-cigarette manufacture and sale from controlled imports. It said that domestic trade was not prohibited by the monopoly rules, while imports required fiscal permission and payment of specified classes of charges.

That report and the 1907 notice concern different regulatory questions. Fiscal treatment alone does not establish pharmaceutical authorization, while this named-product authorization does not show that import controls disappeared.

The notice gives no recipe, cannabis concentration, clinical findings or indication that recreational use was authorized. The entry consequently documents a prescription pharmacy channel for this particular preparation and leaves Austria's historical score allocations unchanged.
