Trinidad and Tobago rewrote its cannabis law in a fortnight at the end of 2019. The Dangerous Drugs (Amendment) Act passed the House of Representatives on 11 December and the Senate on 13 December, received assent on 18 December and was brought into force on 23 December. It took a country where possession could mean years in prison to one where an adult may hold an ounce and grow four plants.

## The new tiers

| Amount of cannabis | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Up to 30 g, or 5 g of resin | no offence |
| Up to four growing plants | no offence |
| More than 30 g and up to 60 g | an offence dealt with by a fixed penalty notice of 2,000 dollars; unpaid, a court fine of up to 50,000 dollars or 30 hours of community service |
| More than 60 g and up to 100 g | a fine of 75,000 dollars, or 50 hours of community service if it cannot be paid |

Above those amounts the ordinary offences apply, and the same act raised several of their maximum fines, some to three million dollars.

## Where it may not be used

Smoking or using cannabis in a public place is an offence handled by the same 2,000-dollar ticket. A public place is any area the public has access to, and a home that is not used for business is excluded. Vaping counts as smoking. The minister may list approved public places by order.

A separate section sets a fine of 250,000 dollars and five years in prison for having cannabis on a school bus or on premises where children are present for education, sport or culture, and for driving a vehicle, aircraft or ship under its influence.

## Old cases

The act reaches back.

- A person charged before it came into force with possessing up to 60 g may apply to the court for a discharge.
- Records of possession of up to 100 g are to be expunged by the Commissioner of Police, and a convicted person may apply to have the offence removed.
- A person whose offence is expunged may apply for a pardon.

A court may also order counselling, treatment or psychological evaluation before sentencing or discharging anyone under this part of the law.

## What it did not create

The act redefines cannabis and removes offences. It licenses no one to sell.

The index moved [Trinidad and Tobago](/country/tt/) from 6 to 39 with the [2019 act](/history/trinidad-decriminalisation-2019/), the largest single rise in its record.
