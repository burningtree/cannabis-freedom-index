On 13 November 1984 Djibouti marked the Islamic New Year with a collective
pardon — and made clear who was not invited.[@1]

People convicted under the poisonous-substance and narcotics laws were excluded
from the pardon, which otherwise fully remitted eligible prison terms of eight
months or less.[@1] The decree was immediately enforceable.[@1]

Its drug exclusion cited the French 1970 law, showing the older legislation
still used as a reference nearly a decade after independence.[@1]
