In September 1970 Portugal made using drugs a crime for the first time. Decree-Law
420/70 punished consumption with up to two years in prison and set up the repression of
trafficking — though, as legal scholars note, it punished trafficking only
moderately.[@1]

## A moral law out of step with its model

The decree was influenced by the 1961 Single Convention, yet it departed from that
treaty's spirit.[@1] Where the convention framed control around health and welfare,
the Portuguese law stressed the immorality of drugs and the user's lack of criminal
liability was denied from an exclusively public-security perspective, to the detriment
of health.[@1]

This was the law of the late dictatorship: use had never been a crime before
1970.[@1] The regime that wrote it fell four years later, and illegal drug use —
cannabis first, then heroin — grew into a visible social problem only after the 1974
revolution ended the dictatorship.[@1]

## The slow walk back

The 1970 framework lasted, but its meaning shifted. In 1976 Decree-Law 792/76 recast
drug use as a complex medical-psychological problem and created the Centre for Drug
Study and Prophylaxis.[@1] In 1983 Decree-Law 430/83, shaped by the 1971 Convention on
Psychotropic Substances, put the user at the centre and encouraged treatment and
reintegration.[@1] The 1993 drug law kept use punishable but made the sanction almost
symbolic: up to three months, or a fine, with courts steered toward treatment.[@1] It
dealt with acquisition and possession for personal use alongside use itself and even
cultivation for personal use; only amounts above five days' use drew up to a
year.[@1] New intermediate categories — "less severe trafficking" and the
"dealer/user" — softened the edges further.[@1]

The endpoint came with [Law 30/2000](/history/portugal-decriminalisation-2001/): use,
acquisition and possession for personal use became administrative matters handled by
Dissuasion Commissions, and the hard/soft drug distinction was abolished.[@1]

## What moved in the index

The [1970 criminalisation](/history/portugal-use-criminalised-1970/) put
[Portugal's](/country/pt/) possession and consumption scores at 2 and enforcement at
3, where they stood until the reforms that culminated in 2001.
