Aruba gave cannabidiol, or CBD, a medicinal status in December 2019 and excluded CBD preparations containing no more than 0.2% THC from a narcotics designation.[@1,2] The rules responded to growing use of cannabis preparations for health reasons, with the minister calling for safeguards to protect consumers.[@1,2]

## People were already using cannabis preparations

The regulations' explanations described increasing use of preparations that were not standardised.[@1,2] Health minister Dangui Oduber chose to address that use through medicine and narcotics rules, with separate decisions about CBD's classification and the products containing it.[@1,2]

The regulations were signed on 20 December, published on 23 December and took effect the following day.[@1,2,3] For people seeking CBD products, the package therefore changed the rules from 24 December 2019.[@1,2,3]

## A THC ceiling shaped the exception

One rule designated CBD and its preparations as medicines, including preparations combined with THC.[@1] It also allowed CBD preparations with no more than 0.2% THC to be stocked and supplied by people beyond the categories normally named in the medicines law.[@1]

A second rule addressed cannabis preparations, extracts and tinctures under the narcotics ordinance.[@2] It made an exception for CBD and its preparations with THC content at or below 0.2%.[@2] The percentage was therefore a condition attached to the CBD exception.[@2]

## The supplement exemption lasted only weeks

A companion regulation dealt with products used for purposes other than medicine.[@3] It excluded specified external-use products, herbs, supplements, foods and drinks from medicinal classification when they contained no more than 0.2% THC.[@3]

That arrangement was short-lived.[@4] In January, Oduber withdrew the companion rule because it did not fully match the intended CBD policy.[@4] The [withdrawal took effect on 17 January 2020](/history/aruba-cbd-supplement-exemption-withdrawn-2020/), leaving the separate medicine designation and narcotics exception outside the repeal's scope.[@1,2,4]

## CBD imports became a regulated medicinal route

Aruba later [created a medicinal-cannabis agency](/history/aruba-medicinal-cannabis-bureau-2020/) to oversee a proposed cultivation, manufacturing and trading system, with duties covering quality, permits and stocks.[@5]

The health inspectorate's current guidance treats CBD products with no more than 0.2% THC as medicines.[@6] Businesses seeking to import, store and distribute them need permission from the health minister, following an inspectorate assessment of legal requirements, quality and safety.[@6]

That permission process is the practical commercial route described today for low-THC CBD in [Aruba](/country/aw/).[@6]
