From 11 July 2024, registered Isle of Man pharmacies could apply to dispense cannabis-based medicines on private prescription. Private prescribing clinics could also register locally, opening a route beyond the island’s single-pharmacy pilot.[@1]

## Patients had demonstrated a demand

The [pilot that began in December 2022](/history/isle-of-man-medical-pharmacy-pilot-2022/) used Karsons pharmacy in Onchan. It accepted prescriptions from registered clinics in England, leaving patients dependent on that combination of an off-island prescriber and one island dispensing location.[@2]

More than 650 patients, aged between 18 and 86, used the service during its first twelve months. Pharmacies dispensed an average of 335 items a month, most for chronic pain conditions.[@1] These figures counted patients and prescription items respectively: they showed the scale of the pilot, rather than a count of people receiving publicly funded treatment.[@1,2]

## More pharmacies could apply, and clinics could register

The new arrangement allowed any registered island pharmacy to seek a dispensing licence. It also accepted registration of private cannabis prescribing clinics on the island.[@1] Health Minister Lawrie Hooper said the pilot had shown a local need and expressed hope that patients would gain a choice of prescribing clinic and dispensing location.[@2]

Prescriptions still had to come from doctors on the General Medical Council specialist register, with inspections and reporting requirements attached to the service.[@2] The licensing announcement therefore opened applications; patients’ actual choice depended on providers obtaining approval and offering a service.[@1,2]

## Two approvals followed the July opening

By [1 August 2024](/history/isle-of-man-second-pharmacy-approved-2024/), two pharmacies had secured approval: Karsons and Kingsley Muti at the Balthane Industrial Estate in Ballasalla.[@3] The report described both applications as approved by the Department of Health and Social Care.[@3]

Patients continued to pay for their own prescriptions and associated fees, with cannabis-based products available through private prescriptions rather than the island’s ordinary NHS prescribing route.[@3] Expanding the number of potential providers addressed geography and convenience while retaining that private-payment model.[@1,3]

## A patient community developed alongside dispensing

In January 2026 the Green Lounge in Ramsey announced planning approval for an adult-only venue where pass holders could vaporise their own prescribed cannabis.[@4] Its founders described reducing stigma and challenging misconceptions as part of their purpose.[@4] The [Isle of Man profile](/country/im/) follows both the dispensing route created by the pharmacy expansion and the later development of patient social spaces.[@3,4]
