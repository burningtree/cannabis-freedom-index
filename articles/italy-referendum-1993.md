Italian voters reversed the criminalisation of personal drug possession in April 1993. The referendum did not legalise supply or erase every sanction. It removed the provisions that could turn possession for one's own use into a criminal case and left an administrative system behind.

## Undoing the 1990 law

Italy's 1990 drug law had introduced a “daily average dose” and a progression from administrative measures to criminal punishment. The referendum sought to repeal the provisions supporting that system, together with related rules affecting treatment and doctors' reporting obligations.

The Constitutional Court declared the question admissible in February 1993. It described the central purpose clearly: decriminalising the offence constituted by possessing drugs for personal use while administrative sanctions remained possible. The court also held that this approach was compatible with the international drug conventions.

## The vote

Voting took place on 18 and 19 April as part of a larger package of referendums. The drug question passed, with 55.3% voting yes according to the published result.

Presidential Decree No. 171 formally listed the repealed provisions. Issued on 5 June, it took effect on 20 June and removed Article 76 along with the statutory quantity machinery and related language in other provisions.

The formal decree converted the voters' decision into the operative text of national law.

## Administrative consequences remained

After the repeal, possession intended for another person remained within the criminal supply provisions. Possession solely for personal use was decriminalised, a distinction later confirmed by Italy's Constitutional Court when it explained that criminal relevance depended on the substance being intended for third parties.

Personal possession could still bring administrative consequences under Article 75, including measures affecting a driving licence or passport. The referendum therefore restored the broad approach that had existed before [criminalisation in 1990](/history/italy-criminalisation-1990/), not a system of legal cannabis sales.

## What moved in the index

The vote raised [Italy's](/country/it/) possession score from 1 to 3, enforcement from 2 to 4 and consumption from 1 to 2. Cultivation and sharing stayed at 1, and access at 0. The score change reflects removal of criminal punishment for personal possession while administrative penalties and the supply ban remained.
