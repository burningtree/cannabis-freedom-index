In October 2025 Volt Cyprus called for legal adult possession and a licensed cannabis market overseen by an independent authority.[@1] It is the first detailed regulated-market proposal in Cypriot politics.[@1]

## What Volt wants

Adult possession would become legal, and production, trade, retail and cannabis clubs would operate under licences.[@1] An independent authority would oversee the market, with quality controls on products and tax revenue from legal sales.[@1]

## Where it lands

The proposal meets a law that punishes unauthorised possession with up to eight years and unauthorised growing with up to life imprisonment, softened only by a two-year ceiling for qualifying young first offenders.[@2] Volt's answer to that regime is not lighter punishment but a different system: the party's FAQ presents regulation as the alternative to prohibition.[@1]

Patients get a specific mention. The current medical route runs on ministerial approval and pharmacy preparations, with imported oils documented since 2021; Volt's market would sit alongside it rather than replace it.[@1]

The proposal is still only that — a party policy, not a bill. But it moves the island's debate past the question of how harshly to punish users and onto the question Malta answered in 2021: who should be allowed to grow and sell.[@1] It is traced in [Cyprus's](/country/cy/) history as the first call for a full regulated market.
