On Thursday 15 October 2015 a regulation on the use of cannabis for medical purposes took effect in Croatia. It allowed importers and pharmacies to put medical cannabis products on the Croatian market. The news site tportal covered the first day, and its report is the basis for what follows.

## Who can get it

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Illnesses | multiple sclerosis, cancer, epilepsy and AIDS |
| Recommendation | from a specialist |
| Prescription | written by the family doctor |
| Dispensing | at a pharmacy |
| Forms | dried plant tops for teas or inhalation; solutions, drops and oil capsules by mouth |

## The minister's promise

The health minister, Siniša Varga, said patients would not have to wait months: supply should begin within a few weeks. Patients could also bring preparations in from elsewhere in the EU "without fear" of misdemeanour or criminal penalties. He expected little abuse because only a limited number of patients qualified, and said prices would be set by the market.

## The first-day doubts

- **Patients.** Ratko Martinović of the association Medicinska konoplja called it a hollow regulation made for the elections. Legalisation made sense, he said, only if patients got better medicine at a lower price than on the black market, and pharmacy prices would be higher.
- **Family doctors.** Ines Balint of the family medicine association said doctors could prescribe only ready-made medicines registered in the EU, none of which was then available in Croatia, and were waiting for guidelines.
- **Pharmacists.** Mate Portolan of the pharmacists' chamber said pharmacies needed guidelines, recipes, equipment and training.
- **Price.** Jakov Radošević of the wholesaler Medika said a month's treatment could cost up to 800 euros; an unofficial estimate put it at about 4,000 kuna.

Three ready-made medicines were registered in the EU, but no manufacturer had applied to register one in Croatia. The ministry said wholesalers would be allowed to import them once a patient had a prescription.

## What it did not allow

Growing was not permitted, for patients or for companies. An amendment to the Act on Combating Drug Abuse that would let pharmaceutical companies grow medical cannabis under strict control was to go to parliament only after the elections.

## Why the index moved

Only access changes, from 0 to 1: there is a lawful route to cannabis medicines, limited to four groups of illness and reached through two doctors and a pharmacy. The position of everyone else stayed as the [2013 reform](/history/croatia-misdemeanour-2013/) had left it. The total moves from 19 to 20.
