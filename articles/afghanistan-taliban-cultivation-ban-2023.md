On 19 March 2023 the Taliban's supreme leader, Hibatullah Akhundzada, issued an order banning cannabis cultivation across Afghanistan. The order was not the country's first cannabis ban, but it came from a government that had already banned opium and was willing to send growers to court.[@1]

## The order

Order No. 510 declared growing cannabis absolutely prohibited. Crops were to be destroyed, the interior ministry was to bring growers before the courts, and judges were to punish them under sharia. The newspaper that reported it noted that Akhundzada's earlier ban on poppy had not stopped the opium trade.[@1]

The decree did not set out a penalty scale for growers. It left the sentence to the courts, as the Taliban have done with other offences.[@1]

## A country long known for hashish

Afghanistan had produced hashish for centuries. Farmers in Balkh and along the Khyber trade routes grew cannabis and sold charas, the hand-rubbed resin, and the country was still described in 2010 as the world's largest hashish producer.[@3] The plant was part of the rural economy in the north and centre, not a marginal crop.

The 2023 order therefore hit an established trade. It also followed the Taliban's 2022 opium ban, which cut opium cultivation sharply; officials and observers watched whether cannabis would take its place.[@2]

## What happened

The cannabis ban has been enforced less thoroughly than the opium ban, and hashish remains widely used.[@2] No national cultivation or production figures have been published since the takeover.

The courts have continued to hear cases. In May 2025 a Taliban court sentenced eleven people for hashish and tablet trafficking to prison terms of seven months to three and a half years, with 10 to 39 lashes.[@4] That case concerned trafficking rather than growing, but it showed that the criminal system was still using the old drug offences alongside the new decree.

## Not the first ban

Afghanistan's [1924 penal code](/history/afghanistan-penal-code-1924/) forbade domestic cannabis production and use, and a [1957 law](/history/afghanistan-nominal-ban-1957/) repeated the ban without making it effective. In [1973](/history/afghanistan-ban-1973/) the monarchy renewed prohibition under American pressure, and in [1997](/history/afghanistan-taliban-radio-hashish-ban-1997/) the Taliban's radio announced a ban on hashish production, sale and use. The 2023 order is the latest in that line, not a new departure. The [Afghanistan profile](/country/af/) sets out the current penalties and the limits of the evidence.
