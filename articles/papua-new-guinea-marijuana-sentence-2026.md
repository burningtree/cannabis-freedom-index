A district court in Papua New Guinea's highlands took one sitting to give a
26-year-old man a choice between a fine of 30,000 kina and twenty years in prison.
The case shows how the country's 2022 drug law works in practice.

## The case

On 4 August 2026 police and soldiers at the Magarima checkpoint in Hela Province
stopped a man from Jangome village, in Tari. They found 60 rolls of marijuana
weighing about 4.7 kg. He was handed to investigators the next day, arrested and
held in the cells at Tari police station.

He appeared before the Tari District Court on 10 August. The magistrate found him
guilty at that first appearance and ordered him to pay 30,000 kina, or serve twenty
years.

The provincial police commander, Inspector Arau Mairi, said the case "should send a
strong message", and warned that anyone growing, carrying or selling drugs "will be
identified, arrested and brought before the courts".

## The law behind it

The police report names no statute, but the sentence fits the
[Controlled Substance Act](/history/papua-new-guinea-controlled-substance-act-2022/),
in force since 2022, which grades cannabis offences by weight.

| Quantity | Maximum |
|---|---|
| 10 g or less | 2 years |
| More than 10 g, less than 2 kg | 15 years |
| More than 2 kg | 16 years to life |

At 4.7 kg the man fell in the top band. A year earlier a court in Madang gave two
men [25 years each](/history/papua-new-guinea-long-sentences-2025/) for 14 to 20 kg,
citing the quantity and the need to deter others.

## A fine or a lifetime

The alternative is the striking part. Thirty thousand kina buys freedom; without it
the same offence costs twenty years. The report does not say whether he could pay.

Courts in Papua New Guinea have had this discretion for a long time. In
[1973](/history/papua-new-guinea-henderson-ruling-1973/) the Supreme Court held
that a mandatory minimum for possession did not stop a magistrate from imposing a
fine instead.

## In the index

No law changed, so the entry moves no score. It is recorded because it shows what
the tiers of the 2022 Act mean when they are applied in
[Papua New Guinea](/country/pg/): for less than five kilograms, twenty years unless
the fine is paid.
