Vietnam made continued drug use after repeated education and compulsory treatment a criminal offence from 22 May 1997.[@1,2] The basic sentence was three months to two years in prison, rising to two to five years for repeat offending.[@1] The same amendment expressly named cannabis in cultivation and possession offences, with life imprisonment or death available for large quantities.[@1]

## Parliament expanded the drug chapter

The earlier [1985 code](/history/vietnam-organising-drug-use-1986/) punished organising drug use with six months to five years in prison, or three to ten years in aggravated cases.[@3] A [1989 amendment](/history/vietnam-death-penalty-1989/) then grouped unlawful manufacture, possession, sale and transport into an offence whose highest band included death.[@4]

Parliament's next expansion replaced those two rules with a chapter separating cultivation, manufacture, possession, transport, trade and other drug conduct.[@1] It also added the offence of using drugs after earlier interventions had failed.[@1] Parliament adopted the amendment on 10 May; the Supreme People's Court confirmed that it became operative when promulgated on 22 May.[@2]

## The use offence depended on previous interventions

The new use offence required repeated education and prior administrative placement in a compulsory treatment establishment, followed by continued illegal drug use.[@1] Those conditions defined who could receive the basic three-month-to-two-year prison sentence.[@1] A repeat conviction for this offence carried two to five years.[@1]

Cannabis growing had its own conditions.[@1] A person who continued cultivating after repeated education, help to stabilise their livelihood and an administrative sanction faced six months to three years.[@1] Organised cultivation or repeat offending carried three to seven years.[@1]

## Cannabis weights could lead to capital punishment

Possession began with a two-to-seven-year prison band.[@1] The highest quantity band, carrying life imprisonment or death, included at least five kilograms of cannabis resin or seventy-five kilograms of cannabis leaves, flowers or fruit.[@1] The separate transport and trade offences referred back to the possession offence's quantity tables.[@1]

## Courts had to respect the commencement date

The Supreme People's Court instructed courts to apply the new offences and heavier penalties to conduct from 22 May onwards.[@2] Earlier conduct remained subject to the older rules where the amendment would make punishment harsher; changes abolishing an offence or reducing punishment could benefit earlier defendants.[@2]

The [replacement code from 2000](/history/vietnam-cannabis-penalties-2000/) retained both the qualified use offence and the cultivation conditions, carrying these rules into the next stage of [Vietnam's history](/country/vn/).[@5]
