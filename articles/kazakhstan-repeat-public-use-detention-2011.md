In January 2011 Kazakhstan began using short detention against people who repeatedly used drugs in public. A first offence still brought a fine. A third within a year could bring fifteen days in custody.[@1]

## The fine-only rule

A 2006 law had created a specific administrative offence for non-medical drug use in public places. The fine was one to two monthly calculation units, and a repeat within a year brought five to ten. There was no detention.[@2] The rule was part of a system that treated public use as a nuisance and a health problem rather than a serious crime.

## The 2010 amendment

Law 372-IV of 29 December 2010 replaced that article. It raised the ordinary fine to five monthly calculation units and the repeat fine to ten. It also added fifteen days' administrative detention for a person who had already been penalised twice for public use within a year.[@1] The amounts were set in monthly calculation units, the index used across Kazakhstan's administrative penalties.

The amendment took effect ten calendar days after its first official publication. The publication record lists Kazakhstan Pravda of 11 January 2011, which is why the change is dated to January.[@1] The detention was administrative, not a criminal sentence, but it meant that a repeat user could lose their liberty without a criminal conviction. Because the penalty sat in the administrative code, it produced an administrative record rather than the criminal record that follows a conviction under Article 296.

## The move into criminal law

The separate administrative offence did not last. Kazakhstan's 2015 Criminal Code made non-medical public use a crime under Article 296(1), with a fine of up to 80 monthly calculation indices, corrective work, up to 80 hours of community service or arrest of up to 20 days.[@3] The [2006 entry](/history/kazakhstan-public-drug-use-fines-2006/) records the fine-only rule, and the [2015 entry](/history/kazakhstan-criminal-code-2015/) records the move into the criminal code.

The 2011 amendment is therefore a middle step: public use was still administrative, but repeat use could already lead to detention. The [Kazakhstan profile](/country/kz/) sets out the current penalties for possession, use and supply.
