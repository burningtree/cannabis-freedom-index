At the end of 1948 Seychelles updated its drug list for the post-war pharmacopeia — and Indian hemp came with it. Ordinance No. 35 of 1948, passed by the Legislative Council on 29 December and published in Gazette No. 3 of 10 January 1949, rewrote section 15 of the principal Dangerous Drugs Ordinance No. 26 of 1935.[@1]

## Synthetics in, codeine exception out

The amendment is mostly about laboratory opiates: new paragraphs add dihydrodesoxymorphine (desomorphine), pethidine, amidone and methyldihydromorphinone (Metopon) with their salts and preparations, while the old codeine and dionine exceptions in paragraphs (h) and (i) are cut back to low-dose medicinal mixtures.[@1] Morphine esters and ecgonine preparations are swept in too.[@1]

## Hemp preparations, except external ones

Between the pethidine and the amidone sits paragraph (l): "any preparation, not being a preparation capable of external use only, made from extract or tincture of Indian Hemp".[@1] Internal-use hemp medicines thus joined the controlled-drug list alongside the new synthetics — the same formula Britain was circulating to its colonies in those years for bringing cannabis preparations under import, export and improper-use controls without touching external remedies.[@1]

## A list, not a crackdown

Nothing in the ordinance changes penalties or enforcement; it widens the schedule the 1935 law operates on.[@1] The [country page](/country/sc/) runs from the 1882 ban through this update to the modern misuse law.
