In March 1973 Bahrain replaced its 1959 drug ordinance with a sterner code. Decree-Law No. 4 of 1973 on controlling narcotic substances and preparations, signed by Isa ben Salman Al Khalifa, cancelled the 1959 ordinance and took effect on publication in the Official Gazette on 8 March — with cannabis (Indian hemp) and cannabis resin named in its schedule.[@1]

## Ten years for supply

Article 23 drew the hard line: up to ten years in prison or a 10,000-dinar fine, or both, for importing or exporting without authorisation, for cultivating, producing, extracting or manufacturing for trafficking, and for possessing, buying, selling, giving away or facilitating use of narcotics outside the law's permissions.[@1]

## Three years — or a hospital bed

Personal possession drew up to three years or 3,000 dinars — but the court could instead confine the defendant in a designated hospital for treatment, on its own motion or the health minister's recommendation, until a ministerial committee reported and the court decided what next.[@1] Circulation inside hospitals and health units ran under the minister's own rules, and authorised holders faced three months or 500 dinars for hiding their records.[@1]

## Fees, ministers, and a cancelled ordinance

The law left fees, hospital designations and treatment committees to health-ministry decisions, and gave the respective ministers implementation.[@1] The [country page](/country/bh/) continues from this decree.
