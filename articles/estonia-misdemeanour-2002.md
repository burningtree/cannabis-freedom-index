On 1 September 2002, Estonia divided drug possession in two. A new Penal Code made holding a small quantity for personal use a misdemeanour punished with a fine — no prison, no criminal record — while large amounts stayed criminal. It was the single biggest easing in Estonia's recorded history, lifting the score from 11 to 19 in one step.

## From three years to a fine

The starting point was the Soviet one. Since the [1974 union decree](/history/estonia-soviet-possession-crime-1974/), possession without intent to sell had been a crime carrying up to three years in prison, and independent Estonia kept the structure through the 1990s. A 1997 Narcotic Drugs Act set up the scheduling system but left the penalties alone. The 2002 Code broke with it: small possession left the criminal law for the misdemeanour track, where the sanction is money rather than custody. Distribution and large holdings remained criminal offences, punishable with up to five years in prison.

## Ten people as the boundary

Estonian law draws the small–large line functionally rather than in grams: a large quantity is one enough to intoxicate at least ten people. Courts have given that test teeth — in 2014 the Supreme Court treated 7.5 grams of marijuana at 7 to 10 per cent THC as already enough to cross it. The rule of thumb in circulation is that up to 7.5 grams counts as personal use, but the ten-person test means potency matters as much as weight, and a strong batch can turn a small bag into a large quantity.

## The score it bought

The 2002 reform moved three segments at once — possession, enforcement and consumption all softened — which is why the jump is eight points, from 11 to 19. Lithuania followed a year later with its own small-possession misdemeanour tier, then reversed it in 2017; Estonia's line held. Nothing since has reversed it: the 2019 authorisation of the cannabidiol medicine Epidyolex added the final point to reach 20. Two decades on, the misdemeanour line still defines everyday Estonian drug policy, and the [Estonia profile](/country/ee/) follows it to the present.
