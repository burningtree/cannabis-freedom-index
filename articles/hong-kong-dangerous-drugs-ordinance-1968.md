Hong Kong's Dangerous Drugs Ordinance received assent on 7 November 1968.
It replaced an existing ordinance and reorganised offences around a distinction
the government wanted to emphasise: drug supply and personal consumption should
attract different responses.

This was a revision of an established system. The legislature had already
[passed Indian-hemp controls in 1928](/history/hong-kong-indian-hemp-amendment-1928/).

## What the government wanted to change

Introducing the replacement bill on 9 October, the Attorney General said much
of it reproduced existing law. Some provisions previously contained in regulations
would move into the ordinance itself.

The new provisions had a harder edge. The government wanted more severe
penalties for trafficking, stronger prosecution evidence rules and more lenient
treatment of possession for personal consumption. The speech also acknowledged
that a person could be both a consumer and a distributor, complicating that
distinction in practice.

Those were legislative objectives. They do not establish that courts or police
consistently achieved the intended separation.

## Two possession offences

Section 7 concerned possession for unlawful trafficking, whether the intended
trafficking was by the possessor or another person. On indictment, its statutory
maximum included life imprisonment and a HK$100,000 fine.

Section 8 addressed possession otherwise than for trafficking and consumption,
subject to the ordinance's authorisations or a licence. The original maximum
was three years in prison and HK$10,000. These are the 1968 text's limits,
rather than today's penalties or evidence of a typical sentence.

The First Schedule included cannabis, resin, extracts and tinctures within
its controlled categories.

## A separate rule for growing plants

Section 9 prohibited cultivating cannabis plants. It also covered dealing in,
supplying, importing and exporting plants, with separate provisions for possession
and transit.

On indictment, the maximum was fifteen years and HK$100,000. The statute thus
distinguished plant offences from the separate offence concerning possession
of a dangerous drug for personal consumption.

Its general definition of cannabis referred to flowering or fruiting tops,
while section 9 expressly addressed plants. The
[1978 definition amendment](/history/hong-kong-cannabis-definition-1978/)
belongs to a later stage of this legal history.

## Assent was a separate date

The original printed ordinance records the Acting Governor's assent on
7 November. Section 1 left operation to a later date appointed by gazette notice.
This entry therefore records adoption rather than claiming commencement on
the same day.

For [Hong Kong's timeline](/country/hk/), the ordinance is a documented revision
of drug control whose offence structure matters alongside the dates at which
it was adopted and brought into operation.
