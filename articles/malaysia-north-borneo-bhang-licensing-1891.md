An 1891 North Borneo ordinance authorized a monopoly over sales of bhang or ganja and required sellers to hold licences, according to a later League of Nations report. The report also recorded a major gap: it could not say whether the authorized monopoly was actually operating.

## An earlier law in a later official survey

The source is the League’s summary of answers to its 1921 opium questionnaire, dated 1 June 1922. Its section on Indian hemp identifies the ordinance through North Borneo’s government reply.

The date 1891 therefore refers to the reported ordinance, rather than to the questionnaire or its publication. The original enactment was not retrieved for this article. The summary does not supply a chapter number, exact commencement date, penalty schedule or complete set of exceptions.

## Licence requirements and monopoly powers

The report describes two legal features: authority to establish a monopoly over bhang or ganja sales, and prohibition of sale without a licence. These support recording a regional sales-control measure before 1900.

They do not establish a general possession, cultivation or consumption ban. Those activities would require their own provisions or other evidence. Likewise, statutory authority for a monopoly should not be converted into a claim that a particular concession holder had already been appointed.

## What the officials could not confirm

The League expressly stated that the reply gave no information on whether a monopoly existed in practice or on the extent of drug use. That qualification is part of the evidence, not an incidental omission to be filled with an assumption.

The summary cannot establish shop numbers, sales volume, revenue, enforcement intensity or the effectiveness of the licensing requirement. Further research needs the ordinance itself, implementing notices and administrative accounts.

## A separate colonial jurisdiction

The [Straits Settlements’ excise law](/history/malaysia-straits-ban-1870/) offers a useful comparison: its consolidated text expressly provides for a bhang concession and licensed shops. That text supplies details about its own jurisdiction; its fees and penalties cannot be transferred to North Borneo.

Its checked status records comparison of the narrow historical claim with the official League summary, rather than direct verification of the original 1891 ordinance.
