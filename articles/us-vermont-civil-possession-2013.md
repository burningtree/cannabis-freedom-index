Vermont's 2013 reform replaced criminal penalties for small adult possession with a civil
violation. It did not make the conduct fully lawful.

Act 76 was signed on 6 June. The relevant provisions took effect on **1 July 2013**. For people
aged 21 or older, possession of no more than one ounce of marijuana or five grams of hashish
carried maximum civil penalties of $200 for a first offence, $300 for a second and $500 thereafter.
The act expressly ruled out a criminal-history record for that violation.

The distinction from legalisation remained practical. Cannabis was still contraband subject to
seizure, impaired-driving rules still applied, and municipalities could regulate public use.
Cultivation remained within the criminal provisions. The act also established a separate youth
procedure, so the adult fine schedule should not be presented as the complete rule for minors.

Parrella's overview calls the change legalisation while also mentioning fines. Reading the
original act resolves that ambiguity. These are historical 2013 rules, not Vermont's current
possession or growing limits.
