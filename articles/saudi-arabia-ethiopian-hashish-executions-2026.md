On 27 July 2026 Saudi Arabia executed five Ethiopian men for smuggling hashish. Five
others had been executed on 23 June. According to Human Rights Watch, the latest
five had no lawyer, and their hearings lasted between three and twenty minutes.

## The law

Saudi drug law rests on a [1987 fatwa](/history/saudi-arabia-death-penalty-1987/)
prescribing death for anyone who brings drugs into the kingdom, and on the
[2005 anti-narcotics law](/history/saudi-arabia-anti-narcotics-law-2005/), under
which importing or receiving drugs for distribution can be punished by death.
Personal possession carries six months to two years.

For almost three years the penalty was not used. A
[moratorium](/history/saudi-arabia-drug-execution-moratorium-2021/) on drug
executions operated from February 2020, without ever being written into law, and
ended in [November 2022](/history/saudi-arabia-drug-executions-resume-2022/).

## The five men

All five came from Ethiopia's Tigray region. By the account Human Rights Watch
gathered, one had been living in Yemen without work for more than a year when, in
March or April 2023, a Yemeni man offered the group a job: carry a package a short
distance. The man then disappeared. They were arrested on a road, having crossed
into Saudi Arabia without knowing it.

They spent more than three years in detention at Khamis Mushait, in Asir province.

## The trial

- Three court sessions, of three to twenty minutes each, two of them held remotely.
- A translator at only two of the three.
- No legal representation.
- Documents in Arabic, which they could not read, that they were made to sign.

Unable to write Arabic, at least one asked a Saudi prisoner to write an appeal for
him. The source did not know whether it reached the court.

On 12 May the patriarch of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church appealed to both
governments to halt the executions. Ethiopia's foreign ministry said in July that it
"remains closely engaged" with the Saudi authorities, without saying whether that
covered death sentences. A UN working group has urged Saudi Arabia to restore the
moratorium.
