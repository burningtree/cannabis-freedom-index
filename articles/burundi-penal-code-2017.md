Burundi's revised Penal Code retains a short but severe set of drug offences. The 2017 text bans
growing, holding and consuming controlled plants and substances, with prison available even for
personal use.

## Control begins outside the code

Law No. 1/27 of 29 December 2017 revised the Penal Code. Its narcotics section begins at Article
505, but it does not contain its own drug schedule. Instead, a substance or plant is a narcotic
when an order by the minister responsible for public health classifies it that way.

That means this document establishes the criminal rules but, by itself, does not identify
cannabis by name. Reading the code and the ministerial classification together is necessary to
prove how a particular cannabis product is scheduled.

## One broad prohibition

Article 506 prohibits cultivation, sale, transport, possession and consumption of narcotics,
except in cases and under conditions fixed by a health-ministry order. Article 507 gives the
general penalty for breaching the regulatory provisions: one to five years of penal servitude
and a fine of 100,000 to 250,000 Burundian francs, or either punishment alone.

The following articles separate out particular conduct. Illegal production, import, manufacture
or export carries ten to fifteen years and a fine of up to one million francs; the term doubles
when the offence is committed by an organised group. Facilitating another person's use, whether
for payment or free of charge, carries one to five years and a fine of 50,000 to one million
francs.

## Use is separately punishable

Article 510 specifically punishes illicit consumption or possession of a classified substance
with one to five years and a fine of 50,000 to 100,000 francs, or either penalty. For every
consumption offence, Article 513 tells the judge to impose socio-judicial supervision as an
additional sentence.

The court must also order confiscation of the controlled plants or substances and the equipment
used for consumption, manufacture or transport. Confiscated crops are destroyed, at the
offender's expense where the authorities carry out the destruction.

This structure supports the strict present-day account on the [Burundi profile](/country/bi/),
but the code is not evidence of a legal retail or personal-cultivation exception.

In the index, the 2017 revision does not move a score. Burundi was already recorded as prohibited
under an estimated earlier restriction, so this event documents the later penalty framework
without assigning an unsupported date to the original ban.
