The amendment effective on 1 January 2022 changed who could produce Czech medical cannabis. Instead of relying on one supplier selected through a state tender, Czechia created a route through which any applicant meeting the legal conditions could seek permission to grow.

## From one contractor to licensing

The [2013 medical law](/history/czechia-medical-2013/) had established a tightly controlled system. Domestic production later began under a public contract, and the [first Czech-grown batch](/history/czechia-first-domestic-medical-cannabis-2016/) entered distribution in 2016.

According to the legal analysis published by epravo.cz, only one selected supplier had previously been able to cultivate medical cannabis and deliver it to the State Institute for Drug Control, SÚKL. The 2022 amendment abandoned that procurement model. Applicants could approach SÚKL directly and qualify under statutory rules.

## Two permissions, not an open market

Opening the application process did not make medical cultivation ordinary agriculture. A producer still needed a SÚKL licence to cultivate medical-cannabis plants and a Ministry of Health permit to handle addictive substances and preparations.

An applicant also needed an indoor cultivation facility when applying. The operation had to follow good cultivation practice, maintain the required quality and comply with rules governing such matters as security, storage, transport and waste. The amendment therefore expanded potential participation while keeping production inside the pharmaceutical-control system.

## Where the crop could go

A licensed grower could distribute compliant cannabis within Czechia, including to pharmacies. Export was also possible with permission from the Ministry of Health. These channels were for medical cannabis; they did not authorise sales to recreational consumers.

The reform's stated expectation, as described by epravo.cz, was that greater competition would make medical cannabis more available and less expensive. That was a policy expectation rather than a guaranteed result of the law.

## What moved in the index

Nothing moved in the Cannabis Freedom Index. Patients already had a legal prescription-and-pharmacy route, so changing the production and licensing structure did not create a new access score. The event is recorded because it materially changed the national supply system behind medical access on the [Czechia profile](/country/cz/).

The reform changed the suppliers who could enter that system, not the legal route a patient had to follow.
