Venezuela's first cannabis ban has no date on it. No Venezuelan law against the plant has
surfaced from before the international era, so the file estimates the restriction to the
years after the 1925 Geneva convention, which put Indian hemp under international
control.[@2] By 1950 the ban was certainly in place: a United Nations survey published in
January 1951 states that all the American states prohibited the keeping and consumption of
hemp.[@1]

## Ganja on the coast first

The plant got there before the law. Indian indentured labourers carried ganja culture to
Venezuela's Caribbean coast, and by 1895 some had become specialised producers — growing in
Venezuela and smuggling the crop back to Trinidad to dodge customs.[@3] The first printed
record is an 1895 travel account, with a British Guiana inquiry following in 1897.[@3]

## An estimate, honestly labelled

The window runs from the Geneva convention to the UN survey: 1925 to 1950.[@1][@2] It is an
estimate, not a found statute — the scores start from full prohibition because by mid-century
no American state allowed the plant.[@1]
