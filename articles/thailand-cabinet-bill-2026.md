Thailand opened its cannabis market in 2022 without a law to govern it. Four years
later the cabinet approved one, and its purpose is to close most of what opened.

## A market without a statute

On [9 June 2022](/history/thailand-decriminalisation-2022/) the cannabis flower was
taken off the narcotics list. Dispensaries multiplied, especially in Bangkok and the
tourist towns, while a bill to regulate them stalled in parliament.

The government then tightened by decree. A
[2024 notice](/history/thailand-thirty-gram-presumption-2024/) treated more than 30
grams as intent to sell. On
[25 June 2025](/history/thailand-prescription-only-2025/) the flower was
reclassified as a controlled herb, so that buying it requires a prescription and
sales are limited to licensed medical premises.

## What the bill does

The draft Cannabis and Hemp Act, announced by the public health minister, Pattana
Promphat, on 22 September 2026, puts that rule into a statute and adds to it.

- **Growers** must register their plantations, with size and location, so cannabis
  can be traced from cultivation through processing to sale.
- **Dispensaries** keep their existing licences only until they expire, in stages
  through 2028. To continue, a shop must become a licensed medical facility with a
  qualified practitioner on site.
- **Penalties** for violations rise, and inspections increase. The report gives no
  figures.

More than 10,000 shops and cultivation sites, over 95 per cent of those operating,
have been entered into a government tracking system. Those outside it, Pattana said,
"should be presumed to be operating illegally".

## The politics

The turn is being made by the party that started it. Bhumjaithai campaigned on
decriminalisation before the 2019 election and its leader, Anutin Charnvirakul, led
it as health minister. He is now prime minister, and his party backs tighter
controls.

## What happens next

The bill goes to parliament, with no timetable given. An earlier cannabis bill
stalled there, so approval by the cabinet is not the end of it.

Because the prescription rule is already in force, the bill changes little in
practice until it passes, and the index records it without moving
[Thailand's](/country/th/) score. What it would change is permanence: an Act of
parliament is harder to undo than a ministerial notice.
