Spain's cannabis clubs have never been legal. They stand on three legal props, none
of them written for cannabis, and in July 2026 the cabinet proposed to remove one.

## The three props

**Private use.** Spanish criminal law punishes trafficking and promotion, not
possession for oneself. The Supreme Court said so in
[1974](/history/spain-supreme-court-1974/) and the penal code followed in
[1983](/history/spain-personal-use-1983/).

**Shared consumption.** Courts extended that reasoning to closed groups of adults
who already use cannabis and share it among themselves, without profit and out of
public view. On that basis [associations](/history/spain-social-clubs-2001/) began
growing for their members.

**The smokers' club.** The 2005 tobacco law banned smoking in enclosed public
places but exempted private smokers' clubs. That is what lets a cannabis club allow
smoking on its premises.

## How firm the ground is

Not very. In [2015](/history/spain-supreme-court-clubs-2015/) the Supreme Court
convicted the organisers of three clubs, holding that what they did was not genuine
shared consumption. Catalonia's attempt to regulate clubs by law was
[annulled](/history/spain-catalonia-club-law-2017/) by the Constitutional Court.

The clubs carried on regardless. The network peaked at an estimated 800 to 1,000 in
the late 2010s, the largest in Europe.

Outside the club door the law is plain. Having or using cannabis in public brings
a [fine of 601 to 30,000 euros](/history/spain-public-safety-law-2015/); one
federation of clubs counted 130 million euros in such fines in 2023.

## The draft

On 21 July 2026 the Council of Ministers approved a draft overhaul of the tobacco
law. Two provisions matter here:

- it removes the exemption for private smokers' clubs;
- it extends the smoke-free rules to herbal products that are smoked, vaped or
  inhaled, which covers cannabis without naming it.

A club could still exist as an association and still, on the shared-consumption
argument, distribute to its members. What it could no longer do is let them smoke
there.

## Where it stands

The draft had not completed its passage when this was written, and the clubs
operate as before. The government has been clear about the wider question: on
9 June 2026 the secretary of state for health ruled out recreational legalisation.

A separate reform before Congress would cut the minimum fine for public possession
to 100 euros. It has not passed either.

Because nothing is law yet, [Spain's](/country/es/) score does not move. If the
tobacco reform passes as drafted, the access score, which rests on tolerated clubs,
will need to be looked at again.
