Saudi Arabia's prohibition is nearly as old as the kingdom. In 1934 the founding king, Abdulaziz bin Abdulrahman Al Saud, issued the country's first anti-narcotic legislation, the Prevention of Narcotic Drug Trafficking and Use Law. Its title names both halves of the offence: trading in drugs, and using them. The state's own encyclopaedia, Saudipedia, gives the year but no day or month, and does not reproduce the text.

## The line of laws

| Year | Measure |
|---|---|
| 1934 | Prevention of Narcotic Drug Trafficking and Use Law, issued by King Abdulaziz |
| 1955 | Council of Ministers resolution setting penalties for smugglers, their assistants, dealers and users |
| 2005 | Law of Combating Narcotics and Psychotropic Substances, the statute now in force |
| 2009 | Statute of the National Committee for Narcotics Control approved |
| 2010 | Implementing regulations issued |

The 1955 resolution is the first point at which the source describes penalties, and it lists four groups: smugglers, those who help them, dealers and users. Use was punishable from the start of the written record.

## What the current law adds

The 2005 law defined the offences and penalties again and set up a licensing system for making medical preparations that contain narcotic or psychotropic substances, with procedures for dispensing them. That is the only lawful channel the source describes, and it concerns medicines, not cannabis.

The law also has a treatment track. Within three months of the start of treatment the clinic's doctor must report on the patient to an addiction cases committee, which decides whether the person has recovered or needs longer. Fifty hospitals were named as psychiatric clinics for the purpose. Disclosing information about an addict during interrogation or trial is itself punishable, with up to three months in prison or a fine of up to 30,000 riyals.
