The most famous cannabis recipe in English was printed in a respectable cookbook in
1954, and American readers did not get to see it.[@1,2]

## The book

Alice B. Toklas published *The Alice B. Toklas Cook Book* in 1954. Among its recipes
was one for "Haschich Fudge", a mixture of spices, nuts, fruit and cannabis.[@2]

Toklas presented it without alarm. She called it "the food of paradise", said it was
"easy to whip up on a rainy day", suggested where the cannabis might be found, and
advised that two pieces were quite enough. She later said the recipe had come from
her friend Brion Gysin, who had sent it in spelling cannabis "canibus" without her
realising what it was.[@2]

## The cut

The recipe was removed from the first American edition. It appeared in later ones.
The omission did nothing to keep it quiet: the fudge caused controversy in the
press — Time reviewed it in October 1954 — and the recipe became a sensation in its own right.[@1,2]

## Afterlife

By the 1960s the fudge belonged to the counterculture, and Toklas's name was attached
for good to cannabis baked into sweets. The 1968 film *I Love You, Alice B. Toklas*
took its title from it.[@1,2]

## Where the law stood

In 1954 cannabis in the [United States](/country/us/) was governed by the
[Marihuana Tax Act](/history/us-marihuana-tax-act-1937/), stiffened in
[1951](/history/us-boggs-act-1951/) with mandatory minimum sentences. A second round
of minimums [followed in 1956](/history/us-narcotic-control-act-1956/).

The cookbook changed no law and moves no score. It is in the history because it shows
how early, and how casually, cannabis as food entered print.
