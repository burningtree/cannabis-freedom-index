Until 2003, holding drugs was not a crime in Serbia. Making them, selling them, even lending someone the equipment to make them — all criminal. But simple possession, for personal use or otherwise, had never been incriminated. That changed with a single amendment, and the next two decades swung between punishing users and sparing them.

## Two articles and no possession

The starting point was the 1976 Criminal Code of socialist Yugoslavia, which systematised drug crime into just two articles: unauthorised production and sale, and enabling another person to enjoy intoxicating drugs. Neither covered holding drugs without intent to sell, so personal possession sat entirely outside criminal law. A 1990 amendment added the equipment offence — making, buying or lending the tools and materials for manufacture — but possession itself stayed untouched through the federation's collapse and the wars that followed.

## 2003: possession becomes a crime

The Law on Amendments and Additions to the Criminal Code of 2003 closed the gap at a stroke. Anyone possessing substances declared narcotic drugs faced a fine or up to three years in prison, and the offence was renamed to include possession alongside production and circulation. The same amendments raised the minimum prison terms for production and sale to five and seven years. Courts quickly put the new summary offence to work — including against serious dealers, where intent to sell could not be proven and the possession paragraph served as the fallback charge.

## Six years of custody, then a softer code

The harsh settlement lasted barely three years. The [new Criminal Code](/history/serbia-new-criminal-code-2006/), adopted in September 2005 and in force on 1 January 2006, kept the possession offence but cut penalties across the board and let courts remit punishment for offenders holding drugs for personal use — or for those who disclosed their supplier. The 2009 amendments then narrowed criminal possession to small quantities for personal use only, and in 2019 the pendulum swung back with [longer sentences](/history/serbia-drug-sentences-raised-2019/) for growing and large stocks. Serbia's score round-tripped with it: 11 down to 9, back to 11, down to 9 again. The [Serbia profile](/country/rs/) carries that line to the present.
