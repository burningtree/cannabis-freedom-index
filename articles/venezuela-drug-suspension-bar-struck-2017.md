Venezuela's Supreme Court removed a barrier to suspended drug sentences on 1 June 2017. The review followed a cannabis case in which two men received four-year sentences, yet the offence's higher statutory maximum blocked them from seeking suspension under the drug law.[@1]

## A case involving cannabis material and seeds

Keyner Rafael Jiménez Jiménez and José Antonio Cañizalez Olivo admitted responsibility in a case involving 27.7 grams of marijuana material and seeds. They received four years in prison under the offence covering unlawful concealment of controlled seeds, which ordinarily carried six to ten years.[@1]

The distinction between the sentence imposed and the offence's possible maximum mattered. The general criminal-procedure rule allowed consideration of a suspended sentence when the actual sentence did not exceed five years. The drug law added another test: the offence's statutory maximum could not exceed six years.[@1]

That additional test excluded the men even though their four-year sentences met the general sentence-length condition.[@1]

## Earlier guidance favoured individual treatment

The court traced the dispute through its [2014 binding ruling](/history/venezuela-small-drug-cases-suspended-sentences-2014/), which allowed alternatives for lower-quantity drug cases. That guidance included smaller trafficking cases and the lesser bands for seeds, resin and plants.[@1]

It also relied on the principle that a case involving very small quantities should be assessed differently from one involving large quantities. The court discussed the social harm of the conduct and the need for punishment proportionate to that harm.[@1]

The drug law's extra maximum-sentence condition remained an obstacle to applying those principles in individual cases.[@1]

## The court annulled the extra barrier

The Constitutional Chamber held that the restriction undermined equality, rehabilitation and social reintegration. It annulled the six-year statutory-maximum condition with prospective effect and ordered publication of the decision.[@1]

The decision removed one condition from the drug law. Applicants still had to satisfy the general requirements for suspension, including an actual sentence of no more than five years, as well as the remaining applicable conditions.[@1]

## Suspension meant supervision

People granted suspension came under a probation delegate's supervision. The delegate monitored the conditions imposed by the court and reported whether they were being fulfilled. The judgment described this as part of the general suspended-sentence procedure.[@1]

For people with short actual sentences in lower-quantity cases, the removed condition had been decisive. Their cases could now be assessed under the remaining requirements. This distinction sits alongside the possession, growing and treatment rules in the [Venezuela profile](/country/ve/).[@1]
