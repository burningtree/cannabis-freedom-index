Kosovo Police’s 2025 threat assessment described a cannabis market supplied by imports alongside growing inside the country.[@1] The report identified marijuana as the most widespread drug across urban centres and rural areas, linking the market to neighbouring Albania and to criminal networks operating across borders.[@1] Its evidence combined recorded cases, seizures and police intelligence.[@1]

## Growing was becoming more organised

The assessment described outdoor plantations in isolated areas, especially around Peja and Gjakova, and indoor cultivation in northern Kosovo.[@1] Some indoor sites used ventilation, lighting, heating and humidity equipment to control growing conditions.[@1]

Across 2021–2024 police discovered sixteen cultivation laboratories, eight of them in northern Kosovo, and arrested thirty-three people in connection with those cases.[@1] The report also described criminal groups hiring experienced workers from Albania for cultivation and processing.[@1] These details put local production alongside the established cross-border supply routes.[@1]

## Police cases covered users and suppliers

For 2024 the marijuana table recorded 443 trafficking and possession cases and 584 arrested suspects, while the seizure table recorded 1,279.8 kilograms.[@1] The combined case category covered different conduct: the [2019 criminal code](/history/kosovo-criminal-code-2019/) retained a fine or possible imprisonment for first-time personal possession below three grams, while distribution and sale carried a fine and two to twelve years.[@3]

Growing cannabis for drug production carried one to ten years, with confiscation of plants and equipment.[@3] Specified aggravating circumstances, including group involvement and large quantities, could bring a fine and three to fifteen years.[@3]

## Treatment and reporting developed alongside policing

The European Commission’s 2025 assessment described progress implementing the national drug strategy and action plan adopted in 2024.[@2] It reported an [early-warning system established in November 2024](/history/kosovo-drug-early-warning-2024/) and a National Drugs Observatory that had begun operating that year.[@2]

The Commission also identified treatment-data gaps caused by the lack of a functioning health information system.[@2] Police had [destroyed seized drugs in March 2025](/history/kosovo-seized-drugs-destroyed-2025/) under a prosecutor’s guideline, while a formal national destruction protocol remained outstanding.[@2] The [Kosovo profile](/country/xk/) brings those market and enforcement findings together with the separate possession, growing, supply and scientific-research rules.[@1,3]
