Police caught Alice Magagula uprooting plants in a dagga field as big as two
football pitches; officers counted 9,705 plants, and there may have been
more.[@1]

The magistrate gave her four years in prison.[@1] On appeal the High Court
applied the Phiri sentencing bands: as a cultivating partner expecting a share
of the proceeds she faced serious punishment, but only one day's proved
participation cut the term to two years, with one year suspended.[@1]

She had said her poverty-stricken sister-in-law asked her to help transplant
the plants; she knew it was illegal and expected a reward when the dagga
sold.[@1]
