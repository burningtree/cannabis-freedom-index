The 2018 health law promised medical cannabis under conditions the minister would set; the
order of 27 February 2021 set them. Growing and producing narcotics for medical and
scientific purposes needed a five-year authorisation, reserved to Congolese organisations
operating at industrial scale.[@1]

The order drew a hard line around the plant: medical varieties had to contain no more than
0.2% THC with high CBD content, and every non-medical purpose stayed prohibited.[@1] The
shape proved durable — the revised order of 24 November 2022 kept the industrial-scale
authorisations, the THC ceiling and the ban on prescribing or dispensing narcotics in
kind.[@2]

What the orders built was a licensed production framework with no patients at the end of
it. Authorisations run to exclusively industrial organisations, imports and exports of
semi-finished and finished products need authorisation while raw cannabis exports are
prohibited, and no operating patient supply was verified.[@2]
