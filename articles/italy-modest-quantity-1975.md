Italy's Law 685 created an express exception from punishment for specified
personal-use possession. Dated 22 December 1975, it entered into force on
publication on 30 December. Its final article expressly tied commencement to
publication, while repealing most of the
[1954 narcotics law](/history/italy-law-1041-1954/).

The change mattered because the earlier law had placed unauthorised possession
within the same three-to-eight-year penalty range as several supply offences.
The new exception changed the consequences for consumers without removing
cannabis from the controlled system.

## What Article 80 exempted

Article 80 distinguished therapeutic and non-therapeutic personal use. Its first
paragraph exempted illicit purchase or possession for personal therapeutic use
where the quantity did not appreciably exceed treatment needs in the person's
particular circumstances.

The second exempted illicit purchase or possession of modest quantities for
non-therapeutic personal use. It also referred to substances previously held
and consumed exclusively by the holder. These were provisions declaring conduct
non-punishable, even as the article continued to describe the acquisition or
possession as illicit.

The non-therapeutic exception required seizure and confiscation of the
substances. Avoiding criminal punishment therefore did not give the person a
right to keep the drugs.

## A quantity without a gram limit

The statute supplied no fixed weight defining a modest quantity. Article 98
instead provided for a judicial inquiry assisted by an expert with specific
competence. The assessment was to rely principally on the toxic properties of
the substances in relation to the holder's physiological and psychological
characteristics.

Where the judge found the conditions for non-punishability, proceedings were
not to continue. Otherwise, the papers went to the competent prosecutor.
The exception thus required an assessment rather than operating as a simple
published gram allowance.

## Sharing and growing remained controlled

Article 12 directed cannabis and its products into Table II, while treating
tetrahydrocannabinols separately in Table I. Article 72 retained penalties for
specified dealings in modest quantities intended for other people's
non-therapeutic use, including free transfers. Small-scale sharing did not
automatically receive the personal-use protection.

Article 26 also prohibited cultivation of Indian hemp, subject to limited
authorisations for universities and public research laboratories. A possession
exception could not simply be carried across to growing one's own supply.

## Treatment alongside the exception

Article 95 recognised voluntary treatment and requested anonymity. Other
provisions imposed reporting duties and allowed judicial intervention,
including hospital treatment under specified conditions. Article 101 provided
for assistance by a defender and technical advisers in the relevant proceedings.

For [Italy's history](/country/it/), the reform combined relief from criminal
punishment with continuing confiscation, supply controls and treatment powers.
Its statutory protections do not, by themselves, establish how consistently
consumers received them in practice.
