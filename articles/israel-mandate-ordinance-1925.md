On 15 July 1925 the British Mandate government in Jerusalem enacted the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance. It made hashish and preparations of Cannabis Indica "dangerous drugs" and banned their manufacture, import, export and possession. A person convicted could receive a year in prison, a fine of £E 200, or both. The ordinance replaced the military government's [1919 notice](/history/israel-military-hashish-ban-1919/), which had banned cultivating and possessing hashish.[@1]

## A corridor between Lebanon and Egypt

The timing was international. The Geneva opium convention, signed on 19 February 1925, brought "Indian hemp" and its resin into the League of Nations' drug-control system for the first time. It required parties to stop exporting resin to countries that had banned it and to require import and export certificates for the international trade in the plant. Palestine lay between the Lebanese and Syrian growing areas and the Egyptian market, the route the new controls were meant to police.[@2] The mandate's schedule used the older name Cannabis Indica rather than the treaty's "Indian hemp".[@1]

## What the schedule covered

The ordinance's first schedule listed "Hashish or any preparation of Hashish" and "any preparation of Cannabis Indica similar in character to Hashish" beside prepared opium. The second held raw and medicinal opium, morphine, cocaine, heroin, and tinctures and extracts of Cannabis Indica. Importing or exporting any scheduled drug, and making or holding a first-schedule drug, carried the same maximum. Licensed pharmacists and doctors could hold the second-schedule drugs; the first schedule had no such exception. Magistrates could issue search warrants, and section 12 repealed the 1919 notice.[@1]

The new law concentrated on trade and possession. It created no cultivation offence: the 1919 notice had dealt with cultivation, and the ordinance did not re-enact that clause. The [1936 ordinance](/history/israel-dangerous-drugs-1936/), which replaced it, is the version that became Israel's drug law.[@3]

## A dead letter

The ordinance did not stop the traffic. Haggai Ram describes Palestine as the most important hashish waystation in the interwar Levant, with supplies moving from Lebanon and Syria to Egypt and consumption rising among urban working-class Arabs. The British administration had neither the money nor the will to enforce the law, and smugglers used the new borders as cover.[@3] The 1936 ordinance raised penalties and added the café and pipe offences that made the trade more visible to police.[@3]

## What survived

In 1948 the new state [kept](/history/israel-law-administration-1948/) the Mandate's drug law, and in 1973 the Knesset reissued it as the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance [New Version]. That version still supplies the twenty-year maxima for cultivation and supply, and its Second Schedule still draws the 15 g line between personal use and the presumption of dealing.[@4] The 2022 administrative fines changed how small possession is handled, not the architecture. The [country profile](/country/il/) sets out the fines, the criminal bands and the medical programme.
