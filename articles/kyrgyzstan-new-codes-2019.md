Kyrgyzstan’s justice reform brought small drug possession and small unauthorised cultivation under a new misdemeanour code on 1 January 2019. The code provided fines and probation-style restrictions on liberty. For adults, the basic restriction category lasted three to six months and could shape travel, reporting and daily life.[@1]

## Harm-reduction workers warned about debt

Before the reform took effect, Ranar Foundation lawyer Denis Kucheryaviy described a man identified by the pseudonym Aybek. A case involving 3.5 grams of hashish had led to three years in prison, and after release he still faced an unpaid fine and difficulties obtaining identity documents, according to the lawyer.[@2]

Sergey Bessonov, head of the Harm Reduction Network, feared that larger fines would deepen problems for people already unable to pay. He called for alternatives to punishment and effective treatment. AFEW published their concerns in February 2018 while the new legislation was still awaiting commencement.[@2]

## Small offences entered a separate penal code

The new code included small manufacture, acquisition, possession, transport or sending of drugs without intent to supply. A neighbouring rule covered sowing or growing prohibited drug-containing plants in small quantities. Both listed a category-two fine with a category-one restriction of liberty.[@1]

A finding under the misdemeanour code did not create a criminal record. It nevertheless involved a court-imposed punishment, and restriction of liberty meant supervision at the person’s home rather than separation from society.[@1]

## Probation rules reached into everyday decisions

An adult in the basic restriction category had to report to probation, obtain permission for travel abroad and keep officers informed about residence, work and study. The rules also required permission for temporary absence from the place of residence and prohibited alcohol and drug use.[@1]

These duties help explain the practical weight of the reform beyond the fine itself. The legal response to a small amount could affect a person’s movements and obligations for months, while harm-reduction workers worried about the financial burden.[@1,2]

## Replacement codes changed the response again

The misdemeanour code was later replaced as new criminal and offences codes took effect on 1 December 2021.[@3] The [replacement rules](/history/kyrgyzstan-small-drug-offences-2021/) treated small possession and small unauthorised cultivation as administrative offences, with fines and, for possession, community work as an alternative.[@4]

The [country profile](/country/kg/) follows those later rules. The 2019 episode shows why a reform described as humane could still draw opposition from people working directly with drug users.[@2,4]
