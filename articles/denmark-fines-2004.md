For more than thirty years a person caught in Denmark with a little cannabis for their own use [was normally let off with a warning](/history/denmark-warnings-1969/). That ended in the early 2000s. The Transnational Institute's country report on Denmark, on which this account rests, describes the turn as one "from tolerant to repressive".

## The politics

A right-wing government was elected in 2001, replacing a centre-left one led by the Social Democrats. The report lists what fed the change: concern about new kinds of drug use among the young, such as ecstasy; the growth of cannabis "hash clubs" at private addresses; and criticism of the practice of not penalising possession. The new government ran on a "tough on crime" agenda. Its 2003 action plan was called "The Fight Against Drugs".

## The rule

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| possession for own use, up to 10 g, not penalised | possession up to 9.9 g "punished, at the very least, with a fine" |
| police distinguished users from sellers | possession of any amount results in a fine, not a warning |

A further change in 2007 raised the fine by 50 per cent for a second charge and 100 per cent for a third.

## The exceptions, on paper and in practice

The 2007 change also allowed a warning in place of a fine for heavy drug users, for "social causes". Two other exceptions are mentioned: buyers who give evidence against a dealer, and tourists, who are asked to leave the country.

Research by Esben Houborg and Michael Mulbjerg Pedersen, cited in the report, found that the social exception is rarely used, and that police officers interviewed were not always aware of it. There are, the report says, multiple cases of fines being issued to people with a history of problem drug use and treatment.

## Christiania

The same action plan contained plans to close the cannabis market in the Freetown Christiania, which had mostly been tolerated until then. In 2004 the police raided Pusher Street, closed the stalls and arrested 60 dealers and 20 lookouts.

## Why the index moved

Possession falls from 3 to 2: a small amount is again punished, though with a fine. Enforcement falls from 5 to 4, because the police now issue that fine as a rule where they used to warn. The other scores were already at their floor for a country without lawful supply. The total moves from 22 to 18.
