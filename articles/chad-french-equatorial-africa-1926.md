On 29 August 1926 France banned growing hemp across French Equatorial Africa and ordered its use as a narcotic repressed, in a decree published in the Paris Journal officiel.[@1] For Chad, then part of the federation, it was the first cannabis ban on record.[@2,1]

## Laws that missed

Earlier rules had barely bitten.[@1] French colonial drug laws of 1916 and 1918 left magistrates unsure whether the haschisch named in the legal text was the same thing as the local diamba, so prosecutions stalled on a word.[@1] A 1918 decree closed a related loophole by making clear the controls applied even to colonised subjects.[@1]

The governors were keener than the statutes. Back in 1907 the governor of French Congo ordered his administration to stamp out the still fully legal plant, calling diamba selling criminal profiteering from a public poisoning that grew worse every day.[@1] One observer in Gabon claimed whole villages had been emptied by the drug.[@1]

## The 1926 decree

The decree's title said what it did: prohibit hemp cultivation and repress its employment as a narcotic in French Equatorial Africa.[@1] It covered the whole federation — Chad, Ubangi-Shari, Middle Congo and Gabon — and reached the Journal officiel as a report and decree pair, the form colonial legislation took from Paris, at page 10017.[@1,2]

The wording finally matched the governors' ambitions. Where the 1916 and 1918 texts had tangled over haschisch, the 1926 decree named the crop in the ground and the use to be repressed.[@1]

## What came next

The decree stayed Chad's recorded starting point: the index still traces the country's cannabis prohibition to August 1926, with no earlier ban and no later repeal on record.[@2] The same decree anchors the histories of the Central African Republic, Congo and Gabon, which inherited it from the same federation.[@2] It remains the base layer under [Chad's](/country/td/) modern cannabis scores. More than seventy years later the plant was still beyond the state's reach in places: a 2000 study reported several hectares of cannabis on the islands of Lake Chad that the local police had no means to go and destroy.[@3]
