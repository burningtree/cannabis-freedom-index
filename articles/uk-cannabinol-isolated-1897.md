British researchers gave cannabinol its name in 1897 while trying to solve a
problem that frustrated doctors: cannabis medicines varied greatly in strength.
The substance they isolated was real, but their belief that it was the plant's
active intoxicating principle did not survive.

## Searching for a standard medicine

Nineteenth-century physicians had repeatedly complained that one cannabis
preparation could behave differently from another. Some stopped using the drug
as unreliable or dangerous; others wanted a standard preparation or an
isolated active ingredient.

Chemists had pursued that target for decades. Earlier extracts and proposed
constituents either remained mixtures or failed to reproduce cannabis's
characteristic effects. The work of Wood, Spivey and Easterfield appeared to
offer a way through.

## Cannabinol gets its name

Working with material from India, the three researchers obtained the same red
oil or resin from several starting materials, including cannabis resin, charas
and an earlier extract called cannabin. They named the substance cannabinol and
treated it as the active ingredient they had been seeking.

They sent a sample to C. R. Marshall at Cambridge for pharmacological testing.
Marshall tested cannabinol on himself as well as considering its possible
medical use. He described intoxication, laughter, altered judgement and later
visual hallucinations. A person with insomnia who received it slept through
the night, although hallucinations came first.

## A provisional conclusion

Marshall was cautious about claiming a useful medicine without more
experience. W. E. Dixon, working with cannabis and cannabinol in London, was
more enthusiastic about cannabis as a therapeutic agent while still warning
that preparations varied in strength.

The chemical conclusion proved premature. In 1898 Wood and Spivey rejected
their own identification of cannabinol as cannabis's active principle. Marshall
continued researching it and later used the original sample when studying the
poor tests then available for measuring cannabis potency.

Cannabinol remained an identified cannabis constituent, but the principal
intoxicant now known as THC was not isolated and characterised until
[1964](/history/israel-mechoulam-thc-1964/).
