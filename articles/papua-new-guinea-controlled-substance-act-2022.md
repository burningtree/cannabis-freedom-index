On 31 March 2022 Papua New Guinea's Controlled Substance Act commenced alongside the
Dangerous Drugs Act, which remained in force. Cannabis, its resin, its oil and
cannabis plants went into Schedule I — described as having no medical value — and a
new set of quantity tiers in Schedule VII set the punishment.[@1,2]

## The tiers

Ten grams or less can bring up to two years in prison, a K15,000 fine, or both; more
than 10 g but less than 2 kg up to fifteen years, K25,000, or both; and more than
2 kg from sixteen years to life, K26,000 to K2 million, or both.[@1] Cultivation is
graded by plant count, with seedlings, cuttings and saplings counted together: fewer
than five plants up to two years, more than five up to fifteen, more than twenty from
sixteen years to life.[@1]

## Two charging routes

The new regime did not replace the old one cleanly. Weeks before commencement, a January 2022 amendment
had rewritten Dangerous Drugs Act section 3 with a maximum of forty years and/or
K1 million for knowing unauthorised possession, conveyance or cultivation — and
prosecutors kept using it for cannabis.[@3] A June 2024 conviction for 1.3 kg of
marijuana ran under section 3(1)(d): K15,000, with three years in default, the
prosecutor invoking the forty-year maximum.[@4] The Controlled Substance Act says it
prevails where criminal laws conflict, but which charge a defendant faces still needs
legal assessment.[@1]

Sentences since run from eighteen months for nine packets to twenty-five years for
14–20 kg — with the tiers a floor for argument, not a ceiling on what the older
forty-year route allows.

[Papua New Guinea's profile](/country/pg/) reflects the two-track system the 2022 Act
created: [the new regime](/history/papua-new-guinea-controlled-substance-act-2022/)
and the old maximum that survived it.
