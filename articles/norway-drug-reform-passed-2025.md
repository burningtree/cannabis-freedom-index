Norway's drug reform arrived in instalments, and the last one has not switched on yet. In June
2025 the Storting adopted the Støre government's bill — the third act after the 2021 defeat and the
2022 court rulings: use and possession of small amounts stay
forbidden, but the fine will no longer land on the criminal record, dependent users are to get
help instead of punishment, and under-18s caught with drugs for personal use must attend a
municipal advisory meeting rather than pay up.[@1] Police may still test for drug use, but only
where needed to clear up a case.[@1] The April proposal it grew from had kept small-amount use
punishable by fine or up to six months, while sparing adults with serious drug problems, sending
young people to a drug advisory unit, and giving police testing powers.[@2] As of August 2026 no commencement date had been set, so the
old rules still applied — though the new code was expected during 2026.[@1]

## The 2021 defeat that changed practice anyway

The bill's opponents had won the big vote four years earlier: a 2021 government proposal to
decriminalise personal possession was voted down in parliament.[@1] But the debate moved practice
even without a new law. In 2021 the Director of Public Prosecutions instructed police that
coercive measures — house searches, urine samples, seizing mobile phones — are not lawful in cases
about drug use alone, and the number of Norwegians punished for use fell sharply.[@1]

## The court that drew the line

Then came the judgments. In April 2022 the Supreme Court concluded that people dependent on drugs
should not risk punishment for keeping small amounts for personal use, noting that the
parliamentary parties agreed on this even though they had failed to legislate it.[@1] The
Attorney General converted the rulings into police guidelines the next month.[@1] The statute book
now distinguishes between users who are punished and dependent users who are not — though the
criteria stay blurry.[@1]

## What is still moving

The reform keeps generating paperwork. In June 2026 the Justice Ministry proposed a simplified
on-the-spot ticket for minor possession, with no coercive measures and nothing on the later
police certificate.[@1] A month later consumption rooms opened to smoking and sniffing as well as
injecting, and the youth advisory units lost their power to demand drug tests.[@1] Step by step,
[Norway](/country/no/) is replacing the criminal record with the appointment slip.
