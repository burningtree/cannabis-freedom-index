Uruguay's rule that holding a drug for one's own use is not punished comes from Decree-Law 14.294, promulgated on 31 October 1974 and published on 11 November. Article 31 lists the trafficking offences and then takes the user out of them.

## The exemption

Article 31 punishes anyone who imports, distributes, transports, stores or sells the listed substances, or who holds them "not for his own consumption". The words that matter are the last ones. In the wording the article has carried since Law 17.016 of 1998, it adds that a person "shall be exempt from punishment" who holds "a reasonable quantity intended exclusively for his personal consumption, according to the moral conviction that the judge forms". The judge must give reasons. The original 1974 text could not be opened for this article; the Transnational Institute describes the 1974 article in the same terms.

No quantity was fixed. The judge decided, case by case, what counted as an amount for personal use.

## What stayed criminal

| Article | Conduct | Penalty today |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | producing a drug without authorisation | 20 months to 10 years |
| 31 | importing, distributing, transporting, selling, holding not for consumption | 2 to 12 years |
| 34 | supplying or inducing others to use | 2 to 10 years |

Growing was production. TNI points to the result: a person who grew a small amount for their own use committed a crime, while a person who bought the same amount did not.

## The user before the judge

Article 40 dealt with the person found using, or carrying a drug for personal use. They were brought before the duty court, examined by two doctors within 24 hours, and, if found to be an addict, ordered into treatment in a public or private institution or as an outpatient.

## How it worked

The open wording cut both ways. A 2010 study cited by TNI found that people arrested with under 10 grams of cannabis made up 44 per cent of drug prosecutions, and that 11 per cent of prisoners were held for drug offences. It also cites a 66-year-old jailed for three months for growing cannabis for medical reasons. Law [19.172 of 2013](/history/uruguay-legalisation-2013/) changed both points: it set 40 grams as the personal amount and allowed six plants at home.

## Why the index moved

Possession rises from 1 to 5 and consumption from 1 to 6: holding a drug for one's own use carried no penalty, though the amount was left to a judge. Enforcement rises from 2 to 5. Cultivation and sharing stay at 1, since both remained serious crimes, and there was no lawful way to obtain cannabis. The total moves from 11 to 32.
