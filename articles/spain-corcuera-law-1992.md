Spain has not punished private drug use as a crime since 1983. On 21 February 1992 it drew a line around that freedom. Organic Law 1/1992 on the Protection of Citizens' Security, known by the name of the interior minister who sponsored it, José Luis Corcuera, made using or holding drugs in public an administrative offence. It took effect on 13 March.

## Article 25

The drug provision is a single article.

- Consuming drugs in public places, streets, establishments or public transport is a serious infraction.
- So is the unlawful possession of drugs, "even if not intended for trafficking", where no crime is involved.
- Leaving the instruments of consumption behind in such places is covered as well.
- The sanction may be suspended if the offender completes a detoxification treatment at an accredited centre.

A second provision made it a serious infraction for the owner or manager of a public establishment to tolerate drug use or dealing on the premises.

## The fine

| Class of infraction | Fine in pesetas |
|---|---|
| Minor | up to 50,000 |
| Serious, which includes Article 25 | 50,001 to 5,000,000 |
| Very serious | 5,000,001 to 100,000,000 |

For an Article 25 infraction the authorities could also suspend a driving licence for up to three months.

## The part that fell

The law was nicknamed the "kick-down-the-door law" for Article 21.2, which let police enter a home without a warrant in cases of flagrant drug offences. The Constitutional Court struck that paragraph down in judgment 341/1993 of 18 November 1993. Article 25 survived and stayed in force until a new public safety law replaced it on [1 July 2015](/history/spain-public-safety-law-2015/).

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Spain](/country/es/) from 34 to 30 with the [1992 law](/history/spain-corcuera-law-1992/). Possession and consumption each fall from 4 to 3: what a person does at home stayed outside the law, but carrying or smoking cannabis in the street now meant a fine of at least 50,001 pesetas. Enforcement is unchanged, because the penalty is administrative and could be avoided through treatment.
