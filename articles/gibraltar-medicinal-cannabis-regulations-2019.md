Gibraltar's 2019 regulations created a limited route for cannabis-based medicines
through its public health service. The legal change took effect on 16 October,
when the regulations were published in the gazette. The government's explanatory
announcement followed on 22 October.

That distinction matters in a timeline: the announcement describes the policy,
but the original instrument supplies its commencement date.

## An earlier route for a mouth spray

The [February 2017 amendment](/history/gibraltar-sativex-regulations-2017/)
had already made a narrower change. It placed a precisely specified cannabis
extract, delivered as a metered mucosal mouth spray, in Schedule 2 of the drug
regulations. The explanatory memorandum identified Sativex and described access
under the framework governing medicines.

This was a product-specific exception. It should not be read as an opening
for all cannabis products or as permission to grow cannabis at home.

## Access through trained public doctors

The 2019 scheme added a separate category for approved cannabis-based products
for medicinal use. Practitioners had to be registered doctors employed by the
Gibraltar Health Authority who had completed the relevant training. Products
required GHA approval after consultation with those practitioners.

The government explained that public dispensing would be confined to the GHA
pharmacy at St. Bernard's Hospital. Existing products obtainable through local
pharmacies would retain their availability.

## Four groups of conditions

The regulations listed treatment-resistant multiple-sclerosis spasticity,
refractory epilepsy, severe life-altering pain and intractable chemotherapy-related
nausea and vomiting, each with qualifications concerning unsuccessful standard
treatment. Patient possession depended on diagnosis and supply by, or under the
direction of, an authorised practitioner.

The instrument also expressly prohibited administering the products by smoking.
The permission was tied to medical treatment and specified products, rather than
a general personal-use exemption.

## Legislation and implementation followed different paths

The [Medicinal Cannabis Act 2021](/history/gibraltar-medicinal-cannabis-act-2021/)
received assent on 24 September. It set out a broader licensing architecture
covering production, import, export, marketing and supply. Its commencement,
however, depended on ministerial notice. The official text remained marked
“Not In Force” when checked on 20 October 2026.

A separate [September 2023 announcement](/history/gibraltar-medicinal-cannabis-supply-agreement-2023/)
reported completed doctor training and an agreement with Cantourage UK, subject
to final detail, to enable prescribing for eligible patients. It did not identify
a first dispensing date or patient totals.

Together, these records give [Gibraltar's history](/country/gi/) several distinct
milestones: a narrow medicine exception, a wider patient framework, an uncommenced
industry statute and a later supply arrangement. Treating each as the same
moment would obscure what patients and prospective producers could actually do.
