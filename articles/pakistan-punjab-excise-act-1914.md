The Punjab Excise Act of 1914 put cannabis under the same licensing system as liquor: bhang, charas and ganja could be made, moved and sold only through licensed vendors.[@1] Baluchistan followed with its own excise regulation in 1915.[@1,2] It was the subcontinent's first cannabis regime built on licences rather than prohibition, and Pakistan kept that licensed trade after independence.

## One licence system for drink and drugs

The Punjab Act consolidated the law on importing, transporting, making, selling and possessing intoxicating liquor and drugs, cannabis among them, so that they could be bought only from licensed vendors.[@1] The point was control and revenue, not a ban: the plant stayed on sale, but every step from manufacture to the shop counter needed the excise department's permission.

## What counted as an intoxicating drug

The follow-up Baluchistan regulation shows the vocabulary the system used. An intoxicating drug meant the fruiting tops of the Indian hemp plant known as bhang, siddhi or ganja; charas, the resin from the plant; mixtures of those; and anything else the authorities declared to be one.[@2]

The regulation then fenced that trade with licences: manufacture and sale of excisable articles each needed one, growing a hemp plant without authority was an offence, and licensees worked under measurement, testing and inspection duties with penalties for breaches.[@2]

## Licensed shops outlive the empire

The licensed trade survived independence. [The 1931 Dangerous Drugs Act](/history/pakistan-dangerous-drugs-act-1931/) rewrote the Punjab law so that bhang, siddhi, ganja and charas stayed intoxicating drugs under it while putting hemp extract and tincture under licence. [West Punjab's 1949 amendment](/history/pakistan-west-punjab-excise-amendment-1949/) punished consuming ganja or charas in breach of excise law, while licensed sale of charas and bhang continued. Only [the 1979 prohibition](/history/pakistan-prohibition-1979/) closed the shops. Pakistan's current law is on [its profile](/country/pk/).
