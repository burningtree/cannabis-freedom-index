São Tomé and Príncipe published a new Penal Code on 6 August 2012. Its final provision delayed
commencement for three months, putting the code into force on 6 November. The drug chapter kept
cannabis illegal but created a more graduated response to personal conduct than the Portuguese
law it replaced.

## A three-day threshold

Article 283 grouped acquiring, holding, consuming and cultivating drugs for personal use. When
the amount did not exceed what one person would need for three days, the maximum was three months
in prison or a fine of up to 60 days. Above that quantity, the maximum became one year or a fine
of up to 100 days.

The code did not define a fixed number of grams in the opened provisions. Describing the rule as
a three-day quantity is therefore more faithful than inventing a numerical possession limit.

## Courts gained limited discretion

An occasional consumer whose case fell within the basic three-day provision could be excused
from punishment.

Someone who voluntarily sought treatment received a statutory guarantee of anonymity. Health
professionals were bound by secrecy and generally did not have to disclose treatment information
to police or courts. These safeguards did not legalise cannabis use, but they distinguished a
treatment request from ordinary criminal enforcement.

## Supply remained trafficking

The softer personal-use provision did not extend to sharing. Article 278 treated offering,
selling, distributing or giving a listed drug to another person as trafficking, normally
punishable by two to twelve years. A substantially less serious case carried one to five years.
Cannabis flower, resin and oil appeared in schedule I-C.

The present [São Tomé and Príncipe profile](/country/st/) follows the code as consolidated through
its 2021 amendment. No later change found in that text removes the three-day framework.

In the index, the 2012 code moves possession from 0 to 1 because a small personal amount now has
a fine as an alternative, a three-month ceiling, and possible exemption for an occasional user.
The other five segment scores do not move: cultivation and consumption remain offences, prison
remains possible, sharing is trafficking, and no recreational access exists.
