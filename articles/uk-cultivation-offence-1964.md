The Dangerous Drugs Act 1964 received royal assent on 10 June 1964. It is short, and its long title names cannabis twice: it was passed "to create certain offences in connection with the drug known as cannabis and to penalize the intentional cultivation of any plant of the genus cannabis". Possession, import and supply had been controlled for decades. The plant in the ground had not.

## Two new offences

| Section | Who commits it | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | The occupier of premises, or a person concerned in managing them | Permitting the premises to be used for smoking cannabis or cannabis resin, or for dealing in them "whether by sale or otherwise" |
| 10 | Any person | Knowingly cultivating any plant of the genus cannabis, except under a licence from the Secretary of State |

Both are made offences "against the principal Act", the Dangerous Drugs Act 1951, so they borrow its penalties and its powers of entry and search. The 1964 Act itself states no prison term for them.

## Written for the Single Convention

The rest of the Act rearranges the 1951 law around the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, signed in New York on 30 March 1961. A new schedule replaces the old list of controlled drugs, and the older treaties named in the 1951 Act are to be read as references to the new one.

The definitions come from the Convention as well. "Cannabis" means "the flowering or fruiting tops of any plant of the genus cannabis from which the resin has not been extracted", and cannabis resin means the separated resin, crude or purified. Extracts and tinctures of cannabis are listed separately in the schedule. The cultivation offence is wider than the definition: it covers any plant of the genus, whether or not it has flowered.

## A licence, in principle

Section 10 leaves one door open. Growing is lawful under a licence from the Secretary of State, or in Northern Ireland from the Ministry of Home Affairs, on whatever conditions the issuing authority thinks proper. Breaking a condition is itself an offence.

## Why the index moved

The index lowers cultivation from 3 to 1 and the total from 20 to 15. Until June 1964 a person in Britain who grew the plant committed no offence by growing it; afterwards knowingly growing a single plant without a licence was a crime. The other scores were already low and stay where they were. Enforcement drops three years later, when police gained the power to stop and search for drugs.
