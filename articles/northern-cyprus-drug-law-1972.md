The drug law numbered 4/1972 brought together controls on cannabis imports, distribution, premises and cultivation in the legal framework now applied in Northern Cyprus. Its closing provisions replaced the earlier Dangerous Drugs Law, Chapter 248, and a 1961 amendment, while preserving existing permissions. The 1972 date identifies this replacement law, rather than the beginning of cannabis prohibition on the island.[@1]

## Cannabis had its own place in the law

The law’s first substantive part covers cannabis resin and preparations containing it. Import and export require permission, while manufacture, possession, sale and distribution fall under controlled authorisation rules. A separate provision makes allowing premises to be used for cannabis smoking or trading an offence.[@1]

Cultivation receives an unusually explicit provision. Growing a Cannabis sativa plant is an offence without the prosecution having to show that flowering or fruiting tops have developed. The wording therefore does not provide a small household crop exemption simply because the plants are young.[@1]

## A statutory ceiling is different from a sentence

Section 24 supplies the ordinary maximum: fifteen years in prison, a fine, or both. Higher eighteen- and twenty-year maxima concern drugs in Part I of the annex. That annex is a different list from the cannabis provisions in Part I of the law’s body.[@1]

A Supreme Court judgment in October 2015 reproduced the ordinary fine ceiling as fifty Turkish lira after redenomination. The judges also examined how drug type, quantity, the defendant’s role and surrounding circumstances affect sentencing. English sentencing guidance discussed in that judgment should not be read as the local cannabis penalty.[@2]

## Three plants could still bring imprisonment

In January 2019, a Nicosia court sentenced Arda Çelik to eighteen months for growing three plants from seeds brought from Amsterdam and importing three grams of cannabis from the south of the island. The report records the judge identifying fifteen years as the cultivation maximum. The eighteen-month sentence covered the offences together; it was not a statutory tariff for three plants.[@4]

## Treatment later offered a conditional exit

The [2016 supervised-release law](/history/northern-cyprus-drug-treatment-option-2016/) created a route for eligible people convicted of possession or use to undertake treatment before sentence. Successful completion avoids a sentence and a conviction entry. Growing, manufacturing, selling and trading convictions are excluded, leaving those activities within the ordinary criminal process.[@3]

The [Northern Cyprus profile](/country/xn/) distinguishes these criminal controls from the later treatment option. Their practical consequences depend on the offence and the defendant’s eligibility, alongside the court’s assessment of the individual case.[@1,2,3]
