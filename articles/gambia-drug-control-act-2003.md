Gambia brought a new drug-control law into force on 3 October 2003, replacing the [1996 decree](/history/gambia-drug-control-decree-1996/). Cannabis fell within its prohibited-drug system, covering unauthorised growing, supply, use and possession. The law became the basis for later changes to enforcement and punishment.

## Cannabis sat within the prohibited-drug rules

The law distinguished prohibited drugs from other controlled drugs. Cannabis and cannabis resin appeared in the prohibited list, and the definition of cannabis also covered extracts and tinctures.

That classification affected what people could do with the plant. The prohibited-drug rules covered cultivation, transport, supply, acquisition, use and possession, alongside manufacture and international trade.

The minister could permit strictly limited medical or scientific use by specially authorised people working in medical or scientific establishments. That placed those exceptions under official control.

## Possession could carry a substantial fine or prison

The published law, incorporating the 2005 amendments, set a minimum fine of 250,000 dalasi or at least three years in prison for unauthorised possession of drugs in the first two schedules, including cannabis. Courts could impose both punishments.

It also made the occupants of shared premises responsible for explaining drugs found there. The law treated the drugs as jointly possessed unless the occupants proved that they were present without their knowledge or consent.

That rule could affect people sharing a home or other premises, as well as the person who physically held the cannabis.

## Quantity could lead to a trafficking charge

The published law set a further threshold for cannabis holdings. More than ten grams of resin or two kilograms of cannabis triggered a presumption of possession for trafficking, with the burden on the accused to prove otherwise.

The general trafficking punishment combined a minimum fine of one million dalasi with at least ten years in prison. A [2011 amendment](/history/gambia-death-penalty-abolished-2011/) later raised that fine tenfold.

## Later reforms separated some first offenders

The new law also set out how cases under the repealed decree should continue. Part-heard cases would finish under the old rules, while other earlier offences could be charged under corresponding sections of the replacement law.

A [2005 amendment created an autonomous drug agency](/history/gambia-autonomous-drug-agency-2005/). In [2014, first-time cannabis possession gained its own penalty scale](/history/gambia-first-offenders-2014/), with lower fines for smaller holdings and prison remaining possible. Those changes shaped the later operation of the drug law in [Gambia](/country/gm/).
