In 1895 the French protectorate of Tunisia banned imports of kif, hashish and shira — and its shira came from Malta.[@1] The island better known for hemp rope was, for a time, a hashish entrepôt.[@1]

## A ban with a hole in it

The 1895 ban covered kif, hashish and shira but not takruri.[@1] Shira was a name for cannabis resin also recorded in what is now Syria; Tunisia had already banned its import once before, in 1884.[@1]

The French colonial sources geographer Chris Duvall cites say Tunisian imports came from Malta — even though shira was being produced closer by, around Constantine in Algeria, by 1921.[@1] How the resin reached Malta, and who carried it across a few hundred miles of sea, the note does not say.

## A rope island in the resin trade

Malta's hemp past was maritime: qanneb, the Maltese word from Arabic qinnab, means hemp.[@2] The island's hemp vocabulary is traced in [qanneb](/culture/language/qanneb/). An island that rigged ships with hemp was well placed to move resin between shores.[@1]

## From entrepôt to legaliser

The 1895 note is a single customs fact, not a Maltese cultivation story: nothing in it says the resin was grown on the island.[@1] But it anchors Malta in the region's cannabis history a century before prohibition arrived in force with the [1939 dangerous-drugs law](/history/malta-dangerous-drugs-1939/) — and more than a century before Malta became, in [2021](/history/malta-legalisation-2021/), the first EU country to legalise. The island went from supplying a banned market abroad to regulating its own at home.
