Transnistria’s replacement administrative code in 2014 put several drug offences on a different track from a criminal prosecution. Small possession could still bring up to thirty days in custody, while use without a prescription had its own penalty of up to fifteen days. Growing below the criminal threshold could bring a warning or fine.[@1]

## An administrative case could still mean custody

The code replaced the administrative rules introduced in 2002. It set out offences, penalties and procedures together, including a rule that the new code would begin three months after official publication.[@1]

For possession, the dividing line was whether the conduct met the separate criminal offence. Buying, keeping, transporting, making or processing drugs without intent to supply, when it fell outside that offence, could bring a fine of 50–300 statutory calculation units or administrative arrest for up to thirty days.[@1]

Administrative arrest means detention imposed as the punishment itself. Courts could choose custody instead of a fine for the same offence, so a small-possession case could involve actual loss of liberty.[@1]

## Holding, using and growing had separate rules

The criminal code treated possession of a legally defined large amount more severely: its ordinary tier allowed a fine, community work, corrective labour or up to three years in prison. An especially large amount carried three to ten years.[@2]

The administrative code also punished using drugs without a doctor’s prescription. That offence carried a fine of 50–150 calculation units or up to fifteen days’ arrest. A person who voluntarily sought medical help for that use could be exempt from administrative liability under the article.[@1]

Growing had another dividing line. The criminal cultivation offence required a large scale, while the administrative code allowed a warning or a fine for cultivation that did not meet the criminal offence. It also required landholders to destroy wild cannabis after an official order, with penalties for failing to act.[@1,2]

## The rules reached gardens and driving rights

A later [police report from Dubossary in 2018](/history/transnistria-medical-homegrow-police-2018/) described officers finding cannabis in a woman’s garden and dried cannabis held by her brother. They reported the cultivation and possession separately, showing how more than one drug allegation could arise from the same household.[@3]

In [2023, a new penalty reached beyond fines and detention](/history/transnistria-drug-use-driving-ban-2023/): specified drug-use offences also brought a one-year restriction on driving rights.[@4]

The [Transnistria profile](/country/xt/) records the territory’s separate legal system. These provisions make the administrative label consequential: it distinguishes the procedure and offence, while still allowing detention and other restrictions.[@1]
