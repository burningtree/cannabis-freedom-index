Türkiye sharply increased the punishment for cannabis users and traffickers when its criminal-law amendment took effect on 1 August 1953.[@1] Using drugs or keeping them for use now carried three to five years in prison and a fine.[@1] Manufacturing, importing or exporting cannabis without permission carried life imprisonment, with death prescribed in specified aggravated cases.[@1]

## Personal-use penalties rose from months to years

The [1941 rules](/history/turkey-drug-use-penalties-1941/) had punished drug use or possession with two to six months in prison and a fine.[@2] Repeat offending could add internal exile, requiring the person to live in a small town away from home, while people considered dependent could be confined in hospital for treatment.[@2]

The 1953 amendment replaced that short prison range with three to five years.[@1] It added a fine of 100 to 1,000 lira, and repeat offenders faced at least a year of internal exile outside drug-producing areas, under police supervision.[@1] Hospital confinement for people considered dependent continued until medical recovery was established.[@1]

## Cannabis joined the harshest trafficking tier

The law grouped cannabis with heroin, cocaine and morphine for its heaviest drug penalties.[@1] Unauthorised manufacture, import or export involving any of those drugs carried life imprisonment.[@1] Domestic sale, giving drugs away, transport and other listed supply activities involving cannabis carried at least ten years, alongside a fine and internal exile.[@1]

Capital punishment applied when the life-sentence offence involved specified aggravating circumstances, including an organisation formed to commit drug crimes or the use of minors.[@1] The law treated an agreement between two or more people to commit these crimes as an organisation.[@1] Separate rules also converted the life sentence to death for specified medical professionals and other aggravated circumstances.[@1]

## Later reforms removed different parts of the punishment

The [1965 sentencing law](/history/turkey-internal-exile-abolition-1965/) abolished internal-exile penalties throughout the criminal code and special laws.[@3] Drug imprisonment and capital punishment remained under the separate drug provisions.[@1,3]

In [November 1990](/history/turkey-drug-death-penalty-repeal-1990/), parliament replaced the drug-related death clauses with life imprisonment.[@4] A [June 1991 amendment](/history/turkey-personal-use-sentence-reduction-1991/) then reduced drug use and possession for use to one to two years, with protection from prosecution for people who sought treatment before an investigation began.[@5] The penalties introduced in 1953 were dismantled in stages across [Türkiye's later history](/country/tr/).[@3,4,5]
