A cannabis prohibition proposal in Natal failed to produce a rule in 1884. The episode is preserved in the Indian Immigrants Commission report of 1887, as quoted and discussed by historian Craig Paterson.

## A request without a rule

The Medical Officer of Pietermaritzburg Circle requested prohibition. The Executive Council divided over the issue and issued no rule. The available account does not establish a precise date, a complete draft or proposed penalties, so this event retains year-only dating.

The episode matters in interpreting the [1870 immigration legislation](/history/south-africa-natal-1870/). Section 70 supplied powers for restrictions targeting Indian immigrants. The existence of those powers is separate from evidence that officials exercised them.

## A continuing campaign

The [1887 commission](/history/natal-law-tightened-1887/) invoked the unsuccessful request when asking the Governor in Council to act. Its report therefore preserved evidence of disagreement within colonial government, as well as the campaign for prohibition.

This record is an unsuccessful regulatory initiative. It should not be counted as a new offence or a completed prohibition. Paterson’s thesis identifies the commission report’s page 6 as its source; the original 1884 correspondence and council minutes were not inspected. They remain the appropriate documents for establishing the proposal’s exact wording and the reasons for the council’s division.
