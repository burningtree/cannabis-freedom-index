Brazil's Supreme Federal Court ended criminal liability for possessing cannabis
for personal use in June 2024. The ruling did not legalise possession, but it
changed the consequences from criminal punishment to non-criminal measures and
gave police and courts a national quantity guideline.

## A case that lasted nine years

The court was reviewing article 28 of Brazil's 2006 Drug Law. The case began with
a man convicted after he was caught with three grams of cannabis in 2010, and the
constitutional proceedings started in 2015. Voting was interrupted several times
before a majority supported decriminalisation.

The court distinguished use from trafficking rather than creating a legal cannabis
market. Acquiring, keeping, transporting or carrying cannabis for personal use
remained prohibited, and the substance could still be seized. The available
responses became administrative: a warning about drugs or attendance at an
educational programme, rather than a criminal conviction.

## Forty grams or six plants

The court established a presumption of personal use for up to 40 grams of cannabis
or six female plants. That benchmark remains in place until Congress adopts a
different rule.

It is a presumption, not an automatic permission to possess. The purpose is to
help distinguish a user from a trafficker, while other evidence can still affect
how a case is classified. Nor did the ruling authorise selling or sharing.

The decision addressed cannabis specifically. It did not create an equivalent
quantity presumption for every drug covered by article 28.

An August 2024 Superior Court of Justice decision showed the precedent in
practice. That court set aside the conviction of a person who had received a
six-year-and-nine-month trafficking sentence after 23 grams were found at home.
It sent the matter to the special criminal court only to consider whether a
non-criminal sanction should apply.

That later case demonstrated why the classification rule mattered: a quantity
below the threshold had previously led to years of imprisonment for trafficking.

## In the index

The ruling raised [Brazil's](/country/br/) possession score from 2 to 3,
cultivation from 1 to 2, enforcement from 2 to 3, and consumption from 1 to 2.
Sharing stayed at 1 and access at 1: no legal supply system was created, and the
conduct remained unlawful even though personal use no longer brought criminal
liability.
