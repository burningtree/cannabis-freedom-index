A Solomon Islands parliamentary committee began examining the Dangerous Drugs
(Amendment) Bill 2025 on 20 February 2026. The bill would raise the general
assessor-court maximum in section 39 of the Dangerous Drugs Act, a ceiling that also
applies to existing cannabis offences. It creates no new cannabis offence.

## A higher general maximum

The bill would substitute section 39(2)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs Act. For offences
without a specific punishment, a court sitting with assessors could impose a fine
of up to 15 million penalty units, life imprisonment, or both.

That ceiling would cover very different kinds of offending. Committee member John
Dean Kuku questioned applying the same maximum across users, manufacturers and
traffickers. Director of Public Prosecutions Andrew Kelesi responded that the wide
range was intended to preserve judicial discretion, allowing courts to distinguish
between different categories of offender.

## The gap was not about cannabis

The government's urgency followed warnings about Part III of the Act, the schedule
for prepared opium and later synthetic drugs. Prime Minister Jeremiah Manele said
the law criminalised importing, exporting and transporting those scheduled drugs
but did not expressly criminalise their possession, supply, manufacturing or
production.

Director of Public Prosecutions Andrew Kelesi raised the problem after a foreign
national was allegedly found with methamphetamine. Cannabis was not the gap:
section 8 already expressly criminalises possessing, growing, selling and giving
Indian hemp. The bill does not add cannabis offences; it raises the general
penalty that a court sitting with assessors may impose on the existing ones.

Chief Magistrate Ricky Iomea told the committee that the amendment would make the
law more consistent for scheduled drugs. He said courts would consider the drug
and quantity, the person's role, the harm caused and how prevalent the offending
was when choosing a sentence.

## Still before Parliament

The Bills and Legislation Committee heard from both Iomea and Kelesi. The proposal
was expected to be finalised and tabled in Parliament, while the committee's
hearing was due to continue the following week. It remained before Parliament on
28 October 2026 and had not become law.
