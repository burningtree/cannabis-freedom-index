A Solomon Islands parliamentary committee began examining the Dangerous Drugs
(Amendment) Bill 2025 on 20 February 2026. The proposal would close gaps identified
in the existing Dangerous Drugs Act and sharply increase the maximum punishment
where that Act did not already specify one.

## A broad new maximum

The bill would amend section 39 of the Dangerous Drugs Act. For offences without a
specific punishment, a court could impose a fine of up to 15 million penalty
units, life imprisonment, or both.

That ceiling would cover very different kinds of offending. Committee member John
Dean Kuku questioned applying the same maximum across users, manufacturers and
traffickers. Director of Public Prosecutions Andrew Kelesi responded that the wide
range was intended to preserve judicial discretion, allowing courts to distinguish
between different categories of offender.

## The loopholes behind the bill

The government's urgency followed warnings about the wording of the existing law.
Prime Minister Jeremiah Manele had said it criminalised importing, exporting and
transporting dangerous drugs but did not expressly criminalise possession, supply,
manufacturing or production.

Kelesi had raised the problem publicly after a foreign national was allegedly
found with methamphetamine. The gap meant that possession or manufacture of that
drug was technically not illegal under the existing provisions described to the
committee. Cabinet then fast-tracked reform.

Chief Magistrate Ricky Iomea told the committee that the amendment would make the
law more consistent by expressly covering possession, supply, distribution and
manufacturing. He said courts would consider the drug and quantity, the person's
role, the harm caused and how prevalent the offending was when choosing a
sentence.

## Still before Parliament

The Bills and Legislation Committee heard from both Iomea and Kelesi. The proposal
was expected to be finalised and tabled in Parliament, while the committee's
hearing was due to continue the following week. It had therefore not become law
at the time of the inquiry.
