Jordan retained mandatory death for specified international drug-gang trafficking offences when its replacement drug law took effect on 15 August 2016. The law carried forward a capital-punishment rule already found in the earlier drug law and kept personal drug use under a separate offence.

## The earlier law already punished international-gang trafficking with death

The [law replaced in 2016](/history/jordan-drug-law-1988/) contained an international-gang death clause in its amended text. It applied that punishment to aggravated trafficking involving international drug gangs and certain connections with other international crimes.

The replacement retained this structure. For someone accused of trafficking cannabis, the alleged purpose and international criminal connections could determine whether the case exposed them to a long labour sentence, life imprisonment or death.

## Trafficking covered growing as well as selling

The trafficking offence reached a broad range of acts carried out for trade, including producing, possessing, transporting and supplying drugs. Growing drug-producing plants for trade also came within it.

The basic trafficking punishment was at least fifteen years of temporary hard labour and a fine of 10,000 to 20,000 Jordanian dinars. Aggravating circumstances, including repeat offending and specified involvement of officials or minors, could raise the punishment to life labour with a larger fine.

This made the purpose of cultivation particularly important. Growing for personal consumption was dealt with in the personal-use offence, while cultivation for trade attracted the trafficking penalties.

## The capital clause required specified international connections

The death clause applied to specified aggravated trafficking linked to international drug gangs or other international crimes. Its examples included weapons smuggling and currency counterfeiting, and it covered multinational criminal organisations.

These links formed part of the capital offence. The law’s ordinary personal-use section instead prescribed prison and a fine, with a court able to substitute treatment.

When the [2016 personal-use rules](/history/jordan-first-offence-prosecution-2016/) took effect, they also protected a first offence from becoming a criminal precedent or security record. Someone who sought treatment before being caught retained protection from prosecution.

## Later reforms kept the capital clause

The [2021 amendment](/history/jordan-drug-penalty-tiers-2021/) changed personal-use penalty bands, broadened first-offence record protection and amended supply offences. The international-gang death clause continued in the consolidated law.

That combination remains central to [Jordan’s cannabis rules](/country/jo/): personal use, distribution and trafficking carry distinct punishments, while specified international trafficking can carry death. The capital provision explains Jordan’s death-penalty classification alongside its separate rules on treatment, records and CBD products.
