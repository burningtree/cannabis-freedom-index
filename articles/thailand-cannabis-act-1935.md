Siam published its Cannabis Act on 5 May 1935. The five-page law established
controls over cultivation, seeds, trade, possession and consumption, alongside
permissions and transitional arrangements that make its operation more complicated
than a single day of prohibition.

The act bears the Buddhist Era year 2477 in its title. The gazette carrying it
is dated 5 May 2478, corresponding to 1935. The title's year and the publication
date should therefore be distinguished.

## Publication preceded operation

Section 2 provided that the act would operate after ninety days had elapsed
from publication in the Royal Gazette. Publication on 5 May did not itself
make every prohibition immediately enforceable that day.

Section 3 also allowed royal decrees to exempt individual provinces temporarily,
for no more than two years from the act's commencement. The statute authorised
that possibility; this article does not establish which provinces actually
received exemptions.

## The plant, its parts and its seeds

The definition of cannabis extended to every part of the plant and substances
within it, listing leaves, flowers, tops, fruits, resin and stems. The act also
defined smoking broadly enough to include other forms of consumption.

Section 5 prohibited cultivation and possession of cannabis seed. Existing
growers received a one-year period from commencement to harvest and dispose
of their crop. The interior minister could authorise particular people to
cultivate or retain seed for experiments or medical purposes.

## Trade and use had their own transition

Section 6 required permission for imports and prohibited exports. Section 7
prohibited possession, purchase, sale, distribution and consumption, while
allowing individual permission to possess cannabis for medical purposes.

People already possessing, buying, selling, distributing or consuming cannabis
were given a separate one-year transitional arrangement to bring that activity
to an end. These provisions help explain why publication, general commencement
and the expiry of transitional protections are different points in the law's
history.

## Penalties and objects

Breaches of the cultivation, seed or import/export provisions could bring up
to one year in prison, a fine up to 500 baht, or both. Breaches of the possession,
trade, consumption or equipment provisions could bring up to six months,
200 baht, or both.

Section 8 separately restricted cannabis pipes and other smoking equipment,
with written permission available for museum preservation.

The [Thai timeline](/country/th/) records the gazette publication. The statute
supports an account of staged restrictions and exceptions, rather than an
assumption that every cannabis practice ended on 5 May 1935.
