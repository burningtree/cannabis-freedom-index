Moldova approved a drugs and addictions strategy for 2026–2032 on 21 October 2026.[@1] It called for prevention, community treatment and recovery to work alongside action against trafficking and emerging drugs.[@1] Addiction services were to be integrated with mental-health care, with better continuity of treatment and support beyond Chișinău.[@1]

## Health and enforcement were to work together

The government described a fragmented response in which police pursued trafficking, doctors treated intoxication and dependence, and schools and social services handled other consequences.[@1] The strategy sought coordinated intervention, including earlier help, overdose prevention and measures addressing HIV and viral hepatitis.[@1]

It also covered alcohol, tobacco, gambling and problematic digital behaviour.[@1] A national implementation programme for 2027–2032 was to identify concrete measures, responsible institutions, costs and outcome indicators.[@1] Approval set the direction for developing those services.[@1]

## European support helped prepare the strategy

A [Council of Europe project launched in May 2025](/history/moldova-drug-strategy-project-2025/) supported Moldova in developing a comprehensive drugs and addictions strategy.[@2] The two-year project put health and human rights at the centre of policy, including treatment and care in custodial settings.[@2]

At the Chișinău launch, participants from Poland, Romania, Ireland and Portugal shared experience in prevention, treatment, harm reduction and enforcement.[@2] Moldova's interior, health and justice ministries took part in the first steering committee, discussing the project's planned activities and responsibilities for coordination.[@2]

## New substances required faster information

In [August 2026](/history/moldova-controlled-tables-warning-system-2026/), the government had approved an early-warning mechanism for emerging psychoactive substances.[@3] The medicines agency's permanent drug-control committee was assigned technical and operational support for the system and cooperation among participating institutions.[@3]

The same announcement reported approval of new national controlled-substance tables and annual limits for specified drug precursors.[@3] These decisions sought to improve identification, collection, analysis and transmission of information about substances posing new public-health risks.[@3]

## Implementation would reach communities

The October strategy connected that warning system with doctors, emergency services and law enforcement, while emphasising reduced stigma and access to recovery.[@1] For people affected by drug use, its promised changes concerned how institutions offered support and responded to risk.[@1] The [Moldova profile](/country/md/) places those commitments alongside the existing cannabis offences and penalties.[@1]
