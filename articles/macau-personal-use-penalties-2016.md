Macau published a substantial tightening of its drug law on 28 December 2016.
Law 10/2016 raised penalties for personal-use conduct and made quantity central
to deciding whether such conduct remained within the personal-use offence.

The amendment operated within the
[2009 framework](/history/macau-drug-law-2009/). Cannabis was already controlled;
this was a change in consequences and legal classification.

## A higher personal-use penalty

The original Article 14 punished illicit consumption and specified acts done
solely for personal use, including cultivation, preparation, acquisition and
possession. It provided up to three months in prison or a fine of up to sixty
days.

The replacement provision set imprisonment at three months to one year, with
an alternative fine of sixty to 240 days. A fine expressed in days is a legal
sentencing measure, not that number of days in prison or a fixed cash amount.

The amended offence therefore preserved a distinct personal-use category while
increasing its statutory punishment.

## Quantity could change the applicable offence

New paragraphs directed conduct involving more than five times a listed daily
reference quantity to the production, trafficking or lesser-gravity provisions,
as applicable. The calculation included quantities intended entirely for personal
use, as well as quantities divided between personal use and other unlawful ends.

This means that proving personal intention alone did not necessarily keep a case
within Article 14. It also means that crossing the reference quantity should
not be described as automatically producing one identical trafficking sentence:
the amendment expressly referred to Articles 7, 8 or 11 according to the case.

## Five reference quantities were never permission

The original 2009 law already used five times the daily reference quantity when
assessing the lesser-gravity production and trafficking category. The amendment
gave that comparison a further role in the treatment of personal-use conduct.

Neither version created a lawful allowance to possess five days' supply. The
personal-use offence continued below the threshold. General unauthorised production
and trafficking penalties also changed, with the ordinary minimum rising from
three to five years while the fifteen-year maximum remained.

## Publication and operation

Article 4 delayed most of the amendment's operation until thirty days after
publication. Its separate schedule amendment was to operate the following day.
The event records publication and distinguishes these two commencement rules.

For [Macau's history](/country/mo/), the change shows how a legislature can
retain a personal-use offence while increasing both its punishment and the
circumstances in which more serious offence provisions apply.
