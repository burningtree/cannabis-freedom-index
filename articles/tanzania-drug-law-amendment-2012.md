In 2012 Tanzania made its drug penalties among the harshest on earth. The Written Laws
(Miscellaneous Amendments) (No.2) Act, passed 16 April, assented 15 June and published 27
July 2012, amended the 1995 drug law to require life imprisonment on conviction for
possession or trafficking of narcotic drugs, which include cannabis.[@1]

It replaced the old use and premises provisions with sections 17A and 17B: using drugs,
being found in a place used for them, or holding a pipe or utensil carried a fine of one
million shillings, ten years in prison, or both, while an owner or occupier who allowed
premises to be used, or held preparation equipment, faced life imprisonment.[@1]

One tier survived for the smallest cases: the small-quantity personal-use tier in section
17 remained, with a 200,000-shilling fine or seven years for cannabis.[@1] The life terms
set in 2012 still anchor the mainland ladder — thirty years for trafficking up to 100 kg,
life above it — with courts since testing the law's edges, as in the 2022 appeal that
threw out a thirty-year possession sentence over a broken chain of custody.
