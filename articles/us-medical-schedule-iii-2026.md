The United States moved part of its cannabis system out of federal Schedule I on
28 April 2026. The order was consequential but narrow: it covered FDA-approved
products and cannabis under qualifying state medical licences, not the adult-use
market or every cannabis product.

## Two categories move

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the DEA order on 23 April. It became
effective with publication five days later and placed two categories in Schedule
III:

- marijuana contained in an FDA-approved drug product; and
- marijuana subject to a qualifying state medical-marijuana licence.

The order followed a [December 2025 executive order](/history/us-executive-order-14370-2025/)
directing the attorney general to expedite rescheduling. It relied partly on the
attorney general's duty to align federal scheduling with the 1961 Single Convention,
allowing the change to take immediate effect without ordinary notice-and-comment
rulemaking.

## What Schedule III permits

Schedule I substances cannot lawfully be manufactured, distributed or sold under
federal law and face stringent research restrictions. Schedule III substances may
be handled by entities with valid DEA registrations that comply with federal rules
on prescribing, security, labelling, inventories and records.

The new regulations create an expedited registration process for state-licensed
medical manufacturers, distributors and dispensers. DEA is to grant registration
unless doing so would conflict with the public interest or the Single Convention.

Research also becomes easier. A DEA-registered researcher may obtain marijuana
from a state licensee holding a valid federal registration.

## What stayed in Schedule I

Unlicensed crops, bulk marijuana and extracts or derivatives outside an
FDA-approved product remain in Schedule I. So does marijuana in state adult-use
systems. The order also did not settle whether marijuana foods, dietary supplements
or unapproved drugs comply with federal food and drug law.

An expedited hearing beginning on 29 June was set to consider moving marijuana
more broadly to Schedule III through formal rulemaking. That was a separate process,
not part of the April order.

## In the index

The change strengthened federal recognition of medical cannabis but did not alter
the national patchwork of state possession, growing or adult retail rules. The
[United States](/country/us/) score therefore does not move.
