On 25 June 1992 Brunei's Sultan signed an emergency order that made the death penalty mandatory for trafficking more than 500 g of cannabis or 200 g of cannabis resin. Import and export carried the same thresholds, and possession for trafficking crossed into the death band above 600 g of cannabis or 300 g of resin. Cannabis was already illegal; the order made the largest amounts a capital offence.[@1]

## A drug law built in stages

Brunei's [Misuse of Drugs Act](/history/brunei-misuse-of-drugs-1978/) had come into force on 1 July 1978, banning possession, consumption, cultivation and supply.[@3] In December 1982 the government introduced the death penalty for drug offences. A 1984 order made it mandatory for trafficking, importing or exporting more than 15 g of heroin or morphine, possessing more than 30 g for trafficking, and manufacturing those drugs.[@2] Cannabis was not in that capital schedule. The 1992 order changed that through the Constitution's emergency powers rather than a new Act.[@1]

## The 500-gram line

The order set death at more than 500 g of cannabis and more than 200 g of resin for trafficking, import and export. Below those lines, trafficking between 330 g and 500 g carried twenty to thirty years and fifteen strokes; smaller amounts five to twenty years and five to fifteen strokes.[@1] The same order set a presumption that anyone holding more than 15 g of cannabis, or 10 g of resin, was presumed to be trafficking or to possess the drug for trafficking. That put the burden on the accused to prove otherwise; anyone with more than 500 g was presumed to be trafficking, and the top band carried death.[@1]

Amnesty International, relying on Reuters, later described the change as covering more than 200 g of cannabis or opium. The order sets 500 g for herbal cannabis; 200 g is the resin threshold for trafficking and import or export.[@1,2]

## Who it reached

The death sentence was mandatory; a judge who convicted someone on the top cannabis band had no discretion.[@1,2] The [last known death sentence](/history/brunei-last-death-sentence-2017/) in Brunei was passed in 2017, and no execution is known since 1957.[@4] A man arrested in January 2016 and charged over 756.33 g of cannabis faced a trafficking charge carrying death. In June 2026 prosecutors replaced it with two possession-for-trafficking charges, for 200 g of cannabis and 84.17 g of cannabis mixture.[@5] He pleaded guilty and was sentenced on 27 June 2026 to five years and eight months and five strokes, after about ten years; the court refused probation and cut a year for the delay.[@5]

## What survived

The current revised edition of the Act still carries the 500 g and 600 g lines, and death remains mandatory for the top band.[@3] Cannabis moved to Class A in 2012, and the [2013 revision](/history/brunei-class-a-2013/) kept the thresholds. The sentence remains on the statute book, and the [Brunei profile](/country/bn/) sets out the penalties that still apply.
