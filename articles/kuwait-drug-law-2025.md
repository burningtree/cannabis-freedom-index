Kuwait’s combined drug-control law took effect in December 2025, replacing its separate narcotics and psychotropic-substances statutes. It retained death or life imprisonment for specified trafficking offences, while setting separate rules for personal use, unpaid supply and rehabilitation. HFW’s published account gives commencement as 15 December.[@1,2]

## Personal use and trafficking had different penalties

The ordinary personal-use offence covered consumption, possession, buying and specified other conduct for personal use. It carried up to five years in prison and a fine up to KD5,000. The maximum rose to ten years and KD10,000 in specified places, including prisons, schools and treatment centres.[@1]

Importing, smuggling, producing or cultivating for trafficking could bring death or life imprisonment. The law also provided capital or life penalties for specified possession for trade, sale, promotion and exchange offences.[@1] These continued the history of [capital drug penalties expanded in 1995](/history/kuwait-capital-drug-penalties-1995/).[@5]

## Unpaid sharing had its own rule

Providing a controlled drug for consumption without payment carried five to ten years in prison and a fine of KD5,000–10,000. The statute distinguished that conduct from the specified trafficking and paid-supply offences carrying capital or life penalties.[@1]

Personal-use cases also had treatment options. Prosecutors could order up to three months in rehabilitation instead of starting a prosecution. Courts had a separate power to order treatment instead of punishment, subject to conditions and reports from the centre.[@1]

## Testing powers depended on official decisions

The law allowed authorities to introduce testing for public appointments and periodic or random workplace checks. It also permitted testing requirements for driving and weapons licences and marriage procedures. The wording granted powers to the relevant authorities to introduce those requirements.[@1]

A separate provision addressed first detections at official entry points involving specified personal-use food, drinks or body-care products containing controlled substances. It provided for confiscation and a written undertaking, with prosecution possible for repetition.[@1]

## Licensing continued beyond the criminal penalties

The law was signed in November and published in the gazette on 30 November.[@1,3] It regulated medical prescribing, pharmaceutical manufacture and institutional cultivation alongside offences and punishments.[@1]

In [August 2026](/history/kuwait-research-growing-rules-2026/), published Health Ministry rules added detailed controls for licensed institutional growing.[@4] The [Kuwait profile](/country/kw/) brings these licensing rules and penalties together.
