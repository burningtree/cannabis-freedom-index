Bosnia and Herzegovina's state drug law took effect on 15 February 2006, bringing nationwide controls over possession, production and the movement of controlled drugs.[@1] It also preserved a permit route for hemp grown for fibre and specified seed purposes, while criminal offences continued to matter at entity level.[@1,2]

## A nationwide layer of control

Parliament [adopted the law in November 2005](/history/bosnia-narcotics-law-2005/); it appeared in the official gazette on 7 February 2006 and entered into force on the eighth day after publication.[@1] Its stated purpose included implementing the international drug conventions and coordinating prevention and enforcement across the country.[@1] The resulting system made the state list of controlled substances and the rules governing their lawful circulation central to what people and businesses could possess or handle.[@1]

For an individual, unauthorised possession under the state law carried a fine of BAM1,000–3,000.[@1] That administrative penalty sat alongside criminal law: the Federation's code punishes unauthorised drug possession with up to one year in prison.[@2] A reader therefore needs both the nationwide controls and the rules applying where an alleged offence occurred to understand the possible consequences.[@1,2]

## Hemp needed a permitted purpose

Farmers could grow hemp for fibre, animal-feed seed, further propagation and seed processing, with a permit from the foreign-trade ministry.[@1] A production-and-purchase contract with a registered operator was normally required; a grower registered for the relevant activity could also qualify directly.[@1] Individuals growing without the required hemp permit faced BAM5,000–10,000 fines.[@1]

The law required landholders to destroy uncontrolled plants growing on their land, with agricultural inspectors able to act if the plants remained.[@1] It also required reporting circumstances suggesting that a permitted hemp crop could be diverted into illicit drug production.[@1]

## Rescheduling still needed prescribing rules

The distinction between a controlled substance and an available medicine remained important when the government [approved medical rescheduling in December 2025](/history/bosnia-medical-use-approved-2025/).[@3] At the September 2026 follow-up, authorities were still working on the indications, authorised doctors, doses and pharmaceutical forms needed for practical prescribing.[@3] The Federation planned a new prescribing and dispensing rulebook for early 2027, while Republika Srpska reported its amendments nearing completion.[@3] Those unfinished steps explain why the [country profile](/country/ba/) distinguishes medical recognition from a functioning route to pharmacy treatment.[@3]
