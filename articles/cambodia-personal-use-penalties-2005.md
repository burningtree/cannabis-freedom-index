Cambodia changed the penalties for personal drug use in May 2005: storing, buying or growing drugs for oneself, and unlawful consumption, carried seven days to one month in prison.[@1] A fine could be added, while prosecutors and judges retained discretion for very small quantities used in customary village practice.[@1]

## The original law had allowed a fine alone

The [law approved in December 1996](/history/cambodia-ban-1996/) had already prohibited cannabis cultivation, subject to limited research permission.[@2] It dealt separately with growing, buying and storing drugs for personal consumption, and with consuming them unlawfully.[@2]

For those personal-use offences, the original text allowed six days to one month in prison, a fine of 100,000–1,000,000 riel, or both.[@2] That choice mattered because the wording expressly allowed a financial penalty without imprisonment.[@2]

The 2005 version instead prescribed seven days to one month, with a possible additional fine of 25,000–100,000 riel.[@1] The fine range became lower while the ordinary penalty wording required imprisonment.[@1,2]

## Customary consumption still had a narrow exception

The amendments retained discretion where an offence involved a very small quantity consumed as a usual habit.[@1] A prosecutor could drop charges; once a case reached court, a judge could forgo punishment or issue a warning.[@1]

The law tied that discretion to non-addictive consumption by villagers following ancestral practice.[@1] It therefore identified particular people and circumstances for possible leniency, while leaving the decision with prosecutors and judges.[@1]

Repeat offending, or an offender working in health management or care, doubled the ordinary personal-use penalties.[@1]

## Treatment remained part of the legal response

The amended law also protected people who voluntarily sought treatment for the consumption offence before prosecution.[@1] Prosecutors could direct people into treatment, and courts could take completion into account.[@1]

Those rules connected punishment with a treatment system: whether someone appeared voluntarily, complied with an order or completed treatment could affect what happened to their case.[@1]

## The next law replaced the personal-use scheme

The [2012 drug law](/history/cambodia-drug-control-law-2012/) expressly replaced the 1997 legislation and its 2005 amendments.[@3] It set a basic two-to-five-year prison penalty for unlawful keeping of drugs in its first two tables, and separate penalties for cultivation.[@3]

The older customary-consumption idea survived in the new consumption rules.[@3] In [Cambodia](/country/kh/), that limited discretion continued alongside criminal penalties and increasingly detailed treatment procedures.[@3]
