On 27 October 1934 the Soviet Union's Central Executive Committee and Council of People's Commissars banned sowing opium poppy and Indian hemp across the whole union, except for crops devoted exclusively to the medical and scientific needs of the USSR.[@1] The decree took effect the following year, which is why the ban is filed under 1935 in the republics — including [Uzbekistan](/history/uzbekistan-soviet-ban-1934/) and the other copies of this shared event.[@1]

## Quotas for poppy, a ban for hemp

The decree built a planning apparatus around the poppy. Every year by 15 August, the Union agriculture commissariat, together with the foreign-trade commissariat and the republic health commissariats, set the poppy area for the next operating year, and republic land organs issued permits within it.[@1] A further instruction was to govern how district plans were divided, permits granted and sowings recorded.[@1]

Indian hemp received no such quota system. The text simply prohibited sowing it, with the sole exception of medical and scientific crops — an exception that covered both plants.[@1] Ordinary fibre hemp went unmentioned: the ban named only the Indian drug plant.[@1]

The decree closed an earlier, looser regime. It repealed a 1927 measure on accounting for and sowing opium poppy, replacing administration with prohibition.[@1] Even within the permitted poppy area, land organs could only license sowings up to the year's set figure, and every district plan, permit and sowing was to be recorded under a central instruction.[@1]

## Punishment and code changes

Unauthorized sowings of either plant drew criminal liability with mandatory confiscation of the crop.[@1] The union republics were instructed to write matching additions into their criminal codes, extending the union ban into republic law.[@1] A 1927 decree on accounting and sowing opium poppy was repealed as superseded.[@1]

The decree said nothing about possessing or consuming the drugs — only about sowing them. Everything beyond the field was left to the republic criminal codes it ordered amended.[@1]

## One decree, many republics

Signed by Kalinin, Molotov and Enukidze as No. 110/2466, the decree reached every republic at once — the reason identical first-ban entries stand for Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Ukraine, Azerbaijan, Georgia and Kyrgyzstan.[@1] The story continues in each republic's later drug laws, and on [Kazakhstan's page](/country/kz/).[@1]
