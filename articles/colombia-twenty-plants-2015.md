A man in Colombia was given more than five years in prison for one cannabis plant. His
appeal produced the rule that still governs home growing there: up to twenty plants is
not a crime.

## The case

Police had found him with a freshly cut plant weighing 124 grams. A lower court treated
it as production. In August 2015 the Supreme Court overturned the sentence, holding that
the plant was meant for his own consumption.

The court then set a general line. Growing up to twenty plants is not an offence, the
counterpart for growers of the twenty grams a person may carry.

## Where the rule came from

Colombian courts had been moving this way for two decades. In 1994 the Constitutional
Court held that possessing drugs in amounts for personal use was lawful. The government
confirmed a figure of 20 grams for cannabis in 2012. What the 2015 ruling added was
the source: until then a user could lawfully hold cannabis but had no lawful way of
producing it.

## What it did not settle

The ruling protects a grower from prosecution. It creates no licence, no registry and no
right to sell, and anything above twenty plants is still cultivation in the criminal
sense. Later the same year the president signed legislation for medical cannabis, a
separate, licensed system.

Twenty plants is far more than other countries that allow home growing: Uruguay permits
six, Germany and Czechia three, Malta four. The difference is that in
[Colombia](/country/co/) the limit was drawn by judges deciding what personal use could
plausibly mean, not by a parliament deciding how much to allow.
