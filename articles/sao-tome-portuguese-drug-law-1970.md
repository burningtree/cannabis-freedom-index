On 26 October 1970, Portugal extended Decree-Law 420/70 to its overseas provinces, with Macau the
stated exception. Portaria 537/70 therefore brought the metropolitan drug law to colonial São
Tomé and Príncipe. The change replaced the narrow reach of the territory's [1958 cannabis
order](/history/sao-tome-cannabis-order-1958/) with a far broader criminal regime.

## Cannabis was expressly listed

The decree's drug list included the flowering or fruiting tops of the cannabis plant, excluding
seeds and detached leaves when those tops were absent. It separately named cannabis resin and the
concentrated preparations commonly known as hashish.

Article 2 covered importing, exporting, buying, obtaining, producing, preparing, cultivating,
prescribing, administering, possessing, storing, transporting, selling and offering listed drugs.
The ordinary penalty was imprisonment for two to eight years plus a fine of 5,000 to 500,000
escudos.

## A separate rule for personal use

Conduct intended only for the offender's own use, and supply to another person without profit,
carried a lower ceiling: imprisonment for up to two years and a fine of 500 to 50,000 escudos.
That was still a sharp change from the 1958 order, which specified only a fine and did not
expressly create offences for possession or cultivation.

The decree was not an absolute bar to every use. Article 3 preserved activities authorised for
medical, therapeutic, scientific or industrial purposes under the applicable rules. That
exception did not create open patient access; it placed lawful activity behind authorisation.

## A colonial law with a long afterlife

The National Assembly's explanatory material for São Tomé and Príncipe's later Penal Code said
that courts had continued using Decree-Law 420/70 against drug possession and trafficking. The
1970 extension thus provides the identifiable legal bridge between the short colonial cannabis
order and the country's modern national penal legislation after independence.

In the index, this event moves possession from 10 to 0, cultivation from 10 to 1, enforcement from
4 to 2, and sharing from 10 to 0. Consumption and access remain at 1 and 0. Those scores reflect
the express personal-use prison exposure, the cultivation offence and the severe treatment of
supply—not an inference from the earlier order.
