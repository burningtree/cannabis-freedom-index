Italy's Law 242 of 2 December 2016 supported hemp cultivation and its agricultural and industrial supply chain. It gave qualifying crops a route outside the ordinary authorisation requirement. The measure is often associated with the subsequent “cannabis light” market, but its text was a law about cultivation and specified uses, not a general permission to sell recreational cannabis.

The distinction matters when reading its THC thresholds. A protection for a compliant farmer is not automatically a product standard for everything a shop might sell.

## An agricultural framework

Article 1 identifies the law's environmental and agricultural objectives and ties its scope to varieties in the common catalogue of agricultural plant species. The framework therefore starts with the identity of the crop, rather than a retailer's description of it as light cannabis.

Article 2 permits cultivation of those varieties without authorisation. It also lists possible outputs, including food and cosmetics produced under their own sector rules, materials for industrial and craft uses, and other specified applications. The separate rules attached to these outputs remain important: permission to grow a crop does not displace food or cosmetic regulation.

## Testing the crop

Article 4 provides for inspections and laboratory sampling. It requires sampling in the grower's presence where crops are taken for testing, and a sample for possible counter-analysis. Results are to be based on plant samples prepared and analysed through the prescribed method.

This was more than a declaration that hemp was allowed. It supplied a procedure for distinguishing a qualifying agricultural crop from one raising problems under drug control, and for giving the farmer evidence with which to contest a test.

## What the tolerance protected

Under the original article 4, a crop testing above 0.2% and within 0.6% THC did not create responsibility for a farmer who had complied with the law. The provision addressed variation found during cultivation checks.

It did not say that every retail flower product below 0.6% was authorised. That frequently blurred distinction between cultivation protection and commercial permission helps explain why the law's association with “cannabis light” needs careful qualification.

## The longer hemp history

Italy's [hemp rope-makers](/culture/traditions/italian-hemp-rope-makers/) preserve an older history of the crop as fibre and skilled work. The 2016 framework gave that agricultural and industrial identity a modern legislative expression.

The subsequent [hemp-flower restrictions](/history/italy-hemp-flower-ban-2025/) belong to a separate event. This article describes the original law's scope and safeguards, rather than presenting its historical thresholds as a complete guide to what may be sold in Italy today.
