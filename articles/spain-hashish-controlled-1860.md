Spain's pharmacy ordinances of **1860** explicitly placed hashish in a poison catalogue. The measure is more specific than a bare statement that Spain “controlled cannabis”: it connects a named substance to rules governing drug dealers and pharmacy practice.

## A royal decree and a named catalogue entry

The royal decree is dated **18 April 1860**. Its ordinances appeared in the *Gaceta de Madrid* on **24 April**, across pages 1–2. Catalogue No. 2 includes **Haschisch**. Article 59 gives that catalogue legal significance by declaring its entries poisonous substances for the relevant drug-trade provisions.

The database uses the royal decree's date. A separate commencement date has not been established, and the later publication date should remain visible when reconstructing the sequence.

## What a drug dealer had to obtain

Article 57 covers sales of poisonous substances by *drogueros*, whether the substance was medicinal or otherwise. It applies to wholesale and retail dealings and to sales to the public and to pharmacists.

Before selling, the dealer had to require a dated and signed note from a known, responsible person. That note had to spell out the quantity requested and its intended use. Hashish's presence in Catalogue No. 2 brings it within this rule.

This is a written-order safeguard, but the article does not say that the signer must invariably be a doctor. Describing it simply as a universal medical-prescription requirement would replace the actual wording with a different rule.

## Pharmacy practice and the wider trade

Other provisions explain the setting. Article 2 reserves the preparation and dispensing of medicines to legally authorized pharmacists, subject to the ordinances. Articles 8–9 require secure storage of poisonous and powerful medicinal substances, with the pharmacist retaining the key.

Articles 54–55 distinguish materials with industrial as well as medicinal uses from exclusively medicinal drugs. They restrict preparation and certain retail sales by drug dealers. Article 60 extends the chapter's obligations to chemical manufacturers and others who occasionally sell medicinal drugs or poisons, rather than limiting it to established drug-dealing businesses.

These provisions should be read together without assuming every cannabis product or transaction had an identical legal treatment.

## What this establishes

The original gazette establishes an express hashish classification and concrete commercial safeguards. It does not establish a general offence of possessing cannabis, an across-the-board cultivation ban, or how frequently officials enforced the rules. The previous database claim that the measure was weakly enforced has therefore been removed: the ordinance itself cannot substantiate that conclusion.
