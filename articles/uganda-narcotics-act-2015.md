Uganda's new drug law exposed people with unauthorised cannabis to ten to
twenty-five years in prison, a substantial fine, or both, and made trafficking
punishable by life imprisonment plus a fine. Giving cannabis away counted
within its definition of trafficking, while growers needed the health minister's
written consent.

## The earlier law already punished possession and smoking

The [1993 drug law](/history/uganda-national-drug-authority-1993/) prohibited
possession without lawful excuse and cannabis smoking, including hosting it
and holding smoking utensils. For Class A offences without a separate penalty,
it provided up to five years in prison, a fine, or both.

The later law replaced the principal narcotics sections of that statute.
Although assented to in 2015, it was published and commenced in February 2016.
It brought possession, trafficking, cultivation, drug proceeds and international
drug-control obligations together in a dedicated law.

## A fine option sat alongside much longer imprisonment

For cannabis possession, the prison range became ten to twenty-five years.
The alternative fine started at 500 currency points, equivalent to ten million
shillings, or three times the drug's market value, whichever was greater.
Courts could impose both imprisonment and a fine.

Possession exceptions covered authorised licence holders, health professionals
holding drugs for medical purposes, people obtaining them for medical purposes
from a practitioner or on prescription, and people authorised by regulations.
The authority could license specified drug production, distribution and trade.

Trafficking carried life imprisonment in addition to a fine. Its broad definition
covered sale, supply, transport and giving drugs away, with exceptions for
authorised activities and professional medical supply.

## Growers and people smoking faced separate offences

Growing a prohibited plant required written consent from the health minister.
Unauthorised cultivation could bring at least five years in prison, a fine,
or both; a second or subsequent offence carried life imprisonment.
Courts convicting an offender had to direct destruction of the associated
prohibited plants.

Smoking or otherwise using drugs, allowing premises to be used, and holding
utensils for illicit use carried one to five years, a fine, or both. These
rules reached people supplying a place or equipment as well as those
consuming cannabis themselves.

## Khat growers challenged how parliament had passed the law

The challenge came from an association representing growers and dealers of
khat, which the new law also controlled. In [2023](/history/uganda-court-nullifies-act-2023/),
the Constitutional Court found that parliament had passed the bill without
the required quorum and annulled the whole law.

Parliament then enacted a [replacement](/history/uganda-narcotics-act-2024/),
continuing the overhaul of [Uganda's cannabis law](/country/ug/).
