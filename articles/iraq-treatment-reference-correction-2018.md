Iraq corrected its drug law in January 2018 so that the court-ordered treatment alternative expressly referred to personal-use offences.[@1] The change replaced a reference to a different offence involving premises where people used drugs.[@1,2]

## One reference determined which offenders the rule covered

The [2017 law](/history/iraq-narcotics-law-2017/) created a one-to-three-year prison offence, with a fine, for importing, possessing, producing or growing drugs for personal use.[@2] Its treatment chapter allowed courts to consider hospital or outpatient care.[@2]

But the original opening of the court-treatment article referred to the following article number, which dealt with allowing or knowingly being present at drug use on premises.[@2] The correction changed that number to the personal-use offence.[@1]

For a person convicted of holding or growing cannabis for themselves, that connection identified the offence for which the court could choose the treatment options.[@1,2]

## Hospital and outpatient options followed medical reports

One option was admission to a health institution for someone whose dependence had been established.[@2] A medical committee would report to the court, which could order release or continued admission.[@2]

Another was attendance at a psychological and social clinic once or twice a week for someone shown to use drugs.[@2] A doctor's report, due within ninety days of starting those visits, informed the court's decision to stop or continue them.[@2]

The law also allowed follow-up outpatient visits after release from a health institution.[@2] Treatment therefore involved continuing medical advice and court oversight.[@2]

## Earlier treatment permission had been narrower

The [1979 reform](/history/iraq-drug-treatment-hemp-reform-1979/) had allowed six months in a designated health facility when dependence arose from a medical condition.[@3] Its exception sat alongside a personal-use maximum of fifteen years.[@3]

The new law reduced that prison range and described several treatment routes, including protection from prosecution for people voluntarily presenting themselves to a specialist hospital.[@2] The correction addressed the separate route chosen by a court after an offence.[@1,2]

## The notice also repaired pharmacy rules

The same notice corrected the schedules governing licensed pharmacists' handling of psychotropic substances and replaced an earlier correction.[@1] It was published in the official Gazette on 15 January 2018.[@1]

In [Iraq](/country/iq/), the corrected text gave courts a clearer statutory connection between personal-use offences and the treatment choices already described in the law.[@1,2]
