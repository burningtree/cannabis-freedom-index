Germany's Federal Constitutional Court did not legalise cannabis in 1994. It upheld the criminal law, but required prosecutors to use its safety valves in minor personal-use cases.

## A constitutional challenge to prohibition

Several courts asked whether the Narcotics Act's cannabis offences were compatible with constitutional protections. The challenged rules covered conduct including acquisition, possession, import and supply.

The Federal Constitutional Court rejected the broad claim that personal freedom created an unrestricted "right to intoxication." It accepted that legislators could use criminal law to restrict cannabis and held the core prohibitions constitutional.

## Proportionality for small personal amounts

The court drew an important line around low-level cases. Where a small quantity was intended only for occasional personal use and the conduct did not endanger other people, the individual's wrongdoing and culpability could be slight.

The ban survived constitutional review because German law already allowed courts to refrain from punishment and prosecutors to discontinue proceedings. In those minor cases, the court said enforcement authorities generally had to decline prosecution under the proportionality principle.

That was not a declaration that possession had become lawful. Acquisition and possession continued to satisfy the criminal offence, and the judgment expressly justified treating them as punishable in general. It changed the expected response to a limited class of cases rather than removing the prohibition.

## No national gram limit

The judgment spoke of a small quantity but did not create a single number of grams for Germany. It instead placed responsibility on enforcement authorities and the states to apply the discontinuance rules in an essentially uniform way.

That distinction matters: the decision established a constitutional principle for prosecutorial restraint, not a statutory possession allowance. The resulting practice could still depend on the state and the circumstances, including whether use was occasional and whether anyone else was put at risk.

## What moved in the index

The ruling raised [Germany's](/country/de/) possession score from 1 to 2 and enforcement from 3 to 4, reflecting routine discontinuance for qualifying small personal-use cases. Cultivation and sharing stayed at 1, consumption stayed at 3 and access stayed at 0. The modest movement records restrained enforcement inside a prohibition, not legalisation.
