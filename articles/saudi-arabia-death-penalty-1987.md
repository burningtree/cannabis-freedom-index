In February 1987 Saudi Arabia's Council of Senior Scholars issued a fatwa that made drug smuggling a capital offence. It prescribed death for people who brought drugs into the kingdom or received them from abroad, and for repeat dealers. Judges soon began applying the rule to trafficking cases, including hashish.[@1]

## The fatwa

The council's ruling was not a statute. It was a religious opinion that gave judges a basis for imposing death in the most serious drug cases. That mattered in a system where the death penalty for smuggling had previously been available only through a narrower route.[@1]

The fatwa drew the line at import and repeat dealing rather than at quantity. A person who brought hashish into the country, or who was convicted again of dealing, could be sentenced to death regardless of the weight involved.[@1] It did not define what counted as importing or as a repeat, leaving the scope to the judges who applied it.[@1]

## Codification

The rule was later written into the Law of Combating Narcotics and Psychotropic Substances, issued by Royal Decree M/39 in 2005. Article 37 makes death the penalty for smuggling, importing, exporting, manufacturing or cultivating with intent to promote, and for repeat promotion. A court may reduce the sentence to at least fifteen years and a fine in mitigating cases.[@3]

The 2005 law kept the fatwa's logic: death attached to the act of bringing drugs in or dealing repeatedly, not to a threshold in grams. Article 38 covers ordinary dealing with five to fifteen years and a fine; Article 41 covers personal use with six months to two years.[@3]

## A resurgent practice

Saudi Arabia paused drug executions for part of 2021, then resumed them in 2022. The numbers rose sharply. Human Rights Watch counted 345 executions in 2024 and 356 in 2025; 240 of the 2025 executions were for non-lethal drug offences, and 98 solely for hashish.[@2]
