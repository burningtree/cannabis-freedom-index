On 23 February 1956 San Marino passed Law No. 7, its first narcotics law, built on the 1931 limitation convention.[@1] Illicit possession, use, import, export or trafficking of a narcotic substance drew three to eight years in prison plus a fine.[@1] There was only one oddity: the law never said whether cannabis counted.[@1]

## The unnamed drug

A 1973 United States government survey spotted the gap and wrote it down plainly: the 1956 law did not specifically include cannabis in its narcotics list, though it referred to other decrees that probably did.[@1] For seventeen years the republic's cannabis prohibition rested on a probably — a convention-based list that named everything except the plant, plus unspecified decrees that supposedly caught it.[@1]

## Named at last

The [1988 narcotics law](/history/san-marino-narcotics-additions-1988/) ended the ambiguity from the other direction: instead of amending the 1956 list, it annexed its own table naming marijuana, hashish, THC, hashish oil and sinsemilla as the cannabis group, and annulled the lenient tail of the 1956 law's article 6.[@2] Simple possession now faced the same scale as supply — the full story belongs to the 1988 article.[@2] The fix also built in its own updating mechanism — the Captains Regent could amend the drug tables by decree — so the next substance would not need a new law.[@2] A 1981 decree's drug tables, cited in the 1988 filing, show the kind of secondary legislation the 1956 law had leaned on all along.[@2]

The two laws stood together for nine years.[@3] Law No. 139 of November 1997 repealed them both and re-enacted the cannabis core with fuller procedure.[@3]

## Medicine, sixty years on

In 2016 an istanza d'Arengo — a citizens' public initiative — asked the government to legalise medical cannabis, and the government agreed, starting work on a cultivation plan and the international treaties needed to supply it.[@1] Patients can now obtain medicines such as Sativex for approved conditions, including multiple sclerosis and neuropathic pain, at no cost to residents.[@1] From a law that could not say the plant's name to free prescriptions in sixty years: the 1956 statute remains the recorded start of [San Marino's](/country/sm/) prohibition, gaps and all.[@1]
