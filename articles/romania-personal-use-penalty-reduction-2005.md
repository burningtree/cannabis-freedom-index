From 7 March 2005, people caught with cannabis for themselves could receive a fine or six months to two years in prison.[@1] The amendment put cannabis and other drugs in the lower “risk” category on a different personal-use penalty from drugs in the higher category.[@1]

## Personal use gets a separate penalty

Romania had adopted a [dedicated drug law in 2000](/history/romania-drug-control-law-2000/).[@1] The 2004 amendment rewrote its personal-use offence, covering growing, producing, preparing, buying and possessing drugs for oneself.[@1]

For cannabis, the practical choice now included a fine.[@1] The prison range was six months to two years.[@1] For drugs classified as high-risk, the amended law retained two to five years.[@1] The distinction concerned the substance involved as well as the purpose for which a person held or grew it.[@1]

There was no gram allowance in the rewritten offence.[@1] A person could still be prosecuted for personal possession, and the fine was one of the punishments available within criminal law.[@1]

## The law also promises help

The amendment gave much more space to treatment and social support.[@1] It defined integrated assistance as a combination of health, psychological and social services intended to address dependence, rehabilitation and social reintegration.[@1] Evaluation and individual care were part of that approach.[@1]

It also set out a route through which participation in an assistance programme could affect punishment.[@1] Those provisions had a different starting date: they were tied to the entry into force of the new Penal Code.[@1] The immediate penalty change and the treatment provisions therefore had separate timetables.[@1]

## Two dates mattered

The law was published on 7 December 2004.[@1] Its general commencement rule allowed three months before it took effect, giving the personal-use amendment its March 2005 starting date.[@1] The Gazette expressly separated the deferred treatment provisions from that general rule.[@1]

## Another reduction follows in 2014

When the [new Penal Code took effect in February 2014](/history/romania-penal-code-2014/), the cannabis minimum fell again, from six months to three months.[@2] The two-year maximum and fine alternative remained.[@2] The same reform provided assessment and, with the person's agreement, admission to an assistance programme; following it could allow a court to waive or postpone punishment.[@2]

Those successive changes shaped the personal-use rules in [Romania](/country/ro/): an offence could bring a fine, prison or a treatment-linked outcome, depending on the applicable conditions.[@1,2]
