Chad's 2017 Penal Code treats some drug offenders as patients first and prisoners
second — but only after the prison term. Article 38 allows courts to confine a person
with drug dependence in a special health institution for up to two years where the
conviction carries at least two years in prison.[@1]

Confinement does not replace punishment. Under Article 41 it begins when the principal
prison sentence ends or is suspended, and the court may shorten it after medical advice
that release no longer threatens public order.[@1] The code took effect on 1 August
2017, following the earlier pharmacy law's power to order drug users into
treatment.[@1]

The rule sits inside a system whose penalties remain only partly mapped: the 2000
pharmacy law's one-to-six-year narcotics tier overlaps with the 1995 drug law in ways
the retained sources have not reconciled, so confinement attaches to sentences whose
own consolidation is still unverified.
