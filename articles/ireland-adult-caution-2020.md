Since 14 December 2020 an adult caught in Ireland with cannabis for their own use can be dealt with by a formal police caution and not taken to court. No law was amended to do it. The national police, An Garda Síochána, added the offence to an existing scheme with the agreement of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

## The scheme

The Adult Cautioning Scheme dates from 2006. It lets the police close certain minor offences with a caution where prosecution is not judged necessary. Four offences were added in December 2020:

| Offence | Law |
|---|---|
| Trespass in a manner likely to cause fear | Public Order Act 1994, section 13 |
| Control of access to certain events | Public Order Act 1994, section 21 |
| Casual trading without a licence | Casual Trading Act 1995, section 3 |
| Simple possession of cannabis or cannabis resin | Misuse of Drugs Act, section 3 |

The police notice is specific about the last: only cannabis and cannabis resin qualify, and no other controlled drug may be dealt with under the scheme.

A caution can be considered only for an offence committed on or after 14 December 2020. It is not applied to earlier cases.

## What it changed in practice

Under the [1977 Act](/history/ireland-misuse-of-drugs-1977/) a first or second offence of possessing cannabis for personal use could only be fined, but it still meant a court appearance and a conviction. The caution removes both for a person who qualifies. It remains a discretion: the offence is still on the statute book, and the police may prosecute.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Ireland](/country/ie/) from 12 to 14 with the [2020 extension](/history/ireland-adult-caution-2020/). Enforcement rises from 2 to 3, because the ordinary outcome of a first possession case can now be a caution with no prosecution. Possession stays at 1: the offence itself was not touched.
