An account in Bernardo Parrella's 2014 book describes an Italy–Nepal project intended to connect
Himalayan hemp-working communities with alternative textile trading channels. He dates its
operational start to **2011**, following several years of preparation.

The project, called *Italia-Nepal canapa solidale*, sought to help communities preserve their
knowledge of growing and working hemp while participating in a supply chain directed towards
Italy. Parrella presents textiles as the central product and seed or oil exports as possibilities.
Those possibilities should not be recorded as completed shipments.

The account places the project within a broader discussion of Himalayan household and craft
uses of cannabis. Its description of a community tradition is not evidence that every community
used the same methods, or that particular practices had continued unchanged for millennia.

This entry records a reported trade and cultural-preservation initiative. No independent audit
of participants, export volumes, earnings or later outcomes was obtained. The project did not,
on the evidence reviewed, establish a change in Nepal's cannabis law. Its history entry therefore
moves no freedom score.
