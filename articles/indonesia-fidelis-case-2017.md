Fidelis Arie Sudewarto grew cannabis because nothing else eased his wife's pain. He was
arrested, she died a month later, and a court sent him to prison.

## The illness

Yenni Riawati had syringomyelia, a rare disease of the spinal cord. After many
treatments failed, her husband, a 36-year-old father of two from Sanggau in West
Kalimantan, read online that cannabis oil could help. Nobody could tell him how to get
permission to use it. He grew the plants at home and made the extract himself.

By his account it worked: she could sleep, and she ate. She did not know what the
medicine was.

## The arrest

On 19 February 2017 the national narcotics agency arrested him for growing 39 plants.
The treatment stopped. Yenni died on 25 March.

## The trial

In his defence statement Fidelis read a letter to his late wife. "Only through cannabis
oil," it said, "can I see you smiling again."

On 2 August the Sanggau district court found him guilty of planting, possessing and
providing cannabis under the 2009 Narcotics Law.

| | Prison | Fine |
|---|---|---|
| Prosecutors asked for | 5 months | 800 million rupiah |
| The court imposed | 8 months | 1 billion rupiah, or one more month |

The judges gave more than the prosecution had sought. The presiding judge said the
panel had weighed legal, social and philosophical considerations equally.

## The reaction

A criminal justice reform group said the court had ignored the fact that Fidelis had no
other way to treat his wife, and called on the president to reconsider the war on
drugs. His lawyer said the family had not decided whether to appeal: he wanted to think
first about his two children.

## What it changed

In law, nothing. Indonesia's ban on medical use stayed in place, and the case moves no
score. What it changed was the argument: for the first time the national debate about
cannabis in [Indonesia](/country/id/) had a face, and it was not a trafficker's.
