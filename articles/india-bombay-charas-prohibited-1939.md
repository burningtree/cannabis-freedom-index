From 1 August 1939 no one could transport, possess or sell charas in Bombay or in Ahmedabad.[@1] In parts of two other districts the province cut how much ganja or bhang a person could carry without a permit. People already using hemp drugs could keep a limited amount if a doctor certified them.[@1]

## Left to the province

Bombay kept its own excise system. [The Dangerous Drugs Act of 1930](/history/india-dangerous-drugs-act-1930/) then became the national narcotics law, and it still left growing, selling, possessing and consuming hemp to the provinces. Making an extract or a tincture needed a licence. Bombay's 1939 step is one of those provincial rules. The Government of India called it a step toward extending prohibition, and reported it to the League of Nations in a return dated at New Delhi on 12 December 1940.[@1]

## Two cities, and a smaller allowance

| Place | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| Bombay and Ahmedabad | Transport, possession and sale of charas prohibited from 1 August 1939 |
| Parts of Ahmednagar and Kanara | Without a permit: half a tola of ganja, about 5.85 grams, or two tolas of bhang, about 23.4 grams |

The report gives those gram weights itself.[@1] The smaller allowance applied to transport and possession. In the same areas the permit-free amount of opium was cut to a quarter of a tola, about 2.9 grams.[@1] Ganja and bhang in those districts stayed within the new amounts. Charas in Bombay and Ahmedabad was closed altogether: transport, possession and sale.

## A permit for people already using it

People described as addicts of hemp drugs could still possess and consume the drug they needed, up to maxima the province set, on a permit issued against a certificate from a registered medical practitioner.[@1] The same sentence covers opium addicts. The League circulated this return from Geneva on 9 May 1942.[@1] The maxima themselves are left unprinted, so the permit is the part a reader can check: a certificate from a registered practitioner, then a permit, then a capped amount of the drug.

[The same year's return](/history/india-hemp-extract-preparations-1939/) records a separate central rule on preparations of Indian hemp extract, and [Bihar's extension of prohibition](/history/india-bihar-ganja-prohibition-areas-1939/).
