For half a century one of Europe's best-known cannabis markets was a street in the
middle of Copenhagen where selling was illegal. In the end it was the neighbours, not
the police, who closed it.

## A market without a law

Christiania was declared open on 26 September 1971, on an abandoned military site.
Cannabis was sold openly there from the start. Danish law never allowed it: the
[1955 Act](/history/denmark-euphoriants-act-1955/) prohibits possession, although for
35 years from [1969](/history/denmark-warnings-1969/) a small amount normally brought
only a warning.

The residents drew their own line. In 1979 they ran a 40-day "junk blockade" against
the sale of hard drugs. Hashish stayed.

## Pressure

In 2002 the government set out to make the trade less visible, and sellers answered
by hanging camouflage nets over their stalls. Police raids followed in 2004, the year
[fines replaced warnings](/history/denmark-fines-2004/) across Denmark. The trade came
back.

## Shootings

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| April 2005 | A gang kills one man and wounds three |
| 31 August 2016 | A man carrying takings shoots two police officers and a civilian; residents vote two days later to remove the stalls, and tear them down the same day |
| 26 August 2023 | Two masked gunmen kill a man of 30 and wound four |

After 2016 the police estimated that sales fell by about three quarters. By June 2023
sellers were back at makeshift stalls, with lookouts to warn of the daily raids.

## The residents close it

In August 2023 residents blocked the entrances with concrete barriers. After the
killing that month they voted to close the street and asked the government for help.
On 6 April 2024 it was shut for good: with police and residents working together, the
street itself was dug up.

The closure moves no score for [Denmark](/country/dk/). The law had not changed; one
place where it was not applied had ended.
