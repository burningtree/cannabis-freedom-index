On 1 February 2014 a new Penal Code took effect in Romania, and the drug law was amended to match it. The change did not decriminalise anything. It cut the sentence for a person caught with drugs for their own use and added the option of a fine.

## Before and after

| | Before 2014 | From 2014 |
|---|---|---|
| Possession for personal use | prison of two to five years | three months to two years in prison, or a fine |

Community service can replace or accompany either penalty.

A report circulated by the International Drug Policy Consortium at the end of that year described the old regime through examples: up to three years for a joint, two to five years for a dose of heroin. Dealing had carried ten to twenty years and international trafficking fifteen to twenty-five.

The same report stresses what the reform did not do. The fine is a criminal penalty and leaves a criminal record, so a person fined for a few grams of cannabis is still convicted of a crime.

## Cannabis in the Romanian law

Law 143 of 2000 sorts drugs into "risk" and "high-risk". The cannabis plant and its resin are risk drugs, the lower class, which is the class the three-months-to-two-years range applies to. Romania had first restricted hashish in the 1920s, and a narrow 2013 law allowed medicines derived from cannabis with less than 0.2% THC, with almost nothing reaching patients.

## After the reform

The penalty has not moved since. A bill to stop prosecuting possession of up to three grams was rejected in 2023 by every committee that examined it.

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Romania](/country/ro/) from 9 to 11 with the [2014 code](/history/romania-penal-code-2014/). Only enforcement changed, from 1 to 2, because a fine or community service became a lawful outcome where the old law set two to five years. Possession stays at 1: it is still a crime that ends in a criminal record.
