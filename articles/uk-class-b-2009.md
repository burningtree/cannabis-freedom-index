On 26 January 2009, five years almost to the day after downgrading it, the United Kingdom returned cannabis to Class B. The government did so against the recommendation of the body the law requires it to consult.

## The order

The Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 (Amendment) Order 2008 was made on 10 December 2008. It moves cannabis, cannabis resin, cannabinol and cannabinol derivatives from Class C to Class B. Its preamble records that the draft was laid before Parliament "after consultation with the Advisory Council on the Misuse of Drugs", which is the consultation the 1971 Act demands. Consulting is all the Act demands; the minister is not bound by the answer.

The maximum sentence for possession went back from two years to five.

## Three home secretaries

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| January 2006 | Charles Clarke, on the Advisory Council's advice, decides to keep cannabis in Class C |
| 18 July 2007 | Gordon Brown, the Prime Minister, tells the Commons that the Home Secretary is reviewing the question again |
| 7 May 2008 | Jacqui Smith confirms cannabis will return to Class B, despite the Advisory Council's recommendation |
| 26 January 2009 | The reclassification takes effect |

Senior scientific advisers objected. Professor David Nutt and John Beddington considered the move politically motivated and not scientifically justified.

## Afterwards

The question did not close. In 2012 the Commons Home Affairs Committee split three votes to three on whether to say that cannabis should go back to Class C; the chair's casting vote kept the recommendation in its report. In 2022 a Home Secretary was reported to be considering Class A. Neither came to anything, and cannabis has stayed in Class B.

## Why the index moved

The index moved the [United Kingdom](/country/gb/) from 20 to 14 with the [2009 order](/history/uk-class-b-2009/), undoing the whole of the [2004](/history/uk-class-c-2004/) gain: possession from 2 to 1, enforcement from 4 to 3 and consumption from 2 to 1.
