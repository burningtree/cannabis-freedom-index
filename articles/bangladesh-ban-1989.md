Until the end of the 1980s a person in Bangladesh could buy ganja in a shop approved
by the government.[@2] Thirty years later the same country wrote the death penalty for
cannabis into its law.[@1]

## A colonial inheritance

Under British rule ganja in Bengal was not banned but taxed: it was grown under
licence and sold by licensed vendors, and the system survived independence and the
creation of Bangladesh. A growers' cooperative formed in Naogaon in 1917 served some
7,000 farmers working about 10,000 hectares under government patronage, and from 1918
to 1947 the trade yielded about 55,000 maunds a year.[@2]

## Two steps to a ban

Hussain Muhammad Ershad took power in a bloodless coup in 1982.[@1] His government
[banned growing cannabis](/history/bangladesh-cultivation-ban-1987/) in 1987 and
banned its sale in 1989.[@2,3] The ban left about 50,000 people from 7,000 families
without their living; many switched to rice, mustard and vegetables, while the
growers' cooperative warehouses were sealed with more than 226 maunds still
stored.[@2] Since late 1989 cultivating, transporting, selling, buying
and possessing it have all been illegal, and the Narcotics Control Act of 1990 made
the ban complete: deemed in force on 2 January 1990, it repealed the colonial Opium,
Excise, Dangerous Drugs and Opium Smoking Acts.[@3,4]

## What remained

The plant did not go away. A UN profile of 2005 reported cultivation in the
north-western districts of Naogaon, Rajshahi, Jamalpur and Netrokona and in the
south-eastern hills, where it was said to be increasing. Enforcement is described as
lax and the drug as still popular. At the yearly festival in Kushtia in honour of the
folk singer Fakir Lalon Shah, the authorities reportedly tolerate its use.[@1]

## The other extreme

The [Narcotics Control Act of 2018](/history/bangladesh-narcotics-control-act-2018/)
gave courts the power to impose a death sentence for possessing more than two
kilograms of cannabis. Harm Reduction International condemned it that October.

| | Rule |
|---|---|
| Before 1987 | Licensed growing, government-approved shops |
| 1987 | Growing banned |
| 1989 | Sale banned |
| 2018 | Death possible above two kilograms |

In the index the 1989 ban ended legal access for [Bangladesh](/country/bd/) and cut
its other scores to the level of an ordinary prohibition; the 2018 Act lowered them
again.
