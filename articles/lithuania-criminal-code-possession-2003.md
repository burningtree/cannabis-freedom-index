On 1 May 2003, Lithuania quietly decriminalised the small-quantity drug user — without legalising anything. The new Criminal Code, confirmed as Law VIII-1968 of 26 September 2000, took effect that day and split possession in two: ordinary unauthorised possession stayed a crime, but a small amount for personal use became a criminal misdemeanour instead.

## From three years to a misdemeanour

The starting point was the Soviet one. Since the [1974 union decree](/history/lithuania-soviet-possession-crime-1974/), possession without intent to sell had been a crime carrying up to three years in prison. Article 259 of the 2003 Code lowered the ceiling — a fine, arrest or up to two years for possession without intent to distribute — and carved out the small quantity entirely. That tier drew community service, restriction of liberty, a fine or arrest rather than a prison sentence, and the Code let offenders off entirely if they voluntarily sought medical help or surrendered the drugs.

## Fourteen years of the softer tier

The misdemeanour tier defined Lithuanian drug policy for fourteen years. Small possession sat in an administrative register of the criminal law: still forbidden, still punishable, but no longer the kind of crime that filled prisons. The release provision pointed the same way — a user who sought treatment or handed over their drugs walked away from liability altogether, a health exit from a criminal statute. Growing stayed criminal throughout, with only the large-quantity maximum rising in 2006. Then, on 1 January 2017, parliament abolished the distinction — possession of any quantity became a criminal offence leaving a criminal record, with small amounts drawing a fine, arrest or up to two years. Contemporary reporting noted the break explicitly: administrative penalties for possession had existed until 2017, and were abolished from then on.

## A round trip on the score line

The two reforms mirror each other almost exactly. The 2003 Code lifted Lithuania's score from 11 to 17 as possession, enforcement and consumption each softened a level; the [2017 recriminalisation](/history/lithuania-criminalisation-2017/) put all three back, returning the score to 11. Few histories show the mechanism of decriminalisation and recriminalisation as cleanly: the same three segments, moved one way by a misdemeanour tier and moved back when it was repealed.
