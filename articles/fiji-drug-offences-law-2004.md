Fiji signed a drug law on 7 July 2004 that put unauthorised cannabis possession, use, growing and supply under a shared maximum penalty: life imprisonment, a FJ$1 million fine, or both. The law also defined medical exceptions and gave police and customs officers powers to investigate drugs. Judges later developed guidelines to distinguish small possession cases from more serious offending.

## Cannabis users and growers faced the same statutory ceiling

Fiji already restricted cannabis under its [older drug law](/history/fiji-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1938/). The new legislation brought a wide range of conduct together, from acquiring and using drugs to cultivating, manufacturing and supplying them.

For people sharing cannabis, the definition of supply mattered: it included giving it away as well as selling it. Unauthorised imports and exports carried the same maximum penalty as possession and cultivation. The law put the burden of proving lawful authority on the accused.

The life sentence was a maximum available to courts. The statute also allowed a fine alone, leaving judges to decide the appropriate punishment in individual cases.

## Medical treatment had express exceptions

The law exempted possession of drugs lawfully prescribed or supplied for treating a person’s medical condition, including treatment of someone in their care. It also recognised authorised professional possession, supply and administration.

A separate exception covered people entering, leaving or transiting Fiji with up to one month’s lawfully prescribed or supplied treatment. The Health Minister could license drug imports, exports or manufacture in consultation with the pharmacy authorities.

These rules made lawful medical authority a central distinction within a law otherwise prohibiting cannabis activity.

## Investigators gained specified powers

Police and customs could seek judicial permission to intercept communications and use tracking devices. The legislation also addressed controlled deliveries, searches of people and vessels, search warrants and emergency searches.

It replaced the corresponding provisions of the older drug law. Its commencement depended on a date appointed by the minister in the Gazette, the official publication for legal notices.

## Courts later separated small possession from cultivation

In [2012](/history/fiji-small-cannabis-sentences-2012/), the Court of Appeal set weight bands favouring sentences outside prison for possession of up to 100 g. Disagreement over applying those bands to growing led to [separate cultivation guidelines in 2023](/history/fiji-cultivation-guidelines-2023/).

[Fiji’s 2026 rewrite](/history/fiji-counter-narcotics-law-signed-2026/) went further in its design, separating personal use from supply in the statute itself. The sequence shaped the distinction between the law’s broad maximum penalties and the sentences cannabis defendants actually faced in [Fiji](/country/fj/).
