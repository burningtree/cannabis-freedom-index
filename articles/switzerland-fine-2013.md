On 1 October 2013, Switzerland replaced an ordinary criminal complaint with a fixed CHF 100 fine for qualifying adult cannabis use. The reform simplified punishment; it did not make consumption legal.

## A ticket instead of proceedings

An adult caught consuming cannabis while holding no more than ten grams could receive an immediate fixed fine instead of being reported for ordinary prosecution. The fine was payable within 30 days, did not take prior convictions into account and produced no entry in the criminal record.

The Federal Council set the commencement date after Parliament adopted the amendment in September 2012. Minors remained subject to juvenile criminal procedure, and cases outside the statutory conditions continued through the ordinary system.

## Why ten grams mattered

The amendment also added a definition to Article 19b of the Narcotics Act: ten grams of a cannabis-type narcotic counted as a minor quantity. That provision concerned acts preparatory to personal consumption, while the new ticket procedure itself referred to consumption.

This distinction created uncertainty over possession alone. Some authorities treated holding no more than ten grams for personal use like a ticketable offence; another reading was that preparatory possession was not punishable but still had to be referred to judicial authorities. The [Federal Supreme Court resolved the penalty question in 2017](/history/switzerland-ten-grams-2017/), holding that possession alone within the limit was not punishable.

## National rule, uneven practice

The reform aimed to reduce justice-system work and costs and to put cannabis users on a more equal footing across the cantons. A later evaluation commissioned by the Federal Office of Public Health found that implementation still varied.

That unevenness matters because the change was procedural. Cannabis use remained prohibited, and the simplified sanction depended on the facts and on how police and cantonal authorities applied the rules.

## What moved in the index

The reform raised [Switzerland's](/country/ch/) possession score from 2 to 3, enforcement from 3 to 4, sharing from 1 to 3 and consumption from 1 to 2. Cultivation stayed at 1 and access at 0. The possession and sharing gains reflect the new ten-gram minor-quantity rule and its legal grey area; the later 2017 judgment produced the clearer possession rule.
