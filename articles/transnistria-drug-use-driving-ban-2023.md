Transnistria added a one-year restriction on driving rights to specified drug-use penalties in 2023. A person punished for using drugs without a prescription faced the restriction alongside the existing fine or administrative arrest. Someone who had no driving licence would also have to wait before taking a driving test.[@1,2]

## The prosecutor proposed another consequence for use

Parliament approved the changes on 25 October. Explaining the prosecutor’s proposal, legislative committee chair Galina Antyufeyeva said people held administratively liable for drug use would be unable to take a driving test for a year, linking that interval to treatment and further offending.[@2]

The president signed the administrative-code amendment on 8 November. Its commencement clause provided for the law to take effect fourteen days after official publication. The parliamentary vote and presidential signature were therefore stages before the new penalties began to apply.[@1]

The proposal reached people through the drug-use offence itself. The restriction followed from that use offence even when the conduct took place away from a vehicle.[@1]

## A fine or detention now came with a year’s restriction

The ordinary use offence already allowed a fine of 50–150 statutory calculation units or administrative arrest for up to fifteen days. The amendment attached the one-year driving restriction to either alternative. Both someone fined and someone detained faced that additional consequence.[@1]

The law also changed specified public-use penalties. The ordinary public-use paragraph paired a fine of 100–150 calculation units or up to fifteen days’ arrest with the same driving restriction. A further public-use paragraph allowed higher fines, compulsory work or longer arrest, again with the one-year restriction.[@1]

These penalties sat within the administrative system described in the [replacement code adopted in 2014](/history/transnistria-administrative-drug-code-2014/). That system could impose detention even when the case was outside a criminal prosecution.[@3]

## Getting medical help remained relevant

The administrative code exempts someone who voluntarily approaches a medical institution for treatment connected with drug use without a prescription from liability for that use offence. It also permits a person recognised as drug-dependent to enter medical and social recovery with consent under the conditions in the article.[@3]

Possession has a separate administrative rule. Where the conduct falls below the criminal offence, it can bring a fine or up to thirty days’ arrest; possession and use continued to be distinct offences.[@3]

For people affected by the 2023 change, the practical consequence extended beyond the original fine or detention: it also delayed access to driving. The [Transnistria profile](/country/xt/) records this added consequence alongside the territory’s other cannabis restrictions.[@1,2]
