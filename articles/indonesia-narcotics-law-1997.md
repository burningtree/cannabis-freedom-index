On 1 September 1997 President Soeharto signed Law 22 on Narcotics. It replaced the law of 1976, Indonesia's first of its own after independence, and it did two things that still define the country's approach to cannabis: it sorted drugs into three classes with cannabis in the first, and it wrote death into the penalties.

## Class I

Article 2 divides narcotics into Class I, II and III. Article 5 says what Class I may be used for: the development of science, and nothing else.

The 1976 law had kept cannabis available for medical as well as scientific purposes. Under the new classification a doctor could no longer use it.

## The penalties

| Offence, Class I | Article | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Using it oneself | 85 | 4 years |
| Possessing or growing | 78 | 10 years, and a fine of up to 500 million rupiah |
| Carrying, sending or transiting | 81 | 15 years |
| Giving it to another person to use | 84 | 15 years |
| Producing | 80 | death, life, or 20 years |
| Importing, exporting or selling | 82 | death, life, or 20 years |

Personal use of a Class I drug carried up to four years, against two for Class II and one for Class III. The 1976 law had set two years for personal use and six for growing or possessing.

The step from fifteen years to death is the step from carrying a drug to selling it. Handing cannabis to someone else to use sits just below, at fifteen years.

## Addicts and their families

The law treated addiction as something to be reported. An addict is required to undergo treatment. Parents or guardians must report a child who is addicted, and an adult must report himself or be reported by his family.

Failure is punished. An adult addict who knowingly does not report faces up to six months' detention or a fine of two million rupiah; a parent who does not report a child, up to six months or one million; other family members up to three months. A child reported by the parents is not prosecuted.

A judge may order treatment for an addict whether or not the person is found guilty, and time in treatment counts towards a sentence.

## Why the index moved

The [law](/history/indonesia-narcotics-law-1997/) took [Indonesia](/country/id/) from a total of 3 to 0, the bottom of the scale, where it has stayed. Enforcement fell from 1 to 0 with the arrival of the death penalty and the longer terms, and access from 1 to 0 because Class I closed the medical route the 1976 law had left open. Possession, growing, sharing and use were already at 0. The law of 2009 that replaced this one also places cannabis in Class I.
