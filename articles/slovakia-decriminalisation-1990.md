On 1 July 1990, eight months after the fall of the communist government, Czechoslovakia stopped treating the possession of a drug for one's own use as a crime. The change applied across the federation, so it is also the highest point of Slovakia's score since 1938. It lasted there for four years.

## One clause

Act No. 175/1990 amended section 187 of the [1962 criminal code](/history/slovakia-criminal-code-1962/). Two things changed.

- The offence was extended to psychotropic substances as well as narcotics.
- Possession became an offence only when it was for another person.

Under the old text a person could be punished simply for holding a drug. Under the new one, holding it for oneself was no longer within the criminal law.

## What it was not

The amendment decriminalised; it did not regulate. Producing, importing, exporting, procuring for others and possessing for others all stayed crimes. There was no licensed sale, no rule permitting home growing and no medical programme.

The federal government set up a national narcotics advisory commission the same year, as it began to work out a wider drug policy.

## The two republics afterwards

| | Slovakia | Czech Republic |
|---|---|---|
| Possession for own use not a crime | 1990 to 1994 | 1990 to 1998 |
| Recriminalised | 1 October 1994 | 1 January 1999, above a "small" amount |

Slovakia reversed the reform within two years of independence and has kept possession a crime since, with a penal reform in 2024 softening the sentences. The Czech Republic took a different road, and the gap between the two countries' scores today starts here. The same step is described for Czechia [here](/history/czechoslovakia-decriminalisation-1990/).

## Why the index moved

The index moved [Slovakia](/country/sk/) from 12 to 25 with the [1990 amendment](/history/slovakia-decriminalisation-1990/): possession from 1 to 3, enforcement from 2 to 5 and consumption from 2 to 4. Cultivation and sharing stay at 1 and access at 0, because growing and supplying remained crimes and there was nowhere lawful to obtain the drug.
