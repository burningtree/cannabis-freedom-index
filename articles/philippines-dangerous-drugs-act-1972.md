Republic Act 6425, the Dangerous Drugs Act of 1972, was approved on 30 March 1972, six months before martial law. It repealed Articles 190 to 194 of the Revised Penal Code and put one statute in their place, and it governed drug offences in the Philippines for thirty years.

## Prohibited and regulated

The Act sorts drugs into two classes. Prohibited drugs are opium and its derivatives, cocaine, hallucinogens such as LSD, and Indian hemp, "otherwise known as marijuana", defined as every kind and class of the plant with its resin and extracts. Regulated drugs are sedatives, amphetamines and hypnotics.

## The penalties

| Offence, prohibited drugs | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Importation (Section 3) | 14 years and 1 day to life, and a fine of 14,000 to 30,000 pesos |
| Sale, delivery, transport (Section 4) | 12 years and 1 day to 20 years |
| Keeping a den (Section 5) | 12 years and 1 day to 20 years |
| Manufacture (Section 7) | life imprisonment to death |
| Cultivation of plant sources (Section 9) | 14 years and 1 day to life; the land is confiscated |
| Possession or use (Section 8) | 6 years and 1 day to 12 years |
| **Possession or use of marijuana** (Section 8) | **6 months and 1 day to 6 years, and a fine of 600 to 6,000 pesos** |

The last row is the Act's one concession to cannabis. For possession and use, marijuana alone among the prohibited drugs gets a lower range. Growing it does not: the cultivation section makes no exception.

## Treatment in place of trial

The Act also created a route out of prosecution. A drug dependent who voluntarily enters a treatment centre and keeps to its conditions is exempt from criminal liability for possession or use. An accused found to be dependent can be committed for treatment, with the case suspended. A first offender under 21 may have sentence deferred and be placed on probation for six months to a year, with the record expunged on completion.

A nine-member Dangerous Drugs Board, three of them appointed by the President and six sitting as cabinet secretaries, was set up to run the system.

## Why the index moved

The index moved the [Philippines](/country/ph/) from 4 to 2 with the [1972 Act](/history/philippines-dangerous-drugs-act-1972/). Enforcement fell from 2 to 1: sale now started at twelve years and cultivation at fourteen. The lower range for marijuana possession kept it from falling to 0 until [1982](/history/philippines-penalties-raised-1982/).
