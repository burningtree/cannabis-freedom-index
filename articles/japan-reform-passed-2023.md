Japan's upper house, the House of Councillors, approved a comprehensive rewrite of the country's cannabis law on 6 December 2023. From 12 December 2024, using cannabis became a crime punishable by up to seven years in prison, while the same law removed the ban on medicines made from the plant. The reform moved possession and use into the narcotics law and left cultivation in a renamed statute.[@1,2]

## A law with a gap

The [Cannabis Control Act](/history/japan-cannabis-control-act-1948/) banned possession but said nothing about use itself. Police could arrest someone for holding cannabis, not for having consumed it. Arrests reached a record 5,783 in 2021, about 70% of them people in their twenties or younger, and the health ministry argued that the missing offence had made the drug seem less dangerous than others.[@1,3] Japan had opposed the UN drug commission's 2020 move to remove cannabis from its strictest schedule, saying the medical evidence was not strong enough and that looser rules could hurt young people.[@3] The ministry had also watched cannabidiol epilepsy medicines win approval in the United States and Europe while Japanese patients could not legally use them.[@3]

## What changed on paper

The amendments deleted the clause that barred cannabis-derived medicines and moved the plant into the Narcotics and Psychotropics Control Act. Unauthorised use carried up to seven years, and possession rose from five to seven. Transfer carried up to seven years, and import, export or manufacture one to ten.[@1,4] Growing stayed in the renamed Cannabis Cultivation Regulation Act. A licensing system followed on 1 March 2025: Type 1 growers produce industrial material under prefectural licences, Type 2 growers produce pharmaceutical ingredients under the health ministry, and a separate licence covers research. Type 1 growers must use seed from plants with no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC on a dry-weight basis.[@2,4]

## A narrow door for patients

The medical route ran through the pharmaceuticals regulator. Only a cannabis-derived medicine approved by the regulator and prescribed by a doctor could be used; there was no legal market for herbal cannabis.[@4] Jazz Pharmaceuticals' cannabidiol for severe childhood epilepsies was one candidate. Its Japanese Phase 3 trial in 62 children missed its main goal in August 2024, though the company said it would keep collecting data and discuss an application with regulators.[@5]

## What it meant

The use offence took effect on [12 December 2024](/history/japan-use-criminalised-2024/) and carried the same maximum as possession.[@1,4] The medical route remained open, but Jazz's candidate had missed its main trial goal.[@5] The reform had given patients a route to regulated medicines and made the act of use itself punishable.[@4] The [growing licences](/history/japan-cannabis-cultivation-licences-2025/) that followed in March 2025 completed the design: a tighter product regime alongside a narrow medical opening.[@2] The medical door opened only as far as the regulator allowed; the criminal one stayed wide. [Japan's profile](/country/jp/) sets out the changes and their limits.
