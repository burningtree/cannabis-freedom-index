In 1993 Sweden raised the penalty for using drugs from a fine to six months in
prison.[@1,3] The point was what the higher penalty allowed the police to do.[@1]

## How Sweden got strict

Cannabis came under Swedish drug rules [in 1930](/history/sweden-narcotics-decree-1930/).
The turn towards strictness began with an experiment in the other direction: from
1965 to 1967 doctors in Stockholm legally prescribed amphetamine and morphine to
users. It ended after a young woman died of an overdose of drugs traced to a
participant. The psychiatrist Nils Bejerot, who opposed the scheme, reported a sharp
rise in injecting during it.

The [Narcotic Drugs Penal Act](/history/sweden-narcotics-penal-act-1968/) of 1968
followed, and penalties were raised again in 1969, 1972 and 1981.

## Use becomes a crime

Possession was already illegal. From
[1 July 1988](/history/sweden-use-criminalised-1988/) so was having used a drug, in
any quantity, punishable by a fine.[@3]

## The six-month rule

In Swedish law the police may carry out a body search only when the suspected offence
carries at least six months in prison. A fine-only offence gave them no such power.
So in 1993 the maximum for drug use was raised to six months. From then on, a
person suspected of having taken a drug could be taken in for a urine or blood test.[@1]

The tests became routine: about 27,500 were carried out to verify drug use in 2006,
besides some 10,000 in traffic and other cases.[@1]

## What it achieved

Supporters point to low use: among 16-year-old boys it fell from about 16 per cent in
1971 to 5 per cent in 1983, and a UN report praised the results. Critics answer that
treatment resources shrank from the 1990s and that drug deaths roughly doubled
between 2005 and 2013, taking Sweden from ninth to second in Europe.[@1,2]

The Left Party now supports decriminalising private use, as do the youth wings of
five of the seven main parties. The government proposed tougher penalties in 2023.[@1,2]

The 1993 change moves no score for [Sweden](/country/se/): the 1988 law had already
set consumption at its lowest.
