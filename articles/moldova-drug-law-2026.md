Moldova's Law 201/2025 took effect six months after its publication on 28 August
2025. The wide-ranging drug-control law replaced the 1999 statute and set a
specific authorization route for industrial and horticultural hemp while keeping
cannabis cultivation for drugs prohibited.

## Cannabis and hemp separated

The law defines cannabis as the flowering or fruiting tops of plants in the genus
*Cannabis*, and their resin, when they contain enough tetrahydrocannabinol to
produce psychoactive effects. Seeds and leaves not accompanied by flowering tops
are excluded from that definition.

It separately defines industrial or horticultural hemp as a cannabis plant with
no more than 0.3% THC, confirmed by chemical analysis in an accredited laboratory.
The permitted purposes include fibre, stalks, seeds, oil, biomass and other
derivatives. Medical, recreational and experimental use are expressly outside
that hemp definition.

## Cultivation remains controlled

Article 14 prohibits unauthorised cultivation of plants under national control.
It also specifically prohibits growing controlled plants, including cannabis, to
obtain narcotic or psychotropic substances.

The exception covers industrial or horticultural hemp grown for the listed
materials and derivatives. That cultivation requires an authorization from the
National Food Safety Agency under regulations approved by the government and
within approved annual estimates. The agency receives and examines applications,
issues authorizations and supervises legal hemp cultivation.

The law also limits the starting material. Distributors of seed intended for
sowing may deliver it only to holders of the relevant cultivation authorizations.
The permitted varieties must be entered in the European Union's common catalogue
of agricultural plant varieties or in an EU member state's national catalogue.

## Replacing the earlier law

Law 201 was adopted on 10 July 2025 and published in Moldova's Official Monitor on
28 August. Article 80 made it effective after six months and repealed Law 382/1999
at that point. Existing permits issued under the earlier law remain valid until
their own expiry dates.

## In the index

The replacement law did not move [Moldova's](/country/md/) six scores. Personal
psychoactive cannabis cultivation remained prohibited, while the newly detailed
route concerns authorised low-THC hemp. The entry records the legal framework and
its effective date rather than an expansion of personal access.
