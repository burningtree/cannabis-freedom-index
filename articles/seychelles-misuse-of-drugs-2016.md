Seychelles changed how courts could punish cannabis offences when its replacement drug law took effect on 1 June 2016. Judges gained room to impose sentences below the old mandatory minimum, and prisoners could ask a tribunal to reconsider the time they still had to serve.

## Rising prison numbers pushed a new approach

Home Affairs Minister Charles Bastienne presented the replacement to parliament against a background of growing drug dependence and imprisonment. He described the increase in people serving drug sentences as worrying and unsustainable, even as detoxification services and programmes expanded.

Parliament approved the bill unanimously. Its stated aims combined stronger action against trafficking with treatment, education, rehabilitation and a return to ordinary life for people dependent on drugs. It replaced the [1990 drug law](/history/seychelles-misuse-of-drugs-act-1990/).

## A judge could consider the person before the court

The practical difference appeared in [Paul Ah Kong’s cannabis case](/history/seychelles-ah-kong-suspended-sentence-2016/) later that year. He admitted trafficking 115 grams, an offence prosecuted under the previous law that had carried a mandatory minimum of sixteen years.

Judge Burhan explained that the replacement had removed that minimum for the offence. Applying the principle that an offender should benefit from a favourable change in the law, he considered Ah Kong’s guilty plea, first conviction and family circumstances.

The result was two years in prison suspended for two years, together with a fine of 15,000 Seychelles rupees. A six-month prison term applied if the fine went unpaid.

## Prisoners could ask for a review

The reform also reached people already serving sentences under the previous law. In [June’s public notice](/history/seychelles-drug-sentence-review-2016/), the judiciary explained how to apply for a review of the outstanding portion of a term.

Applicants could represent themselves. The tribunal would consider the seriousness of the offence, the sentence available under the new law, prison conduct, rehabilitation and prospects of reintegration. It could confirm or change the sentence while leaving the conviction in place.

## Medical access needed further rules

The new law also allowed controlled drugs to be dealt with for medical or scientific purposes under regulations. The question of who could use cannabis medically soon reached the courts.

[Ralph Volcere sought access for his mother](/history/seychelles-volcere-medical-rules-2019/), who had Alzheimer’s disease. That case produced an order to write regulations in 2019. [CBD medicine rules followed in 2020](/history/seychelles-medical-2020/), adding a restricted patient route to the drug controls in [Seychelles](/country/sc/).
