Until December 2024 Japan's cannabis law had a gap that surprised everyone who noticed
it. Possessing cannabis was a serious crime. Using it was not an offence at all.

## The gap

The Cannabis Control Act banned growing, possessing and transferring cannabis. It said
nothing about consuming it. Prosecutors could reach a user only through a possession
charge, which meant finding the drug; a positive test alone was not enough.

The government came to see this as a weakness, especially with the young. Arrests for
cannabis were running at about 6,000 a year, rising fastest among people under 30.

## The reform

Amendments in force from 12 December 2024 did three things.

- **Use** became an offence in its own right, with up to seven years in prison.
- **Possession and transfer** now carry up to seven years, raised from five.
- **Medicines** derived from cannabis can be approved through the health ministry's
  normal procedure.

## The other half

The reform is usually described as a tightening, and for users it is. But it also ended
a ban on medical use that was almost as old as the law. Epidiolex, a cannabidiol
medicine for severe childhood epilepsy approved in the United States in 2018 and in
Europe in 2019, had been unavailable in Japan. There is now a route by which it and
similar medicines can be authorised.

The rules for CBD products were rewritten at the same time, with limits on THC set by
concentration. CBD from stalks or seeds remains permitted; from leaves or flowers it
does not.

## In the index

For an ordinary adult the change is all in one direction. The consumption score fell
from 1 to 0, since use is now itself a crime. [Japan](/country/jp/) was already near
the bottom of the index, and this took away the one point that had set it apart.
