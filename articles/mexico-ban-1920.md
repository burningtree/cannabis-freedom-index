Mexico banned marijuana seventeen years before the United States did. The decree said
why in its title: it was aimed at vices that "degenerate the race".

## A local matter first

Through the nineteenth century, health was the business of towns and states. Mexico City
[regulated marijuana](/history/mexico-city-marijuana-regulations-1869/) in 1869, with a
month in jail for possession, and Guanajuato, Oaxaca, Querétaro and Zacatecas followed
with rules of their own. The federal government stayed out. Its
[penal code of 1871](/history/mexico-federal-penal-code-1871/) made no drug a crime, and
its sanitary codes listed cannabis as a medicine.

## The revolution's constitution

That changed with the [1917 Constitution](/history/mexico-constitution-1917/). Delegates
steeped in the eugenic ideas of the time wrote into Article 73 a duty to campaign
against "substances that poison the individual and degenerate the race". Drug control
became a national task.

## The decree

The first law under that mandate came on 15 March 1920: "Provisions on the sale of
products that can be used to promote vices which degenerate the race and on the
cultivation of plants that can be used for the same purpose".

It drew a line between drugs. Opium, morphine, codeine and heroin were still treated as
medicines, under strict control of import and sale. Marijuana was taken off the list of
regulated medicines altogether. Selling it without a permit brought a fine, and so did
possessing it. Anyone who could not pay went to jail for fifteen days.

## Who it was for

The historian José Domingo Schievenini reads the decree as the work of a new elite
trying to re-found a state after a civil war. The drugs of the poor made a convenient
explanation for disorder that had more to do with the generals' own feuds. Marijuana
users were not yet criminals in the full sense. They were declared degenerate.

## What followed

The sanitary code of [1926](/history/mexico-sanitary-code-1926/) sent users to a ward in
the national asylum, and the penal codes of
[1929](/history/mexico-federal-penal-code-1929/) and
[1931](/history/mexico-federal-penal-code-1931/) made every act involving the plant a
"crime against health", with up to five years in prison.

In the index the 1920 decree is the point at which possessing and growing cannabis stop
being free in [Mexico](/country/mx/). The constitutional clause behind it is still in force, with "the
race" replaced by "the human species".
