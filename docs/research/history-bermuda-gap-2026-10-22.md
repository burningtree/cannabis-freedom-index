# Bermuda: medicines, decriminalisation, record clearing and blocked licensing

Researched 22 October 2026 after the verified Guam batch. Bermuda had five history
events and no articles. This pass adds seven tagged events and six articles, revises
four events and updates the profile. Current writing rules and category priority were
re-read before drafting. No subagents, commits or staging operations were used.

## Primary legal texts read

All local evidence is retained under `tmp/history-bermuda-gap-2026-10-22/`. The official
Bermuda Laws site returns PDFs for its older `/Laws/...` URLs, even though its homepage
is now a Blazor application. Those direct primary URLs were recovered and downloaded.
No browser automation or undocumented app API was needed.

- **2014 medicines law:** [Cannabinoid Pharmaceutical Products Act 2014](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2014/Cannabinoid%20Pharmaceutical%20Products%20Act%202014),
  four pages, full text read. Assent/operation **22 December 2014**. Section 2 adds
  dronabinol/Marinol and nabiximols/Sativex to the pharmacy prescription schedule;
  Section 4 lists these and nabilone/Cesamet in the drug regulations' medical schedule.
  Sections 2–3 also alter scheduling powers, medical advice and medical-purpose
  definitions; Section 5 revokes the former designation order. This is a verified
  pharmaceutical route predating the existing November 2016 court event. It is not
  evidence of unrestricted cannabis flower, oils, local growing or pharmacy stock on
  commencement day. [Contemporary Senate reporting](https://www.royalgazette.com/other/news/article/20141215/cannabis-medicine-bill-passes-in-senate/)
  was read for Michael Fahy's phased-reform explanation and Diallo Rabain's criticism
  that the range excluded other products. These are attributed political positions,
  not new medical efficacy findings.
- **2017 decriminalisation:** [original amendment](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2017/Misuse%20of%20Drugs%20%28Decriminalisation%20of%20Cannabis%29%20Amendment%20Act%202017),
  two pages, full text read; second page rendered and visually checked. Assent and
  operative date **20 December 2017**. Section 6(1) excludes cannabis at/below seven
  grams from the possession prohibition, subject to prosecution where evidence shows
  supply intent. Section 25A permits seizure of any amount and requires education/
  treatment regulations, especially for minors. The act does not itself prove actual
  programme delivery. Schedule 8 says **seven grams**, not strictly less than seven.
  A parliamentary draft surfaced with a June commencement clause; it is not the final
  enacted text and was not used to date the reform.
- **2019 hemp law:** [original amendment](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2019/Misuse%20of%20Drugs%20%28Hemp%29%20Amendment%20Act%202019),
  two pages, full text read. Assent/operation **26 November 2019**. Cannabis definition
  excludes hemp, resin and listed separated stalk/fibre/seed materials; hemp means
  cannabis sativa or a part with **not more than 1% THC**, subject to a ministerial order
  changing the concentration. Purpose explicitly concerns hemp imports, possession,
  supply and sale. Do not infer an unlimited authorisation for all processed cannabinoid
  products: resin and cannabinoids have separate controls. Current Section 11 still
  refers to cultivation of **any plant of the genus Cannabis**, subject to regulations;
  the hemp amendment did not rewrite that clause.
- **2020 record-clearing law:** [as-enacted law](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2020/Expungement%20of%20Convictions%20Act%202020),
  seven pages, full substantive text read. Assented **5 August 2020**, operative
  **11 March 2021**, not an immediate 2020 right. Sections 5–7 require an application
  and ministerial decision, allow appropriate representatives for deceased or disabled
  people, reasons within seven days of refusal and a 21-day Supreme Court appeal.
  Section 8 governs future treatment as not having committed/been prosecuted/convicted/
  sentenced and provides evidence, disclosure and employment protections. Section 4
  preserves the validity of the original conviction/sentence and creates no new right
  or liability: do not describe it as compensation, automatic release or blanket
  reimbursement of penalties. Original Schedule covers qualifying simple-possession
  conduct before **20 December 2017**. Contemporary commencement evidence explains
  seven grams or less. The entry records 11 March legal commencement and expressly
  says online applications launched the next day.
- **2023 missing-weight amendment:** [BR 87/2023](https://bermudalaws.bm/Document/7385175a-cd33-4c0d-8fde-5063aa7bb39d),
  two pages, full text read; page 1 rendered/visually checked. Made and operative
  **3 November 2023**, not the earlier announcement date. New Section 6A treats a
  relevant possession record omitting quantity as at/below the statutory threshold
  where the conviction predates 20 December 2017 and the penalty did not exceed a
  **$1,000 fine OR twelve months' conditional discharge**. It also expressly clears
  additional sentencing elements with a qualifying conviction and deletes “simple”
  from the Schedule. This is an application-based presumption, not an automatic
  declaration that every old conviction is erased or an exemption for a known amount
  exceeding seven grams.
- **Consolidations:** downloaded [Misuse of Drugs Act 1972](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1972/Misuse%20of%20Drugs%20Act%201972)
  and [Expungement Act](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/2020/Expungement%20of%20Convictions%20Act%202020).
  Read relevant definitions, Sections 4–12/25A, controlled-drug and seven-gram schedules,
  amendment notes and the record-clearing provisions. Do not portray the current 1972
  consolidation as the unamended 1972 law. Its note gives staged commencement between
  16 June and 8 September 1973; the as-enacted annual-law route returned 404. No invented
  first ban or single commencement date is published from that consolidation.

## Case, implementation and policy evidence

The [2016 contemporary account](https://www.royalgazette.com/other/news/article/20161125/people-can-apply-for-medical-cannabis/)
was read. It reports **9 November** hearing before judge Stephen Hellman in Michael
Brangman's case, confirming the minister's power to consider a personal medical import/
possession application, while retaining power to refuse. The article was published 25
November. The original written judgment/order was not recovered: the government's
2016 judgment index was downloaded and searched without a Brangman match. Do not
claim the court enacted general medical legalisation or awarded a licence. The event
is narrowed to the reported clarification and the original ministry power is separately
supported by the drug law. It becomes `other`, tagged court/patients.

[Prosecutors' 16 February 2017 release](https://www.gov.bm/news/formal-police-caution-simple-possession-cannabis-respect-offence-under-misuse-drugs-act)
expressly publishes **draft** caution guidance and ties commencement to the criminal-
procedure amendment. Under three grams, admission, no other drugs, first/second cautions,
then counselling/assessment/testing on a third incident. The new event is `debate` on the
publication date, not a false precisely dated commencement. The later [government fact
sheet](https://www2.gov.bm/articles/public-awareness-fact-sheet) says the caution policy
ceased to have effect. It supplies the remaining public/private consumption prohibition,
seizure/search explanation and the historical fact that the 2017 law did not erase prior
records. That last point is not repeated as a blanket account of today's law.

[18 December 2019 consultation](https://www.gov.bm/news/minister-presents-medicinal-cannabis-bill-consultation)
was read. Attorney-General Kathy Lynn Simmons proposed prescribed cannabis **for
inhalation**, licensing local production/manufacturing and an authority. It expressly
distinguishes the pharmaceutical products already available through existing laws.
[26 February 2020 statement](https://www.gov.bm/news/minister-proposes-new-cannabis-reform)
was downloaded/read: low consultation response, concerns about complexity/cost and
requests for broader regulation led to an expanded plan for personal/commercial growing,
retail and other activities. Both are `debate`, with no enacted local-growing or shop
access inferred from a proposal.

[12 March 2021 commencement/launch statement](https://www.gov.bm/news/ministerial-statement-expungement-convictions-act-2020)
was read: law effective 11 March, online process 12 March; effects of discriminatory
past enforcement, incomplete older records, and no guarantee that US authorities change
their own immigration decisions. [Application guidance](https://www.gov.bm/online-services/cannabis-conviction-expungement-application)
was also checked. No form was submitted or personal application data accessed.
[29 September 2023 announcement](https://www.gov.bm/news/intention-make-expungement-convictions-criminal-records-regulations-2023)
was read for the **roughly forty of fifty-one applications unprocessed**, not forty
refusals. The legal amendment was still awaiting formal publication; the actual November
regulation controls the event date. The minister described collaboration with police
on a solution and additional penalty-zone elements; no number of successful later orders
is invented.

The governor's [May 2022 statement](https://www.gov.bm/print/html/node/1175236) records
House approval **25 March, 18–6**, Senate tie/rejection **30 March**, and reserved assent.
Its page/signature dates differ within May, so no separate exact-day May event is
invented. The [UK statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/governor-of-bermuda-update-on-cannabis-licensing-bill)
is dated **6 September**, published 7 September. Retain the statement date. Attribute the
treaty-incompatibility conclusion to the UK; do not present the statement as a court
judgment or an uncontested universal interpretation. The bill never became the licensed
adult-use regime. The [23 July 2026 ministerial statement](https://www.gov.bm/news/advancing-public-safety-across-the-ministry-of-national-security)
was re-read: Ryan Robinson Perinchief prioritised hemp and medical work, with drafting
instructions and a Q4 target, while adult-use work would take longer. Rewrite the entry's
old closing disclaimer into that substantive explanation; keep `debate` and add patients
to its hemp tag. A target is not evidence of commencement.

## Corrections and scoring boundaries

The inherited **one-point medical-access step moves from the 2016 event to the 2014
named-medicines law**. The latter takes the former's exact `before` vector; the 2016
context event loses its vector. This is a correction to the timing/cause of an existing
step, without a change in the current numeric ratings or total. The law expressly names
permitted medicines and the later government medical proposal acknowledges existing
pharmaceutical access. Do not infer universal availability, affordable prescriptions,
actual stock on 22 December 2014, or the first discretionary medical licence ever.
Earlier general ministerial powers and the wider historical medical baseline still merit
research. Every other prior `before` vector is untouched.

The current scores, recreational/medical status and dependency metadata are preserved,
not certified afresh. The 1925 first-restriction estimate is unresolved. Mechanical
score continuity does not verify a local prohibition based only on international-treaty
dates, nor current prosecution frequencies. Expungement events are `easing`, tagged
`amnesty`, and explicitly unscored because they concern historical records, with current
offence/penalty bands unchanged. Hemp is `hemp`; proposals are `debate`; medicinal
pharmaceutical permission is `medical`. All eleven added/revised entries have tags.

The profile replaces “no medical cannabis law” with the actual 2014 prescription route,
removes the unqualified claim that prior possession convictions stand, and describes the
2021 application/2023 presumption. It removes the unsupported **2,000 g per patient per
year** entitlement: the contemporary report discusses an import quota and pending
commercial import application, not a verified statutory allowance for each patient. No
new quota event is published without the primary instrument. It also replaces the
unsupported claim that no clubs are known with the legal restrictions on collective
supply/growing. Searches returning this project's live website were excluded as circular
evidence.
