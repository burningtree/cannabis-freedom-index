## Measurement and classification

Blood serum and whole blood are different sample matrices. Do not directly compare
Germany's serum value with the British whole-blood value or infer that one rule is
more permissive simply because its number is larger. A roadside screening result is
not itself a quantified evidential blood result.

The `driving.status` field summarises the legal trigger, not road safety or a waiting
time after consumption. `limit` denotes a quantified threshold; `zero` denotes a
presence-based prohibition; `impairment` denotes an ability/intoxication standard in
the provision reviewed. A country can have additional offences alongside that
headline rule. Medical prescribing does not create a general permission to drive
while impaired.

No fixed “safe after X hours” advice was added. The two books supplied earlier are
historical sources and do not establish present-day traffic penalties. The profile
citations link to the official sources actually read.

Remaining gaps include medical/novice rules in the new profiles, fuller testing
procedures and a primary-source review of the Dutch penalties. Ukraine's official
code was inaccessible in this session, so no Ukrainian driving section was added.

### Findings retained for further classification review

These findings were researched but **not** added to the profiles. The schema's
`zero` category means detectable THC and `impairment` requires demonstrated impairment.
A prohibition on driving after consumption alone does not establish either evidential
standard. Assigning a category without examining regulations or case law would
claim more than the statute supports.

- **Bangladesh:** [Road Transport Act 2018](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1262.html),
  section 49(1), first part (a), prohibits driving after drinking alcohol or consuming
  intoxicants. Section 92(1) provides up to three months' imprisonment, up to Tk10,000,
  or both, and deduction of one driver point for violating that first part. The
  Tk25,000 figure in section 89 concerns section 46 and must not be substituted for
  the intoxication provision. The current official Bengali text was read in full;
  an evidential THC threshold and fixed driving-ban duration were not established.
- **Sri Lanka:** Motor Traffic Act section 151 prohibits driving on a road after
  alcohol or drug consumption. Section 151(1C) provides medical examination for
  suspected drug use; section 151(1D) permits regulations about drug concentrations.
  No operative THC regulation was verified. The [official Motor Traffic (Amendment)
  Act No. 10 of 2019](https://www.parliament.lk/uploads/acts/gbills/english/6132.pdf),
  sections 7–8, updates the ordinary offence to LKR25,000–30,000 or up to three months'
  imprisonment or both, plus suspension up to twelve months. Public passenger
  carriage has up to six months and licence cancellation. The underlying trigger
  and testing provisions were read in a [third-party reproduction of the 2024
  consolidation](https://lankalaw.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Motor-Traffic-Consolidated-2024.pdf);
  a primary-source consolidation and implementing regulations remain to be checked.

Follow-up research should resolve these consumption-based rules before classifying
them, and establish Nigeria's testing and disqualification rules. No waiting time
or general medical exemption was inferred from silence in these sources.

### Liberia

The government-published PDF was downloaded and its complete relevant clauses
read. Section 10.90(1) covers driving ability impaired by alcohol or a narcotic
drug; subsection (3) separately covers non-narcotic drugs causing dizziness or
inability to drive safely. Subsection (2)'s habitual-narcotic-user prohibition
does not state a contemporaneous impairment requirement. Subsection (4)
expressly excludes lawful entitlement to drug use as a defence.

Section 10.90(5) lists a $1,000 ceiling **or** one year's imprisonment, not
both by default. The profile labels this as published statutory wording;
no unsupported conversion into US dollars, current Liberian dollars or a modern
ticket amount was made. Section 2.61(b) requires licence revocation after a
section 10.90 conviction. Section 2.69 permits a new application after a year
from surrender/receipt by the Minister, subject to the cause for revocation and
investigation of character, habits and driving ability. This is not automatic
reinstatement after a fixed one-year suspension.

Section 10.92's deemed consent to breath, urine or blood samples is expressly
for measuring alcohol and requires reasonable grounds for alcohol-impaired
operation. Section 10.94's refusal hearing likewise identifies alcohol.
Its one-year refusal revocation and hearing mechanism were therefore not
presented as an established cannabis-refusal penalty. For the alcohol procedure,
section 10.92 also records information and independently selected testing rights
and prohibits forcibly taking a specimen against physical resistance.

still invokes the Vehicle and Traffic Law; this is evidence of the framework's
use, not certification of each old penalty. A 2025–2026 reform/validation process
was located, but no final enacted replacement was established. Current drug

### Sierra Leone

### Bahrain: the original English version is not the amended tariff

Read the official [Arabic original Traffic Law 23/2014](https://www.lloc.gov.bh/Legislation/HTM/K2314),
its linked English DOCX, [Law 37/2018](https://www.lloc.gov.bh/Legislation/HTM/K3718),
the [official amendment catalogue](https://www.lloc.gov.bh/Legislation/Amendments/K2314)
and [Decree-Law 30/2025](https://www.lloc.gov.bh/Legislation/HTM/L3025).
The catalogue identifies Gazette 3828, published 21 August 2025; amendment
article 3 commences the following day, **22 August 2025**, rather than the
19 August signature date.

Article 35's original administrative rules remain separate: six-month licence
withdrawal for the drug-influence/consumption-during-driving offence or refusing
an authorised examination/trying to escape. Repeating the act within a year
of the prior act produces a one-year administrative period, and repetition
again produces two years. The examination method is delegated to arrangements
between the Interior and Health ministers; an operational cannabis sampling
matrix or quantitative cutoff was not established.

The 2025 replacement article 51 changes ordinary criminal ranges to two months–
one year and BHD1,000–2,000, or either punishment. A further crime or traffic-rule
violation caused by that driving raises the ranges to four months–two years and
BHD2,000–4,000, or either. Doubling on repetition within a year uses the date of
judgment; do not confuse it with article 35's administrative act-date clock.
The published original English still shows the older one-month minimum and
BHD500–1,000 ordinary fine, so it was not used for the new tariff.

Amended article 54 permits an additional court licence suspension up to one year.
Its commencement depends on the execution, suspension or substitution of the
sentence, with further timing qualifications in the amended paragraph. It is
not an automatic immediate one-year ban, and is distinct from article 35.

New article 51 bis supplies separate fatal-collision tiers: drug influence or
consumption during driving causing death has two–seven years and BHD3,000–10,000,
or either penalty. Combining it with specified red-light, speed, wrong-way or
yellow-line emergency-lane violations increases the prison range to three–ten
years. Article 54 bis permits confiscation in specified aggravated cases while
protecting good-faith third parties. These are not ordinary drug-driving tariffs.
The added injury provisions also have distinct grades; the country text flags
the separate harm offences without flattening all of them into one sentence.

### Kuwait: read the replacement article, not the stale police page

The court must withdraw the licence for one–three years; on repetition it may
