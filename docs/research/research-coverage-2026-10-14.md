## Botswana: a substantive correction

Read the Attorney General's current [drug-law consolidation](https://e-laws.botswanalaws.com/principal-legislation/illicit-traffic-in-narcotic-drugs-and-psychotropic-substances/),
[Act 26 of 2025](https://e-laws.botswanalaws.com/PDF/Bulletin/Botswana2025pdf/026.pdf)
and [commencement record](https://e-laws.botswanalaws.com/act-bulletin-2025/act-26-of-2025-illicit-traffic-in-narcotic-drugs-and-psychotropic-substances-amendment-act/).
The original PDF is scanned: pages 2–3 were rendered and read visually. The
commencement record identifies **22 December 2025**, through S.I. 163/2025.

Section 4(2A)–(2E) covers **60 g or less**, with up to P10,000 or prescribed
alternatives, an express exclusion of imprisonment, 21-day payment notices,
mandatory rehabilitation as prescribed and non-custodial alternatives for
nonpayment. The previous profile's up-to-three-years statement for this category
was obsolete. Implementation of programmes and post-amendment sentencing were
not verified. Preparations have a separate paragraph 4(1A), and the larger-quantity
paragraphs leave exactly 1 kg unresolved. Do not extend the low-level protection
to every preparation, supply offence or importation.

The [Road Traffic Act](https://e-laws.botswanalaws.com/principal-legislation/road-traffic/)
already prohibits drug-unfitness driving in section 46(2), with section 46(4)
penalties. This contradicts the broad legal characterisation reported in the
February 2026 announcement; retain that as an attributed proposal rather than
proof of an enacted change. Section 53(3)'s two–five-year licence rule overlaps
with subsection (3A)'s one–three-year rule. The profile preserves this inconsistency
rather than choosing a range without case law. Section 47's alcohol testing power
must not be described as a cannabis breath-test regime. Section 48 drug specimen
evidence and refusal consequences were separately read.

Scores remain unchanged: possession is still a criminal offence, consistent with
the existing possession level 1. No post-reform practice finding justifies changing
the overall enforcement score. The new easing event is unscored context.

## Tanzania: mainland and Zanzibar distinguished

Read the [official 2023 revised Road Traffic Act](https://elibrary.osg.go.tz/items/68154db4-f433-4bc1-8f5f-4f9e7c6c615d),
published by the Government Printer and hosted by the Solicitor General. Its
renumbered drug-driving offence is **section 53**, not section 44 as in older
editions. Sections 33(1)(a) and 75(2)(b) supply the licence and court consequences.
The 2025 upload date does not turn these figures into a verified 2026 ticket tariff.

Also downloaded the [Zanzibar Road Transport Act 7 of 2003](https://www.zanzibarassembly.go.tz/storage/documents/acts/english/all/1674632166.pdf).
Its PDF has no extractable text. Printed pages 81–82, PDF pages 93–94, were rendered
and read: section 123 distinguishes driving, being in charge, and repeat licence
consequences. Later Zanzibar amendments remain unverified. No currency conversion,
THC threshold or nationwide identical sanction was inferred.

## Mauritius: verified rule, incomplete current sanctions

Read official [Act 6 of 2019](https://landtransport.govmu.org/Documents/Legislations/act0619.pdf),
[Act 6 of 2022](https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/act0622.pdf)
and [Act 15 of 2026](https://president.govmu.org/president/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/15_The-Road-Traffic-Amendment-Act-2026.pdf).
The 2022 law corrects the schedule numbering: zero-tolerance THC appears in the
Thirteenth Schedule, with the field assessment in the Fourteenth. Read section
123G's screening, laboratory and warned-refusal distinctions. The government's
[2024–25 performance report](https://mroiti.govmu.org/AnnualReports/Annual%20Report%20on%20Performance%20FY%202024-25_Rodrigues%20%26%20Outer%20Islands%20Division.pdf)
confirms drug-driving laboratory reporting since July 2022; these cases concern
all drugs, not cannabis alone.

The ministry's `Road Traffic Act-I9.pdf` is an older reprint and the registrar's
`ROAD_TRAFFIC_ACT.pdf` is an incomplete extract. Neither supplies a complete current
consolidation. Act 15/2026 has phased proclamation, including changes to interim
licence orders and new vehicle seizure provisions. No blanket commencement date
or current ordinary tariff was assigned without the operative proclamation.

An indexed GIS announcement at `?id=2356&page_id=4042` describes September 2026
implementation but live retrieval returns “No communique found”. Its presentation
also risks merging fatal-collision fines and ordinary driving penalties. It was
not used as a current tariff source. A private consolidation was useful for locating
issues but was not substituted for primary enacted text. Current sanctions,
medical defences and seizure commencement remain explicit follow-up gaps.
