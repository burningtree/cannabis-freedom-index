# National scores follow nationwide laws

23 October 2026. Issues 8, 9 and 10. Read `docs/DATA-MAINTENANCE.md` (the score
timeline, and the note that Act XII of 1896 had to be read before it was
scored) and `docs/WRITING.md`.

## What was wrong

Madagascar's only scored event was the 1787 Merina smoking ban in central
Imerina. That one step carried today's possession, cultivation, enforcement,
sharing, consumption and access scores. The colony-wide 1915 decree, the 1958
cultivation and possession penalties, and the 1960 sentence increase were
unscored for that reason.

India's 1793 Bengal retail licence was the first scored event for the whole
modern country, a drop from 100 to 96. Act XII of 1896 then dropped the
national score from 96 to 40. A Burma reprint of that act, already read for
the Myanmar gap note, extends it to the North-Western Provinces and Oudh, the
Punjab, the Central Provinces, Burma, Coorg, and Ajmere and Merwara. Bengal
stayed under its own excise law. It is not a law for all of British India.

South Africa's first scored event was the 1903 Orange River Colony ordinance,
so the national score left 100 in 1903. The first nationwide prohibition is
the 1922 Customs and Excise amendment.

## What the scores do now

Current profile scores are unchanged.

Madagascar: 1915 is the first national step, from 100 to 55. Preparing,
selling and consuming smoking cannabis became offences, with up to a year in
prison or a fine. Possession and cultivation were not yet named offences, so
those segments stay at 10. In 1958 they become crimes with eleven days to one
month or a fine (55 to 10). In 1960 the same conduct carries six months to
five years or a fine (10 to 3): possession and cultivation move to 0, and
enforcement moves from 2 to 1. In 1997 small personal quantities, including
growing, are two to six months or a fine, and use is scored with possession
(3 to 9, the current profile). The 1787 event stays in the history as a
regional, uncertain, briefly applied smoking ban, with no score.

India: 1793, 1896 and the 1925 Central Provinces charas ban are unscored
provincial events, so neither Bengal's licence nor Act XII is a dated
nationwide step. The 1985 ban still starts from the licensed-shop baseline
(40: possession 5, cultivation 2, enforcement 5, sharing 3, consumption 4,
access 5), because state excise and licensed sale in much of the country are
documented and a score of 100 would read as no cannabis rule at all. The ban
falls from 40 to 11. The 2001 quantity bands still rise from 11 to 13.

South Africa: the Orange River Colony ordinance, the Cape poison listing and
the 1870 Natal enabling power are unscored. The 1922 national ban falls from
100 to 11, and the later steps are unchanged.

`npm run history:test` checks that 1787, 1793, 1896 and 1903 do not move a
national score, and that those regional events are not the first scored step
for their modern country.

Bangladesh still starts in 1793 because its entries place the Bengal licence in
territory that is now Bangladesh, including the Naogaon growing tract.
Pakistan's 1914 first step is the Punjab excise act, with Baluchistan's own
regulation the next year. That chain remains the documented licensed trade
the 1979 prohibition replaced.
