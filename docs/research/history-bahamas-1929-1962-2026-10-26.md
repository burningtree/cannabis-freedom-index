# Research pass: Bahamas 1929 lineage and 1962 amendment from UN primaries

Research checked on 26 October 2026. The Bahamas' Wiki-only 1962 definition
entry is now sourced to the amending Act itself as filed with the United
Nations, with an exact assent date and the conspiracy section the amendment
added; the 1929 first-ban entry gains the Act's verified later lineage. The
1929 Act's own text remains untraced. No scores, profiles or timelines
changed.

## What was verified (all sources actually read)

### 1962 Indian hemp definition (entry upgraded, no new article)

- UN E/NL.1963/11 (27 May 1963), Bahama Islands filing, read in full via
  the UN Digital Library: The Dangerous Drugs Amendment Act, No. 26 of 1962,
  assented 23 May 1962 by Governor Stapledon. It deletes the section 2
  definition of "Indian Hemp" in Ch. 44 and substitutes: "Indian Hemp"
  includes all parts of the plant cannabis sativa whether growing or not
  from which the resin has not been extracted, the extracted resin, and every
  compound, manufacture, salt derivative, mixture or preparation of plant or
  resin. It also inserts section 25A: conspiracy to commit or abet any
  offence under the Act carries the same punishment and forfeiture as the
  offence, including agreement with a person beyond the jurisdiction.
- Entry `bahamas-indian-hemp-definition-1962`: date refined 1962 to
  1962-05-23, description rewritten with per-sentence citations, Wiki source
  replaced by the UN filing. Category stays `other`: the amendment broadened
  a definition and added an ancillary offence rather than creating a new
  possession/cultivation ban with a scorable step. Too thin for a standalone
  article; left as an entry.

### 1929 first ban (entry strengthened, text still untraced)

- UN E/NL.1953/42-43 (2 April 1953), read in full: 1952 Dangerous Drugs
  (Dispensing of Narcotics) Rules and Sale of Drugs Rules made under "The
  Dangerous Drugs Act (Ch. 12 of 1938–1939)", published in the Bahamas
  Official Gazette 18 December 1952. "Narcotics" there means morphine,
  opium, codeine, heroin, cocaine and their salts; no cannabis inference
  drawn from it.
- UN E/NL.1960/7 (20 March 1960), read: Dangerous Drugs Amendment Act No. 55
  of 1959 (assented 3 September 1959), amending section 25 (now of Ch. 44)
  on possession/storage offences.
- Entry `bahamas-dangerous-drugs-1929` keeps its 27 February 1929 date from
  the Wikipedia article (only source for the original Act) but now traces
  the Act's continuation: revised as Ch. 12 of 1938–39, then Ch. 44,
  amended 1959. No article: the 1929 text itself is still missing.

## Dead ends recorded, not forced

- The 1929 Act text: not in the UN filings found (earliest Bahamas filing
  located is E/NL.1953/42-43, rules only). Bahamas Gazette 1929 not
  digitized in any reachable collection; OAS CICAD historical compendium
  link is dead; CRL holds only an unidentified single page of a 1926
  ordinance from another colony's bound volume.
- Yugoslav Criminal Code of 1929 (rs/hr/si/ba first bans): enactment 27
  January 1929 and force 1 January 1930 confirmed via the Pastovic
  unification paper, but the "up to 6 months for serving narcotics" article
  text not found in a readable source (Google Books API quota-exhausted,
  Hathi behind a bot check, Serbian digital library TLS failure). Left
  untouched.
- Ethiopia 1930 Penal Code drug provisions: the 1930 code's existence is
  well attested but no readable text or secondary confirming narcotics
  coverage found; the generic Duvall-based [1900, 1920] estimate stands.
  The 1957-code commentary blog post only illuminates the 1957 code (arts.
  510, 773–788).
