## Corrections and score reconstruction

The 1958 order’s preamble expressly concerns stopping **medical** cannabis use.
Its translated operative wording is “import, consumption and sale for medical
purposes”. The previous article asserted non-medical coverage without discussing
that limitation, then described site methodology instead of the event. The new
account follows the stated medical purpose and reproduces the scope in paraphrase.
The Portuguese original remains a retrieval task: the English modifier’s scope
should not be made to prove a general recreational ban.

Removed the 1958 `before` vector and supplied an explicit `unscored` explanation.
Its former score step assumed a general consumption offence and closure of
recreational access. Those assumptions are unsupported by the translated order’s
medical purpose. The first documented broad recreational restriction is now the
1970 extension, with the unregulated vector as its `before`. The 1958 event stays
in the timeline as the earliest documented cannabis restriction; category `ban`
follows the first-restriction priority rule even though no recreational score is
assigned. This does not prove the absence of an earlier general restriction.

The 1970 entry is explicitly the extension order’s **Portuguese publication**,
26 October. It directed overseas publication; the actual São Tomé bulletin and
local commencement date remain unverified. The existing dated anchor is retained
with that limitation made public. The decree itself was published **3 September**,
not 30 September as the CEJ manual’s citation appears to say. Its article 2 fines
are **10,000–100,000 escudos** for the ordinary offence and **5,000–50,000** for
personal/non-profit conduct. The previous article’s 5,000–500,000 and 500–50,000
figures were wrong. Article 3’s lawful-purpose exceptions and the extension’s
overseas prescription rules are retained.

The 2012 code was published 6 August, with commencement three months later:
**6 November 2012**, already correctly dated in the event. Trafficking is article
**279**, not 278. Article 283 retains personal use as a crime, permits a fine,
sets higher possession/acquisition/cultivation penalties above a three-day
quantity, and permits exemption for an occasional consumer in the basic case.
The previous consumption score 1 implied lightly enforced illegality without
supporting practice evidence. Corrected it to **0, “Use itself is a crime”**, and
also corrected the 2012 `before` consumption value to 0 so the code does not carry
an invented consumption tightening. The 1970 law already criminalised personal
drug conduct and specified consumption offences. Current exact score is **9**,
previously 10. Other current component scores and both legal-status labels stay
unchanged. Possession’s 2012 easing is still 0→1. The code event is now `other`: it
also raised ordinary trafficking’s ceiling from eight to twelve years, so its
combination of lighter user penalties and harsher supply penalties is a mixed reform. The reconstructed chain is
100→7 in 1970, then 7→9 in 2012, with no direction warnings.

The profile’s unsupported claim that the Penal Code gives the health ministry
medical/veterinary/scientific/teaching authorisation powers was removed. The drug
chapter refers to authorised activities but does not supply that claimed detailed
licensing scheme. Generic authorisation references do not establish patient
access, so medical classification stays `none`; no operating medical programme
or cannabis medicine supply has been verified.

## The separate 2012 law remains an important legal retrieval task

The CEJ manual says parliament approved the separate drug law on **18 October
2011** and it was promulgated on **2 October 2012**, becoming Law 11/2012. The new
event records promulgation, expressly distinguished from commencement. A Coimbra
legislation bibliography and a police thesis identify the gazette date as
**28 December 2012**; the original gazette was not retrieved. Macau’s official
CPLP legal catalogue confirms the law’s existence but requires login to read it.
The São Tomé parliamentary mirror was inaccessible. No authentication was bypassed.

The manual explains that the code and separate law partly duplicate offences
with different wording. Its authors favour the code’s criminal penalties and
retention of the separate law’s other rules, including procedural provisions.
The profile attributes that interpretation where it introduces the penalty
account. It is not presented as a controlling judicial decision. Obtain the full
gazette, amendments and local case law before claiming the overlap is definitively
resolved or certifying a current medical licensing system.

## Enforcement dates and limits

The 2017 seizure involved **more than** one kilogram and a Lagos–São Tomé ship;
two detainees, no verified sentence. The new article supplies statutory and later
enforcement context while keeping the report’s allegation status.

Operation 971 is dated **2024-07**, the precision supplied by the retrospective
2025 report. Nine detainees were seven men and two women, all São Toméan. A first
judicial hearing is not a conviction. No exact day, crop count or final sentences
were added.

The 29 July 2025 park operation involved about 30 grams of cannabis **and three
doses of amphetamines**, with three trafficking suspects and bail. The amount
does not prove a fixed cannabis trafficking threshold or a conviction. Neither
enforcement report justifies changing the national enforcement score or estimating
annual arrests. New entries are unscored context.
