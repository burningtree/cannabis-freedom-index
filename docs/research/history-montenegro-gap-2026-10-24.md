## 2026 amendment: distinguish the proposal from the enacted text

The cabinet approved a drug-law proposal on 17 July; its announcement was published on 20 July. The complete government PDF (`75bcde52-c123-45eb-a6ea-1f8148f93bde`) is explicitly marked **PREDLOG**. Read the operative draft, especially definitions, EUDA coordination/data reporting, police possession, advertising, early warning and changes to individual penalties. It keeps the 0.2% industrial-hemp definition, existing possession fine and permit system, while adding an individual advertising offence and new-psychoactive-substance controls. These are presented as **proposals** in a debate entry.

The Official Gazette registry (`https://www.sluzbenilist.me/propisi/396958`) records a drug-law amendment in Gazette 117/2026, published and effective **7 August 2026**. This enacted milestone is mentioned with the registry as source; detailed draft wording is not silently presented as the final law. The full final act remains a retrieval task.

The parliamentary legislative-committee report of 28 July was read through web extraction (`https://zakoni.skupstina.me/zakoni/web/dokumenta/sjednice-radnih-tijela/4543/12250-24-7-26-2-.pdf`). It records accepted technical amendments, including correction of the proposed advertising fine's cross-reference and conjunction. A publicly accessible Kruna article-52 preview, though labelled with the 2026 amendment, omitted the newly proposed point 9 even after the committee had approved its correction. Consequently it is not relied upon as a complete current consolidation. No authentication or free-reading limit was bypassed.

The profile retains the existing personal-possession fine and supply penalties, now tied to more recent statutory reproductions. The draft and publicly indexed 2026 criminal-code amendment lists did not identify a change to those core penalties; this is not certification of all final 2026 wording. Retrieve the full final drug-law amendment and criminal-code amendments in Gazettes 117/2026 and 137/2026 before asserting exhaustive current coverage. The draft alone is insufficient for adding an enacted advertising offence to the profile.

It explicitly dates the fine and hemp provisions to the **2013 consolidated text**, avoiding an unsupported claim that the exact later wording first applied in 2011. Original 2011 and 2013-amendment FAO PDFs returned access errors and require later retrieval.
- The 2014 hemp implementation is unscored: it details an existing licensed food/industrial route. It does not prove a new personal-growing entitlement. The statutory misdemeanour for breaching hemp permits does not establish that every few-plant personal grow avoids the criminal production offence. Obtain judicial treatment of that boundary before changing cultivation 1.
