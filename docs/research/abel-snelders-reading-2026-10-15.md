## Evidence and limits

The Dutch offence percentages describe recorded offences, not national prevalence.
Seizures are detected quantities, not complete imports. Snelders calls the common
one-in-ten interception assumption arbitrary before using it for reconstruction.
His consumption calculation also makes assumptions about regular use and joint
size. None is imported as a measured total.

Kralingen's 70–80% is an undercover-police estimate about a particular festival
crowd, quoted through Snelders from De Kort. No national extrapolation or claim
of a newly examined police archive. The existing event already mentions the festival;
no separate festival event was manufactured from this passage.

Abel's chapter includes period racial labels, alarmist official statements and
sweeping explanations of musicians' motives. These are not treated as current
science or as uniform experiences of Black communities. New Orleans press reports
and the Fossier/Stanley publications remain leads for examination of the originals.

The tea-pad entry has an older March 1931 date for Armstrong's arrest, while the
existing timeline uses 1930. This discrepancy remains a specific follow-up for
original court/news documentation; this pass does not silently invent an exact date.
