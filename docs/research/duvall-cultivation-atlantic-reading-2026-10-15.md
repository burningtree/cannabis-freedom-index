## Evidence cautions and next work

- Clarke's account explicitly distinguishes Maconie and Makiah. In the BSI copy,
  the word on p. 10 is typographically/OCR distorted; p. 11 prints Makiah clearly.
- Clarke's statements about indigenous status, intoxicating effects and medicine
  are historical claims, not botanical conclusions or treatment advice. His
  language about “frenzy” is not a neutral account of present-day effects.
- Duvall p. 136 gives eight days for the 1830 Rio ordinance; existing site sources
  say up to three. Do not silently switch the penalty on this basis. Retrieve the
  original provision, and distinguish its text from later quotations. Existing
  score data and that article were not changed in this pass.
- Duvall's reference to Dória in 1917 may concern publication rather than the 1915
  congress. No event date changed without separate bibliographic verification.
- Retain unreviewed notes and pp. 140–158 of chapter 7 as follow-up, before chapters
  8–9 on labour, buying and banning. Chapter 2 and the rest of chapter 1 remain unread.
