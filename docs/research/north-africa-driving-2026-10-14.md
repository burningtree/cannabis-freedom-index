## Morocco

Read the General Secretariat of Government's [Arabic consolidation of Road Code
52-05](https://www.sgg.gov.ma/Portals/1/textesconsolides/52_05.pdf), dated **10 July
2024**, including article 183 as amended in 2016. Extracted text was cleaned only
of bidirectional control characters and whitespace to make it readable; statutory
words and numbers were retained.

- Article 183: six months–one year and MAD5,000–10,000, or either punishment;
  court-ordered suspension six months–one year. Repeat within five years of a final
  judgment for similar conduct doubles both penalties and suspension.
- The provision includes accompanying instructors, narcotics, specified prohibited
  medicines and refusal of the cited tests/checks. It uses influence wording; no
  numeric THC threshold or medical-cannabis exception was verified.
- Article 99: six points for drug-influence driving or the listed refusals. This
  is separate from court suspension and is not automatically an immediate roadside
  deduction. The table distinguishes prohibited medicines with a different point row.
- Articles 213–215: screening followed by medical, clinical and biological checks
  after a positive result, refusal or inability to screen; the interval is to be
  as short as possible within the same day. No laboratory cutoff was inferred.

The [NARSA educational page](https://www.narsa-securiteroutiere.ma/fr/drogue-au-volant-connaissez-vous-les-risques/)
was also read. Its general language about immediate points was not substituted for
article 99. No numerical crash-risk claim from that page was imported. The official
consolidation itself says Gazette texts govern. Later amendments after July 2024
remain unverified and the profile states that limit.

## Algeria

Read [Official Gazette 36 of 17 May 2026](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2026/F2026036.pdf),
containing **Law 26-09 of 12 May 2026**. Used both layout and raw text extraction
because the PDF's two columns can interleave related provisions. Promulgation
on 12 May is distinguished from Gazette publication on 17 May; the event is dated
to promulgation, not an invented nationwide commencement date.

Article 137 covers drivers and accompanying learner supervisors: one–two years
and DZD100,000–200,000. Article 143 extends the relevant intoxication penalties to
refusal of prescribed examinations. Article 160 permits, rather than invariably
requires, licence withdrawal/suspension up to five years. Article 173 addresses
vehicle immobilisation on suspected drug influence, a separate measure.

Articles 79–80 establish professional transport recruitment analyses and periodic
and unannounced examinations. Article 105 provides for detection equipment whose
specification requires regulation; no particular device's nationwide deployment
or THC cutoff was established. Articles 187–190 address implementing rules,
repeal of Law 01-14, retention of its implementing texts and publication. The new
code must not be presented using old offence numbers or old penalty ranges.

No claim was made that drug driving was first criminalised in 2026, or that the
new ordinary sanction was higher than every prior amended sanction. This is an
unscored `other` event documenting the replacement statutory framework. Cannabis
possession and supply scores are untouched.

## Tunisia deferred

The file is the **2012** Official Printing Office edition; its website upload date
is not the date of the law. Its dedicated intoxication and refusal clauses reviewed
refer to alcohol, including articles 92–93 on licence withdrawal. The file also
contains driving-training and medical-aptitude materials, which must not be
mistaken for a cannabis-specific penal offence.

The server certificate was expired; the public PDF was downloaded with certificate
verification disabled, without credentials. A current drug-specific offence,
applicable evidential standard and later amendments were not established. No
Tunisia driving field was added and no conclusion that drug driving is lawful was
inferred from this research gap.
