## Changes and corrections

- The 2009 small-personal-possession event now explains the fine/up-to-three-year offence, existing possibility of waiver, separate cultivation offence and later 2019 changes. It remains **other**, because separating offences was not itself decriminalisation and personal-use waiver already existed.
- The controlled-substances law entry uses **22 December 2010**, the Assembly’s adoption date, and explicitly describes adoption. Its original text, not just the 2018 consolidation, confirms the cannabis threshold, licensed hemp purposes and destruction duties. Commencement is not equated with adoption.
- Added the **1 August 2013** hemp rulebook, requiring registered varieties **below 0.3% THC**, an application before sowing/by **30 April**, seed and land records, eligibility documents and a permit for one production year. This is hemp regulation, not adult home-growing legalisation.
- The patients’ request is corrected to **18 February 2015**: the report published on 19 February says it was delivered the previous day. The event is **debate**, replacing the previous medical category, and identifies Milan Savić and the association’s request without endorsing the claimed treatment outcomes.
- The ministry committee’s 2016 recommendation is **debate**, dated **January**, as the exact committee decision day was not established. Contemporary reporting says registration and prescribing arrangements were still being pursued. The article does not claim pharmacies actually stocked the medicines.
- The 2017 Green Party bill now has a parliamentary source. Added the **26 March 2018** agenda refusal, with **ten supporting votes and 149 MPs not voting**. This was refusal to schedule consideration, not a merits vote rejecting the bill. The article connects the earlier patient campaign, medicine proposals, bill and agenda outcome.
- Added the **23 January 2019** court judgment upholding Marko Luković’s three-year sentence for production/possession for sale after collecting flowers near the Morava, drying and packaging them. The judgment distinguishes cultivation from further processing. It does not establish that he grew those plants, so no such claim is made.
- Added the **1 December 2019** amendment: cultivation raised from **six months–five years to two–eight years**; large possession newly carries **three–ten years**; smaller personal possession retains a fine/up to three years/possible waiver. It also changes aggravated supply and equipment offences. There is no fixed gram or plant allowance.
- Added the **25 January 2024** Supreme Court judgment upholding Senad Makaji’s three-year sentence for about **372 g** for personal use. The original trial sentence was thirteen months; the appeal applied the large-quantity offence. Judges assess large quantity case by case. Neither 372 g nor any smaller quantity is presented as a universal legal cutoff.
- The profile now separates cultivation from production and distinguishes giving drugs for consumption (**six months–five years**, article 247) from production/supply (**three–twelve years**, article 246). It removes unsupported blanket claims that most cases receive deferred prosecution and that no clubs are known.
