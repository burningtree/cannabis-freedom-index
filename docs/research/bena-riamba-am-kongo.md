# Bena Riamba — original Am Kongo engraving

## Original source and extraction

- Book: Hermann von Wissmann, Ludwig Wolf, Curt von François and Hans Mueller,
  *Im Innern Afrikas*, third edition (Leipzig: F. A. Brockhaus, 1891).
- [Smithsonian digitization at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/iminnernafrikasd00wiss).
- Plate title: *Am Kongo*, labelled “S. 414”, facing page 414.
- Extracted from the original book’s `iminnernafrikasd00wiss_0481.jp2` scan using
  the Archive BookReader image service at scale 1 and rotation 0. The endpoint was:
  https://ia903208.us.archive.org/BookReader/BookReaderImages.php?zip=/23/items/iminnernafrikasd00wiss/iminnernafrikasd00wiss_jp2.zip&file=iminnernafrikasd00wiss_jp2/iminnernafrikasd00wiss_0481.jp2&id=iminnernafrikasd00wiss&scale=1&rotate=0
- Downloaded page: 2782 × 4357 pixels. Crop rectangle: x=170, y=680,
  width=2380, height=3020. The crop removes blank page margins while retaining all
  seven figures, the complete calabash pipe, original title and all printed names.
- Saved as `src/assets/culture/bena-riamba-am-kongo-1891.jpg`, JPEG quality 92.
  No colour correction, retouching or sharpening; the paper tone and engraved lines
  are preserved. The article’s contain fit displays the full cropped plate.
- The modern academic PDF was used for identification only. No asset pixels were
  extracted from it.

## Identification and historical context

[Duvall’s supplied excerpt](https://assets-us-01.kc-usercontent.com/f7ca9afb-82c2-002a-a423-84e111d5b498/9d65da15-ba28-46d3-aacd-425f5269973d/978-1-4780-0394-6_601.pdf),
*The African Roots of Marijuana* (2019), p. 21, figure 1.4 (PDF page 27), identifies
Chingenge, Sangula and Mukenge as Bena Riamba political leaders, seated left to right,
and identifies Mukenge’s calabash water pipe. The original labels are Tschingenge,
Sangula-Meta and Kalamba. The entry makes the Kalamba/Mukenge naming difference explicit.
The original title and labels were visually checked before choosing the crop.

The entry describes a religious and political movement among Luba-speaking Kasai
communities, commonly translated as Sons of Hemp. Shared smoking and friendship
are described alongside leadership and warfare, not as proof of universal equality
or peace. Bashilenge is treated as a historical ethnographic designation, rather
than automatically a preferred self-description. European explorers’ mediation
and dependence on African guards, porters and leaders are acknowledged.
