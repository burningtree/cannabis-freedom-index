## Primary texts recovered

### 1989 ordinance

https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/gin/ordonnance-n27-du-10-avril-1989_html/ordonnance_027.pdf

Complete ordinance 027/PRG/SGG/89 signed **10 April 1989** by General **Lansana Conté**.
Article 16 expressly makes it effective on signature. Preamble cites the army's takeover on
3 April 1984, Second Republic, preservation of existing law and earlier repeals. These are the
article's sourced political context; no unsourced account of public motives is supplied.

- Articles 3–5: prohibited drug activities, cultivation/production/import/export/trafficking
  **five–twenty years AND 100,000–100 million Guinean francs**. Facilitation two–ten years
  OR fine OR both. Do not carry the facilitation fine-only option into Article 5.
- Article 9 excludes the suspended-sentence rules for offences referred to below it. Do not
  casually describe its scope as every offence in the ordinance.
- Article 11: personal possession/use **two months–one year OR 20,000–500,000 francs OR both**.
  Prosecutor-directed treatment/medical supervision; prosecution withheld after completed care
  under its first-offence/repeat-case qualifications. Courts can also compel treatment.
- Article 12: evading treatment brings Article 11 penalties, which may be doubled.
- Articles 13–14: drug-impaired driving/refusal rules; no new driving profile section this batch.
- Earlier national penalties remain unrecovered. Category `other`, not an invented first ban
  or a single-direction change from an unknown immediate predecessor.

The SHERLOC text record links to an attachment record that returned a browser 404, but the
record HTML exposed the actual PDF attachment, successfully downloaded. Full text extracted;
relevant complete pp. 4–5 rendered and visually read.

### 1998 code

https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/gin/code-penal_html/Guinea_Code_Penal.pdf

**31 December 1998**, Law 98/036, signed at the end by General Lansana Conté. The first-page
heading says 1988, an obvious internal typo: the law number and final signing page say 1998,
and the text refers to laws adopted in the 1990s. Do not create an extra 1988 event.

- Articles 378–381: general prohibition; ordinary cultivation/production and trafficking
  ten–twenty years **OR** 5–100 million francs **OR both**.
- Article 383: direct high-risk supply one–five years OR 100,000–500,000 OR both.
- Article 385: personal possession/use six months–two years OR 100,000–500,000 OR both.
  Compared with 1989, this raises the personal-use range. General production/trafficking gains
  the express fine-only option but has a higher minimum prison term: mixed reform, `other`.
- Article 391: prosecution withheld after completion of prescribed care; court/investigating
  judge treatment powers. No claim that actual care was available or commonly used.
- Article 607 repeals contrary earlier rules, especially the 1965 Penal Code. The exact earlier
  drug penalties still require the 1965/1988 original texts.

Relevant complete pp. 70–71 read and rendered, treatment continuation read in text extraction.

### Signed 2016 code, including scanned cover/final page

https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/loi-n2016-059-an-portant-code-pnal_html/Code_penal_Guinee.pdf

200 PDF pages. The first and last pages have image-based clauses missing from text extraction.
**Visually inspected both**: cover identifies Law 2016/059/AN; final p. 200 has **26 OCT. 2016**
and President **Alpha Condé**'s signature. Article 1011 explicitly enters into force on
promulgation. The entry is therefore dated **2016-10-26**, replacing a year-only date and a
source labelled February 2016. Do not use a draft filename as enactment evidence.

- Article 819: ordinary high-risk cultivation/production/trafficking ten–twenty years OR fine
  equal to triple seized value OR both.
- Article 832, PDF p. 163: small personal **buying, possession AND growing**, one–three years
  OR triple-value fine OR both. Expressly includes cannabis oil and non-oil derivatives.
  No gram/plant threshold. The code says `sans préjudice de dispositions plus sévères`, not
  the unqualified override of ordinary offences found in some neighbouring laws; do not
  rewrite it as an unconditional exemption from all more serious charges.
- Article 831: use outside prescription prohibited; confiscation independent of prosecution.
- Articles **821 and 835 overlap**: 821 directly supplying high-risk drugs five–ten years OR
  triple-value fine OR both; 835 direct narcotic supply one–three years OR 1–5 million OR both.
  Profile states both, without inventing which would necessarily govern every cannabis gift.
- Article 839: optional permanent exclusion of convicted foreigners and other additional
  restrictions, throughout the drug title. Unlike some neighbours, this is not restricted to
  ordinary trafficking articles only.
- Article 841: compulsory treatment instead of/additional to punishment; refusal **two–five
  years in prison**. Do not call it unconditional voluntary diversion.
- **Article 1010**, read from scanned p. 200: gentler special-law provisions prevail over
  contradictory general-code provisions. A fully consolidated assessment of any surviving
  earlier special drug law requires further legal research. The profile describes what the
  2016 code says; do not claim every earlier milder provision was repealed by this code.

Waiver bullets (minority, no recidivism, solemn undertaking) have uncertain cumulative versus
alternative scope. The 2019 defence argued for two criteria to suffice; this is an advocate's
argument, not a judicial ruling resolving the issue. No universal adult exemption is claimed.

## Practice: prosecution requests are not sentences

Original Guinéenews court report, downloaded and read from its article body:
https://guineenews.org/2019/01/17/justice-larticle-832-du-code-penal-pour-sauver-des-presumes-drogues/

Publication **17 January 2019**, hearing **Wednesday 16 January**, eleven defendants aged
roughly 18–22 arrested in Ratoma in December 2018. Most denied the charges; several described
being at kiosks/cafés or working. Prosecutor Daouda Diomandé requested acquittal of four,
one year in prison for three, and six months suspended plus 500,000 francs for the rest.
Defence lawyer **Abdoulaye Barry** challenged possession evidence and the missing drug
valuation, and sought an exemption for one client who admitted preparing cannabis to smoke.
Judge **Aboubacar Mafering Camara** deferred judgment to the following Wednesday.

The old profile incorrectly treated requests as imposed sentences. No final judgment was
recovered here. The new entry is `enforcement`, with enforcement/sentencing tags, not `court`
(a tag for decisions) and not a change in the law. No quote from the report is reproduced.

## Scores and direction

The current profile vector is unchanged: 1,1,1,1,1,0. The 2016 event gains `before` =
1,0,1,1,1,0. This locates the documented shorter personal-growing tier in 2016: the 1998 code
had put cultivation under the ordinary production range, without a separate small personal
crop clause in the complete drug chapter read. The chain becomes **100 → 7 in 1916 → 9 in
2016**, rounded. Earlier colonial severity remains inherited/estimated; no new claim is made
that later national penalties were literally already in the 1916 text.

Category `other` for 2016 reflects the mixed rewrite: shorter personal cultivation, a higher
written personal-possession range, value-based fines and treatment consequences. No fabricated
whole-point loss in other segments is inferred from prison maxima alone. Sentencing frequency
and the continued applicability of gentler special laws remain follow-ups. Structural chain
checks cannot establish how often people were actually jailed.
