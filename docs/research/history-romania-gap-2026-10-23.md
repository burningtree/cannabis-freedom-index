# Romania: medicine controls, personal-use penalties and the three-gram proposal

Research date: 23 October 2026. The preceding goal turn made verified progress by correcting
three country profiles and converting their citations. This continuation returns to the active
history/article goal. Romania had ten events and one article; this batch leaves twelve events
and five articles. Larger unresolved gaps remain in its early prohibition history.

## Changes made

- New entry and article `romania-cannabis-medicine-controls-2006`: Law 339/2005 took effect
  seven months after its 5 December 2005 publication, on 5 July 2006. The article explains
  cannabis's medical schedule, pharmaceutical preparations, prescriptions, patient possession
  and the cultivation/handling authorisations. Category **other**, tagged **patients**: this is
  a replacement control system, not evidence that medical cannabis was first permitted then.
- New entry `romania-three-gram-proposal-2023`, 8 May: records the bill's introduction, proposed
  administrative fine, prescription exemption and use of fine revenue. Category **debate**;
  tags **thresholds, campaign, patients, treatment**.
- Revised `romania-personal-use-penalty-reduction-2005` and added an article. The original Gazette
  supports six months to two years or a fine for risk drugs, two to five years for high-risk
  drugs, a three-month commencement, and a separate deferral for treatment-linked provisions.
  Added tags and an explicit reason for leaving the whole-point scores unchanged.
- Revised `romania-penal-code-2014` and rewrote its article. Added the assessment, consent and
  programme-compliance route to waiving/postponing punishment alongside the reduction in the
  prison minimum. Removed the old article's repetition of the 2013 medical-legalisation claim
  and the overstatement about final parliamentary rejection in 2023.
- Corrected `romania-medical-2013`, retaining its ID to preserve links, and added an article.
  Source and dating checks are documented below. Category now **debate**, tag **patients**.

- Corrected `romania-3-grams-rejected-2023` to the Senate vote on 18 September and added an
  article. The deciding lower-house stage must be distinguished from Senate rejection and
  committee reports. Tags **thresholds, campaign, patients**.
- Corrected only the Romanian profile's production and medical context blocks: removed the
  invented 2013 origin, blanket 0.2% medicine threshold and unverified absolute claims about
  licences/products. Current CMS guidance supports authorised medical handling and
  pharmaceutical-only prescribing; the statutory reproduction supports prescription/quantity
  rules. Both revised blocks cite inline. Scores, statuses and other profile blocks unchanged.

## Sources actually read

### Original 2004 Gazette and 2014 implementing law

[Law 522/2004 in Official Gazette 1155, 7 December 2004](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2d/Monitorul_Oficial_al_Rom%C3%A2niei._Partea_I_2004-12-07%2C_nr._1155.pdf)
was downloaded and extracted; printed pages 2 and 4 were rendered and visually read. Page 2,
Article I point 6 contains the revised personal-use penalties. Page 4, Article II sets three
months for general commencement and defers Articles 19¹–19² until the new Penal Code. Thus
7 March 2005 remains the penalty-change date, while the Gazette does not prove treatment
waivers were already operating on that date.

[Law 187/2012, reproduced by the Romanian Financial Supervisory Authority](https://asfromania.ro/uploads/articole/attachments/60801bd912f77503976495.pdf)
was downloaded and read in extracted text: Article 81 points 3 and 11–12, printed pp. 29–31,
and Article 247, p. 93. It supplies the new three-month minimum, high-risk range, assessment
and consent, programme-compliance court outcomes and 1 February 2014 commencement. It is a
legal text, not evidence of how often courts used these routes. No claim about their frequency
or programme participation was added.

The existing 2000-law entry is retained as sourced background. Its original-text and old
historical-consolidation URLs returned 502 to the web reader and HTTP/2/empty-response errors
to local curl during this pass. They were not relabelled as newly read. The current official
portal's bibliographic/publication material was accessible, but it defaults to later versions;
those were not substituted for the original offence text. A new article about the 2000 law
was therefore left for a later pass.

### Medicine controls and the 2013 correction

[Law 339/2005, full statutory reproduction](https://www.medfamsv.ro/index_htm_files/lege_339_2005.pdf)
was downloaded and read, especially Articles 5, 11–12, 36–41 and 56, and Table II narcotics
item 18. PDF pages 8 and 12 were rendered and visually inspected. The text includes cannabis,
resin, extracts and tinctures in the medical-interest schedule; it restricts prescriptions to
industrial/pharmacy preparations and permits the prescribed quantity on prescription. Article
56 expressly says seven months after publication. The reproduction bears a medical-practice
footer and is identified as a reproduction, not as an official Gazette scan.

[Official Ministry of Justice record for Law 339/2005](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/66456)
was opened and its publication metadata read: Gazette 1095, 5 December 2005. Subsequent attempts
to retrieve focused parts were intermittent/502; the full downloaded reproduction supplies the
substantive articles. A secondary index's contradictory generic commencement metadata was not
used over the statutory seven-month rule. The new event's category makes no claim about how
this permission compared with the repealed 1969 law, or about an operational patient market.

[Romania Insider, 10 October 2013](https://www.romania-insider.com/news/castle-craig-clinic-romania-denies-allowing-use-of-medical-marijuana)
was read in the web reader, including its attributed statements by Sorin Oprea and Anca
Crupariu. The local site returned 429, so there was no repeated scripted retrieval. This is a
contemporary report quoting agencies via Castle Craig, not a separately recovered agency
press release. The revised entry and article attribute the claims accordingly. No purported
new 2013 statute or statutory 0.2% medicine limit was found or retained.

[CMS Romania legal guide, 18 June 2024](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-a-legal-roadmap-to-cannabis/romania)
was read at its medical-use section: authorised cultivation/import/sale under Law 339/2005,
pharmaceutical products only. Its discussion distinguishes the hemp cultivation threshold
from final THC-containing products. The profile corrections avoid inferring a medicine limit
from a hemp threshold. No current product-authorisation inventory was independently audited.

### Three-gram bill and Senate vote

[Senate original bill PDF, L384/2023](https://www.senat.ro/legis/PDF/2023/23L384FG.PDF?nocache=true)
was downloaded; both pages were rendered and visually read because the scan has no searchable
body text. Proposed Article 4¹: up to three grams for personal use, administrative fine
1,000–3,000 lei; medically prescribed cannabis possession exempted from Articles 4 and 4¹.
Fine revenue: prevention and medical, psychological and social assistance. Article III calls
for a special medical-cannabis law. This is the sponsors' bill, not an enacted statute, despite
its formal law layout. The explanatory memorandum was downloaded but mostly scanned and was
not treated as read evidence for empirical claims.

[Senate legislative record](https://www.senat.ro/legis/lista.aspx?an_cls=2023&nr_cls=L384)
was downloaded and its chronology read: introduction 8 May; favourable Economic and Social
Council and Legislative Council opinions 7 June; Senate rejection 18 September; transmission
and registration in the Chamber 20 September; joint committee rejection report 12 December.
The file labels the stage as sent to the Chamber for debate. The batch does not invent a
later final plenary rejection or claim that every advisory body opposed it.

[Electronic roll call](https://www.senat.ro/VoturiPlenDetaliu.aspx?AppID=BF471C89-21B1-46A4-BD96-638B8E0255E0&Cod=25408&Data=2023-09-18)
was read: 108 present; 103 for the rejection report, one against, four abstentions. This is the
source used for the count. The contemporary AGERPRES story reports 107 for; that conflicting
number was not copied into the database.

[AGERPRES introduction report, 8 May 2023](https://agerpres.ro/viata-parlamentara/2023/05/08/camera-deputatilor-propunere-legislativa-pentru-dezincriminarea-posesiei-de-canabis-pentru-consum-pr--1103965)
and [rejection report, 18 September](https://agerpres.ro/politic/2023/09/18/dezincriminarea-posesiei-de-canabis-in-limita-a-trei-grame-respinsa-de-senat--1171281)
were downloaded and their article bodies read. Used for sponsors' stated reasoning and the
reported opposition speeches respectively. Sponsors' empirical predictions and youth survey
claims were not presented as independently established results.
