## Primary records read

### 2004 drug law

Health Ministry PDF: <https://www.health.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Fiji-Illicit-Drug-Act-2004.pdf>.

Downloaded `act-2004.pdf` and extracted text. Read the assent page, sections 1–5, 9–12, section 39 and the cannabis schedule entries; reviewed the arrangement for the investigation/search provisions used in the article and the relevant opening text. This is a selected-clause review, not a claim to have checked every investigation provision.

- Assent **7 July 2004**; section 1(2) requires a ministerial Gazette commencement notice. The notice/date was not recovered. The new event says the law was signed and describes its terms conditionally where appropriate.
- Sections 4 and 5 allow a maximum **FJ$1 million fine OR life imprisonment OR both**. Life is not mandatory. Section 5 expressly includes use and administration, not just possession. The profile's earlier claim that use was covered only through possession was corrected.
- Supply includes giving, distribution, sale and offers. The statute gives possession and supply the same maximum. Seru paragraph 6 confirms that the earlier Sulua guideline had been applied across the listed verbs. Removed the profile's unsupported blanket assertion that the same bands always treated supply more seriously.
- Section 11 exempts lawfully prescribed/supplied treatment and authorised professional activity. Its traveller clause covers up to one month's lawfully prescribed/supplied treatment for the person or someone in their care. This is statutory wording, not verification that any foreign cannabis prescription automatically satisfies Fiji's import/medicines requirements. No individual travel approval or operational patient programme was verified.
- Section 39 replaces the corresponding parts of the old Dangerous Drugs Act, not necessarily that entire Act in 2004.

### Possession guidelines dated 31 May 2012

Original Sulua PacLII URLs were inaccessible (403 via local retrieval; unavailable in the browser search tool). They are **not** recorded as having been read. The accessible original **2023 Seru judgment** identifies Sulua v State [2012] FJCA 33 and its date at paragraph 6, describes the scope, and reproduces all four bands at paragraph 19. That later primary judicial record is the cited basis for the new 2012 entry and article.

Bands reproduced in Seru: 0–100 g non-custodial, with suspended/short custody only in the worst cases; 100–1,000 g one to three years; 1,000–4,000 g three to seven; 4,000 g and above seven to fourteen. The source's boundaries overlap: no invented exclusive endpoint or automatic entitlement was added. The no-prison guideline is not decriminalisation. Removed a blanket statement that a conviction is always entered, since discharge with a warning is one of the stated outcomes. No account of Sulua's individual defendants or the original majority/dissent was published from unread secondary leads.

### Cultivation judgment, 25 May 2023

Original court PDF: <https://judiciary.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/25.05.2023_COA_Crim._AAU-115.2017_Jone-Seru-v-State_Judgment_Prematilaka-RJA.pdf>.

Downloaded `seru-2023.pdf`, read paragraphs 1–56 and the relevant comparative passages; inspected rendered PDF pages 16–17, including the sentencing matrix on page 16. The appended case table was not independently verified case by case.

- The Legal Aid Commission supplied 33 High Court examples. Paragraph 9 describes them as 2019–2022, but the appendix includes earlier sentencing dates. Published count only, with no invented uniform year range.
- Weight of wet versus dried plants, roots and different judicial approaches drove the inconsistency. The judgment contrasts 196 plants/7 years 7 months with 46 plants/11 years 4½ months; these examples are attributed to the court's comparison, not independently re-researched convictions.
- Plant categories: **100 or more; more than 50 but fewer than 100; 10–50; fewer than 10 for personal use without intended sale**. Leading/significant/lesser roles determine a second axis. Fewer than ten does not by itself guarantee a non-custodial outcome: that range belongs to the lesser-role cell.
- The largest category/leading role has an **18-year starting point**, **16–20-year range**. Lesser role/smallest personal band runs from non-custodial to suspended sentence. These are guidelines with individual adjustments, not fixed statutory minima.
- Seru grew 20 plants weighing 5.5 kg. His nine-year sentence stood. Paragraph 53 refers to an eight-year starting point although the table's corresponding cell says nine; the article does not repeat that discrepancy as a new rule.

### Resentencing judgment, 30 May 2024

Original court PDF: <https://judiciary.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/30.05.24-CA-Crim-AAU-0036.2017-AAU0093.2017-Kaoulia-and-Naqekeca-v.-The-State-Judgment.pdf>.

Downloaded `kaoulia-2024.pdf`; read the opening facts, relevant conviction discussion and paragraphs 65–86/final orders. Inspected rendered PDF page 24. The heading spells the defendants **Waisake Kaloulia** and **Kaminieli Naqeleca**, while the file URL uses other spellings; articles follow the judgment's heading.

- The police reported 484 seized plants; the analyst counted 208 complete plants because roots had been removed for transport during a seven-hour return trek. The court discusses that evidentiary difference. No choice of one count as an undisputed total was made.
- Original sentences were **19 and 18 years**, after deductions from a 20-year calculation for pre-sentence custody. They were not both 20-year final sentences.
- Revised terms: **15 and 14 years**, non-parole periods **11½ and 10½ years**, effective from 31 January 2017. Both convictions stood. This is one application of the newer guidelines to older appeals, not automatic mass resentencing or expungement.
- Paragraph 72 reproduces Supreme Court commentary in Ratu: plant count should identify the category, with the nature/size and modest lack-of-sophistication allowance considered in adjustments. No independent new Ratu event was created without recovering that original judgment.

### Hemp amendment signed 29 July 2022

Original enacted two-page Act: <https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Act-41-Illicit-Drugs-Control-Budget-Amendment.pdf>. Read both pages in extracted text and rendered images. Replaced Wikipedia and the profile's draft Bill citation with the enacted text.

- Passed and signed **29 July 2022**. Section 1(2) requires commencement by a separate notice. The event's exact date is now the verified passage/assent date and its title says **approves**, not an assertion that the exemption commenced that day.
- Schedule exclusion covers **hemp or plant parts derived from hemp at no more than 1% THC**. No blanket claim that all oils, resin or processed cannabinoid products are unrestricted was made.
- Added section 32A places the burden on someone claiming hemp in a prosecution to prove the qualifying THC concentration.
- Budget context was read in the official [15 July 2022 Hansard](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Daily-Hansard-Friday-15th-July-2022.pdf), at the Industrial Hemp Export Industry passage (`budget-2022.txt`, lines 1584–1605). Government proposed tested/conditional imports, FNU trials, UNODC cooperation, Canadian seeds and eventual distribution to farmers. Those are reported as plans, not verified deliveries or actual harvests. The speech says below 1%; the enacted law says not exceeding 1%, and the article explains that difference.
- Search indexing of the official Laws of Fiji update tables identifies **1 August 2022, LN 100 of 2022**. The actual table PDFs now returned 404, so that is an **unread primary-source lead**, not a checked commencement finding. Downloaded Parliament's September 2022 `1Acts.pdf`; its row merely repeats commencement by notice. Do not use the neighbouring row's 1 August date as this Act's commencement. The original commencement notice remains a follow-up.

### New law signed 2 October 2026

Original enacted PDF: <https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Act-32-Counter-Narcotics-Act-2026.pdf>.

Downloaded all 172 pages and extracted text. Read the assent and commencement page, relevant interpretation/classification provisions, sections 73–79, diversion provisions 193–199, section 209, repeal/transitional sections 254–255 and the relevant Schedule 2/3/4 entries. This is a **selected-section review**, not a whole-Act audit. Inspected rendered PDF pages 13 and 53, confirming assent/commencement and the personal-use offence respectively.

- Assent **2 October 2026**, Act 32. **Section 1(2) requires commencement dates appointed by ministerial Gazette notice. No such notice was verified through 22 October.** Do not treat this as proof that no notice exists. The article and profile state the commencement mechanism and the signed-law status; numerical ratings were not adjusted on an assumed operative date.
- Current news reports describe passage on 1 October; Parliament's voting index also lists a 29 September item, which may concern the accelerated procedure. The signed Act resolves the assent date used here. No independent third-reading date/vote count was published from those conflicting references.
- Section 79/Class C plant material: maximum **FJ$500 OR three months OR both**; court must avoid custody unless prior convictions or exceptional circumstances warrant it. This is not a mandatory three-month term or a decriminalised offence.
- Section 77/Class C cultivation/supply: maximum **FJ$15 million OR 15 years OR both**. At **28 g or 100 cigarettes** of plant material, the Schedule 4 supply presumption can be rebutted. Cannabis preparations are separately Class B, with a different listed threshold; no assertion that every cannabis product has the plant-material ceiling.
- Diversion requires voluntary responsibility, opportunity for legal advice, consent and an interests-of-justice assessment. Completion bars further action and withdraws a related charge; failure can lead to prosecution. The division excludes children covered by juvenile justice rules. The article does not promise eligibility for every defendant, actual programme availability or automatic removal of earlier convictions.
- September Cabinet rationale was read in [item 8 of the 9 September decisions, published 16 September](https://www.fiji.gov.fj/decisions-of-the-16th-cabinet-meeting-held-on-9-september-2026/). It describes bureau establishment, international cooperation, oversight and treatment/rehabilitation. No operational bureau staffing, service uptake or reduction in arrests was inferred.
- The new schedules mention CBD products and section 209 contains a hemp presumption. Their complete product/medicines interactions were not reviewed; no new CBD market/medical-access claims were published.
