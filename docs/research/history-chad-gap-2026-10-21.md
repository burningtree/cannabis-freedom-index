# Chad: national cannabis rules between colonial controls and recent enforcement

## 1966: recovered original decree

Source: [FAOLEX original scan](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/cha39525.pdf), nine PDF pages.
Downloaded, extracted, and visually inspected complete PDF pp. 2–3 and 8–9. Article 16
explicitly prohibits importing, exporting, cultivating, harvesting, trading, using and
possessing Indian hemp; its historical definition is the flowering and fruiting tops of
the female cannabis plant, Indian variety. The next page permits scientific-research
derogations from this cannabis prohibition by the health and social affairs minister.
Do not turn the general authorisation rules for narcotics into a general medical-cannabis
exception to this specific cannabis rule.

Article 62 requires destruction of cannabis and preparations seized for Article 16
violations. Article 63 grants three months from publication for compliance. The signature
on PDF p. 9 confirms 14 April 1966, President F. Tombalbaye and minister Adoum Hel Bongo.
The event date is signature, not a verified publication or commencement date. The preamble
has apparent printing/date inconsistencies, including a November 1966 reference before
the April signature. The signature and heading agree on April 1966. No new prison term
was inferred from this decree: Article 62 refers to penalties in other legislation.

Category `other`: an independent national restatement of prohibition after the existing
colonial restriction, with a research exception; the recovered texts do not establish a
net harsher or milder change. Tags: trade, science, police-powers. No colonial tag on an
independent government's law, no home-growing tag for general cultivation, no eradication
tag for destruction of seized products. The original 1926 colonial measure remains
unrecovered; its inherited score step is preserved, not independently validated here.

## 2000: pharmacy law contains drug-user offences

Source: [UNODC full legal text](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/tcd/loi-no-24_html/loi_no_24.pdf),
44 pages. Read the relevant complete text and visually inspected PDF pp. 38–40 and 44.
Article 36 adopts the lists in ratified drug conventions. Cannabis applicability follows
that adoption and the already documented Single Convention ratification, not the generic
Article 10 definition alone. Article 134 gives one to six years AND 300,000–3,000,000 CFA
francs OR either alone for narcotics regulatory offences, attempts/agreements, group use
and facilitating another's use, whether paid or free. Article 135 extends the range to
illegitimate possession and false-prescription offences. Article 136 doubles penalties
for illicit cultivation/manufacture and supplying or facilitating use by minors. Preserve
the prison/fine alternatives when expressing the doubled two-to-twelve-year range.

Facilitation also carries a mandatory five-to-twenty-year residence restriction. This is
not described as automatic expulsion of foreigners: that is a separate rule reported
under the 1995 law. Article 137 permits compulsory detoxification, with conditions and
funding to be set by decree; evading an order carries six days–two months AND a fine,
additional to underlying punishments. No treatment-capacity or actual admissions claim.
Article 138 addresses confiscation and specified professional bans.

Signature is 24 November 2000; parliamentary adoption was 30 October. Some catalogue
metadata use the adoption date. Use the signature date and describe the law as signed,
without claiming a separately verified date of operational commencement. Article 153
repeals contrary earlier provisions, specifically the 1965 pharmacy law and 1984/1992
pharmacy texts; it preserves rules pending replacement regulations. It does not resolve
the relationship of every offence with the 1995 drug law.

Category `other`: replacement pharmacy legislation whose penalty direction relative to
the unrecovered 1995 law is unknown. Doubling is within this law's offence tiers, not an
assertion that all growers' sentences doubled from 1995. Tags: sentencing, treatment,
youth. Scores and statuses preserved. Profile now describes sourced historical offences
and keeps the current consolidated position explicitly unverified.

## 2017: confinement after or on suspension of prison

Source: [FAOLEX Penal Code](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/cha213367.pdf), Droit-Afrique
transcription, 127 pages. Complete relevant text read; PDF pp. 11–12 and 127 visually
checked. Article 38 applies after conviction for an offence punishable by imprisonment
of at least two years. This threshold concerns the offence's statutory punishment, not
necessarily the term actually imposed. The court may order a person with drug dependence
into a special health institution for up to two years and shorten that period on medical
advice concerning danger to public order. Article 41 starts the confinement after the
principal prison sentence expires or is suspended. Do not present it as invariably an
alternative to prison or as applying to anyone merely using cannabis.

Article 521 makes the code effective on the first day of the third month after
promulgation on 8 May: 1 August 2017. Category `other`, tags sentencing and treatment.
Comparison with earlier compulsory-treatment rules is incomplete, so no new penalty
increase or relaxation is asserted. The existing profile's incomplete description is
corrected, and the dated event links the earlier treatment power to the new code.

## Outstanding source gaps

The full Law 22/PR/95 of 28 September 1995 has still not been recovered. State reports
CAT/C/TCD/1 (paras. 177–178) and CCPR/C/TCD/1 (para. 194) describe its Article 142 on
foreigners' territorial exclusion after imprisonment. The 2025 destruction report also
cites that law. Neither establishes the full personal-use or cultivation penalty range.
Do not claim the 2000 ranges are a verified exclusive statement of current penalties.
The 1967 Penal Code transcription was searched but supplied no relevant drug-user rule.
The original 1926 colonial law and later implementing decrees also need recovery.
