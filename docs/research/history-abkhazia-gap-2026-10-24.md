### Criminal Code and its dated provisions

`code-current.pdf/.txt`: Criminal Code posted by the Supreme Court at https://supreme.vs-ra.org/images/DOCS/Reglament/UK_RA.pdf . The court's current regulatory-documents catalogue links this file. Its listed amendments run through 4 August 2023. Read articles 54, 79–80, 223, 224, 224.1, 228–229 and relevant footnotes. This replaces reliance on broad press descriptions of the offences.

- Article 228, expressly in the version of Law 2426 of 21 July 2009: sowing/growing prohibited plants, including cannabis, 6–8 years and RUB100,000–150,000. Group, organised-group or large-scale cultivation: 10–12 years. No ordinary few-plant noncustodial exception appears in this provision.
- Article 223, introduced by Law 3187 of 7 August 2012: ordinary non-supply possession/acquisition/transport 1–3 years; large 3–5 with fine; especially large 5–7 with fine. Exemption requires voluntary surrender AND active cooperation. Seizure during arrest or a search is not voluntary surrender.
- Article 224: ordinary supply 5–8 years and fine; middle tier 8–12 and fine. Part 3 expressly notes commencement on 1 March 2020. The later March 2020 wording prescribes 15 years and fine, with life or death absent mitigating circumstances. Article 224.1 has separate import/export/transit tiers and the same March commencement note for part 3. Do not equate every possession, import or gift with the capital tier.
- Article 54 excludes women, people who offended before eighteen and men aged sixty-five by sentencing from death; commutation is provided. Article 79 states general pardon powers. The profile's old blanket no-pardon/no-sentence-reduction assertion is not repeated: the Supreme Court's later mitigation instructions positively contradict the blanket reduction claim.
- Article 80 explains criminal-record status; article 229 criminalises running premises for drug consumption.

`code.pdf/.txt`, initially retrieved from Abkhazia's representative office in Transnistria, is only consolidated through 2012. It was useful as a historical cross-check but is NOT used as the current code. Its general web indexing/publication recency does not update its contents.

The code references a 2009 rewritten cultivation provision and a 2012 introduced possession article. Exact gazette commencement dates and original pre-amendment wording remain retrieval tasks. Events are dated to the named laws, describe their stated penalties and do not claim those dates as recovered gazette commencement dates.

### Administrative use reform actually adopted

`admin.pdf/.txt`: Supreme Court's linked Administrative Offences Code at https://supreme.vs-ra.org/images/DOCS/Reglament/kodeksobadmprav.pdf . The catalogue identifies the consolidated version through December 2024 / January 2025. Read article 45 and Law 5601 of 3 April 2023 amendment note.

Final wording: non-prescribed cannabis OR hashish use 3 days' administrative arrest, repetition within a year 10 days. Other drug use/refusal of examination: 15 days, repetition 30. The cannabis paragraph's note bars renewed liability under the first-use paragraph within fifteen days from the earlier liability decision. Voluntary medical help for illegal use exempts the person from administrative liability under this article; it is not blanket immunity for possession or supply.

The new event uses the law's 3 April date, not an independently recovered publication/commencement date. Public text says “law dated 3 April,” avoiding an invented exact commencement.

`vote2022.html/.txt`: complete Ekho Kavkaza report, 29 December 2022. First reading proposed 3 days for cannabis and 30 days for repetition. Deputy Dmitry Marshania explained the previous uniform 15-day sanction; deputies argued for a lighter cannabis penalty. This remains category debate, now linked to the final 3/10-day statutory outcome. The final repeat figure must not be copied from the earlier proposal.

A 23 March 2023 first-reading report was indexed by Apsnypress but the retrieved URL returned 404. Its snippet mixes exceptions in confusing wording. It was not used to establish final law; the court-posted code is decisive.

### Supreme Court guidance of October 2025

`supreme-new.pdf`, `supreme-plain.txt`, `guidance2025.txt`, `guidance-plain.txt`: official Bulletin 1/2026, 280 PDF pages, new valid host https://supreme.vs-ra.org/images/PDF/bul20261.pdf . Read decision 14 of 17 October 2025, printed pages 83–91, especially introductory reasons and paragraphs 1–3, 12–16, 18, 21–28 and 30. The old hostname redirected PDF requests to the new home page; moving to the verified new hostname recovered the actual PDF. All cited material is text-native.

- Paragraph 3: ordinary non-supply possession liability applies irrespective of quantity below larger tiers. Higher thresholds do not create a small-quantity possession permission.
- Paragraphs 12–16: supply includes sale, gift, exchange, debt payment and loan; individual proof of intent; intermediary roles; lawful authorisation for test purchases. Do not apply the injection-related exceptions as a general cannabis-sharing permission.
- Paragraph 18: drugs not lawfully seized and properly examined cannot become the subject of trial solely because someone confessed. No fabricated case outcome is inferred.
- Paragraph 23: constitutional legal-assistance and self-incrimination protections apply when questioning a detained person becomes incriminating. Administratively detained people cannot be questioned about other investigative materials or criminal cases without a lawyer. The article states that specific rule rather than inventing a broad automatic exclusion remedy.
- Paragraph 26: life/death prohibited with mitigating circumstances; specified cooperation plus absence of aggravation invokes the two-thirds rule. Do not simplify all mitigation to an automatic fixed ten-year term.
- Paragraphs 27–28: cultivation includes creating growing conditions and plant development; combined cultivation, extraction, holding, transport or supply may lead to combined offences.
- Paragraph 30 replaces 2011 and 2012 drug-case guidance. The court discusses errors and unjustified convictions; its general findings are attributed to the court rather than presented as a fresh statistical survey.

The new search registry https://vsdocs.apsny.land/search.php and court catalogue were inspected read-only. No upload, edit or other mutation was made.
