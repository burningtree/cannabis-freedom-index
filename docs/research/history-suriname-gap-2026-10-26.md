# Suriname: filling the 1921–1990 gap

Research checked on 26 October 2026. Read the maintenance and writing guides, README,
book-research rules, score rubric, schema, Suriname profile and all eight existing events.
The previous bug-fix turn made verified progress; this continuation resumes the wider history goal.
The dated history had a 69-year gap between the 1921 petition and 1990 treaty record.
Seven additions reduce the largest gap to 33 years, between 1922 and 1955. Two articles
explain the replacement drug laws. This is progress on coverage, not a claim that Suriname's
legal or scoring history is now complete.

## Sources actually read

- **1955 Opium Act**, G.B. 1955 no. 91, government-communicated English translation in
  UN E/NL.1958/72, all eight pages. The adopted and published date is **22 September 1955**.
  Articles 1–4 distinguish plant tops/resin/preparations from extracts/tinctures, prohibit
  cannabis cultivation and specified possession, use and border movement; articles 6–8
  preserve professional, medical and scientific permissions. Article 12 has a **four-year
  deliberate-offence maximum**, and six months or 3,000 florins for the other branch.
  Article 17 delegates commencement and identifies the predecessor as the **10 June 1928**
  ordinance, G.B. no. 51. The commencement order was not recovered. PDF p. 6 was rendered
  and inspected for the penalty and confiscation clauses; pp. 1–5, 7–8 were read as text.
- **1972 orders**, UN E/NL.1974/47–48, all four pages. Order **6575**, signed **9 December**,
  lists THC as item 14, with salts/isomers/preparations, and withdraws Order 1038 of
  18 February 1972. It was published **20 December** and commenced the next day. PDF p. 4
  was rendered and inspected. The withdrawn February text is not available here, so this
  is an express designation, not an asserted first-ever THC ban. Companion Order 5976
  mainly lists other drugs and is not given a separate cannabis event.
- **1980 procedure amendment**, Decree C-31, S.B. 1980 no. 116. Read the opening,
  Article IV item 71 (Opium Act), and Article V item 108 (commencement). The decree was
  signed 11 November and took effect **15 November 1980**. Item 71 rewrites seizure and
  confiscation, replaces article 12(3) with mandatory drug forfeiture and deletes 12(4)–(5).
  Printed/PDF pp. **15 and 23** were rendered and inspected. The SRIS scan was downloaded
  with a standard User-Agent after an initial request was rejected; no access barrier was bypassed.
- **Criminal Code**, historical consolidation through S.B. 2004 no. 105, as reproduced by
  WIPO Lex: article **49a** and annotation **42**. The annotation identifies insertion by
  **S.B. 1980 no. 67**. The court may order government care after an Opium Act conviction
  if in the convicted person's interest. No precise day, individual right to diversion,
  replacement of prison, cannabis-only use rate or routine implementation is established.
- **1998 Drugs Act**, original government-communicated text in UN E/NL.1999/55, all sixteen
  PDF pages, especially articles 2–4, 6–8, 11–12, 17 and List II part B. The thirteen-year
  possession/use/domestic-supply tier and life-or-twenty-year cultivation/import tier are
  already in this original text; they must not be attributed to the 2002 amendment alone.
  The Act was signed **12 February 1998** and published **17 May 1998**; neither date is
  treated as commencement.
- **National Assembly consolidation**, as amended in 2002: articles 11 and 17, with
  footnote 3 on printed/PDF p. **8** identifying **20 January 1999**, S.B. 1999 no. 23,
  as commencement. That page was rendered and inspected. Original and consolidated
  penalty provisions agree; the later annotation is used only for the commencement date.
- **Government explanatory memorandum to the 1998 Act**, reproduced on Suriname.nu's
  customs-law pages: introduction, replacement-versus-amendment discussion, treaty and
  regional-cooperation background, and the commentary on cannabis products, preparation,
  conspiracy and increased penalties. Read the reproduced government account, not the
  website's own legal advice. No unverifiable individual sentencing practice is inferred.
- **National Anti-Drugs Council, National Drugs Master Plan 2006–2010**, December 2005:
  section 2.2, printed p. 10. It explains the 1955 law's replacement and identifies its
  last amendment as S.B. 1988 no. 37. The amendment's contents remain unrecovered.
- **Koloniaal Verslag 1923, II Suriname**, reporting 1922: printed/PDF p. **8**,
  “Opiumverordening”, read in text and visually. Fourteen **combined opium/ganja**
  possession convictions: twelve fines of f10–500 and two six-month sentences. No
  detected cannabis-growing offences. The OCR's prison duration was checked against
  the scan: six months. These are not cannabis-only statistics or a general sentencing rate.
- **Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch West-Indië (1914–1917)**, online DBNL edition:
  “Ganja”, p. **306**, and “Opium”, p. **539**. Read both entries and the edition's
  bibliographic/accountability page, which reproduces the title page and identifies the
  Leiden library copy. They date the 1908 ordinance to **17 February**, the import
  deadline to **1 March 1918**, and report 689 kg of ganja imported in 1913. Opium-only
  retail licence conditions are not copied over as cannabis conditions. These are selected
  online reference entries, not a newly imported or fully read local-library book.

UN Digital Library pages failed through the browser fetcher, but their PDF links downloaded
successfully and the actual documents were read. Delpher links to the 1904 pharmacy story
and 1923 prohibition story were inaccessible; neither newspaper is cited as read.
Wikipedia was used to locate these leads and check the earlier narrative, not as a new
source for the added laws.

## Edits and article scope

Added the 1918 deferred import prohibition, 1922 enforcement figures, 1955 replacement Act,
1972 express THC designation, 1980 post-conviction care power, 1980 confiscation rewrite,
and 1999 commencement. The existing 1908 entry now distinguishes the future import
cutoff; the existing 1998 entry distinguishes signature from commencement and links forward.
No other country's records are part of the commit.

- `articles/suriname-opium-order-1955.md`: **368 words**, four sections; earlier trade and
  enforcement, offences and exceptions, later care power and replacement.
- `articles/suriname-drugs-act-commencement-1999.md`: **407 words**, four sections;
  government purpose, conduct-specific penalties, preparation/conspiracy and commencement.

Every article source is cited inline; internal history links were checked. No illustration
was added: the legal documents support the story without a decorative image.

## Score decisions and remaining gaps

The current six scores and profile are unchanged. New procedural, care and product entries
lack evidence for a whole-segment change. The 1955 event is explicitly adoption/publication
with delegated commencement; the 1998 and 1999 records now separate the two stages.
Higher maximum sentences in a replacement prohibition do not alone prove a different
custodial-user band or sentencing frequency.

The existing **1908 100→9 aggregate step remains provisional**. The newly read sources
contradict its former suggestion of an immediate blanket end to imports, and the public
text has been corrected. A defensible six-segment split between the 1908 ordinance and
its 1918 deadline needs the **full 1908/1915 texts and earlier pharmacy/tax rules**. The
1896 pharmacy-only claim still rests on a secondary lead; the 1904 newspaper's underlying
1896 rule needs recovery. This pass therefore does not invent a precise earlier vector
or an extra drop for the already aggregated regime. A zero-flag chain report establishes
mathematical consistency only; it does not validate that old allocation.

Other priorities: the full 1928 law, commencement of the 1955 law, the withdrawn February
1972 order, original S.B. 1980 no. 67 and its commencement, and the 1988 amendment.
The 2018 medical-study committee remains a separate existing triage item to verify against
its appointment source. The goal of complete coverage remains active.
