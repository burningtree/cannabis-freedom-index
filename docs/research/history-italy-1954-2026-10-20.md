# Italy's 1954 narcotics law — 20 October 2026

Added an article, clarified the existing 1954 event and added one unscored 1976
procedural event concerning the replacement of its confinement provisions.

## Evidence and limits

- Visually read the complete Law 1041 in the original 12 November 1954 gazette,
  printed pp. 3690–3695 (PDF pp. 2–7). The scan has no useful text layer; page
  images were used. Also read the official HTML reproductions of Articles 3,
  4, 6, 16, 18, 25 and 26. Article 6 contains no personal-use exception, but a
  shared penalty range does not establish identical sentences in actual cases.
- Article 8 expressly mentions Indian hemp in reporting requirements. Article
  3 calls for a published list; the article does not pretend the statute itself
  supplies an exhaustive list or establishes a first-ever cannabis prohibition.
- Read Normattiva's original commencement record: 27 November 1954. Preserved
  the existing event's 22 October instrument date, while distinguishing
  publication and commencement in its description.
- Read the complete Constitutional Court Order 224/1976 in the official gazette.
  It records the 1975 repeal with its central-office exception and returns the
  confinement case for reconsideration of relevance. It does not strike down
  Article 21, decide the defence-rights challenge or legalise cannabis.
- A consolidated 1975 law hosted by the Interior Ministry was consulted as a
  lead. It contains 1990 amendments and was not used to backdate revised text.
  An original 1975 gazette was downloaded but not read in this pass. The account
  of repeal rests on the original 1976 court order, not that unexamined scan.
- The pre-existing Order 59/1977 citation was retained in the event, but could
  not be loaded from the Court's portal. It is not an article source or the
  basis for a new claim. No enforcement rates or actual medical availability
  were inferred from statutory permissions. No profiles, scores or score
  baselines changed; the existing score vector was preserved without revalidation.
