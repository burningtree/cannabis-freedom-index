## The supposed 1996 drug law was a biodiversity ratification

The old entry attributed drug offences to **113/AN/96**. The official text is instead a
ratification of the Convention on Biological Diversity, dated **3 September 1996**:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/loi-n113-an-96-3e-l-portant-ratification-de-la-convention-sur-la-diversite-biologique/

Read the complete primary text and corroborating September 1996 journal contents. It has
no drug offences or five-year personal-use provision. A commercial cannabis-law site repeats
the same false identification; it is not independent evidence.

## Colonial controls: full official 1932 text

Read the complete official electronic reproduction, including the replacement table:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/arrete-n-18-427-1932-commerce-detention-et-vente-des-substances-veneneuses-a-la-cote-francaise-des-somalis/

The portal labels the page an arrêté, but the reproduced text is the **presidential decree
of 25 May 1932**, signed Albert Lebrun and colonies minister de Chappedelaine. A separate
local promulgation, dated **16 June 1932**, signed acting governor Antonin, identifies the
same decree:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/arabe/texte-juridique/arrete-n-17-427-1932-promulquant-a-la-cote-francaise-des-somalis-le-decret-du-25-mai-1932-modifiant-les-dispositions-du-titre-ii-du-decret-du-26-fevrier-1923-reglementant-le-commerce-la-det/

Both appear in local journal **427 of 30 June 1932**. The history entry uses the local
promulgation date, without claiming an independently established commencement calculation.
The local order has an isolated 1925 reference where its heading/preamble and the decree
itself give 1923; do not silently treat that isolated typo as a different predecessor law.

- Preamble cites **22 December 1916** extension of France's 12 July 1916 poisonous-substances
  law and **26 February 1923** local regulations. Those original instruments were not
  recovered in full. They are background evidence, not a verified first cannabis ban date.
- New Articles 30–31 apply permit controls to manufacturing, extraction, preparation,
  possession, offer, distribution, brokerage, buying, selling, imports/exports and industry.
  Governor permits are personal, with hygiene-council advice; a trafficking conviction
  prevents grant/continuation. Permits identify substances and industrial annual quantities.
- Registration of a public pharmacist's diploma serves as permission for preparation and
  dispensing in that pharmacy. Unauthorised purchasers need a therapeutic prescription;
  hospitals and laboratories have special supply arrangements. No claim of actual cannabis
  prescribing or patient numbers is made.
- Article 32 requires per-operation import/export permissions; export certificates retained
  three years. Article 33 requires labelling and locked storage except coca leaves, with
  seizure of quantities outside storage; regulates postal shipments with professional
  exceptions. Articles 34–36 require purchase/transfer records, including gifts; retain ten
  years. Articles 38–39 regulate renewals and specified prescriptions exceeding seven days.
- Article 2's replacement Table B explicitly lists **Indian hemp, resin, resin preparations,
  extract and tincture**. This is direct local cannabis-classification evidence.
- Transitional Article 4 grants six months from publication for certain new obligations
  under Articles 31, 33 and 34. Do not generalise it into six months' freedom for all cannabis
  activity. The text contains several typographical errors, including 1932 in a 1923 cross-reference.

Category `other`: earlier restrictions demonstrably existed, but full 1923 terms/old table
are missing. A new first ban or an across-the-board tightening/easing is not proved.
The inherited 1900–1920 first-ban estimate remains an unverified legacy scoring anchor;
replacing it requires the original 1916/1923 controls and their cannabis schedules.

## French law locally published in February 1971

Read complete official electronic reproduction:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/arabe/texte-juridique/loi-n-70-1320-relative-aux-mesures-sanitaires-de-lutte-contre-la-toxicomanie-et-a-la-repression-du-trafie-et-de-lusage-illicite-des-substances-veneneuses-j-o-r-f-du-3-1-1971-p-74/

French Law **70-1320 of 31 December 1970**, national JORF 3 January 1971, appears in local
journal **3 of 10 February 1971**. The event explicitly describes local publication, not
signing the French law on 10 February or a separately proved day of local commencement.

Article 4 expressly applies Article 2's criminal/therapeutic rules to overseas territories
and leaves treatment arrangements in specified territories, including the French Territory
of the Afars and Issas, to local assemblies. No evidence of a working local treatment
service or a particular local treatment deliberation was recovered.

- L627: ordinary trafficking **2–10 years OR fine OR both**; production/manufacture/import/
  export prison range **10–20 years**; facilitation and false-prescription offences.
- L628: illicit use **2 months–1 year OR fine OR both**. The local transcription is damaged
  in the fine-only wording, but retains the alternative; later French primary case law also
  quotes the same use/fine-only structure. No later French fine amounts are imported into
  the 1971 local event. Monetary values are omitted because colonial currency application
  was not independently reconstructed.
- L628-1: prosecutorial treatment/medical supervision, successful completion preventing
  prosecution under its conditions. First-offence provisions and discretion for repetition
  matter; it is not a universal repeat-offender immunity.
- L628-2 onwards: court-ordered care, sanctions for evasion, and other treatment rules.
- Foreign-exclusion and doubled repeat penalties are described at their historical scope.

Original French law landing page (scanned issue link):
https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000000321402/

Parallel primary judicial quotation of L628's 2-month–1-year/fine/either structure:
https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000007063047
(14 November 1988; later French fine wording is not used as a 1971 local amount).

Category `other`: the full prior local criminal scheme was not recovered, so no invented
whole-point direction is assigned from the later national ceiling alone.

## A named cannabis conviction and later pardon exclusion

https://www.journalofficiel.dj/arabe/?p=110055

Read complete order **72-295 of 24 February 1972**, local journal 5 of 10 March. It identifies
**Mohamed Aden Darar**, born around 1950 in Djibouti, convicted **29 July 1971** of cannabis
possession and sale: **six months and a three-year residence ban**. Detained 10 July 1971,
released 10 January 1972. The order bars residence in Djibouti district and the Obock and
Tadjourah circles. No weight, personal-use intent or named sentencing statute is supplied;
none is invented. The entry is a court/sentencing/enforcement event dated to conviction,
not a new national cannabis ban or an assumption about all users.

https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/decret-n84-123-pre-portant-mesure-de-graces-collectives-collectives/

Read complete decree **84-123**, dated **13 November 1984**, journal of 15 December.
Article 1 remits eligible firm sentences of eight months or less for offences before
15 October 1984; Article 2(d) excludes convictions under public-health-code L626–L630-2
as drafted by the French 1970 law. Article 4 marks the Islamic New Year and makes it
immediately enforceable. No number of people excluded is stated. This supports continued
reference to French legislation after independence; it does not prove every 1970 clause
survived unchanged until 1995. Category `other`, tags amnesty/sentencing: a legal exclusion
from a new pardon, without an adult-cannabis score change.

## 1995 Penal Code: read general sentencing rules with drug offences

Complete 130-page copy downloaded; relevant sections read and PDF pages **17–18 and 79–80**
visually inspected:
https://www.vertic.org/media/National%20Legislation/Djibouti/DJ_Code_Penal.pdf

Law **59/AN/94 dated 5 January 1995**. Actual commencement **16 April 1995**, expressly
confirmed by Article 2 of the official Book V decree:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/decret-n95-0038-pr-mj-portant-creation-du-livre-v-du-code-penal-relatif-aux-contraventions/
Decree dated 5 March, journal 15 March; signed Hassan Gouled Aptidon.

- **Article 70 spans PDF pages 17–18**: court may impose **only one of the available
  penalties**. The drug clauses' wording `and a fine` must be read with this rule.
- Articles 71–73 allow reduction of criminal imprisonment, shorter ordinary prison terms
  (minimum generally 24 hours), and lower fines (minimum generally 1,000 francs). Do not
  call the drug offence's listed year count a mandatory minimum or inevitable term.
- 355: illicit production/manufacture/import/export, **20 years + 500m francs** listed;
  creating/directing a group preparing those crimes, **life**. Distinguish this from
  a group preparing the possession/supply offences under 356, whose leader faces 20 years.
- 356: transport, possession, offer, supply/acquisition, **10 years + 500m** listed;
  no personal-use gram threshold in this section.
- 357: laundering drug proceeds, **10 years + 50m** listed.
- 358: direct supply to a consumer, **5 years + 5m** listed, rising to **10 years** for
  minors, educational centres or government premises. Gifts are included by cession/offer.
- 359: illicit use, **1 year + 200,000** listed. Article 70 allows a fine alone; 72 permits
  a shorter prison term. No claim about the normal sentence actually imposed is made.
- 363–364: work, weapons, licences, civic/public-office restrictions and confiscation.
- 366: optional foreigner exclusion **permanent or up to ten years**, only **355–357**;
  does **not** list 358 direct supply or 359 simple use. Do not apply it to every user.
- 367–368: confiscation and closure measures for specified offences/venues.

A full consolidated audit of intervening amendments and later special laws remains open.
The general availability of a fine alone is a legal-text finding, not proof of routine
noncustodial enforcement. Personal growing has no separately verified lesser tier; no
automatic equivalence between every home plant and an ordinary production conviction is
claimed. Category other, because comparison against the complete immediately prior local
scheme remains incomplete.

## Commercial free zones and contemporary enforcement

Read complete official decree, **9 October 2003**, journal 15 October:
https://www.journalofficiel.dj/texte-juridique/decret-n2003-0202-pre-portant-reglementation-des-activites-economiques-des-zones-franches-a-djibouti/

Articles 3–8 open zones to broad goods, manufacturing and trade with customs/tax privileges.
**Article 13** expressly excludes cannabis, hashish, cannabis resin and khat leaves (along
with other listed goods). This is a special-zone exclusion within an existing national
prohibition; no new adult-cannabis penalty or changed national status is proved. Category
other, tag trade. Do not extrapolate a national khat ban from this geographic restriction.
