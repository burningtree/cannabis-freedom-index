## Sources read

All numbered laws below are the original text in the Turkish parliament's
Kanunlar Dergisi collection, downloaded and extracted locally under
`tmp/pdfs/turkey-penalties-2026-10-26/`. Bibliographic locators distinguish
printed pages from PDF pages. Critical 1953 penalty/commencement clauses,
1979 cultivation rules and 1990 repeal wording were also rendered and visually
inspected. This pass did not rely on the present consolidation to date or
reconstruct historical amendments.

- [Law 2313](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc012/kanuntbmmc012/kanuntbmmc01202313.pdf),
  printed pp. 1028–1031, all four PDF pages: Article 3's original wording
  refers to the plant described as serving exclusively for making esrar
  (Indian hemp), and prohibits preparation, import, export and sale of the
  drug. Article 22 requires seizure; Article 23 requires destruction following
  a joint health/agricultural report and prescribes **one to six months** for
  growing in breach of the ban. Article 30 replaces earlier Law 1369; Article
  31 commences on publication. Header: adopted 12 June, published **24 June
  1933**, Gazette 2435. The original does not contain the later blanket hemp
  permit regime, so it cannot be presented as if the modern wording operated
  from 1933.
- [Law 4055](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc022/kanuntbmmc022/kanuntbmmc02204055.pdf),
  printed pp. 720–722: revised Articles 403–404 prescribe **two to six months**
  and a fine for drug use or possession, with internal exile for repeat
  offending and hospital confinement for people considered dependent.
  Domestic supply/manufacture/import/export are separately punishable with
  prison, a fine and internal exile. Article 2 commences on publication;
  the header gives **6 June 1941**, not adoption on 2 June.
- [Law 6123](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc035/kanuntbmmc035/kanuntbmmc03506123.pdf),
  printed pp. 841–843 and 852 (PDF pp. 11–13 and 22): Article 403(2)
  explicitly includes **esrar** with heroin/cocaine/morphine in the life
  sentence for unauthorised manufacture, import or export; (5)–(7) prescribe
  death in specified aggravated cases. Article 406 adds professional/other
  capital aggravations. Article 404(2) prescribes **three to five years** plus
  a fine for use or possession for use, repeat-offence exile and hospital
  confinement. Article 4 explicitly commences **1 August 1953**, despite
  adoption on 9 July and publication on 15 July. The first search leads did
  not substitute for this final commencement clause.
- [Law 647](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc048/kanuntbmmc048/kanuntbmmc04800647.pdf),
  printed pp. 669–670 (PDF pp. 5–6): provisional Article 2 abolishes internal
  exile in the criminal code and special laws. Article 20 gives publication
  commencement for this provision; the staged exceptions concern other
  provisions. Publication **16 July 1965**, Gazette 12050. This is a general
  sentencing repeal applied to the specifically documented cannabis/drug
  exile clauses, not a claim that drug imprisonment or death was abolished.
- [Law 2236](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc062/kanuntbmmc062/kanuntbmmc06202236.pdf),
  printed p. 211, complete one-page law: replaces Articles 3, 23 and 28,
  prescribing at least one year and a 2,000–10,000-lira fine for prohibited
  drug-cannabis cultivation, agricultural reports, crop destruction, recovery
  of costs, equipment funding and rewards. Article 2 commences on publication,
  **31 May 1979**, Gazette 16652, rather than adoption on 22 May.
- [Law 3652](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc073/kanuntbmmc073/kanuntbmmc07303652.pdf),
  printed p. 445, complete one-page law: expressly requires permission for
  fibre, seed, stalk and similar hemp growing, prohibits unlicensed growing
  for any purpose, retains crop destruction/cost recovery and specifies a
  fine for permit/area/location violations. The criminal drug-cultivation
  clause retains at least one year's prison. Article 2 and the header give
  **30 May 1990** commencement, not adoption on 23 May.
- [Law 3679](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc074/kanuntbmmc074/kanuntbmmc07403679.pdf),
  printed pp. 92–94 (PDF pp. 5–7): Article 23 replaces the Article 406
  life-to-death aggravations with life served with an initial isolation
  period; Article 27 replaces death with life in Article 403(5)–(7) and
  Article 407(2). Article 29 commences on publication, **29 November 1990**,
  Gazette 20710, after adoption on 21 November. This is the drug death-penalty
  repeal; some secondary summaries incorrectly attribute it to June 1991.
- [Law 3756](https://www5.tbmm.gov.tr/tutanaklar/KANUNLAR_KARARLAR/kanuntbmmc074/kanuntbmmc074/kanuntbmmc07403756.pdf),
  printed pp. 490–491 and 494 (PDF pp. 2–3 and 6): Articles 6–7 replace drug
  trafficking and personal-use rules. Personal use/possession for use becomes
  **one to two years**; approaching authorities for treatment before an
  investigation protects against prosecution for use. Hospital confinement
  for people considered dependent remains. Article 27 and the publication
  header give **14 June 1991** commencement, after adoption on 6 June.
- [Amnesty International, Death Penalty News, June 1991](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/act530031991en.pdf),
  ACT 53/03/91, p. 1, Turkey section: read the execution moratorium since 1984,
  continuing capital sentences/ratification queue, November 1990 reform and
  April 1991 commutation rules. The drug-smuggling prisoners are not identified
  by substance: no cannabis-only prisoner count is inferred. The article uses
  under 200 words from this report and the other sources are statutory texts.

## What changed

Six checked events fill the mid-century and pre-probation sequence: 1941,
1953, 1965, 1979, November 1990 and June 1991. Three new articles explain
capital penalties, crop destruction and capital repeal. The 1933 article is
rewritten against the original statute, replacing its retrospective use of
later rules and removing its claim that no later amendment moved a score.
The 1933 and May 1990 event dates now follow publication commencement, and
both descriptions use their original texts with numbered citations.

The two new scored steps concern enforcement only. The 1941 custodial rule
supports band 1 before 1953. The 1953 capital clauses take enforcement to 0;
the 1990 removal restores the custodial band 1 before the already scored 2006
probation reform. Each new vector retains possession/cultivation/sharing/use
at 1 and access at 0, matching the inherited neighbouring vector. The chain
therefore displays **1933: 100→9; 1953: 9→7; 1990: 7→9; 2006: 9→11**.
Today's profile is byte-identical. Existing before-vectors are unchanged.
The exile repeal is easing but capital exposure keeps enforcement at 0;
the 1979 cultivation and 1991 use changes remain within their existing prison
bands. Their explicit `unscored` explanations distinguish actual statutory
changes from changes large enough to move a segment.

## Remaining questions

The original 1933 law expressly replaces an earlier narcotics statute, and
the Ottoman research already documents much earlier restrictions. Its inherited
100→9 allocation still needs a joint reconstruction of the late Ottoman and
early republican rules, including the 1926 criminal code and the 1928 law.
This batch preserves that starting allocation; it does not verify that cannabis
was free before 1933. The 1941 event likewise does not claim first criminalisation.
The original 2005 code, the full 2014 personal-use revision and current-profile
medical-access claims remain separate follow-up work, not findings of this pass.
