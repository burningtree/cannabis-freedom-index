## Boundaries and further work

Bradford notes varying article numbers across penal-code editions. The article
does not present Article 125 as an independently collated universal numbering.
Original code editions and the 1969/1970 fertiliser edicts remain unexamined.
The chapter routes the latter through Robert Connell Clarke's *Hashish*.

Formal bans and observed open trade are different forms of evidence. No claim of
a legally exempt Chicken Street. No present travel recommendation or current
legal update inferred from historical tourism. The 1973 event's existing issuer
and eradication-payment claims need their own primary-source pass; this reading
does not independently confirm or silently revise them or alter historical scores.

Baba Ku's cannabis-introduction story is a legend. AAN's account of an early
twentieth-century Baba Qu should not be merged into a verified ancient biography.
Hospital admission percentages in historical medical reports are not prevalence
figures or clinical proof that cannabis caused all the conditions recorded.

Document 355 describes diplomatic persuasion and the embassy's assessment of its
own success. It is not independent confirmation of press allegations. Document
372 likewise credits embassy efforts from the US author's perspective. Neither
proves publicity alone caused subsequent laws. The original cited newspaper
articles, and Afghan readers' responses, remain separate research tasks.
