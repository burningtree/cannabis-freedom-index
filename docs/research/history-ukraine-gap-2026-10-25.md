## What the texts support

- 1974 personal making, acquisition, storage, transport or sending without
  intent to sell: up to three years' imprisonment or one year of corrective
  labour. Repetition or specified prior drug crimes: up to five years.
- 1974 supply: up to ten years, possible confiscation; aggravated cases six
  to fifteen years with confiscation. This is more precise than applying a
  generic Soviet trafficking range to the Ukrainian code.
- 1974 cultivation: named Indian, southern Manchurian and southern Chuy hemp,
  and other prohibited drug crops; up to five years, aggravated three to eight.
  This is not evidence that all industrial hemp was prohibited.
- 1974 compulsory treatment: diagnosed people avoiding treatment could be sent
  to treatment-and-labour institutions for six months to two years. The decree
  amended a 1969 law; it did not invent compulsory treatment from nothing.
- 1987 small possession/use: an administrative first tier (up to 100 roubles,
  one to two months' corrective labour with 20% wage deduction, or exceptionally
  up to fifteen days' arrest); repetition within a year after an administrative
  penalty could become a crime (up to two years, corrective labour or fine).
- 1987 growing: named prohibited varieties directly criminal, other hemp
  warning/fine before repeated offending became criminal. No modern gram or
  plant limit is projected into this text.
- Voluntary surrender and medical-help exemptions have offence-specific scopes.
  The article does not claim that surrender excused every supply offence or
  that mere recreational use qualified a person for every treatment exemption.
- The original 1987 health amendment authorised up to ten days' compulsory
  hospitalisation for an examination when a person suspected of non-medical
  use refused examination. It is separate from imprisonment for an offence.

No arrest counts, sentence frequencies or cannabis-only enforcement estimates
were found in these texts. The articles state statutory exposure, not actual
numbers imprisoned.

## Unresolved historical scoring and profile audit

Ukraine's inherited chain has only 1934 (100→21.5) and 2024 (21.5→22.5),
displayed as 100→22 and 22→23. It therefore
attributes the modern administrative-possession and small-grow position to the
1934 cultivation restriction. Inserting a modern-score delta in 1974 would
compound that error: it would imply that the 1974 prison law produced today's
administrative regime or that the 2024 medical law removed Soviet criminal
liability. Neither claim is supported.

The two entries have explicit `unscored` reconstruction reasons. These are real
legal changes whose score allocation remains unfinished, not a finding that the
laws had no effect. Obtain the original pre-1974 criminal code and the original
1984 administrative code, then trace 1991/1995 personal-use amendments, the 2001
code, the 2009 grow threshold and subsequent sentencing amendments together.
The modern profile and both existing `before` vectors are preserved pending
that work; this pass does not certify the old 1934 allocation.

A current-law search also surfaced discrepancies needing a separate joined
profile/timeline audit: Article 44's fine may now be 50–100 non-taxable minimums
rather than the profile's 25–50; ordinary Article 309 penalties have changed;
the profile's public-use wording should be compared with Article 316's defined
criminal public/group offence. EUDA's older Ukraine overview and current
penalty table cover different versions. These search leads have **not** been
promoted to checked current-law facts or copied into the articles.

The first headline deliberately says possession is punishable by prison without
claiming that every prior form of possession was lawful. The old code and
republic-specific earlier amendments still need checking before a first-ever
criminalisation claim can be made.
