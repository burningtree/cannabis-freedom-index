# Earlier territorial cannabis controls: Marshall and Mariana islands

Research date: 23 October 2026. This pass adds the 1980 territorial-code record
for the Marshall Islands and the 23 July 1974 Bermudes judgment for the Northern
Mariana Islands, with two articles. It revises the 1987 Marshall Islands entry
and article to acknowledge the preceding territorial controls. Tags and inline
citations are included. Current profiles and all score vectors are unchanged.

## Sources actually read

- **Trust Territory Code 1980, Title 63**, scanned original retrieved through
  the Internet Archive from CNMI Law Revision Commission's former PDF URL.
  Relevant printed pages 127–142: short title, definitions, marijuana and THC
  in Schedule I, manufacture/delivery, marijuana possession bands, small unpaid
  gifts and conditional discharge. Printed p. 140 was also rendered and visually
  checked: **more than one ounce but less than 2.2 pounds carries three months,
  not one year**; an ounce or less carries a maximum $50 fine. The first extraction
  from a secondary HTML reproduction was not treated as sufficient provenance.
- **Title 1, section 151**, printed p. 75: expressly extends the code throughout
  the territory, including the Marshall Islands. This separate source supplies
  geographical applicability; Title 63 alone does not.
- **Marshall Islands Constitution**, official consolidated publication:
  Article XIII section 1 continues existing law subject to the Constitution;
  Article XIV defines existing law and gives 1 May 1979 as the effective date.
  General continuity does not establish that every clause in a later consolidation
  was already in force in 1979.
- **Trust Territory v Bermudes**, 7 TTR 80–90, 23 July 1974: relevant judgment
  read through dismissal, including footnoted earlier statutory wording. The
  case was heard in the Mariana Islands District. It establishes dismissal of
  Bermudes' possession/sale charges and invalidation of the challenged territorial
  drug provisions, not a verified period of unrestricted cannabis in the Marshall
  Islands. The court left the separate privacy question unresolved.
- The existing Marshall Islands sources read in the preceding research pass
  remain the basis for the 1987 article's later developments; see
  [the earlier memo](history-marshall-gap-2026-10-23.md).

Local research originals, text extractions and the visual check are retained in
`tmp/history-marshall-territorial-gap-2026-10-23/` and the preceding batch directory.
The current CNMI website has migrated URLs: several old `/pdf/ttc/` routes return
HTML rather than a PDF. The cited Wayback routes returned actual scanned code
PDFs. No archive-only schema fields were added.

## Dates and historical scores remain open

**1980 is the code-edition date, not an asserted commencement or new fine-only
reform.** Its entry is category `other` and describes what the edition records.
It does not attribute the code's rules to a new law passed that year.

The code attributes the controlled-substances chapter to Congress of Micronesia
P.L. 5-110. A reproduced 15 September 1975 diplomatic cable reports passage of
a controlled-substances law in 1974, while implementing regulations were being
developed. That lead does not supply the original Act, its exact effective date,
or proof that every 1980 clause was identical at passage. It was not used as a
public source. A scholarly footnote identifying a 1974 NORML newsletter report
also remains a retrieval lead; the newsletter itself was not recovered.

**The Marshall Islands chart's existing 1987 100→11 transition remains provisional
and historically incomplete.** Earlier territorial control is now established,
so 1987 must not be described as the first restriction. Moving that drop to an
invented earlier commencement, or inferring original 1987 penalties from later
consolidations, would add unsupported precision. Retrieve P.L. 5-110, the earlier
regulation and the original 1987 Act before rebuilding the sequence. Small gifts
and possession in the territorial text also need their own historically justified
score allocation. The automatic chain check detects continuity and direction,
not this missing historical evidence.

The Bermudes ruling is category `easing`, with an explicit unscored reason:
reconstruction of the earlier baseline and replacement law's commencement is
pending. The existing Northern Mariana Islands 1925 first-restriction estimate
is likewise still unsupported by an original local instrument. Do not transplant
the Mariana case into a dated Marshall Islands legalisation event.

## Leads rejected or left open

- The Journal retrospective headed “Police destroy marijuana” was read in full.
  Its marijuana seizure belongs to **18 September 1992**, not the 1975 edition
  appearing above it. No 1975 seizure entry was created.
- The Marshall Islands' Public Health, Safety and Welfare Act text has general
  drug-related health powers but did not establish a cannabis-specific 1966 ban.
- Parliament's current Title 7 and repealed-laws index did not recover the older
  territorial repeal or original 1987 Act.
- An unverified reproduced letter about an alleged 1974 Marshall Islands cannabis
  conviction was not used.
