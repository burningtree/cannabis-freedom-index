## Sources actually read

- **Original Narcotics Act B.E.2522 (1979)**, Thai FDA: https://narcotic.fda.moph.go.th/law-type/na-02-1-2522 . Reopened the official page and its PDF viewer, then read the existing downloaded original `tmp/history-2000s-2026-10-17/th1979.pdf/.txt`. Attachment ID 499135076869742592. Read sections 2–4, 7–8, 26, 57, 75–76, 92 and the explanatory note. Signed 22 April; Gazette 27 April; next-day commencement. Original category 5 possession: up to five years AND up to 50,000 baht; possession for distribution and production/distribution/import/export: two to fifteen years AND 20,000–150,000 baht. Original use: up to one year AND 10,000 baht. Section 26 presumes possession for distribution at ten kilograms; this is the original rule, not the present Code. Definitions include cultivation within production and giving within distribution. Section 3 repeals seven laws, including the Cannabis Act B.E.2477, published in 1935. The explanatory note invokes outdated provisions, more effective controls and treaty consistency.
- **Act Promulgating the Narcotics Code and annexed Code B.E.2564 (2021)**, original Thai Gazette, 80 pages: https://narcotic.fda.moph.go.th/law-type/na-10 . Followed the viewer's attachment ID 498847832757903360 and downloaded the PDF successfully to `tmp/thailand-1979-2021/code-thai.pdf`; extracted text alongside it. Read promulgating Act sections 2–4, 8–10 and 23; Code sections 29, 93, 104, 107, 113–114, 162, 164–165 and the explanatory note. Gazette vol.138 part73a dated 8 November; the Act takes effect next day and the Code after thirty days, yielding 9 December 2021. Section 4 expressly repeals the 1979 Act and its amendments; section 8 continues compatible subordinate rules and section 9 qualifies provisions needing new regulations. Category 5 controls continue under section 93. Sections 107/164 create distinct personal-use possession exposure of up to two years OR 40,000 baht OR both; sections 104/162 address consumption with up to one year OR 20,000 baht OR both, subject to exceptions. Section 113 requires voluntary treatment before detection, compliance with prescribed conditions and written satisfactory-completion certification to remove liability. Section 114 addresses qualifying referrals following detection. Section 165 lets courts prioritise treatment and consider individual circumstances. These are statutory routes, not proof of typical sentencing practice or automatic immunity for small amounts. English wording in the articles is a paraphrase of the cited Thai text.
- **Tilleke & Gibbins, 13 June 2022**, read the contemporaneous legal analysis: https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-removes-cannabis-from-the-narcotics-list-whats-next-for-the-industry/ . Confirms the 2019 medical opening, the previous licensing regime and the 9 June 2022 change distinguishing plant material from specified extracts. Used for that historical context, not present-day advice.
