# Guinea-Bissau and Equatorial Guinea primary-law audit, 15 October 2026

This pass corrects two weak profiles and recovers their statutory history. It does not change the
research formula or mark either country complete. Working PDFs, extracted text, page renders and
before/after coverage records are in ignored `tmp/research-progress-pass5/`.

## Guinea-Bissau

Read the Portuguese compilation of Decree-Law 2-B/93, including its preamble, articles 1–42 and
relevant cannabis schedule entries. Visually checked PDF page 7 against the extracted article 20
and treatment text. The compilation is hosted by OGDT; the retained UN mission English translation
was also downloaded and compared. It translates the same law and does not substantiate the
profile's claimed separate 2010 drug enactment. The English text mistranslates some waiver wording;
the Portuguese text is the basis of the corrections.

- Article 20 distinguishes high-risk plants/substances, expressly including cannabis oil (two
  months to one year), other cannabis derivatives (one to six months), and the lower-risk category
  (15 days to three months). The old general three-month-or-fine statement is removed. No universal
  flower/derivative equivalence, fixed gram threshold or fine alternative was inferred.
- A discretionary waiver requires minority, no repeat offence and a solemn undertaking before a
  magistrate, cumulatively. It is not a general adult first-offender exemption.
- Article 3's edited two-year minimum has a footnote saying the official gazette omitted that
  value. The twelve-year maximum is stated directly, with the minimum's uncertainty retained.
- Added actual statutory detail on production authorisations, cannabis product categories,
  voluntary care versus court-supervised treatment, forfeiture and conditional foreigner
  expulsion. No issued cannabis licence, patient programme or industrial-hemp permission is claimed.
- Corrected sharing **1 → 0** and consumption **1 → 0**, matching the rubric's trafficking-equivalent
  transfer and standalone use offences. Weighted total **11 → 9**. This is a correction to the
  assessed legal framework, not a newly enacted 2026 restriction. Possession and enforcement
  ratings remain provisional without representative practice evidence. The existing estimated
  first restriction remains estimated and ends at the corrected current vector; no unsupported
  dated score transition is invented.

Read all 17 articles of the UNODC-hosted French translation of Decree-Law 1/76 of 21 April 1976.
It prescribes death for specified narcotics production/supply and forced labour for other conduct.
Its article 17 refers to a controlled-drug annex absent from the retained copy. Added a context
history entry and explainer with that limitation; did not label 1976 the first cannabis ban, assert
cannabis executions, or assign a death-penalty cannabis score from an unverified schedule.
Article 41 of the 1993 decree independently confirms repeal of the predecessor. Revised the 1993
entry from an unexplained tightening to a framework-replacement context event and expanded its
existing explainer.

## Equatorial Guinea

Read the entire three-page UNODC-hosted English translation of Decree-Law 3/1993. Visually checked
page 2 for the penalty, aggravation, repeal and commencement provisions. The file path uses `gin`,
but the document expressly names Equatorial Guinea and is signed by Obiang; it is not Guinea's law.

- The decree expressly covers possession, cultivation, use and donation. Its medium-term prison
  category is not translated into years without the applicable code. The old one-to-twenty-year
  assertion is removed.
- Medical/scientific authorisation and prescription rules are general controlled-medicines powers,
  not verified cannabis patient access. Professional disqualification applies to specified abuse
  of professional/public office, not all cannabis users.
- The signing date is 15 September 1993. Commencement depends on publication in national media;
  that publication date was not verified and is explicitly distinguished in the event/article.
- Read Decree 480/1966 in Spain's original BOE PDF: its single article applies the revised 1963
  Penal Code. Added a publication-date context event documenting statutory ancestry, without
  claiming that it independently dates the first cannabis prohibition.
- Read A/78/298 paragraph 31, which dates the later Penal Code's commencement to 10 December 2022.
  Current FCDO advice still warns of prison for drug possession. The profile clearly separates
  these sources from the 2000 AFP report; old open-use reporting is not current enforcement data.

The official BOE Guinea portal timed out. Public user-uploaded 2022 code copies were found, one
with only a preview and another a transcription with apparent cross-reference issues. They remain
leads rather than a verified consolidated source. Their drug and driving provisions were not used
to publish exact current penalties. Reconciliation of the 1993 decree with an authenticated 2022
code and later amendments is the next major task. All numerical ratings remain provisional.
No 2022 cannabis reform event or score movement was inferred from general death-penalty reporting.
