### 1931 law: actual commencement and contents

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=275457
Ordres en Conseil IX (1931–1934), appendix pp.421–439, PDF422–440.
The appendix expressly groups items outside chronological order. Its cover-period
ending in 1934 is not the law's date.

Read the petition and royal approval (pp.421–423), articles I–III (424–425),
inspection/penalties (427–430), repeals and commencement (433), definitions
(434–435), and possession, supply, professional authority and records (435–438).
Visually checked printed pp.421, 433 and 436. Article XVI explicitly commences on
registration; the heading records **7 March 1931**. Royal approval was 12 February.
The 1966 original law, definition section p.10, independently identifies the same
1931 law and its registration date.

- Indian hemp: dried female flowering/fruiting tops, resin and resin-based
  preparations (Schedule B). Do not transplant the modern whole-plant definition.
- Authority needed for possession and attempted possession, supply/procurement,
  offering to supply/procure and advertising (Schedule C 1–3).
- Imports into Guernsey through Great Britain, into the other islands through
  Guernsey, and restricted inter-island export, with prescribed formalities (I–II).
- Qualified doctors, chemists and veterinary surgeons have professional authority;
  analysts have possession authority. These permissions do not establish stocked
  medicines or a modern patient programme (Schedule C 5).
- Separate registers, normally same-day entries, two-year retention after last entry;
  withdrawal of convicted licensees' authority with appeal (Schedule C 4–6).
- Article VII distinguishes ten years' penal servitude, two years' imprisonment and
  a £1,000 fine on indictment from twelve months/£250 in police court. This is a
  statutory maximum framework, not proof of typical sentences or small-user practice.

A modern campaign PDF gives 1934. The original registration and commencement text
are stronger evidence. The campaign's chronology is not a source for the new entries.

### 1924 and 1927 predecessors examined

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=275455
Ordres en Conseil VII, 1924 law pp.203–221, including Schedule A,
raw-opium schedules and professional permissions. Read the main law and schedules.
Schedule A names opiates, cocaine and ecgonine; no cannabis/Indian-hemp entry found.
Its petition identifies an earlier **27 August 1921** ordinance and references the
1914 pharmacy/poisons rules. Those earlier instruments remain to be examined.

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=275456
Ordres en Conseil VIII, 1927 supplementary law pp.106 onward. Read the petition and
amending provisions; no named cannabis/Indian-hemp provision found. The 1931 law
repeals both predecessors. This supports an earlier opium framework, not a claim
that the 1924 law prohibited cannabis.

The 1932 amendment in volume IX, pp.197–200, expands the power to amend schedules.
It is not itself proof of a newly introduced cannabis possession prohibition.

### 1949 schedule

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=259184
Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, 1949, printed pp.43–46; read all four pages, visually
checked p.44. Section 5 starts it on **1 October 1949**, not its 21 September making
date. New Schedule A item 11 covers Indian-hemp extracts/tinctures and preparations
made from them, except those capable only of external use. It repeals 1932 provisional
instruments; those must be recovered before calling 1949 the first extract control.
Accordingly the new event says the revised schedule lists these products.

### 1966 law begins in 1967

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=252240
Original Dangerous Drugs Law, 1966 (32 PDF pages). Read cover/registration,
interpretation pp.9–10, sections 2–7 pp.12–15, repeals/commencement p.26 and the
cannabis-extract schedule item p.29. Registration **10 May 1966** is not commencement.

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=259094
Original one-page commencement ordinance XVII/1967, read and visually checked:
all the 1966 law starts throughout the Bailiwick on **22 March 1967**.
Sections 5–6 expressly concern cannabis smoking/dealing premises and knowingly
cultivating a cannabis plant without a licence. The 1931 regime already restricted
production, possession and supply. No first cultivation restriction is asserted.

Also downloaded the original Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, 1967:
https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=259104
Read cover, contents, Part II applicability and commencement; it begins **1 May 1967**.
Do not conflate this separate set of supply/record rules with the law's 22 March start.
Its complete detailed prescription system remains for a later pass.

### 1974 law has two commencement stages in 1976

https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=258398
Original two-page commencement ordinance IX/1976: read both, visually checked p.1.
Sections 1, 6, 9, 21, 30 and 34 start **31 March 1976**; the remaining law begins
**1 June 1976**. The later date covers possession/cultivation sections 4–5.

The archive file linked as the 1974 law actually contains a modern consolidation:
https://guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=251722
Read its cover and amendment disclaimer, section subjects, 33–34 and amendment notes.
Used only for stable section subjects/replacement, not historical penalty amounts.
The cannabis definition was replaced from 15 November 1983: do not backdate its
modern whole-plant wording to 1976. GFSC's separate consolidation was also downloaded
and its repeal/commencement sections examined; it does not prove the operative date.
Original 1974 text remains a further source target.

## Scores and unresolved earlier history

No country profile or score vector changed. The estimated first-restriction window
is narrowed from 1925–1961 to **1925–1931**, because the prohibition is verified by
1931. This changes the interpolation of historical annual scores; the 1925 lower
bound remains an assumption, not a proved date. The estimate's description now
acknowledges the verified 1931 framework and explicitly labels its own date as an
estimate. Its scored baseline remains unresolved: the earlier pharmacy/poison
rules and historic enforcement must be reconstructed before replacing it. The
1931 event supplies the estimate's documented upper bound; a second score drop
would double-count those restrictions. This does not claim that the original law
itself was uncertain or that 1934 was its commencement.

The later statutes replace licensed-control regimes; the 1949 record avoids a
first-control claim, and the 2003 case applies existing premises law. A complete
score reconstruction still needs evidence of changes in actual small-user practice,
medical availability and the earlier statutes. These entries advance the documentary
history without supplying unsupported score vectors.
