# Six additional history articles

Added articles for existing entries, without changing the score timelines:

- `denmark-christiania-junk-blockade-1979`: Christiania's own history dates the wider campaign to 1979–80; Jenny Künkel's CILIP research account dates the forty-day blockade to autumn 1979. The article preserves that distinction and includes coercion/exclusion as well as support for treatment. It does not imply national legalisation of cannabis.
- `netherlands-ruigoord-saved-1973`: regional heritage source confirms preservation in 1973. Community programmes and Christiania's cultural association document the setting and later connections. The article links the Highlife Cup as a separate event and does not reproduce the delegation report's inaccurate claims about Dutch cannabis law.
- `uk-burns-halloween-1786`: Glasgow's first-edition digitisation establishes publication in July 1786; Burns's poem and explanatory notes support the charm and comic episode. Publication is not dated as the origin of the practice, and no intoxication is inferred.
- `france-paradis-artificiels-1860`: BnF's record confirms the 1860 Poulet-Malassis et de Broise edition. The literary analysis uses Baudelaire's public-domain text, accessed through the later collected edition on Wikisource, especially the opening, Morale and the introduction to his treatment of De Quincey. The source edition is distinguished from first publication; moral judgements are not presented as clinical findings.
- `italy-hemp-law-2016`: original Gazzetta Ufficiale text, particularly articles 1, 2 and 4. Farmer protection for crops testing above 0.2% and within 0.6% is distinguished from retail permission; later flower restrictions are linked separately. This is historical analysis of the original framework, not current selling advice.
- `france-cbd-flower-2022`: Conseil d'État's official explanation and judgment 444887. The article describes annulment of a specific blanket restriction, proportionality and the limits of the ruling, rather than general recreational legalisation or a claim that all CBD products are harmless.

Each article is between 350 and 500 words, carries model/date and source metadata, and links to the relevant traditions or related history. Four cultural items were added to the curated Culture History list. Only the Ruigoord article uses a photograph, reusing the already inspected and credited Arthena CC BY-SA 4.0 village image. No substitute institutional stock photographs were added.
