# Cuba: possession penalties before the 1999 and 2022 codes

Research checked on 25 October 2026. Read the maintenance, writing and local-book
research guidance. This pass targets Cuba's sparse eight-entry timeline.

## Applied evidence

- **1958 rebel territory:** Cuba's official *Programa Nacional de Prevención del
  Uso Indebido y Control de Drogas*, held in the WHO policy repository,
  [introduction, PDF page 1](https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/plan-strategy/CUB-AD-17-01-PLAN-STRATEGY-1958-esp-Programa-Nacional-Prevencion-Drogas.pdf).
  Read through the web tool; a direct download returned 403. Its retrospective
  account expressly dates Disposición 6 to **7 October 1958**, says it entered
  into force then, and lists harvesting, trafficking, carrying, keeping and
  smoking marijuana. This is a rebel-territory rule, not proof of the first
  nationwide restriction. No specific sentence was supplied or invented. The
  document is a later national programme: its filename's `1958` is not its
  publication date. Its introduction discusses a programme adopted in 1999.
- **1979 code:** the drug provisions officially supplied to the UN in
  [E/NL.1985/90, within E/NL.1985/88–91](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/50858/files/e-nl-1985-88-91-s.pdf),
  printed/PDF pp. 12–13. Downloaded, extracted and visually checked PDF page 12.
  Article 218 gives **six months to three years** for simple possession.
  Article 217 expressly names marijuana, with **three to eight years** for
  growing/supply, **seven to fifteen** for relatively large volumes, and loss of
  the grower's land/right. Where someone under sixteen was used, the bands were
  **six to ten** and **ten to twenty**. The UN excerpt has no commencement clause.
  The **1 November 1979** commencement comes from the full-text 2024 study by
  Alejandro Simón Valdés and Alie Pérez Véliz,
  [RIDCA, discussion of Law 21](https://aidca.org/ridca5-penal-valdez-perez-veliz-regulacion-normativa-de-las-sanciones-en-cuba-enfoques-teoricos-criminologicos-que-las-han-sustentado/),
  also downloaded; PDF p. 6. Do not confuse adoption, gazette publication and
  commencement dates in secondary bibliographic descriptions.
- **1988 code:** [UNHCR's unamended Law 62 text](https://www.acnur.org/fileadmin/Documentos/BDL/2006/4417.pdf?file=fileadmin/Documentos/BDL/2006/4417),
  read with the web tool, printed pp. 59–60 and 99. Article 191 cuts simple
  possession to **six months to two years**. Article 190 retains the ordinary
  growing/supply bands while reducing the child-related minima to **four** and
  **eight** years. Final provision three expressly starts the code on
  **30 April 1988**. A later consolidation must not be used as its original text.
- **1994 fine option:** [WIPO Lex's annotated consolidation](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/legislation/details/900),
  read as HTML and downloaded as a PDF; printed pp. 88–89, article 191(b).
  Visually checked page 88. The fine of **200–500 quotas**, alone or with the
  six-month-to-two-year prison sentence, is attributed to article 3 of
  Decree-Law 150, signed 6 June 1994 and published **10 June 1994**. The new
  event uses **1994-06**, expressly the publication month. The original
  commencement clause remains unread: **10 June is not asserted as a verified
  commencement day**. Cannabis, cocaine and other drugs have separate bands.
- **1999 and 2022:** reread the existing downloaded Law 87 (article 10 and final
  provisions) and Law 151 (articles 235–236) in `tmp/cuba-history/` and compared
  with WIPO. These establish the later context, the retained simple-possession
  band and why aggravated trafficking maxima must stay separate. Rewrote the
  1999 article with inline citations and links to the earlier possession story.

Local working files and extractions are in ignored
`tmp/history-cuba-gap-2026-10-25/`. No complete source PDF is published by this
batch. The separately retrieved historical book is catalogued as described below.

## Scoring

The profile and current score remain unchanged. The 1958 rule is regional, and
1979 documents a national code without establishing the first prohibition.
The 1988 sentence reduction and 1994 fine option are real legal easing, recorded
as such; both leave prison for personal holdings available within the existing
possession level. There is no new representative evidence that a fine became
usual in practice. No enforcement improvement is inferred from an available
sentencing alternative. The estimated early aggregate remains provisional.

## Earlier 1919 controls: chapter retrieved and read

The publisher's **HTTP** URL finally returned the full 28.8 MB book after HTTPS
and CLACSO requests failed. Read its title, copyright and contents pages, chapter
opening, printed pp. **346–348**, and bibliography pp. **362–364**. Visually checked
the copyright page and p. **347**, where marijuana is explicitly named. The
chapter is Dayron Oliva Hernández's “¿Elementos indeseables y antisanitarios?”,
pp. 329–364, in *El rescate de la memoria*, edited by Jorge Alberto Trujillo Bretón
and Daniel Fessler, Universidad de Guadalajara, first edition 2021, digital ISBN
978-607-571-388-5. PDF pagination is printed page +1 in that chapter.

This is now applied evidence, not a search-snippet lead. A fifth new event and
third new article document the 1919 controls. The event uses **1919**, the known
law year, and describes the law of 25 July; it does not assert that adoption day
as a verified commencement. The author says import/production were reserved to
recognised pharmacists and druggists and identifies a broad trafficking definition
extending to possession. The original law-and-regulations booklet is cited in the
chapter's bibliography but has not itself been read. Prison/fine details are
therefore not converted into a cannabis-specific statutory tariff.

The former **1925–1950** aggregate estimate now uses **1919–1950** and cites the
read chapter instead of Wikipedia. It remains an estimate: the precise first
instrument, cultivation prohibition and medical-access sequence are not yet
reconstructed. The new 1919 event documents the licensed-drug regime as context;
it does not falsely assign today's complete six-segment restriction to the law's
adoption day. The current score stays 3; the annual historical panel changes only
within the earlier estimated window.

The local book is stored in ignored `books/`, with its SHA-256, edition, page
