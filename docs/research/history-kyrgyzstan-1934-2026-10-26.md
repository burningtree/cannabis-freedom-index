## What was verified (all sources actually read)

### USSR decree of 27 October 1934 (Istmat, Russian original read in full)

- Collected Laws of the USSR, No. 56, art. 422 (Moscow 1948 collection):
  union-wide ban on sowing opium poppy and Indian hemp except harvests
  exclusively for USSR medical/scientific needs; annual district quotas by
  the agriculture commissariat (by 15 August, with foreign-trade and
  republic-health agreement); permits via republic land organs; criminal
  liability for unlicensed sowing with mandatory confiscation; republic
  CECs to amend criminal codes; in force from 1935; repeals the 1927
  poppy decree; Kalinin and Molotov.
- New `articles/kyrgyzstan-soviet-ban-1934.md` (368 words, 4 sections,
  both sources cited; links to the 1965 Chuy entry, the Uzbek 1934 entry
  and /country/kg/).

### Kyrgyz Academy of the Ministry of Internal Affairs journal, 2024
(Malabaev et al., printed pp. 88–90, read in full)

- 1950s–60s state hemp/poppy cultivation; autumn influx of addicts and
  speculators (310 kg hashish in three years per Usobaliev's memoirs);
  Frunze tobacco-factory lacing; hashish smoking among school youth;
  rising hashish-intoxication crime; Usobaliev pressing Moscow from 1962;
  chemical-industry substitute by early 1964; 3 June 1964 USSR decree
  banning southern Chuy hemp from 1965, ordering non-narcotic varieties,
  banning household-plot hemp nationwide, liquidating wild hemp;
  conversion of the Chuy station and abolition of the seed station.
