## Thailand: original gazette supersedes summary metadata

Downloaded and visually read all five pages of the Cannabis Act, B.E. 2477,
in the Royal Gazette, volume 52, **5 May 1935, pp. 339–343**, using the original
scan hosted on Wikimedia Commons. Renders and downloads remain in ignored
`tmp/pdfs/southeast-asia-2026-10-20/`.

The Commons description claims commencement on the day after publication.
The actual **section 2, p. 340, says ninety days after publication**. The article
and corrected event follow the scanned statutory text, not that description.
No precise Gregorian commencement day is assigned without separately checking
the applicable counting rule.

Other provisions actually read:

- Section 3: royal-decree provincial exemptions for no more than two years.
  No implementing decrees or actual exempt provinces were verified.
- Section 4: broad plant-part definition and consumption terminology.
- Section 5: cultivation/seed restrictions, one-year transition for existing
  cultivation and individual experimental/medical permissions.
- Section 6: import permission and export prohibition.
- Section 7: possession, purchase, sale, distribution and consumption rules,
  individual medical-possession permission and a one-year transition for
  pre-existing activity.
- Sections 8–10: smoking equipment, museum permission and differentiated
  penalty maxima (one year/500 baht for sections 5–6; six months/200 baht for
  sections 7–8).
- Section 11: ministerial administration and regulations.

The existing `thailand-cannabis-act-1935` keeps its ID, publication date and
before vector, but its title now explicitly says publication and its description
states the delayed operation. Added an article explaining staged application.
The retained score timing still needs review against commencement and the
transitions; preserving a vector does not validate its date or magnitude.

## Vietnam: a reported 1954 instrument precedes the 1968 directive

Downloaded *Laws of Various Asian Countries Pertaining to Cannabis*, Law Library
of Congress, Far Eastern Law Division, **March 1970**. Read and visually
inspected the complete South Vietnam section, **PDF pp. 23–24**, prepared by
**Mya Saw Shin**. The OCR is severely fragmented; the page images supplied the
substantive reading and dates. This is a contemporary government legal survey,
not an original Vietnamese gazette.

It identifies **Order 260-PTT/TTK, dated 31 March 1954**, and describes:

- Article 1: stock declaration within fifteen days of publication.
- Article 2: restrictions on import, export, manufacture, trade and use.
- Article 3: authorised stocks temporarily run down, with 31 December 1954
  as the deadline, and surrender arrangements.
- Article 4: confiscation and disposal of unlawfully held stocks.
- Article 5: the instrument's definition of Indian hemp.

The report says the order itself prescribes no penalties, then discusses a
1955 opium edict addressing abuses by medical professionals. That separate
provision was not converted into an unsupported general cannabis-possession
penalty or a new standalone cannabis event.

Added `vietnam-indian-hemp-order-1954` as checked, unscored context, explicitly
attributing it to the survey. The original order, publication date, territorial
reach and independent commencement were not verified. The report's South
Vietnam heading is retrospective and does not establish a state or its borders
in March 1954.

Read the drug-abuse discussion, pp. 106–107, in George S. Prugh's US Army
official history *Law at War: Vietnam, 1964–1973*, through the Göttingen-hosted
reproduction. Opened its front matter to confirm author, Department of the Army
publisher, 1975 publication and the section title “The Drug Problem.” It describes
an **October 1968** condemnation of cannabis/opium
use and trafficking and directions to provincial chiefs against cultivation.
It does not identify that action as the first cannabis statute. Corrected
`vietnam-ban-1968` accordingly, retaining its stable ID and before vector.
The 1969 eradication entry was not independently audited and remains unchanged.
