## Earlier controls: what was actually read

- Supreme Court, Pueblo v. Tribunal Superior, 84 DPR 140, 13 December 1961: https://app.midpage.ai/document/pueblo-v-tribunal-superior-de-8558422 . Full reproduced opinion read, including notes. Note 1 identifies original cannabis Act 12 of 19 April 1932 and explains that Act 51 of 13 May 1934 re-enacted it. Note 2 quotes the 1934 cannabis rule: cultivation/import/possession/gifts/use and other dealings, misdemeanor, six months–two years. Do not confuse that cannabis-specific Act 51 with the separate general narcotics Act 61 of the same day. Original 1932 instrument and its penalties not retrieved; the article does not repeat Wikipedia's one-month–one-year claim. Adoption date supported, exact 1932 commencement not claimed. FREE before-vector preserved from legacy entry, not independently revalidated by discovering the date.
- Same opinion compares 1934 misdemeanor with 1959 felony; notes 3–4 quote original 1959 cannabis clause and 5–20 years plus discretionary fine ≤$20,000. María Moreno Medina was accused concerning part of a cannabis cigarette found in her bag at Ponce's Bar El Cocodrilo on 8 May 1959. Case dismissal remained for late formal accusation; new harsher law could not apply retroactively. No conviction, pardon or general decriminalisation claimed. Court category enforcement (individual case), tags court/sentencing.
- Archived OGP Narcotics Act: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/48-1959.pdf . Read definitions, prohibitions, cultivation, prescribing, penalties, repealer, commencement and relevant treatment/control sections. Consolidated through 1970, archive warning updated 2025. Original core penalty independently verified through 1961 quotation, avoiding backdating a later amendment. Articles 29–33 and 64: possession/use/medical prescribing banned, industrial cultivation only by permission and continuous strict supervision, fibre stripped of drug substance and sterilised seeds. No THC percentage inserted; no evidence an industrial licence was actually used. Visually inspected PDF pages 13–14. 2025 repeal of surviving pharmacist Article 21 is not a new cannabis legalisation.
- Original Controlled Substances Act 4-1971, recovered via an annotation in the government's current compilation: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/3-duplicados/0004-1971.pdf . Thirty-three scanned two-page spreads. macOS Vision Spanish/English OCR generated locally. Read Article 404 and transition in full, inspected original spreads 18 and 33 (printed pp. 586–588 and 616–617). First possession FOR PERSONAL USE 1–3 years plus optional fine ≤$5,000; subsequent 2–6 plus optional ≤$10,000. Eligible first offenders can receive probation without guilt pronouncement, up to three years, dismissal after compliance/discretion/hearing, confidential court record retained, only once, fingerprints/photos returned. OCR dropped a few line ends; scan controls exact text. Immediately effective for 404 on approval 23 June; other sections mostly 180 days. Benefits reach people convicted afterwards under older charges, not necessarily already final convictions. Category easing, not mere recodification.

## Medical route and actual supply

- Original signed three-page executive order: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2/oe-2015-010.pdf . All three pages visually read. Signed and effective 3 May 2015. Directs health secretary to use statutory powers, reclassify as appropriate within schedules I–V and authorise medical cannabis components; progress report in three months. It does not itself perform all reclassification/licensing or open shops. Existing entry corrected accordingly.
- Original 2017 scan retrieved through PDF annotation: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2/0042-2017.pdf , retained as supporting scan, not fully OCR-read. Main text actually read from the OGP consolidation: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/LeyesOrganicas/PDF/42-2017.pdf (revised April 2024; only later amendment listed is Act 15-2021). Read findings and all relevant definitions, board, medical/consumption/ID/nonresident/licensing/transitional rules and later employment Article 24. Visually inspected consolidated pages 13–14 and 22. Distinguish original 2017 rules from added 2021 employment protection. Smoking banned; vaporisation distinct, original flower exception for terminal illness/absence of suitable alternatives. No claim that later board flower authorisations are fully certified today. Lawmakers criticised the prior executive regulatory approach; do not portray 2017 as first actual access. Transitional certification permits existing businesses to adjust. Medical recognition of nonresident home-jurisdiction ID is express in Article 12 and board-rule duties; profile's earlier assertion that every visitor requires Puerto Rico programme certification was incomplete. No unrestricted tourist adult-use claim.
- Primera Hora, 30 December 2016: https://www.primerahora.com/noticias/puerto-rico/notas/comienza-la-distribucion-de-cannabis-medicinal-en-la-isla/ . Full contemporaneous report read, largely governor's announcement: first two dispensaries, visit, 1,953 registered patients and 23 companies licensed across industry. Figures are attributed to the administration, not a count of dispensaries. Reclassification order dated 17 July 2015 is mentioned in this report; original declarative order was not recovered so no separate event or invented full terms. December 2016 openings independently corroborated in 2020 court facts for Family Dispensary. Other establishments had prequalification/opening publicity earlier in December; not equated with actual dispensing before final approval.

## Patients and premises

- Official Court of Appeals judgment, 23 November 2020: https://dts.poderjudicial.pr/ta/2020/KLAN201900902-23112020.pdf . Read relevant facts and full merits/outcome through web PDF extraction (19 pages). Direct shell download returned 403 and screenshot service failed; don't claim visual verification. Family Dispensary opened 30 December 2016; police closure 10 March 2017 for use-permit mismatch (essential goods vs cannabis), temporary injunction then central permit 18 April 2017, 2019 merits decision upheld in 2020 with counterclaim rejected. Patients joined, later withdrew personal claims. $1,500 costs/fees retained, not a drug-possession fine. A use-permit cease-and-desist is distinguished from revocation of the original permit; do not claim municipality could ban the whole medical programme or that a later permit retroactively cured damages claims.
- Employment protection: Act 15 of 29 July 2021, full operative Article 24(A)–(D) read in medical-law consolidation. Registered, authorised patients who identify themselves to employer protected in employment; employer must establish exception by preponderance. Exceptions real harm risk, interference with essential duties, federal licence/permit/certification/programme/funds risk, or workplace use/possession without written employer permission. Local employer protection for merely employing patients also included. No universal workplace consumption or automatic immunity from testing claimed.
- Disability amendment original: https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2020/0090-2022.pdf . Full text read. Act 90 removes registered patients from the active-illegal-drug-user exclusion in Act 44-1985, not a blanket declaration that every registered patient has a disability. Original copy lacks a readable approval block; certification 17 October is not enactment. Date independently verified through https://sutra.oslpr.org/prontuarios/leyes-aprobadas/137910 , full legislative history read, 14 October 2022 enactment. Senate journal search excerpt agreed but direct URL returned 404, so the live legislative-history link is cited instead.
- Official statistics: https://www.salud.pr.gov/CMS/DOWNLOAD/10985 , August 2026, linked from current health department programme page https://www.salud.pr.gov/CMS/364 . Read all five pages through web extraction: 110,736 active patients, 59 certifying doctors, 4,505 occupational licences (not dispensaries). Anxiety 59.34%, insomnia 55.14%, chronic pain 34.81%, multiple diagnoses per patient. Direct download repeatedly timed out and web screenshots failed; don't claim visual check or infer doctor inactivity from comparison with old registries. Current official 2026 patient data replaces old 2024 figures in medical/access summary; older industry counts retain their dates.
