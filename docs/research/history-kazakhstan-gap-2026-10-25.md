# Kazakhstan: the missing return to criminal possession in 2015

Reviewed 25 October 2026. One new history event, two revised events, one new
article and one rewritten article. This pass fills a major missing legal
transition in a ten-entry timeline. It also resolves Kazakhstan's 2011 item in
the maintenance guide's old unconfirmed-source list. Current profile text,
scores and source records are unchanged.

## Sources actually read

Research files and verification helpers are local under
`tmp/history-kazakhstan-gap-2026-10-25/`.

- **Original 2011 amendment, CDB reproduction:** read article 1(1)(124),
  deleting criminal article 259(1); article 1(4)(9), adding administrative
  article 320(1-1); and article 2's publication-based commencement. The new
  administrative band expressly concerns **large** personal holdings and
  permits up to 200 monthly calculation units or **45 days' detention**.
  Direct Adilet bodies repeatedly timed out or returned JavaScript placeholders.
  CDB supplied the complete relevant clauses; one invalid UTF-8 byte elsewhere
  in the response was replaced during extraction, not silently interpreted as
  legal text. No uncertain character occurs in the cited clauses.
- **2011 Criminal Code consolidation, WIPO Lex 11523:** read the Russian
  article 259, its deletion and amendment note, the remaining especially-large
  personal-possession tier, supply tiers and surrender/treatment note.
  Especially-large holdings remained punishable by three to seven years.
  This later consolidation is not treated as the original 1997 code.
- **Administrative Code, ILO NATLEX 69967:** read the complete relevant PDF
  pages 208 and 215 (viewer numbering), articles 320 and 336-2, from the
  consolidation through January 2013. Ordinary drug handling outside the
  criminal offence allowed a five-to-ten-unit fine or up to ten days' detention;
  the large band allowed the separate 200-unit/45-day maximum. Public use had
  an initial fine, a higher repeat fine and detention after two earlier
  penalties within a year. Direct download returned 403, but the web PDF
  reader exposed the pages; screenshots were requested for pages 208–209.
- **2015 opening Criminal Code edition, WIPO Lex 16084:** read Russian
  articles 10, 296, 300 and 467. WIPO identifies the edition from 1 January
  2015 and its authorised reproduction from Adilet. Article 296(2) has no
  minimum quantity; (3) supplies the large band and (4) the especially-large
  prison tier. Public use is covered by (1). The ordinary/large criminal
  misdemeanours list fines, corrective work, community work and arrest;
  article 467 initially deferred the general arrest provision until 2017.
  Neither article says arrest was actually introduced in 2017: subsequent
  postponements and implementation remain to be checked.
- **2012 national drug report, CADAP**, printed pp. 11–13: read the policy
  rationale, administrative offences and reform account. It attributes reduced
  criminal liability and prison population to the reform but supplies no
  cannabis-specific release count. Downloaded and extracted locally; rendered
  printed p. 12 was visually checked. The first page of the relevant paragraph
  ends before “large”; the continuation on printed p. 13 confirms that band.
- **Soviet decree of 27 October 1934, Istmat node 42491:** complete operative
  text read. It prohibits sowing Indian hemp except for exclusively medical
  and scientific needs, requires criminal liability and crop confiscation,
  directs republics to amend codes and expressly commences **from 1935**.
  Replaces the Wikipedia citation. The old event ID is retained for links.

The CDB, WIPO, ILO, CADAP and Istmat URLs are recorded in the public source lists.
Search snippets were used for discovery, never as substitutes for these passages.
A court-administration author's 2012 explanation of retrospective relief was
also read, but its unidentified drug case is not published as a cannabis case.

## Dating and writing decisions

The old 2011 event used the signing date. It now uses year precision because
commencement depends on publication, whose full official record was not retrieved.
The statute gives ten calendar days after first official publication; the signing
day is retained in the article as signing, not effective date. The new code's
**1 January 2015** commencement is explicit in both article 467 and WIPO metadata.

The 2011 article is rewritten to explain large versus especially-large holdings,
administrative detention, public use and the later reversal. The new 2015 article
explains lesser criminal offences, original penalties, deferred arrest and the
separate cultivation offence. Each is 350–500 words with four short sections,
inline citations and working links to the other event and country profile.
No gram thresholds, cannabis-specific releases or typical sentencing rates are
invented. Original 2015 penalties are distinguished from today's amended amounts.

## Historical scoring and remaining gaps

Current score stays **13.5 (displayed 14)**: possession 2, cultivation 1,
enforcement 2, sharing 1, consumption 1, access 0.

The 2011 `before` snapshot now has possession **2**, acknowledging the smaller
administrative tier with detention, and consumption **2** for the pre-2015
administrative public-use regime. The inherited enforcement **1→2** improvement
remains at the 2011 reform; the national report supports its stated direction,
without establishing a cannabis-specific sentencing frequency.

The new 2015 step lowers consumption **2→1** when public use becomes criminal.
Possession stays **2** across that step: administrative detention was already
possible, and the existing intermediate score also covers the later lesser
criminal offence with short arrest. The narrative records the legal change even
where the coarse score does not assign a separate possession point. Cultivation,
sharing, access and current enforcement remain unchanged.

Exact chain: **100→12.5 (1935)→14.5 (2011)→13.5 (2015)**. The 1935 FREE vector
is preserved. However, assigning all of the pre-2011 restriction to that first
cultivation decree remains provisional. This pass does not establish when
Kazakhstan's earlier possession/use rules first began. Retrieve the republican
Soviet drug laws, the original administrative code, quantity-table history and
later arrest-commencement amendments next. The outstanding missing 1974 Soviet
possession step is not declared solved by a later consolidation.
