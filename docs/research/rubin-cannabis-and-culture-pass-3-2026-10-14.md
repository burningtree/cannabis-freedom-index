## Applied to the atlas and history

**Thandai gatherings:** new tradition on social preparation described by Hasan,
distinct from Holi as a festival and bhang as a word. Friends and relatives,
grinding-stone work, everyday conversation, caste separation, voluntary devotional
consumption and historical licensed supply. No therapeutic/nutritional claims or
instructions. Reused an existing public-domain Amritsar gouache of bhang
preparation, visually inspected; caption explicitly distinguishes it from Hasan's
later fieldwork. No new reproduction-rights uncertainty introduced.

**Bhang / Bhang at Holi:** added chapter references and context linking to the
new tradition. Removed unsupported comparative-potency wording from the language
entry's introduction.

**Ma:** replaced the unsupported explanation that da means great to avoid the
meaning numb. Li describes ma broadening to other fibre plants, and dama as a
specifier for cannabis. Attributed the proposed fibre ideogram and intoxication
etymology as interpretations. Added mourning cloth and papermaking, without claiming
that linguistic evidence establishes prehistoric intoxication. Corrected the
overspecific male/female-flower gloss for variable historical medical names.

**Hemp in the Taoist censer:** added the examined chapter's bibliographic reference
for the existing textual discussion. It does not authenticate cannabis in the
illustrated museum object.

**Medieval Ethiopian pipe event and new article:** retained the stable existing
`ethiopia-cannabis-pipes-1320` ID and approximate timeline date, but replaced the
Wikipedia-only source with the original chapter. Date is associated with level 2,
not a direct determination on the ceramic or its cannabis. Reported as 1320 ± 80 CE.
The ancient residues lacked the standard THC/CBN/CBD spots; faint unidentified spots
were interpreted as long-lived cannabis combustion products by comparison with
modern pipes. B2 yielded two positive results under that interpretation, A2 one;
later testing lacked enough material. The new article explains this distinction,
without pretending a modern reanalysis has confirmed or refuted it. No claim to
identify a particular medieval community or religious practice.

## Research leads and cautions

- Chinese mourning cloth: Li p. 54 supports a short attributed passage. A separate
  substantial tradition entry needs fuller primary-text and material-history work.
  Chinese Text Project pages returned 403. Chinese Notes initially returned a
  partial parsed *Fu Wen* text, but subsequent retrieval returned 404; not used as
  a public citation. A Sacred Texts page returned the *San Nien Wan* translation,
  but other requested chapters failed. No claim to have reviewed the whole Book
  of Rites or established present-day mourning textiles.
- Li's proposed shamanic diffusion, uniform Chinese temperament, violence effects
  and preference for opium are period hypotheses and stereotypes, not findings
  imported into the atlas. His ancient drug-text dates should be checked against
  modern philology before changing existing timelines.
- Hasan's 1959–60 Chinaura table records respondents having no objection to a
  substance, not national prevalence or even necessarily personal use. The prose
  also prints 75% for 45/62, an arithmetic mismatch. Neither figure was imported.
- Hasan's reference to the United Provinces Excise Act IV of 1910 is a legal lead.
  Retrieve the original and distinguish a licensing requirement from a blanket
  prohibition before adding a scored event.
- Soma identifications and historical clinical claims remain attributed debate;
  no choice of plant, dosage effect or present medical conclusion added.
