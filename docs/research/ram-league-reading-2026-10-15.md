## Chronology and source boundaries

Ram's narrative places the committee's establishment in 1934 and its work through
1939. The Mills article describes six years of work without identifying a 1935
conclusion. Its cited summary is an account of unresolved questions, not evidence
for the year currently embedded in the older site's ID. Original minutes are the
next step before settling that chronology or deduplicating related records.

The chapter's 1938 discussion attaches note 77 to a sarcastic exchange, but that
note cites June 2, 1937 minutes. The rewritten article omits that quip rather than
silently assigning it to June 1938. Russell's no-crime-wave statement has a separate
June 18, 1938 reference in note 70. Anslinger's broader 1938 statement is cited
through TNI in note 76. None of those original proceedings was independently read.

The 1934 estimate is routed through Mills's *Cannabis Nation*, not a newly examined
global survey. No consumer count, prevalence calculation or score uses it.
The chapter's blanket description of a 1925 ban is not copied: the existing checked
treaty history distinguishes international commerce controls from an obligation to
prohibit domestic use. The primary treaty text was not newly examined this pass.

Ram notes disagreement about whether Russell's position substantially escaped the
racial categories used by his opponents. The new text preserves that complication.
It does not romanticise a colonial enforcer as an early cannabis-rights advocate.
Historical assumptions about laziness, sexuality, madness and racial susceptibility
are analysed as claims, not reproduced as facts about the populations described.

Other leads need separate work: Warnock's appointment dates vary between the chapter
and older site history; the quoted report title itself refers to 1895–1923. The
Assassins narrative should be checked against primary textual scholarship, and
neither its history nor Ram's broad concluding claims about Mandatory Palestine
are expanded from this one chapter alone.
