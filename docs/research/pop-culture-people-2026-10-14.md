## Sources and boundaries

Lennon: read the University of Nebraska–Lincoln Roz Payne Sixties Archive button catalogue, University of Delaware's Sinclair manuscript finding aid, the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame profile, and the actual *People v. Sinclair*, 387 Mich. 91, decision reproduced by Justia. AP's 2024 Sinclair obituary corroborates the crowd and release chronology. The conviction was for possession; the sale count had been dismissed. Release shortly after the concert is distinct from the 9 March 1972 reversal. Delaware's finding aid compresses these events; the entry follows the court's disposition and date, without claiming that Lennon personally secured a judgment or that the concert legalised marijuana. No song lyrics reproduced. The case has multiple opinions; no single opinion's reasoning is represented as a unanimous holding.

Nelson: read NORML's current advisory-board page and the full 19 June 2020 Keith Stroup recollection, alongside the 28 November 2010 arrest account, plus Country Music Hall of Fame biography. NORML pages were read directly after web parsing omitted some text. Stroup is an involved advocate; his praise is not used as an independent ranking or as clinical evidence. Omitted health/longevity claims and consumer-brand marketing. The 1994 co-chair date is historical; the current page establishes membership, not an independently checked current co-chair title. The Teapot Party proposal is described as advocacy, not a registered party or enacted reform. No final arrest-case disposition was established or claimed.

Chong: read DOJ's 24 February 2003 release, ABC's guilty-plea report, the biography and *Up in Smoke* references, NORML's advisory-board entry and the biography's account of the Free Tommy Chong campaign. Separates criminal paraphernalia sales from personal possession and comic performance. DOJ's claims about harm and culture are attributed to the prosecution, not endorsed. The sentence range is corroborated by biography and contemporary reporting. WIRED’s campaign report was identified in search, but attempts to read its full page returned errors/403, so it was removed from the entry and is not treated as a read source. No unsupported comparison of all other defendants' sentences or assertion that fame was the sole reason for imprisonment. The already existing 1978 film event is linked; no duplicate was added.
