## Primary legislation actually read

Reread the retained original 1995 and 2015 laws and 2017 amendment in
`tmp/history-tanzania-gap-2026-10-21/`. The earlier audit distinguishes original alternatives
from later consolidated mandatory penalties. The 1995 article retains its original penalty
and commencement sources; no later maximum is backdated into it.

The [original 2015 Act](https://elibrary.osg.go.tz/server/api/core/bitstreams/6f7a2608-5c61-4269-8d5e-b6f2f48b69d5/content)
section 11 sets **at least thirty years** for unlawful cultivation and other prohibited-plant
offences. Section 17 provides the separate small-personal-use tier: for the residual drug
group, **a fine of at least 500,000 shillings OR a three-year term OR both**. The holder must
prove personal purpose rather than sale/distribution; quantities are delegated to regulations.
This does not say every small holder receives a fine or that a three-year term is a one-to-five-year
range. The original trafficking rule provided life imprisonment.

Downloaded the actual [G.N. 173 of 2016 from Parliament](https://www.parliament.go.tz/uploads/documents/sw-1738844545-1476204842-GN%20173-DRUGS%20CONTROL%20AND%20ENFORCEMENT%20%28GENERAL%29%20REGULATIONS%2C%202016.pdf)
and a separate TanzLII reproduction. Read regulation 3 and the Gazette heading; visually
inspected printed/PDF p. 4. Publication is **13 May 2016**. Small quantity is cannabis **not
exceeding 50 g**, and resin/oil **not exceeding 5 g**. The boundary is inclusive. Regulation
3(2)'s English wording is awkward; the article does not turn it into an automatic conclusion
that all larger quantities establish intent to sell. These are historical 2016 definitions,
not permission to possess and not the kilogram trafficking thresholds.

Reread the [original 2017 amendment](https://www.dcea.go.tz/uploads/publications/sw1569703938-THE%20DRUGS%20ACT.pdf),
sections 3, 8–9 and 14. Its original lower trafficking tier says **thirty years**, with fifty
kilograms as the cannabis/khat boundary; it does not yet use the later minimum wording.
Treatment requires consent and a written medical assessment, with specified exclusions.
The existing article's October-bill context retains a separate bill source.

Downloaded/read the [original 2021 omnibus amendment](https://media.tanzlii.org/media/legislation/47370/source_file/ce964b2f0ff6439d/tz-act-2021-9-publication-document.pdf),
Act 9 of 2021, especially sections 18–20, and visually checked printed/PDF p. 10. Cover:
published **11 October 2021**, assent **28 September**. Sections 18–19 replace fifty with
**one hundred** in both trafficking tiers. Section 19 also inserts **not less than** before
thirty years. The new `other` event describes both changes, with trafficking/threshold/sentencing
tags. No separate whole-point movement is invented: both tiers remain within the same severe
supply band, and the statutory package changes both quantity range and sentence wording.
The event date is the publication milestone; a separate commencement order was not recovered.

## Two actual cannabis cases

Downloaded/read [Hamis Muhibu Abdallah v Republic](https://media.tanzlii.org/media/judgment/242035/source_file/hamis-muhibu-abdallah-vs-republic-2022-tzca-142-25-march-2022.pdf),
**Criminal Appeal 288 of 2021**, [2022] TZCA 142. The source's signature date is 24 March;
its heading and certification show delivery **25 March 2022**, used for the event. Visually
checked the final page. Police uprooted 535 plants; the trial acquitted his brother but
convicted Hamis of cultivation and possession with concurrent thirty-year terms. The Court
of Appeal held that the retracted confession was corroborated by evidence linking him to
the land and cultivation. It quashed possession because the uprooted crop proved cultivation,
not that separate charged offence, but upheld cultivation and its thirty-year sentence.
The article does not call the field personal home growing, report an acquittal of both counts,
or add concurrent terms to claim sixty years.

Downloaded/read the complete judgment text of [Agnetha Sebastian v Republic](https://sheriahub.com/cases/tz/caselaw/agnetha-sebastian-vs-republic-criminal-appeal-389-of-2020-2022-tzca-412-11-july-2022.pdf),
Criminal Appeal 389 of 2020, [2022] TZCA 412, **11 July 2022**, reproduced by SheriaHub.
This downloaded PDF is a typeset reproduction of the judgment text, not the original scanned
court file; its source locator makes that provenance explicit. Allegation: 414 g of cannabis,
with a thirty-year sentence. The appeal found the sample-to-seized-parcel chain of custody
unproved, reversed the decision and sentence, and ordered release unless lawfully held for
another cause. The article explains chain of custody plainly, attributes disputed police
claims and the defence, and does not turn acquittal into a general cannabis-law invalidation.
Both events are `enforcement`, with court/evidence/sentencing tags appropriate to each case.
