## New York: 1860 controls and the 1862 amendment

Read Francis Tillou, “The Law in Reference to the Sale of Poisons by Druggists”,
*Papers Read Before the Medico-Legal Society of New York*, first series, revised
edition (McDivitt, Campbell & Co., 1874), **pp. 170–171**. The paper was read on
13 January 1870. Title/imprint pages and the substantive provisions were read in
the [Cornell scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/Papers_read_before_the_Medico-Legal_Society_of_New_York%2C_from_its_organization_%28IA_cu31924017503198%29.pdf).
Printed p. 171 (PDF p. 191) was also rendered and visually checked.

The paper reproduces **1860 chapter 442, passed 16 April**, naming cannabis and
its preparations in section 3. It describes recipient/witness records, labels,
physician-order exceptions, a wholesale exception and a restriction to incorporated
cities/villages of at least 1,000 inhabitants. Those limits are included explicitly;
this is not a general national or statewide cannabis possession ban.

It also describes **1862 chapter 273** removing the witness-name requirement and
repealing section 3. The latter is not equated with an end to every applicable
poison-law obligation. No precise day was invented for the amendment.

Applied to:

- `us-new-york-poison-sales-1860`
- `us-new-york-poison-amendment-1862`

Both have articles. This is a contemporary legal reproduction/commentary, not a
claim that the original 1860 and 1862 session-law volumes were inspected. Modern
summaries sometimes omit the geographical limit or overstate the label requirement;
the article follows the reproduced text. The words used in the schedule are
“cannabis and its preparations”, not a substituted modern botanical designation.

## Berar: licences in 1875 and the 1884 fee revision

Read the complete relevant cultivation passage in the Indian Hemp Drugs
Commission's **1894 volume III, Berar memorandum B**, using the
[public transcription](https://abuse-drug.com/lib/Indian-Hemp-Commission-Report/volume-iii-berar-memorandum.html).
The underlying rule is identified there as **Notification No. 55 of 5 August 1874**,
effective **1 April 1875**. The original notification was not separately retrieved.

The text supplies the eight-rupee stamp-paper licence, unlicensed-crop destruction,
spontaneous-plant destruction, village reporting duties, maximum hundred-rupee fine
and informant reward. It also dates the acreage-fee change and vendor accounting
duties to **1884**. The official account's attribution of subsequent cultivation
decline to the revision remains attributed, rather than asserted as a measured
single-cause result.

Applied to:

- `india-berar-cultivation-licences-1875`
- `india-berar-cultivation-fee-1884`

Both have articles. The later Buldana gazetteer corroborates the fees but describes
the revision in **1884–1885** administrative-year terms; no exact day is inferred.
The transcriptions were retrieved after working around an expired website TLS
certificate. That retrieval issue does not turn the transcription into an original
notification scan. Source locators identify memorandum B and its relevant passages
rather than inventing page mapping from a webpage.

## Baroda: translated notification and an explicit commencement clause

Read the [Baroda State memorandum transcription](https://abuse-drug.com/lib/Indian-Hemp-Commission-Report/volume-iii-baroda-statel-memorandum.html),
particularly paragraphs **168–174** and the notification's **clauses 2–12**.
It identifies **Notification No. 10 of 10 December 1891**. Clause 12 puts it into
force on **1 February 1892**. The first drafting pass incorrectly entered 29 December;
comparison with paragraph 168 corrected it before final validation.

Also read the [National Library of Scotland transcription of printed p. 157](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn9/7443/74435585.9.htm),
which contains the concluding clauses, medical exception and commencement clause.
This is a second presentation of the same primary report, not independent evidence
of a different notification. The original-language order was not inspected.

Applied to `india-baroda-hemp-monopoly-1892` and its article. Possession limits,
licensed selling and transport, cultivators' restricted outlet, penalties and the
bona fide medical exception are kept distinct. A monopoly does not mean that all
cultivation or medical supply was prohibited.

## Berar's 1897 law: commencement corrected to 1898

Read the historical “Ganja” account, **paragraph 332**, on the Government of
Maharashtra's [Buldana district gazetteer reproduction](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Buldhana%20District/general_admin.html).
It says the law came into force on **1 April 1898** and reports cultivation prohibition
and supply through Khandwa under **1898–1899**. It does not independently establish
the exact day of the implementing cultivation-ban rule.

Updated `india-berar-hemp-drugs-law-1897` to the effective date and added an article.
Preserved the ID and existing Mills p. 130 citation for the November 1897 orders.
The orders' issue, law's commencement and implementation are now separated.
No duplicate event was created for the same law. The full 1897 instrument and its
implementing rules remain a useful retrieval target.

## Morocco: qualify a previously confident 1890 claim

Read the relevant historical passages in two articles by Pierre-Arnaud Chouvy:

- [*Production de cannabis et de haschich au Maroc : contexte et enjeux* (2008)](https://www.geopium.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Chouvy_2008_EP_2008_Cannabis_Hashish_Maroc.pdf).
- [*Moroccan hashish as an example of a cannabis terroir product* (2022)](https://www.geopium.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Chouvy-2022-Moroccan-hashish-as-an-example-of-a-cannabis-terroir-product.pdf),
  especially **PDF p. 12**, questioning the five douars' historical written basis.

The later article qualifies an account repeated in the author's own earlier work.
Rewrote `morocco-hassan-regulations-1890` and its article, replacing the claim of a
verified 1890 decree with an explicitly attributed, unresolved historical account.
Retained the conventional year and stable ID, but **removed `checked: true`** because
the specific instrument, date and beneficiaries have not been established. No
original decree or continuous legal entitlement is claimed. The previous article's
later protectorate and modern history was removed from this page rather than left
as an unsupported narrative of a continuous privilege.

## Other leads not promoted to findings

- Cape Colony's 1891 pharmacy legislation should not be assumed to have already
  scheduled cannabis. Existing research distinguishes later administrative action.
  No new pre-1900 Cape cannabis ban was added.
- Ceylon's November 1897 gazette and older reports are promising for the existing
  import restriction, but a bill must be distinguished from an enacted ordinance.
  No additional possession offence was added from a search snippet.
- Greece's 1890/1891 circular chronology needs comparison of the original orders;
  no duplicate local ban was created on the basis of a differently dated summary.
- Modern wikis, blogs and social posts were used only as leads. Checked citations
  above rest on substantive text actually retrieved and read.
