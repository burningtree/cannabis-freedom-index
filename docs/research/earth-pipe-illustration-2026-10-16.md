The requested primary image is John Edward Philips, “African Smoking and Pipes,”
The Journal of African History 24, no. 3 (1983), pp. 303–319, p. 306, Figure 2b,
after Henry Balfour, “Earth Smoking-Pipes from South Africa and Central Asia,”
Man 22 (1922), pp. 65–69. Figure 2c is the authorized schematic alternative.

Supporting source: Laufer, Hambly and Linton, Tobacco and Its Use in Africa
(Field Museum, 1930), printed p. 28 (PDF page 34, one-based):
https://samorini.it/doc1/alt_aut/lr/laufer-hambly-linton-tobacco-and-its-use-in-africa.pdf
The page describes surface clay construction, hemp use among the Bechuana,
Balfour's identification of hemp in charred contents, a pit with a bored duct,
and prone smoking. It distinguishes optional water arrangements. Plate V,
figure 2 (PDF page 37) contains a related illustration, but it is not Philips's
Figure 2b and was not substituted for the specifically requested figure.

Text changes foreground earth as the pipe's material, distinguish moulded and
dug forms, retain the southern/central African distribution and prohibition
context, and qualify claims about cannabis, water and durable evidence. The
contradictory claim of a first written description in 1848 was removed because
the entry already cites Campbell's 1822 account.
