# United States, 1862–1891

23 October 2026. The hole ran from Wisconsin's 1862 poison-sales law to the
Bromidia trademark case of 2 April 1891.

## Read

State v. Lee, 137 Mo. 143, decided 19 January 1897, quoting section 3874 of
the Revised Statutes of 1889. The section makes it a misdemeanor to set up,
maintain, open or keep a house, room or place for smoking opium, hasheesh or
any other deadly drug, and sets a fine of 100 to 1,000 dollars. The
information in the case, filed 27 January 1896, charged an opium den at 19
Eighth Street, St. Louis, on 26 January 1896. It did not charge hasheesh.
The trial court quashed the information. The supreme court reversed and
remanded.

## Not opened

A 1952 Missouri Law Review article says the same hasheesh words were enacted
in Laws 1887, page 175, and then carried into the 1889 revision. That session
law was not opened, so the entry is dated 1889, the revision the court quotes.
The day the revision took effect was not in the opinion.

California bills of 1880, 1885 and 1889 that would have covered hemp were
reported as withdrawn or never voted. They are not statutes.

The 1911 entry still calls Massachusetts the first state to restrict cannabis,
on the sources attached to that entry. Those sources were not re-read, and
that title was not rewritten. The Missouri section is an offence for keeping
a smoking place, and the national score still starts at the 1913 bans
(100 to 22).

## Still empty, 23 October 2026

No enacted statute between the 1862 poison laws and the 1889 Missouri section
was opened that names cannabis, hasheesh or Indian hemp. Illinois's 1874
poison-label revision, as described in a 1975 law-review article, names
arsenic, strychnine, corrosive sublimate and prussic acid. Maine's 1887 public
law, quoted in State v. Taplin, names opium, morphine and laudanum. California's
hemp bills of the 1880s were not enacted.
