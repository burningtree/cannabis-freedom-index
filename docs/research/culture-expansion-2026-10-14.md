# Culture expansion — 14 October 2026

Added three language entries and two traditions: Hemp, Pot, Pakalōlō, Shivaratri at
Pashupatinath, and Kanapinis, the hemp man. Counts become 28 language entries and
28 traditions. Existing featured rankings are retained.

## Evidence and scope

- **Hemp:** Online Etymology Dictionary for the proposed Germanic connection;
  Wikipedia for materials and processing; the site's checked US history for the
  2018 definition and the 2025 change due to take effect in November 2026.
- **Pot:** Etymonline's 1938 attestation and Max Wheeler's first-person linguistic
  investigation, LINGUIST List 16.2853. The Mexican-Spanish derivation is disputed,
  not presented as fact. This entry is shorter because the etymological evidence
  does not justify a longer origin narrative.
- **Pakalōlō:** Pukui–Elbert (1986) via the University of Hawaiʻi's dictionary.
  The actual headword is **paka lōlō**, literally “numbing tobacco”; querying
  `pakalolo` returns a spelling suggestion. Downloaded and read the spaced entry
  when the web fetch failed. e-Hawaii independently records Pidgin usage. No claim
  about first introduction, ancient ritual or an individual cultivar is made.
  This is a compact lexical entry because those histories remain unverified.
- **Shivaratri:** Nepal Tourism Board and the festival reference for religious
  context, Kathmandu Post for the 1995 and 2018 restrictions, AP's February 2026
  reporting for continuing practice. Added the latter as a context history event,
  without changing scores or describing tolerance as a permanent legal exemption.
- **Kanapinis:** Kudirka's *The Lithuanians* via the Lithuanian Folk Culture Centre,
  the Klaipėda Ethnocultural Centre and a current festival programme. The hemp-seed
  connection is explicit in Kudirka. No invented date of origin or intoxicating
  ritual is asserted. Historical ethnic caricatures are identified as stereotypes.
