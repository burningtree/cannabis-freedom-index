# Ottoman hashish, 1863–1913

The gap ran from the 5 November 1863 order against coffeehouse and herbalist
sales to the 1913 police regulation. Kaya Göktepe's 2020 article in the Journal
of Ottoman Legacy Studies, read in full from the DOI page, names two decisions
inside it.

## What was added

`turkey-herbalists-hashish-regulation-1864`, 25 April 1864. The Attarlar ve
Kökçüler Nizamnamesi forbade herbalists and root-sellers to sell hashish and
preparations of it. Only a pharmacy could sell it, on a doctor's prescription.
Shops caught making it, selling it, or allowing use were often closed. Category
tightening, unscored. An article was added.

`turkey-hashish-cultivation-ban-lifted-1886`, year only. A decision that year
lifted the cultivation ban and was to punish unlicensed sale and use for
pleasure. No day, fine or prison term is given. Category easing, unscored.

Neither moves the score. The 1933 narcotics law remains the step from 100 to 9.
`node scripts/data-status.cjs chain tr` is the check.

## What was not opened

The regulation text and the 1886 decision file were not opened. Footnote
numbers in the HTML extract are scrambled, so Ottoman archive file numbers are
not attached to these two claims. The 22 June 1886 file cited beside a
coffeehouse imprisonment case was not used as the cultivation decision.

Kızılkaya and Özkan, the source of the 1857, 1863 and 1913 entries, write that
hashish could still be sold freely until 1933. Göktepe describes a pharmacy
rule from 1864 and a punishment for unlicensed sale from 1886. Both accounts
stay on the pages that cite them. The older entries were not rewritten.
