# Somalia and Sudan: primary-law recovery, 15 October 2026

This pass continues the research-coverage audit. It changes evidence and descriptions, without
changing the coverage formula or signing any country off as complete.

## Sources actually read

- Somalia Law 46 of 3 March 1970: original Italian Official Gazette, printed pp. 833–842,
  articles 1–20 and Schedule A, from Roma Tre's Somali legislation archive. Also read the
  four-page UNODC English extract. The extract omits substantive provisions and headings;
  it is not a complete translation. Original article 4 was checked visually against the scan.
- Health Secretary Decree 227 of 26 September 1970: enabling text and cannabis entries in
  its alphabetical schedule, especially printed p. 1157 (Cannabis / Hashish), plus Indian Hemp.
  Unrelated scheduled drugs and other laws in the gazette were not exhaustively reviewed.
- Somalia Decree-Law 5 of 18 May 1984: Somali original, printed pp. 394–395, all seven articles.
  Article 1 was checked visually; article 2's consumption-premises scope was retained.
- Somalia Law 25 of 24 June 1984: Somali original, printed p. 483, both articles. Conversion
  into statute has a different commencement clause from the decree's signature-date clause.
- Sudan Act 1994: Arabic UNODC-hosted PDF, heading, definitions and articles 2–3, 12–25;
  cross-checked articles 15–20 with the SHERLOC HTML transcription. Article 20 was checked
  visually. This is a later consolidation, including a 2009 reference, despite an attachment
  catalogue label of 1996. Its footnotes also contain inconsistent-looking 1974 references;
  no amendment chronology is inferred from them. Drug schedules were not exhaustively read.
- Sudan National Supreme Court, Government v. Z. A. A. J.: entire published English judgment.
  The final order is 16 November 2018; the collection title and docket use 2019. Retain both
  facts rather than silently moving the judgment to 2019. Quantity was evidence of intent in
  that case, not a universal statutory threshold.

Source URLs and locators are retained in the country profiles, history entries and articles.
Working downloads and extracted text are under ignored `tmp/research-progress-pass2/`.

## Database changes

- Replaced four Wikipedia-only scored references in Somalia and two in Sudan with read legal
  texts. Revised other scored descriptions and filled documented context gaps. The documents
  support historical rules, not proof of nationwide contemporary implementation.
- Added four Somali history events: 1970 law, 1970 cannabis schedule, 1984 decree and conversion.
- Added Sudan's 2018 hashish judgment; upgraded the 1994 event with its printed date and a primary
  source. Added the Act's express repeal of the 1924 law as corroboration to that older entry;
  this does not independently prove the old entry's entire description.
- Added explanatory articles for the Somali 1970 law and Sudanese 1994 Act.
- Sudan cultivation score 0 → 1 reflects article 20's lesser personal-cultivation offence;
  consumption 1 → 0 reflects the express consumption offence. Current total rounds 3 → 5.
  The 1994 event now carries the previous score vector so the reconstruction and current
  profile remain connected. This is a research correction, not a 2026 legal reform. The date
  of any subsequent changes to these provisions remains to be established from earlier editions.
- Somalia's scores were retained pending fuller review of current law and practice. The 1984
  consumption-premises offence is not treated as a universal standalone consumption tariff.
- General medical/scientific narcotics licensing powers in either country are not evidence of
  an operating cannabis patient programme. Medical status is limited to reflect the documented
  licensing/research framework; neither access score was increased.

## Remaining gaps and blocked leads

- Somalia's first restriction remains estimated; 1970 is not relabelled as its first ban.
  Current regional amendments, cultivation/production overlap, sentencing and patient access
  still need work. Previously retained EUAA evidence on territorial variation was not reread
  in full during this pass. No population-level cannabis prevalence was newly verified.
- Sudan: obtain the original 1994 edition and a full amendment chronology, and investigate
  wartime implementation and treatment availability. Do not claim the hosted consolidation
  proves every provision's original 1994 wording or all changes through 2026.
- The UN Digital Library English 1996 notification could not be downloaded successfully.
  Somalia's medicines authority compilation URL returned a maintenance page, not a readable PDF.
  Neither blocked item was treated as a read source.
- Kiribati's primary-law and case pages were inaccessible during this pass; its profile was
  left unchanged rather than filling gaps from snippets.
