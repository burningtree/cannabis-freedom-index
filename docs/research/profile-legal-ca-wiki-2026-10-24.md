# Canadian provinces and territories, Wikipedia removed, 24 October 2026

Every province and territory page listed a Wikipedia "Cannabis in …" source. Alberta, Ontario, Quebec and Saskatchewan had no other legal source. The texts below were opened before the pages were rewritten. Scores are unchanged.

Sentences that the opened pages do not state were not carried forward. That includes a home-possession cap of "none" where the provincial page only states the public limit, a claim that provincial offences are ticketed where no ticket schedule was opened, Ontario as the largest private retail market, and a Quebec claim that the SQDC does not sell many edibles.

## Read

- AGLC CannabisSense, possession limits. An adult 18 or older may possess up to 30 g of legally produced dried cannabis in public and may grow up to four plants per residence from licensed seed. https://cannabissense.ca/use-legally/possession-limits
- AGLC CannabisSense, where cannabis may be used. Use is allowed in a single-family home and where the municipality allows it. A motor vehicle is banned except a temporary residence such as a parked RV. Public smoking or vaping is prohibited where tobacco is restricted, where children might be present, and on hospital or school property. https://cannabissense.ca/use-legally/where-can-i-use
- Government of Ontario, cannabis laws, updated 3 September 2024. Age 19 to buy, use, possess and grow. Up to four plants per residence from Ontario Cannabis Store or authorised-store material. Maximum 30 g of dried cannabis in public. Smoking and vaping allowed in private residences and many outdoor public places, including sidewalks and parks, and not in enclosed public places, at schools and playgrounds within 20 metres, or in a vehicle or boat being driven. https://www.ontario.ca/page/cannabis-laws
- Cannabis Control Act, 2017 (Ontario), section 6: only an authorised retailer may sell. Section 10: no person under 19 may possess, consume, purchase or distribute cannabis. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/17c26
- Gouvernement du Québec, Cannabis Regulation Act page. Under 21 may not possess or give cannabis. Public possession stays at the federal 30 g because no reducing regulation was made. The non-public limit is 150 g, including in a private residence regardless of how many adults live there. Personal cultivation is prohibited. Smoking or vaping is prohibited in spaces open to the public, including roads. A municipality may permit smoking in a park. Only the SQDC may sell at retail. https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/alcohol-drugs-gambling/recognizing-drugs-and-their-effects/cannabis/regulating-cannabis-in-quebec/cannabis-regulation-act
- Cannabis Regulation Act, CQLR c C-5.3, sections 7 and 10. More than 150 g outside a public place is a fine of $250 to $750. Cultivating four plants or fewer in a dwelling-house is the same fine. https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/C-5.3
- Murray-Hall v. Quebec (Attorney General), 2023 SCC 10, decided 14 April 2023. The appeal was dismissed. Sections 5 and 10 of the provincial Act remain valid and operative. https://decisions.scc-csc.ca/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/item/19829/index.do
- SQDC frequently asked questions. The maximum THC content of cannabis sold there is 30 percent. https://www.sqdc.ca/en-CA/about-the-sqdc/frequently-asked-questions
- Government of Saskatchewan, cannabis use. Age 19. Public possession 30 g. Public consumption prohibited. Four plants per household. Private retail, with SLGA as regulator. The fine table includes $200 for more than 30 g in public, $200 for consumption other than in a private place, and $200 for distributing more than 30 g in a public place. https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/cannabis-in-saskatchewan/cannabis-use-in-saskatchewan
- Government of Nova Scotia, cannabis laws. NSLC is the only authorised retailer. Age 19. Up to 30 g in public and no stated home limit for personal use. Four plants per household, with each apartment a separate household. Smoke-free Places Act applies to combustible cannabis in public, with a fine up to $2,000. Cannabis cannot be used in a vehicle. Buying other than from the NSLC is a fine of at least $600. https://novascotia.ca/cannabis/laws/
- Government of British Columbia, where you can consume, updated 20 May 2026. Age 19. Adults may generally smoke or vape where tobacco smoking is allowed, with listed bans for parks, playgrounds, schools, public buildings and transit stops. No stated restriction in a privately owned home. A landlord or strata can restrict smoking or growing. Consumption in a vehicle or boat is illegal, with stated residence exceptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/public-safety/cannabis/where-you-can-consume
- Province of Manitoba, public use. Smoking and vaping non-medical cannabis is prohibited in the listed public places and in enclosed public places, with a palliative-care and hospice-room exception. https://www.gov.mb.ca/cannabis/knowthelaw/cantuseinpublic.html
- Government of Prince Edward Island, using cannabis, published 11 January 2019. Age 19. Public possession 30 g. No stated limit on purchased cannabis kept in a private dwelling. Sharing with a person 19 or older is allowed. Consumption in a private home and its yard, or on vacant land with the owner's consent. Not at work or on public property. A provincial fine of $200 to $400, or $400 to $700 for a later offence; an officer may warn instead. One answer on the same page still speaks of legalisation as forthcoming. The possession, storage, sharing and place-of-use answers were the ones used. https://www.princeedwardisland.ca/en/information/finance-and-affordability/using-cannabis-on-pei
- Northwest Territories Liquor and Cannabis Commission, cannabis. Age 19. Possession limit stated as 30 g. Four plants per household, indoors, from approved seed. Use on private property where tobacco smoking is allowed, and on trails, roadways when not driving, and parks not in use for a public event. Prohibited at playgrounds, schools, sports fields and outdoor events. https://www.ntlcc.ca/en/cannabis
- Health Canada, authorised retailers. Ages and retail models for every province and territory. Alberta is the only age of 18. Ontario is private shops plus a government online store. Quebec is government shops, age 21. Saskatchewan, Alberta, Manitoba, Yukon and Nunavut are private licensed shops. British Columbia is government shops, including online, or private licensed shops. Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island and the Northwest Territories are government retail. https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-medication/cannabis/laws-regulations/provinces-territories.html
- Cannabis Act sections 8, 9, 10 and 12, current to 30 September 2026. Public possession over 30 g of dried cannabis is prohibited. An adult may not distribute more than 30 g; distribute includes giving. Unauthorised selling carries up to 14 years. An adult may cultivate no more than four plants in the dwelling-house, and the household may not exceed four. https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-24.5/section-8.html

## Left in place

New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon and Nunavut already cited their own Act, regulations or government page for the local rule. Wikipedia was removed. The federal sections above now carry the 30 g, sharing and selling sentences. A "no provincial cap at home" sentence was removed where those pages do not state it. Newfoundland and Labrador's plant-place rules and Nunavut's 150 g limit were not rewritten.

The Australian national profile, the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory were the next pass. Country profiles outside Australia, and the history file, still cite Wikipedia.
