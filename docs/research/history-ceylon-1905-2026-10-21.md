- Recovered the actual 1905 ordinance in Gazette 6,078, Part II, 6 October
  1905, pp. 637–638, and read both complete pages visually. It defines all parts
  of Cannabis indica and Cannabis sativa, prohibits cultivation and trade,
  sets the 100-rupee/six-month maximum, presumes occupiers to have planted it,
  allows search warrants and specified forced entry, warrantless arrest when
  an officer finds an offence, informer awards, and destruction. Passed
  6 September; assented to 21 September. The event date now records assent;
  a separate commencement date is not claimed. There is no deferred-start

  cultivation prohibition. The text itself acknowledges the missing medicinal

  searches did not establish its final disposition. The event is `debate`,

## Interpretation and scope

The 1905 law is `tightening`: prepared bhang and ganja were already prohibited
in 1897/1899. Its plant definition differs from the scope found in the 1904
judgment. No source read establishes that this particular case caused the
1905 legislation, so the prose gives the sequence and substantive difference
without asserting that causal link. Tags cover colonial rule, sentencing,
police powers and imports/exports. The draft has colonial, retail and trade
tags; medicinal discussion alone does not justify `patients` or `science`.

All existing historical score vectors are preserved without revalidation.
Earlier research already identified that the Sri Lankan score chain needs
substantive reconstruction around the 1897/1899 prohibitions. Adding the
1904 interpretation must not invent a numerical cultivation change.
