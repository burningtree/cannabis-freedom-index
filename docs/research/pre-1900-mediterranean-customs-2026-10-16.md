## Greece–Egypt convention

Signed **3 March 1884**, but article XVI says operative for seven years from
**20 March 1884**. Added `egypt-greek-customs-hashish-convention-1884` using the
latter date. Continued operation and later replacement were not reconstructed.

Article V excludes hashish and other named goods from the general trade
stipulations and reserves Egyptian control on the same terms for Greek and local
subjects. Its search clause requires a duplicate order to the Greek consular
representative, whose attendance/notification cannot delay the search.

Article XI addresses manifests and suspicious stops. Article XIII's general customs
boarding power specifies vessels **under 200 tons** and **within 10 kilometres of
shore**. These provisions are not reported as unlimited cannabis-search powers.
The convention's general eight-percent tariff is not assigned to hashish, which
has its own express exception. Neither is the convention treated as creating a
new Greek domestic cannabis ban or the first Egyptian prohibition.

The British agreement, signed **3 March 1884**, accepts application of the same
customs regulations to British subjects, ships and commerce. It is covered within
the same article rather than turned into another near-duplicate entry. Its
separate precise commencement was not established and is not equated automatically
with the Greek convention's date.

## US extension

Added `egypt-us-customs-hashish-agreement-1885`. Signature **16 November 1884**;
Senate consent **18 March 1885**; presidential ratification/proclamation **7 May 1885**.
The instrument expressly makes it operative upon Senate consent, so the record
uses **1885-03-18**. The proclamation confirms that mechanism. This is application
of Egyptian customs rules to US commerce in Egypt, not a nationwide US cannabis
prohibition. Both events are attached to Egypt for that reason.
