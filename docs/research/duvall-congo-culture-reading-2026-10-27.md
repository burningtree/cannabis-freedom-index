# Duvall reading for the DR Congo profile, 27 October 2026

Chris S. Duvall, *The African Roots of Marijuana* (Duke University Press, 2019),
read in the local PDF (`books/` catalogue id `duvall-african-roots-marijuana-2019`).
Earlier passes are recorded in `docs/research/duvall-african-roots-reading-2026-10-15.md`
(ch. 1, ch. 6) and the pipes/cultivation notes. This pass re-read the passages actually
cited in `countries/cd.yaml` (source key `duvall19`), checking printed pages against the
extracted text. Viewer-to-printed offset varies by chapter; printed numbers below were read
off the extracted pages.

## Pages examined

- ch. 3, printed pp. 60–61: calabash-based water pipes most prominent in western Africa,
  earliest documented in Angola in 1832, widespread from Sierra Leone to South Africa in the
  late 1800s. Applied to `context.culture` (the calabash water pipe sentence).
- ch. 5, printed pp. 105–106: coughing probably sometimes performance (Belgian account);
  Burton heard coughing on the lower Congo in 1874 and called it an affectation. Applied to
  `context.culture` (the smoker's cough sentence).
- ch. 6, printed pp. 112–119: Bena Riamba existed 1870–1890 in the Lulua Valley; society of
  friendship (lubuku); sacramental cannabis smoking; moyo greeting; Chishimbi's theology fell
  to Sangula Meta, probably Mukenge's sister, a zealot for the new regime who once caned
  reluctant porters. Applied to `context.culture` (Bena Riamba and Sangula Meta sentences).

## Interpretation cautions kept

- Duvall's reconstruction is secondary and comparative; the profile repeats no claim about
  drug effects causing peace or violence, and no exact dates beyond the movement's span and
  the 1881 encounter (the latter from the checked history entry).
- Colonial accounts (Belgian coughing anecdote, Burton) are reported as travellers'
  accounts, not neutral facts.
- The 1903/1917 ordinance text itself was read on Leganet (profile source `ord1903`);
  Duvall was not used for any legal claim.

## Addendum, same day: ch. 4 p. 89 for Côte d'Ivoire

Printed p. 89 (viewer p. 93 of the local PDF): the chamba-cognate band runs through
western Africa with a patch from Côte d'Ivoire to Senegal; terms there kept the
initial j sound, commonly written diamba in French and Portuguese, with published
spellings varying among diamba, liamba, riamba and iamba through European
transcription difficulty. Applied to `countries/ci.yaml` `context.culture` (diamba
word patch, spelling variation). Interpretation caution kept: spelling variation
reflects transcription, not distinct plants.

`countries/sn.yaml` `context.culture`. Caution kept: the study itself was not
