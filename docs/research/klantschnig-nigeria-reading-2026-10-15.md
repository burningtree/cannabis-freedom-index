## Limits and leads

Ninety-seven police cases in five months are recorded cases, not a census of users.
The 1962 seizure jump is not evidence that consumption rose ninefold. Colonial
claims that no cannabis grew are administrative assertions contradicted by police
records in the historian's account, rather than evidence of botanical absence.

The chapter's class labels are attributed period language. Its criticism that
Lambo's classifications obscured more privileged patients is the historian's
reading of the study, not an independently established finding from a newly
examined patient archive. Historical definitions of dependence are not current
clinical guidance. No modern treatment recommendation or claim of safety added.

The chapter also offers cultural leads: Lagos nightclubs, women consumers, the
highlife subgenre toye, Orlando Owoh and Fela Kuti. Follow notes 42–46 and 79 to
the original ethnographies, musical biographies and recordings before producing
full cultural profiles. A reference to the subgenre alone is too thin to pad out
a standalone entry. The postwar return-of-soldiers account should not become an
absolute claim that West Africans first encountered cannabis after 1945.
