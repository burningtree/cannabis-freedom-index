# Australia’s 1926 cannabis import controls — 20 October 2026

Added an article and corrected the existing event’s title, description and
sources after rereading `docs/WRITING.md`, including the context requirements.
The account leads with licensed medical/scientific imports, explains the older
drug controls and Geneva context, identifies the officials and links the
earlier Western Australian sales controls and later state restrictions.

## Evidence and limits

- Downloaded the official Federal Register copy of Gazette No. 115, 25 November
  1926. Read pp. 2039–2041 as extracted text and visually checked all three pages.
  Customs Proclamation 134 controls imports; 135 controls exports. Both were
  signed in Melbourne on 20 November by Stonehaven and H. E. Pratten.
- Import conditions 1–4 specify medical/scientific purposes, eligible professions,
  annual renewable licences, separate authorisation for each importation,
  security, disposal records, inspection of books and stock and reasonable
  inquiries into downstream purpose/destination. The cannabis list and definition
  were checked independently of the rules for opium, which was treated differently.
- The import preamble reproduces the earlier 1914 drug list, which supplies the
  before/after comparison. The export preamble cites its predecessor from 1924.
  No independent search for every earlier cannabis customs rule was completed,
  so no claim of the first possible Australian cannabis regulation is made.
- Export conditions require the minister’s written consent and a destination
  government certificate supporting medical/scientific purposes and quantity.
  These are covered in the article on the same change, without creating a
  second event or scoring the export restriction separately.
- Read the 2000 Working Party report, section 9.1, p. 65, for its account linking
  international drug conventions, federal import restrictions and later state
  laws. The article does not repeat its overbroad summary of the conventions as
  banning non-medical use: the original Gazette supplies the specific customs
  rules. No direct evidence of an Australian opposition campaign, public debate,
  arrest count or measured impact was found or invented.
- The report gives Victoria 1927 while the existing state event uses 1928;
  that event also calls Victoria the first state to control cannabis despite
  the sourced Western Australian 1899 sales controls. Resolving its enactment,
  commencement and scope requires a separate primary-law pass. The article
  links the later state chronology without repeating that first-control claim
  or its disputed date.
- Retained year-only dating for the existing event: the signatures and Gazette
  publication are verified, but no separate commencement analysis was made.
  Source locators contain both precise dates. No general possession penalty,
  cultivation ban or patient access in practice is inferred from customs rules.
- Preserved the existing score vector without revalidating its historical
  assumptions. No current scores or country profiles changed.
