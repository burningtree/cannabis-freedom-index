## 1926: the decree expressly includes hashish

The original text is reproduced in the French government's annual report on Cameroon
for 1927 (Paris: Imprimerie générale Lahure, 1928). Read the report's complete relevant
online transcription, including printed p. 44, the decree and Schedule B in the annex,
through the [Scribd reproduction](https://fr.scribd.com/document/978916195/Dossier-R2338-6A-3989-3989-Mandat-Francais-Sur-Le-Cameroun-Rapport-Sur-l-Administration-de-Ce-Territoire-Pour-l-Annee-1927).
The uploader's AI summary is not a source. The primary report's own content is the source.
The [UN Geneva catalogue copy](https://archives.ungeneva.org/mandat-francais-sur-le-cameroun-rapport-sur-ladministration-de-ce-territoire-pour-lannee-1927)
was located through an academic citation but returned a browser-verification page; its
scan has not been downloaded or visually checked. Distinguish this from the PDFs below.

The old event relied on a 1949 citation and said cannabis coverage was unknown. The
report identifies both decrees as dated 9 October 1926 and explicitly lists hashish and
its preparations in Schedule B. Articles 28–29 apply the earlier rules to that schedule
and reserve authorised handling mainly to qualified pharmacists with open pharmacies,
with specified laboratory exceptions. Article 35 recognises supply on prescription.
Recordkeeping and locked storage are prescribed. Articles 44–45 set three months–two
years AND a fine OR either alone for narcotics-rule breaches, group use, facilitation,
false-prescription offences and carrying drugs without a legitimate reason. Article 46
addresses confiscation and closures. OCR interleaves the two columns and sometimes
reads article numbers 30–39 as 50–59; use the continuous sequence, not these OCR errors.
The penalty text itself is clear. Its original francs are not modern CFA francs.

Category remains `other`: the first restriction date and predecessor coverage are still
unresolved. The report describes a 1922 decree concerning opiates, which does not by
itself date a cannabis ban. Do not replace the inherited 1900–1920 estimate with an
opiates-only law or assume no earlier cannabis control. The 1926 record is checked
against the actual reproduced primary text, not a search summary. Tags now identify
colonial rule, sentencing, trade, retail and police powers. The article concerns French
Cameroon; it does not claim the same decree applied to British-administered Cameroon.

## 1955: extension recovered as indexed text, scan pending

[E/NL.1956/147](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1296336/files/e-nl-1956-147-f.pdf)
reproduces Order 2772 of 19 April 1955. The complete indexed one-page text identifies the
local promulgation of Article 6 of France's 1953 law, extending the pharmacy code's
poisonous-substances chapter, including narcotics. It cites local promulgation of the
1926 decree on 27 November 1926 and the 1937 decree on 10 July 1938. Those are distinct
from the original metropolitan signature dates. The local PDF downloads failed or
returned HTML, so the new 1955 event is explicitly `checked: false` pending scan recovery.

The [actual French law](https://bdoc.ofdt.fr/doc_num.php?explnum_id=4) was downloaded
from OFDT and both complete gazette pages visually read (25 December 1953, 11535–11536).
Article 3 inserts doubled penalties for illicit drug cultivation/manufacture and supplying
or facilitating use by minors, and compulsory detoxification for users charged under
specified offences. Article 6 explicitly names Cameroon among the territories covered
by the chapter's extension. Date the local event to 19 April 1955, not the UN circulation
date or the French signature. No absolute prison range for the whole extended chapter
was inferred, and no local treatment capacity or admissions were claimed. Category
`other`, because the comparison with the locally applicable predecessor rules is incomplete.

## 1997: preserve fine-only alternatives and personal-use distinctions

[UNODC full text](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/cmr/loi-97-019_html/Loi_n_97-019.pdf)
downloaded and extracted; complete relevant pages read, with PDF pp. 21, 24 and 28
visually checked. Article 102 covers possession, purchase AND cultivation of quantities
small enough to be considered personal. High-risk plants/substances, including cannabis
oil: two months–one year AND a fine OR either alone. Cannabis derivatives other than
oil: one–six months AND a fine OR either alone. Do not silently turn that distinction
into flower versus concentrates or make the fine-only alternative disappear. No numeric
gram or plant threshold. Article 102(2) permits waiver after a solemn recorded promise
not to repeat the offence; no compulsory-treatment prerequisite is stated for that waiver.

Articles 91–95 likewise preserve prison/fine alternatives. The cultivation fine maximum
is 1,250,000 CFA francs in this text; the trafficking maximum is 125,000,000. Article 113
concerns convicted people with drug dependence and allows treatment instead of or
alongside punishment; evasion allows two–five years, a fine, or both. Article 111's optional
additional penalties apply to the stated Articles 91–99, not automatically the Article 102
personal-use offence. Corrected the profile's overbroad foreigner-exclusion wording and
permanent professional-ban omission. Article 77 is a limited authorisation route for
research, training and forensic work; Article 9 expressly preserves that exception.
No operating patient supply programme inferred.

The [justice ministry's 2017 presentation](https://www.spm.gov.cm/site/sites/default/files/expos%C3%A9_bikele.pdf)
was read in full and its final complete page visually checked. It confirms cannabis and
resin classification and ties the law to international commitments. The presentation's
personal-use fine figures and second-tier wording contain apparent errors; use the law
itself for those details. Its account calls the 1997 law a hardening, but it does not
compare each personal-use provision with earlier law. Category corrected to `other`
for the combined long supply penalties, lighter personal tier, waiver and treatment.
The earlier first-restriction score already incorporates the modern low score. Reallocating
it requires the missing colonial, reunification and post-independence texts, not an
invented before vector. Tags: sentencing, home-growing, treatment, eradication, trafficking.

## 2017: reported operations and proposed punishment are separate events

[Police commissioner Armand Abena's presentation](https://www.spm.gov.cm/site/sites/default/files/expos%C3%A9_abena.pdf)
was downloaded and read in full; complete printed pp. 3–5 visually checked. It dates
the narcotics subdirectorate to 2002; handlers and Malinois dogs received EU expert
training in 2016. A first canine section was stationed at Kousseri for the Maroua–Mora–
Kousseri road. The commissioner reported nearly 200 kg seized and around ten people
brought before the justice system since the start of 2017. These are attributed figures,
not national annual totals and not a count of convictions. Crop raids in the Northwest,
Southwest and West are described with an express acknowledgement of mixed impact.
No crop area or number of farmers was invented.

The new event uses the report date, 23 August, not an invented deployment day. Category
`enforcement`; tags enforcement, police-powers, eradication, trafficking. The article
also describes controlled deliveries and airport teams as the police's account of their
methods. Claims that cannabis causes violence are attributed to the officials where
mentioned, not presented as independently demonstrated causation.

The existing same-day draft-law entry remains `debate`, now tagged sentencing. Its
article distinguishes proposed higher fines, sentencing floors and an exclusion of
suspended sentences from the existing law. No new floor amounts or enactment date
were supplied by the source. The 2014 committee entry changes from `tightening` to
`other`: creating a coordinating body alone establishes no new offence or sentence.
The 1992 committee and 2025 seizure also receive relevant tags.
