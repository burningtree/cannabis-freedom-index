# Yemen: Aden controls and the national drug law

Research date: 25 October 2026. Yemen had four history entries, including a broad
first-restriction estimate. This pass adds five entries and an Aden article, rewrites the 1993 article, and
provides a more precise, primary-sourced account of the national law. Maintenance, writing, score
levels and book-research rules were reviewed before drafting. No book was used.

## Sources read and applied

- [Aden's government report for 1952](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1631392/files/E_NR-1952_30-EN.pdf),
  E/NR.1952/30, all four PDF pages read. Printed pp. 2–3 describe the 1942 drug
  ordinance and explicitly say cannabis imports and sales were prohibited. They
  record eight hemp cases, 1.75 kg seized, fines for all eight accused, confiscation
  and destruction. The report gives 145 shillings but does not make clear whether
  this is each fine or a total; the event does not resolve that ambiguity. Ethiopia
  as the principal source is attributed to the accused, not independently verified.
  The cover calls this the annual report for 1952, prints a 13 July 1952 document
  date, and also “New York, 1953.” The enforcement event uses reporting year 1952,
  not an invented publication day. Its opium-register population figures are
  internally inconsistent and are not applied. The 1942 entry is verified against
  this later government account, not an original 1942 statute that was not found.
- [Aden Government Notice 54 of 5 April 1947](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1296144/files/e-nl-1949-58-59-e.pdf),
  reproduced in E/NL.1949/58–59, printed p. 5/PDF p. 4. All pages read; notice
  visually checked. It adds preparations made from cannabis extract or tincture
  except those capable only of external use. The other notice on the page concerns
  different drugs in 1948. The event uses year precision: the notice is dated
  5 April, but a separate gazette publication/commencement date was not established.
- [Aden Ordinance 13 of 1960 in the original gazette reproduction](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1296146/files/e-nl-1961-26-e.pdf),
  E/NL.1961/26, all 13 pages read, compared with the complete
  [UNODC transcription](https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/yem/dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1960_html/Dangerous_Drugs_Ordinance_13-60.pdf).
  Governor C. H. Johnston assented on 22 December 1960; the gazette is dated
  5 January 1961. Sections 3–9 govern raw cannabis, resin, imports/exports,
  growing and powers to regulate possession/production/distribution. Section 22
  gives ten years/20,000 shillings in the Supreme Court or twelve months/5,000
  shillings in a magistrate's court, with fine alternatives or combinations.
  Section 28 repeals the old ordinance and preserves compatible rules and licences.
  The schedule retains the internal/external cannabis-preparation distinction.
  Neither the text nor a separate commencement notice established the exact start;
  the year-1961 event expressly describes publication. It is not a claim that
  cannabis growing was lawful before this replacement law.
- [Yemen's 1993 Arabic law on the Public Prosecution website](https://www.agoye.gov.ye/page.php?id=304)
  read through all articles and the relevant schedules; compared with the
  [House of Representatives Arabic PDF](https://yemenparliament.gov.ye/uploads/posts/documents/2018/10/1019201893432579.pdf).
  The full PDF was text-extracted; relevant pages 8, 10, 16 and 38 examined, and
  pages 8, 10 and 38 rendered for visual verification. Article 38 provides five
  years for personal-use holdings/growing, possible residential treatment for
  established dependence, and protection from prosecution for a user voluntarily
  entering treatment. Court-ordered treatment has a six-month minimum. Voluntary
  admission is six months to two years, with committee release. Article 56 says
  the law operates from issuance; the signature is 30 March 1993. The prosecution
  copy identifies Gazette issue 6 of 1993, but no original gazette image was read.
  Schedule 1 names cannabis; schedule 5 includes male and female plants; schedule 6
  exempts stalk fibre and seeds treated against germination. Articles 33–35 specify
  the different capital/25-year supply penalties. Articles 36, 43 and 8 support
  the added collateral-consequence section. Existing qat context remains separately
  sourced and was not used to infer a cannabis rule.
- [English translation held by UNODC](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/yem/1993/law_on_control_narcotics_93_html/LAW_3-93control_of_illicit_trafficking_and_abuse_or_narcotics.pdf)
  read for comparison at articles 28–40, 56 and cannabis schedules. It incorrectly
  renders the court-ordered treatment minimum as a maximum and article 56 as
  starting on publication. The two Arabic copies agree on the wording; the Arabic
  PDF images confirm the minimum and issuance clause. The English version is
  therefore not the authority for those claims. It also differs in details of
  repeat admissions and schedule 6; those details are not inferred from it.
- [Sana'a Justice Ministry account of 17 December 2025](https://moj.gov.ye/NewsD/11896)
  read in full, alongside its [28 July 2024 committee report](https://moj.gov.ye/NewsD/2199).
  The later account describes a draft amendment, explanatory memorandum, proposals
  for deterrent penalties and procedural changes, and further submission procedures.
  This supports a proposal entry, not a claim that penalties changed. Officials are
  identified by their Sana'a institutional setting. This account is separate from
  the internationally recognised government's existing 2025 enforcement figures.

## Scope, scoring and unresolved work

The four Aden entries concern the colony only. They are context on today's Yemen
timeline and do not impose the colony's rules on northern Yemen or the surrounding
protectorate. The 1942 and 1961 entries make their dependence on a later official
account and a replacement text clear in the source locators. The 1947 entry concerns
a product-list extension; the 1952 report is an enforcement observation. Explicit
unscored reasons accompany the legal-change categories. National scores remain
unchanged at 2, and the 1993 snapshot keeps the existing 5-to-2 transition. This
pass improves its date and explanation rather than inventing a newly verified
pre-1993 national regime.

The original 1942 ordinance, its precise commencement, pre-1942 Aden controls,
the separate northern regime and post-independence South Yemen remain priority
gaps. The national 1925–1961 aggregate estimate remains provisional. The 1993 law
is not presented as the first prohibition. Search did not establish a subsequently
enacted amendment; the December 2025 ministry report still calls the changes a
draft. This is not an exhaustive 2026 legislative or case-law audit, and the
profile's reporting figures remain limited to the territory stated by their source.

An incidental search result identified Oman's September 2026 replacement drug law
([Decree 67/2026](https://qanoon.om/p/2026/rd2026067/)); it is a priority lead for a
separate current-law pass. Its full provisions have not been checked here.
