# Seychelles: the 1882–1990 gap — 26 October 2026

Read DATA-MAINTENANCE, WRITING and BOOKS guidance. A current timeline audit found
Seychelles had the largest post-1800 interval between recorded events: 108 years,
1882–1990. The new records reduce it to 53 years, 1882–1935. The first-ban account
and inherited score reconstruction remain research targets.

## Material actually read

- Downloaded and read all three pages of E/NL.1953/52, reproducing Seychelles
  Ordinance 35 of 1948. It names the principal Ordinance 26 of 1935. Section 5
  adds section 15(l), preparations from Indian-hemp extract/tincture except those
  capable of external use only. Parliament passed it 29 December 1948; assent,
  bracketed date and Gazette No.3 publication are all 10 January 1949. Visually
  checked the product exception and publication line on printed p.3. The history
  uses the published/bracketed date, not the year in the ordinance title.
  https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1297583/files/e-nl-1953-52-e.pdf
- Read the complete catalogue entry for C.207.M.150.1937.XI in Gale's League of
  Nations reel listing, printed p.1799, reel XI–22. It identifies the text of
  Ordinance 26 of 1935 and its general import/export/sale/manufacture/use scope.
  Together with the actual later ordinance this supports a bounded 1935 adoption
  record. **The original 1935 text was not recovered:** do not claim it newly
  prohibited cannabis, supply a penalty or assign an exact commencement date.
  The 1937 circulation date is not treated as enactment.
  https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/3028000R.pdf
- Downloaded and read all three pages of E/NL.1982/40, reproducing Act 14 of
  1982, especially the cultivation wording and replacement sections 24 and 27.
  Visually checked p.1's plant wording and dates. Passage 22 June, assent
  23 June 1982; the UN circulated it 6 August 1985. No separate commencement
  notice was recovered. The history therefore records enactment year 1982,
  not an invented operative day. Search conditions differ for border arrivals,
  suspected premises/vehicles and personal searches; the article preserves them.
  https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/109513/files/e-nl-1982-40-e.pdf
- Read the complete reproduced trial judgment R v Francois (2000) SLR 103,
  delivered 26 July 2000: involuntary confession excluded; knowing possession
  found; 166.4 g resin after excluding two smaller pieces; threshold strictly
  **more than** 25 g; packaging supported trafficking; cash/untested knife did
  not by themselves establish it. The final order convicts. The ruling explicitly
  dates the 1990 Act's commencement to **1 July 1995**, under S.I.52/1995. This
  supports correcting the existing adoption description and adding commencement
  separately. The 25 g rule is described in the 2000 case, not backdated to an
  unexamined original 1990 schedule.
  https://sheriahub.com/cases/sc/caselaw/r-v-francois-co-34-of-1998-2000-scsc-11-26-july-2000
- Retrieved the Francois appeal listing dated 6 April 2001; it contained no
  substantive judgment. No appeal result or finality is claimed. Do not date
  older decisions mentioned in the trial judgment from their case-filing years.
- Read the complete substantive 7 April 2016 Nation report for the later context:
  unanimous passage, Charles Bastienne's reasons, sentencing discretion,
  rehabilitation and the Criminal Procedure Code change allowing suspended terms.
  Actual June commencement remains documented by the existing 2016 entry.
  https://www.nation.sc/archive/248994/new-law-to-boost-fight-against-drugs

## Retrieval and further leads

UN Digital Library failed through the web tool, but direct download recovered
E/NL.1982/40; the symbol API returned HTML, not a PDF. The same API did recover
E/NL.1953/52. Local signature checks and text extraction distinguish them.
The original 1935 ordinance and 1939 regulations remain targets. SeyLII's 1990
consolidation was blocked; its indexed header is corroboration only, not a claim
to having read that full statute. Twomey's legal-history PDF returned 410.

Read Mills, Cannabis Britannica (2003), printed p.180 and pp.193–194 from the
existing local text, including title/copyright transcription. The book describes
a ten-ton cargo purchased in Bombay in 1922, detained nearly four months by the
Seychelles governor and released when he found no ground to hold it. Its local
arrival/release date is not explicit within the 1922–1924 story, so no dated
Seychelles event is invented. The catalogue records these selected pages and the
correspondence follow-up. This is Mills's account of archival records, not a claim
that the underlying Colonial Office files were read.
