# Korea 1957–1976 — import ban, paper criminalisation, crackdown, total ban

**Original-text correction (27 October 2026):** the “import-only” description
below is superseded by [the primary-law review](history-korea-1957-primary-2026-10-27.md).
The original law names Cannabis sativa L. without an import-only qualification.
The local-hemp distinction is reported practice; its legal interpretation remains open.

Replaces the flat 1976-only story (1976 entry previously had `before` = FREE)
with a four-step chain, all from read sources.

## Sources read

- Matt VanVolkenburg / Jon Dunbar, The Korea Times, "How US-style drug laws
  helped crush 1970s Korean youth culture" (20 Mar 2018). Read in full via
  web_fetch. Covers: 1957 loophole, USFK requests from 1966, THC in the 1968
  Habit-Forming Drugs bill, June-July 1970 passage under US pressure and
  student-smoking reports, camp-town-only arrests 1970-1974, December 1975
  turn against entertainers.
- Same authors, The Korea Times, "Suppression of marijuana, 'degenerate'
  youth culture in 1975" (6 Sep 2022). Read in full. Covers: summer 1975
  anti-marijuana film, November roundup plan, 3 Dec arrests (Lee Jang-hee,
  Shin Joong-hyun, Kim Jung-ho), 80+ within a month, Entertainers'
  Association lifetime bans, mental institutions, 200+ song bans, impotence /
  poster propaganda, Park's "ruin" warning, Feb 1976 blame of the US.
- Gwangju News, "The Godfather of Korean Rock: Shin Jung-hyeon" (2019). Read
  the arrest passage. Covers: 1975 possession arrest, psychiatric hospital,
  prison with torture, blacklist until Park's assassination in 1979.
- Wikipedia "Cannabis in South Korea", History section. Read via web_fetch.
  Confirms: 1957 only outlawed Indian-grown marijuana while the Korean market
  flourished; 1960s hippie/US-base diffusion; 1976 all-forms ban merged into
  the Narcotics Control Act in 2000; Jan 2018 bill.
