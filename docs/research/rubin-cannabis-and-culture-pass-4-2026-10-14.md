## Applied material

**Jamaican herb camps and yards:** new tradition entry. Larger vendors and
social facilities, simpler smoking yards, trust networks and intermediaries,
cultivation risks, adult sociability, class judgements and legal context.
Explicitly distinguishes the informal institutions from later licensed herb houses
and from Rastafari religious practice. Uses the existing checked 1913, 2015 and
2018 history entries for legal background; no claim of direct institutional
continuity, current geographical extent or exemption for informal trade.

**Ganja:** extended the earlier Comitas reference to the examined complete chapter.
Added venues and social meanings, with work-enhancement beliefs attributed to
informants rather than presented as efficacy.

**Spliff:** added Comitas for adult smoking circles and the range of social
occasions, rather than treating the cigarette as exclusively a sacramental object.
Added legal background without reverse atlas cards for general laws.

**Rubin's 1975 ganja vision study:** new dated publication event and compact
article. Sample sixty working-class men, thirty smokers and thirty non-smokers;
ten smokers gave personal accounts of a recurring small dancing figure. Distinguish
those accounts from repeated hearsay and from national prevalence. The analogy
with Plains vision quests is an author's social interpretation, not evidence of
shared origin or an identical religious ceremony. No claim that the reported
vision is a required Rastafari rite or a reproducible pharmacological effect.

## Claims not imported

- Comitas's broad age/class generalisations remain period interpretations. His
  account is not a contemporary national survey. Folk medicinal practices involving
  children are not promoted as treatments or described as instructions.
- A belief that ganja improves work is not measured performance or proof that
  adverse effects cannot occur. Conspiratorial accounts of prohibition are not
  adopted as the documented motives of every lawmaker.
- Rubin's ancient Shen-Nung date, Hebrew etymology, Crusader route and earlier
  European diffusion conjectures are not independently verified here.
- Assertions that an initial dose was pharmacologically insufficient, or that
  culture overrides pharmacology, are not transferred as settled clinical facts.
- No new image: a specifically documented herb camp was not available from these
  chapter pages. A generic ganja farm or Rastafari ceremony would misrepresent the
  subject. No book plate has been copied into the website.

No country profile or score changes, and no additional reverse atlas cards for
shared national-law references.
