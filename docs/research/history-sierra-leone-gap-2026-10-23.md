## Sources read and their limits

### Colonial chronology

Reread Chris Duvall, *The African Roots of Marijuana* (Duke, 2019), printed p. 191 and
chapter 9 notes 47–53, pp. 316–317, from the retained complete book extraction in
`tmp/book-intake/duvall.txt`. Note 52 explicitly identifies the 1920 assertion in Akyeampong
as unsourced and says no listing was located. This contradicts presenting 1920 as a verified
ban. The earlier turn merely marked it unchecked, leaving an affirmative title and chart date.
Those product claims are now removed. This does **not** establish that no earlier restriction
existed: 1926 is the documented statute used here, not proof of an absence of earlier controls.

Downloaded and read the revised [Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, Cap. 154](https://www.sierra-leone.org/Laws/Cap%20154.pdf).
The heading dates the ordinance to 10 April 1926 and lists amendments in 1928 and 1932.
Sections 1–2 and 9–13 establish territory, the Indian-hemp definition, offences concerning
cultivation/possession/resin, and the separate medicine rules. Duvall connects this law with
implementation of the 1925 convention. The surviving PDF is a later revised text. The article
continues to identify that fact; it does not attribute every later penalty or every detail of
that revised edition to the original 1926 enactment. Recovering the original Gazette and
amendments would allow a more exact chronology.

Duvall's p. 191 and note 53 supply the 1933 report identifying diamba as cannabis, quoting a
US Bureau of Narcotics annual report published in 1935. That original annual report has not
been independently recovered; the article cites Duvall, not a falsely claimed reading of it.
The 1851 article retains its original botanical source and Duvall's population-history discussion.
Its new later-law reference has a separate colonial statutory source. The diamba culture page
receives an internal-link/chronology repair and the supporting statute in its existing whole-page
bibliography; its unrelated legacy material has not been re-audited or migrated wholesale.

### Pharmacy and Drugs Act 2001

Downloaded the [original Act from UNODC SHERLOC](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/sle/the-pharmacy-and-drugs-act--2001_html/Sierra_Leone_Pharmacy_and_Drugs_Act_2001.pdf),
extracted it, and visually inspected printed pp. 27, 30 and 31. The Act is signed 30 November
2001, gazetted and commenced **13 December 2001**. Sections 48–49 address possession,
smoking, visiting smoking premises, allowing smoking on one's premises and equipment.
Section 50 requires written Pharmacy Board consent for narcotic-producing plants. Section
59(3) expressly sets at least **15 years** for an individual convicted of an offence relating to
Indian hemp. Section 65 retains the Dangerous Drugs Act; section 66 repeals the 1988 pharmacy
law while saving existing subordinate rules.

The event is `other`, describing a replacement pharmacy law and its overlapping controls.
The original 1988 text was not recovered, so the prose does not claim that all these offences
or that minimum were first introduced in 2001. Its score vector places the directly documented
smoking offence at this statutory milestone. The exact earlier start of a standalone smoking
prohibition remains uncertain. Current consumption changes from 1 to 0 because use itself is
expressly criminal, under both the 2001 law and 2008 section 8. The 1926 record's following
vector retains the old 3-point position; the 2001 record reaches today's 2. Other segment scores
are preserved. This is a coarse legal chronology, not independently established typical
sentencing practice in each intervening year.

### National Drugs Control Act 2008 and amendment

Read the [original 2008 Act](https://www.sierra-leone.org/Laws/2008-10.pdf), particularly definitions,
sections 2–3, 8, 12, 43, 92–93 and schedules. Section 12(3) lets a court reduce imprisonment
or impose a fine where the usual punishment would be harsh or unconscionable. Therefore the
old profile's absolute assertion that no fine-only route exists was incorrect. Section 8
expressly covers consumption, contrary to the old description that use is prosecuted only
through possession. Sections 92–93 repeal Cap. 154, preserve rules/proceedings and address
conflicts with the 2001 pharmacy law. Section 43's cannabis-specific crop destruction rule
is retained with its evidence and urgency safeguards.

**Scheduling anomaly:** as recorded in the earlier research note, the printed schedules put
cannabis and cannabis resin in the anomalous fourth-schedule list headed toxic chemical
inhalants, while the definition of drug names the first three categories. No life sentence
for cannabis cultivation or supply is inferred from section 7's prohibited-drug category.
The profile distinguishes the 2001 cannabis rule from the 2008 general offence. Direct official
judiciary evidence, below, confirms practical use of 2008 section 8 for cannabis charges;
it does not settle every possible scheduling, charging or sentencing question.

The [Library of Congress account of 28 August 2008](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2008-08-28/sierra-leone-new-drug-control-law-passed/)
was read for the aircraft carrying cocaine on 13 July, urgency certificate and UN-assisted
drafting. Cocaine provides the legislative context; it is not presented as cannabis enforcement.
The duplicate Wayback bibliography entry was removed.

Downloaded and read the one-page [Amendment Act 13/2008](https://www.sierra-leone.org/Laws/2008-13.pdf):
passed 21 August, signed 2 September, gazetted/commenced 18 September. It inserts presidential
commencement by statutory instrument. The new event uses 18 September, while the principal
Act keeps its existing year-only event date. The amendment's own commencement is not treated
as proof that the principal Act began that day. No retroactive start date is invented.

### 2023 enforcement and April 2024 emergency

Downloaded and read the [Judiciary's 31 August 2023 report](https://www.judiciary.gov.sl/?p=1430).
Thomas Sowa and John Nyakeh Amara were accused of possessing kush and cannabis under section
8(a), refused bail and remanded. They were two among ten defendants across separate files;
the record does not label all ten cannabis defendants, treat accusations as convictions or
invent the later outcome. This primary evidence replaces synthetic-only sentencing analogies
in the enforcement explanation.

Read President Bio's **full address of 4 April 2024**, [reproduced by Sierraloaded](https://sierraloaded.sl/news/bio-declares-national-emergency-drug-substance-abuse/),
including the five strands, district treatment-centre promise, task-force participants and
justice/enforcement pledges. The speech remains `debate` as an announcement without a scored
statutory amendment. Treatment, enforcement and youth tags were added. The original headline
about a marijuana/fentanyl/tramadol mixture was replaced with the source's account of synthetic
kush and the later testing evidence. Promises are written as promises, not completed services.

Downloaded and read the GI-TOC [June 2024 preliminary testing report](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Lucia-Bird-et-al-FTIR-spectrometer-testing-of-kush-in-retail-markets-Sierra-Leone-and-Guinea-Bissau-GI-TOC-June-2024.pdf),
pp. 1–5, including methods, limitations and findings. It reports synthetic cannabinoids and
nitazenes in sampled kush and rejects untested ingredient rumours; further laboratory
confirmation was pending. The article preserves the preliminary status and does not equate
all kush with cannabis flower. This synthetic-drug science is context for the existing national
emergency entry, not a new scored cannabis-law change.

### Medical production guidelines: a material missed route

The Pharmacy Board's [guidelines index](https://pharmacyboard.gov.sl/Guidelines.php) labels its
medical cannabis link “2025”. Following that link led to the actual
[28-page Version 02 guideline](https://pharmacyboard.gov.sl/admin/gallery/561872ecc0e939bdbe88a7f53ea6442d.pdf),
whose page headers explicitly say issued **15 May 2024**, effective **17 May 2024**, approved
by the Registrar. Downloaded/extracted it; visually inspected printed pp. 4–5 and read pp.
4–8, 12–13 and 22–25 for purposes, applicant categories, security, planting material,
inspection and waste rules. The event uses the effective date in the document itself.

The document relies on section 50 of the 2001 law and describes licensed cultivation,
harvesting and processing of medical cannabis, specifically including production for export.
Applicants include licensed corporate bodies/manufacturers and approved health organisations.
It requires identifiable, traceable planting material and female cuttings, suitable personnel,
security and quality controls, with inspection and potential licence revocation.

The previous profile's absolute “no medical cannabis”/“no production” claims were contradicted
by this primary source. Medical status becomes **limited**, matching the project's definition
that includes production-only routes. No increase to personal cultivation or access scores is
made: a domestic patient prescribing/dispensing programme or actual licences/exports have not
been verified. The history event is `medical`, explicitly `unscored` for the production-only
scope, tagged trade, patients and seeds. Planting statuses become licence-only; retail trade
statuses become unclear because the guideline does not establish a public seed/cutting shop
route. Unrelated driving and UNODC seizure sections are preserved.
