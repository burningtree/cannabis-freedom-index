## Romania (#58)

Decision No. 25 of the High Court of Cassation and Justice, 27 January 2025, was read on the legislative portal. It was published in Official Gazette No. 70 of 28 January 2025 and is binding from that publication. For Penal Code article 336(2), the court requires both the psychoactive substance in a biological sample and the substance's capacity to affect the driver's ability to drive. Paragraphs 147 to 150 say the offence does not require proof of a concrete change in the driver's behaviour, and that detection alone is not enough, because a drug can remain detectable after its psychoactive effect has ended. The ruling sets no numerical THC cutoff.

Law No. 172 of 31 May 2024, Article III, replaces article 336. The ordinary offence is one to five years and a ban on exercising rights. The aggravated cases in subsection (3) are two to seven years and the same ban. The law was published in Official Gazette No. 510 of 31 May 2024. The text that was opened does not state a commencement date. A private consolidation dates the new article from 3 June 2024; that page was read and was not used as the history date. The history entry uses 28 January 2025, the day the court ruling became binding.

Article 66, as amended by the same law, sets the length of a rights ban at one to five years for most listed rights and one to ten years for one of them. The list of rights was not in the amending section that was read, so the profile does not say how long a driving ban lasts. Roadside screening, refusal penalties and the 90-day licence suspension in the old profile were not re-read and were removed. The EUDA country table was not opened.

`driving.status` was `zero`, which the site uses for an offence proved by any detectable THC. The ruling is neither that nor a requirement to prove impaired driving, and it is not a numeric limit. A `capacity` status was added for this standard. The possession score stays at 1. The history entry is unscored because it is a driving rule.

The issue's link for Law 172 pointed at portal document 283096. That page is Government Decision 534 of 2024, about a building project, and it was not used.

## Uruguay (#59)

Law 19.172, article 7, original text on IMPO, replaces article 31 of Decree-Law 14.294. A person who transports, holds, stores or possesses a quantity meant for personal consumption is exempt. The judge assesses that purpose. Up to 40 grams of marijuana is treated as a personal amount. The same article separately covers a harvest of up to six plants at home, and a club's harvest, when grown as the law allows. The text does not require the cannabis to have come from a pharmacy, a club or a registered grow for the personal-use exemption.

Decree 120/014, article 39, authorises possession of psychoactive cannabis for personal use and repeats the 40 gram measure. The IRCCA FAQ named in the issue timed out and was not used. The Transnational Institute overview was not opened. The old "since 1974" sentence was not re-read in the 1974 decree and was dropped. The prison term for unauthorised supply was not in article 31 as read, so the enforcement line no longer states a number of years.

Possession stays at 6. Enforcement stays at 6.

## Jamaica (#60)

The Ministry of Justice fact sheet on the Dangerous Drugs (Amendment) Act 2015 was read from the Financial Investigations Division copy. Paragraphs 28 and 29: a person who does not ordinarily live in Jamaica may apply for a Ministry of Health permit to buy and possess up to 2 ounces at a time for medical or therapeutic purposes. The applicant produces evidence of a recommendation or prescription from a licensed doctor in the country where they live, or signs a voluntary declaration confirming that. A fee is payable. Paragraphs 6 to 10 and 11 to 12 give the J$500 ticket for up to 2 ounces and for public smoking, and the medical-recommendation exception to the possession rules.

The current Ministry of Justice statute reprint is a large PDF and did not finish downloading, so section 7D was not read from that reprint. The 2015 parliamentary bill PDF has no extractable text. The profile follows the fact sheet. It does not say that a dispensary arranges a doctor on the spot, and it does not say how a permit is processed in 2026. The Cannabis Licensing Authority retail note named in the issue was not opened. Visitors stay at `open` because a visitor may lawfully buy under the permit; the chip's "same as residents" is the nearest existing status. Access stays at 4.
