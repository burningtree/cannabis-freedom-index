# Tuvalu: an earlier fine increase, youth reporting and the 2025 penalties

Researched 23 October 2026. Two new entries extend Tuvalu's timeline from two events to four.

The existing article is rewritten to explain the 2025 penalties and licensing power with inline citations. All fourteen existing profile

## Earlier fine amendment and conflicting dates

The original Law Revision (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 2009, Act 4 of 2009, was downloaded
and read in full. Section 5 and Schedule 2 replace $2,000 with $4,000 in the Dangerous Drugs
Act's section 39(2)(a). This is the general High Court penalty clause where no specific penalty
is provided, not a cannabis-specific possession tariff. The later consolidated Act pairs the
$4,000 fine with ten years' imprisonment; it also contains a separate summary penalty.
No inference about sentences actually imposed is made.

The original amendment's title and number say 2009, but its first page expressly prints
Parliament's enactment as **3 July 2010** and commencement as **6 July 2010**. Both extracted
text and a rendered image confirm the year. The current catalogue and the 2022 drug-law
endnote instead give 6 July **2009**. The event uses **2010-07-06**, the commencement printed
on the original amendment. This conflict needs archival gazette or parliamentary confirmation;
the later footnote is not silently substituted for the original. Its source locator states
the conflict. The fine increase is unscored: monetary severity changes inside an existing
prison-backed offence do not justify a separate whole point in the six-segment index.

## Government report: scope and date

Tuvalu's initial child-rights report CRC/C/TUV/1 is dated **16 February 2012** and was
circulated by the UN on **10 October 2012**. The event uses the report date, not the later
circulation date. The full relevant sections (paragraphs 342–346 and 359), cover and context
were read from the official PDF. It cites a **2006** behavioural survey of people aged
**15–24 in Funafuti**, of whom 5% reported marijuana use. This is neither current national
prevalence nor a statistic confined to under-18s. The original survey was not independently
recovered, so the event says the government report cited it.

The report also describes marijuana imports from Fiji, border-control concerns and youth
programmes addressing alcohol and drugs. Its phrase about three cases involving police is
ambiguous about the nature of their involvement; no claim of three police officers being
arrested or convicted is made. The event omits that count. Paragraph 359 identifies updating
drug laws and coordinated programming as priorities. The report's policy description that
illicit use was not directly regulated is not applied as current law after the 2025 amendment.
The report is context, without a score change.

## Colonial continuity and present legislation

The complete relevant 2022 Revised Edition of the Dangerous Drugs Act was read, including
commencement, definitions, sections 4–10, the medical-drug chapter, penalties and endnotes.
It confirms **20 October 1948** commencement and lists numerous later amendments. The 1948
entry now explicitly distinguishes the inherited ordinance from its later consolidated controls;
no current fine or 2025 penalty is backdated to 1948. Its existing first-ban score vector stays.
Cannabis covers either Cannabis sativa or Cannabis indica, or any portion of the plant;
extracts and tinctures also appear separately in Schedule 2.

The 2025 amendment was read directly, including all relevant operative sections, application
form and the complete cannabis rows of Schedule 3. The penalty table was visually inspected.
The official catalogue's existing verified **3 July 2025** commencement remains, consistent
with the Act's commencement-on-publication rule. Its text gives the smallest smoking or
possession band two to five years, and up to one plant one to five years. These are statutory
minimums and maximums, not an assertion of observed routine sentencing.

The printed table overlaps its 'not exceeding' rows and leaves amounts over 10 g through
50 g without a separate smoking/possession row, and four or five cannabis plants without
a separate growing row. The article and profile state those structural facts. They do not
interpolate thresholds, infer which overlapping penalty takes precedence or invent a tariff
for omitted quantities. The law also adds medical/scientific licensing, with application,
record, security and reporting conditions. An issued licence or operating patient service
was not verified, so the existing **limited** medical label and access score 0 remain.

The Traffic Act's relevant sections 23 and 25 were independently downloaded and read before
rewriting the driving block. Impairment by a drug is the offence trigger; the separate 0.08%
figure concerns alcohol, not THC. The actual penalty and potential disqualification are cited.
All planting, clubs, visitors and driving status fields match the baseline. No optional context
section is added merely to repeat a source.

## Rejected lead and retained evidence

The catalogue's 2024 List of Prohibited Imports Regulations was retrieved and read. Its actual
subject is motorcycles, engines and parts, not cannabis. It therefore produces no cannabis event.
Earlier amendment numbers listed in the consolidated law remain retrieval leads; a number in
an endnote alone is not enough to describe a cannabis change.
