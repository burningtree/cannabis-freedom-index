# New Brunswick, the Northwest Territories, and New South Wales, 24 October 2026

Issues #91 to #94. Texts opened before the profiles were rewritten. Scores are unchanged.

## Read

- Cannabis NB, private retailers and FarmGate. Private stores are owned and operated by private retailers under their own names, certified by Cannabis NB and licensed by the Department of Justice and Public Safety. FarmGate stores are owned and operated by licensed New Brunswick producers and sell their own products on site. https://www.cannabis-nb.com/PrivateRetailers-DetaillantPrive/
- Cannabis NB, About Cannabis NB. The 28 July 2026 first-quarter release for 2026–27 says Cannabis NB and its partners are the only legal retailers, and lists 32 Cannabis NB stores, 12 private retailers and 8 farmgate outlets. The same page still carries an earlier release telling customers they can buy online at cannabis-nb.com. Store counts differ between quarterly releases on that page, so the profile names the kinds of outlet and does not freeze a count. https://www.cannabis-nb.com/about-cannabis-nb/
- Government of New Brunswick, Cannabis. Consumption is prohibited in public. It is allowed at home, on the backyard or property, and at an occupied campsite. In someone else's home, the homeowner's permission is required. https://www.gnb.ca/en/topic/family-home-community/housing-property/cannabis.html
- Cannabis Products Act, SNWT 2018, c 6, office consolidation including SNWT 2019, c 29 and SNWT 2020, c 14. Section 5: the Minister designates a person as a vendor to operate a cannabis store. Sections 12 and 13: a community may approve a restriction or prohibition system after a council asks the Minister to hold a plebiscite. Section 14: once a store is operating, a plebiscite cannot restrict sales at that store. https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/legislation/cannabis-products/cannabis-products.a.pdf
- Government of the Northwest Territories, 1 December 2020. ReLeaf NT and Trailblazers Cannabis Shop were designated as private retailers in Yellowknife. The release also says requests for proposals were issued for private retailers in Hay River, Fort Smith, Norman Wells, Inuvik and Fort Simpson. https://www.gov.nt.ca/en/newsroom/gnwt-designates-private-cannabis-retailers
- Northwest Territories Liquor and Cannabis Commission, where to buy. Legal cannabis is sold only through NTLCC-approved vendors or the approved online store. The current list names ReLeaf NT, Trailblazer Cannabis Shop and other vendors, and gives ReLeaf an online store. https://www.ntlcc.ca/en/cannabis/where-buy-cannabis
- Northwest Territories Liquor and Cannabis Commission, cannabis overview. Purchase is limited to approved vendors or the approved online store. https://www.ntlcc.ca/en/cannabis
- NSW Police, Cannabis Cautioning Scheme Guidelines for Police, version 5.0, publication date May 2025. Police may caution an adult for use or possession of not more than 30 g of dried cannabis, or equipment, at most twice. The caution is discretionary. Fresh cannabis, living plants, hash, hash oil and a quantity that is mostly seeds are outside the scheme. An unspent conviction for a serious drug offence excludes the person. Endnote 2 says the threshold rose from 15 g to 30 g. The version history dates version 5 to April 2025 and describes it as minor edits; it does not give a calendar day for the threshold change. https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/858242/Cannabis_Cautioning_Scheme_-_Guidelines_for_Police.pdf
- NSW Police, drug programs and initiatives. The cautioning scheme does not change the legal status of cannabis. Police may charge instead of cautioning. A person can be cautioned only twice. https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/crime/drugs_and_alcohol/drugs/drug_pages/drug_programs_and_initiatives
- Drug Misuse and Trafficking Act 1985 (NSW), current compilation. Section 10 makes possession of a prohibited drug an offence. Section 21 sets the penalty for that division at 20 penalty units or imprisonment for two years, or both. https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1985-226

## Not opened

- A current New South Wales penalty-unit value. The profile states the Act's 20 penalty units and does not repeat the old $2,200 figure.
- A commencement day for the rise from 15 g to 30 g. The May 2025 guidelines are the text that was read. No history entry was added.

Access stays at 6 in New Brunswick and the Northwest Territories because licensed shops with a purchase cap remain the lawful route. Consumption stays at 3 in New Brunswick because public consumption is still prohibited; a backyard and an occupied campsite are private places under that page. Possession stays at 2 in New South Wales, and enforcement at 3, because a small amount is still a criminal offence and the caution is discretionary. The 2000 history entry still describes the original trial as 15 g, which is what that entry is about.
