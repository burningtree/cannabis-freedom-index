## Primary 1993 law

https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/mrt/loi-93-37_html/mauritania-loi_stupefiants.pdf

Downloaded complete 14-page text, extracted and read relevant complete clauses; visually
inspected PDF pages 4 and 11, including Article 13's capital aggravations and Article 39's
fine-only alternative. Signed **20 July 1993** by President **Maaouya Ould Sid'Ahmed Taya**;
Prime Minister **Sidi Mohamed Ould Boubacar** also signed. Article 52 provides urgent
publication; no separately verified commencement/gazette date is claimed.

- Articles 1–2: domestic ministerial schedules distinguish high-risk I/II, lower-risk III,
  and precursors IV. The schedules themselves are absent from this copy. Do not claim a
  particular modern cannabis preparation's domestic classification was newly verified.
- Articles 3–5: production/cultivation, import/export, and broad local trafficking including
  buying, possession and gifts: **15–30 years AND a fine**, death for a repeat offence.
- Article 13: specified organised crime, violence/arms, or death caused by the offered drugs
  can lead to capital punishment. Capital exposure is therefore broader than repetition.
- Article 7: facilitating high-risk use, including premises, **2–10 years AND a fine**.
- Articles 17–21: permanent foreigner exclusion for listed offences **including 38/39 use**;
  optional civic restrictions, travel/passport measures, licence suspension and professional
  restrictions. Article 41's separate user-exclusion clause has inconsistent internal
  references. Do not silently repair it or give a confident universal 1–5-year rule.
- Article 24: initial police custody 72 hours, two possible written 72-hour extensions,
  medical examination at the start and every 24 hours. Article 25 allows night searches of
  specified use/production/storage premises, requiring written prosecutor authority for a
  home unless ordered by the investigating judge. No claim about later procedure-code
  consolidation is made.
- Article 39: illicit **use**: maximum **two years OR a fine OR both**. It does not expressly
  give personal possession the same lesser penalty. Article 5 separately includes possession.
- Articles 42–44, 48 and 51: health-authority assessment and treatment/medical-supervision
  routes; completed prescribed treatment or successful care after alleged use can prevent
  prosecution. Articles 49–50: voluntary treatment and requested anonymity/certification.
- Several use/treatment cross-references are internally inconsistent in this reproduction,
  notably Articles 41, 45–47 and 50. Do not derive a precise treatment-evasion prison range
  from the broken references. The express nonprosecution rules in 44/51 are readable.
- Fine amounts are in the 1993 text's ouguiyas. They are not presented as current post-2018
  MRU amounts. Currency conversion was not independently verified.

## 2008 parliamentary debate

Read complete official AMI bodies:

- **9 January 2008 Senate**, https://ami.mr/fr/archives/5066
  Senate president Bâ Mamadou dit M'baré; **23 for, five against**. Government bill had 87
  articles and three annexes. Some opposition senators asked for delay over fears of
  cultivation/import/export permissions. Health Minister **Mohamed Lemine Ould Raghani**
  cited the three UN conventions and near tension/rupture with the international drug-control
  board; promised to consider recommendations in implementing provisions.
- **21 February 2008 National Assembly**, https://ami.mr/fr/archives/5733
  President Messaoud Ould Boulkheir chaired. Same health minister described control of
  manufacture/import/export/distribution, strict medical purposes, prescribing doctors and
  dispensing pharmacists. Deputies wanted enforcement while protecting pharmacy outlets.
  The report also covers an unrelated audiovisual bill; those sections are excluded.

Later official documents cite **Law 2008-012 of 27 April 2008**, controlling the licit market:
https://www.msgg.gov.mr/sites/default/files/2023-11/J.O.-1545F-DU-15.11.2023-1.pdf
and the government telemedicine decree at
https://mtnima.gov.mr/sites/default/files/2024-02/D%C3%A9cret%20n%C2%B02023-118%20Fixant%20les%20conditions%20d%27exercice%20de%20la%20t%C3%A9l%C3%A9m%C3%A9decine%20et%20les%20domaines%20de%20son%20application%20en%20Mauritanie.pdf

## Capital punishment: newer field assessment

https://www.ecpm.org/app/uploads/2024/10/Brochure-Mauritanie-2024.pdf

Downloaded/read relevant full passages and visually inspected PDF pages 2 and 4 (printed
pp. 2–3 and 6–7). ECPM still lists the four 1993 drug-law capital provisions, Articles 3–5
and 13. Its last-execution dates distinguish civil conviction **1982** and military conviction
**1987**. These are all-crime execution dates, not cannabis-specific counts. No assertion
that people are currently executed for cannabis is made.

A 2012 AMI speech confusingly calls 035/2010 a drug law. Official Ministry of Finance text,
and ECPM's 2024 assessment, instead identify **2010-035 of 21 July 2010** as the
**anti-terrorism law**, replacing 2005-047. Do not invent a 2010 replacement of the drug law:
https://www.finances.gov.mr/Recueil_des_textes_relatifs_a_la_mission_du_Ministere/Textes/TXT%20GL/Loi%202010-035%20abrogeant%20et%20rempla%C3%A7ant%20la%20loi%202005-047%20relative%20%C3%A0%20la%20Lutte%20contre%20le%20Terrorisme.pdf

The 2010 pharmacy law was downloaded and relevant passages searched/read as a follow-up lead:
https://extranet.who.int/cpcd/sites/default/files/public_file_repository/MRT_Mauritania_Loi_No-2010-022-du-10-fevrier-2010-relative-a-la-pharmacie_2010.pdf
It refers to a public-establishment monopoly for internationally controlled narcotic and
psychotropic medicines (Article 37), prescription and pharmacy supply rules, and repeal of
2004/036. It does not establish operating medical cannabis access in the passages read.
No 2010 history event was added.
