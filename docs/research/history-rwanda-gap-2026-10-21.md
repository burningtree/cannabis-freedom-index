# Rwanda: older codes, possession, medical licensing and sentencing discretion

Researched 21 October 2026 against the current writing guide and categories/tags. Before
this pass Rwanda had six history entries and one article. Seven checked, tagged entries
and three articles are added; three existing entries and the medical article are revised.

## Primary sources actually read

- **1977 code:** [Refworld's national-legislation reproduction](https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/1977/fr/40601).
  Read the opening decree, repeal list and attached code Articles 271–277 through the web
  tool. Direct curl returned 403, which is not a read local copy. President Habyarimana's
  decree is dated 18 August 1977. Opening Article 3 delegates implementation to a separate
  presidential order: **the signing date is not a verified commencement date**. The entry
  records the signed code and carries no score. Repeal of the 1903 smoking-hemp ordinance
  is evidence of an older legal inheritance, not evidence that the Congo's original date
  was Rwanda's commencement date. The estimated first restriction stays unresolved.
  Article 275: 3 months–5 years AND 50,000–100,000 francs OR either alone. General Article
  273: same prison range AND up to 250,000 OR either alone; production/import/manufacture/
  export penalties could double. Facilitation Article 274: 3–5 years AND 100,000–500,000
  OR either alone; up to 10 years where use/supply involved under-18s. Article 277 requires
  confiscation and crop destruction.
- **Drug-control law 2012:** [Official Gazette 15 of 9 April 2012, pharmacy-council copy](https://www.pharmacycouncil.rw/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Law-governing-narcotics-drugs-psychotropic-substances-and-precursors-in-Rwanda.pdf).
  The downloaded 30-page Gazette extract has broken embedded-font text. OCR was performed
  on all 30 pages; read the relevant definitions and Articles 3–7, authorisation/records,
  and 28–31. OCR is an aid, not a claim of a complete legal review. Signed 15 February;
  Article 31 makes publication the commencement trigger, hence **9 April**. Article 7
  permits medical/research authorisation and a safeguarded industrial-use exception.
  Do not describe it as the first cannabis ban or an operational medical-cannabis scheme.
- **Penal code 2012:** [original Gazette hosted by Rwanda National Police](https://www.police.gov.rw/uploads/tx_download/Official_Gazette_no_Special_of_14.06.2012-4.pdf).
  Read Articles 593–597 (printed pp. 487–491), repeal and Article 766 (pp. 596–597).
  Page 488 was rendered and visually checked. Signed 2 May, effective on publication
  **14 June**. Use: 1–3 years AND 50,000–500,000 francs; no express fine-alone choice in
  this clause. Making/transforming/importing/selling: 3–5 AND 500,000–5 million; doubled
  for international acts. Facilitation: 1–3 AND 500,000–5 million; inducing a child:
  more than 5–7 years AND fine. Mixed changes from 1977, without a verified whole-point
  personal-enforcement delta. General suspension/mitigation possibilities mean the article
  describes the drug clause, not a claim that every convicted person necessarily served prison.
- **Government's 2014 UN report:** [CCPR/C/RWA/4](https://www.minijust.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minijust/Publications/Reports/UN_reports/4_2014_CCPR_STATE_REPORT_2014.pdf).
  Read cover and paragraphs 166–167 with footnote 105. Received **11 July 2014**, distributed
  30 October. New history entry uses submission, not the earlier statistical year. The
  government reports 1,910 incarcerated for smoking cannabis in **2011** and 2,402 arrests
  connected with banned substances across **2011–2012**. The latter covers all drugs; the
  paragraph highlights ages 18–35, but no independent age-by-offence table was read. Do not
  convert incarceration into convictions, current rates or a proven policy change in 2011.
- **2018 law:** [complete original Gazette](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/rw/2018/rw-government-gazette-dated-2018-09-27-no-special.pdf)
  and [Ministry of Justice copy](https://www.minijust.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minijust/Publications/Laws/Offences_and_penalties_in_general_2018.pdf).
  Both downloaded; read Article 263 (pp. 226–228), general community-service rules and
  commencement Article 335 (p. 275). Pages 226–227 rendered and visually checked. Signed
  30 August, effective **27 September**. Original law already states **1–2 years OR community
  service** for use. Three supply tariffs: life; 20–25 years; 7–10 years, with different fines.
  The first category is **life AND more than 20m–30m francs**, not a combined 20-to-life range.
  English/French duplicate the word “severe” for the two top tiers; the Kinyarwanda terms
  `bihambaye` and `bikomeye` align with the distinct 2019 category names. English use wording
  also omits terms found in other languages. Do not invent a quantity threshold from `bito`
  or treat a translation discrepancy as a verified separate reform.
- **2019 classification:** [Health Ministry's Gazette extract](https://www.moh.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Moh/Publications/Laws/new_law/Ministerial_Order_List_of_narcotics_and_their_categorization-43-57__3_.pdf).
  Read Articles 2–5, 7–9 and Annex I; printed p. 50 rendered and visually checked. Signed
  4 March by Diane Gashumba; effective on publication **11 March**. Annex I explicitly
  places cannabis in Category I, “very severe drugs,” with cocaine, heroin and opium.
  The former 2015 order is repealed but its original contents have not been compared:
  the entry is `other`, not an invented first ban/reclassification tightening. Article 7
  preserves medical/research authorisation under the 2012 law.
- **2019 amendment:** [original Gazette from Justice Ministry](https://www.minijust.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minijust/Publications/Laws/Offences_and_penalties_in_general_2019.pdf).
  Read Article 1 (then-current mitigation), full Article 11 replacing Article 263 and
  Article 14. Signed 8 November; effective **29 November**. The English/French text now
  expressly includes being caught with narcotics in the use/possession clause; 1–2 years
  OR community service is retained, not newly introduced. Supply tiers retained. Full
  ministerial mapping is needed because translated names in the statute are inconsistent.
  The intent/quantity boundary between personal possession and punishable storage is not
  established by these clauses. No safe gram limit is published. New entry is `other`.
- **2021 cannabis order:** [Law Reform Commission's original Gazette extract](https://www.rlrc.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/RLRC/Laws_of_Rwanda_v2/Domestic_laws/Laws_in_force/7._Administrative/7.8._Health/7.8.3._Tobacco_and_drugs/7.8.3.8_Cannabis_and_cannabis_products_MO_no_003_of_2021.pdf).
  Read definitions and Articles 3–14, security, transport/tracking/reporting and 24; printed
  p. 13 rendered and visually checked. Signed 25 June, effective **28 June**, correcting
  the existing signing-date event. Eight permissions cover cultivation/genetic material,
  planting-material imports/exports, processing, product imports/exports, finished-product
  registration and research. Licences renewable after five years; shipment permits separate.
  **The legal text is not export-only:** Article 14 permits medical use on specialist
  prescription. The definition requires written Ministry authority for the prescriber.
  Remove the old article's claim that nothing in the order reaches a resident and the
  profile's categorical “no real patient programme” claim. Practical availability is separate.
- **2023 amendment:** [Gazette of 5 December](https://www.minijust.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minijust/Official_gazettes_2/_______2023_Official_Gazettes/December/OG_n___Special_of_05.12.2023_Imiburanishirize_Imanza_inshinjabyaha_PP___Ibyaha_Ibihano_muri_rusange_CP.pdf).
  Read Law 059/2023 Articles 1–3 and 23. Printed p. 40 rendered and visually checked.
  Signed 4 December, effective **5 December**. Mitigated life sentence floor falls from
  25 to 15 years; fixed terms of at least six months can fall to half the offence minimum;
  fines can fall to a quarter of the minimum; community service may be suspended. These
  are judicial powers, not automatic resentencing or a cannabis exception. Refusal to
  perform community service properly can send the remaining sentence to prison. The
  event is `easing`, explicitly unscored: personal use already allowed community service
  and the evidence does not establish a whole-point practical enforcement shift.

## Later infrastructure and patient availability

[Eastleigh Voice's full 15 May 2025 report](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/rwanda/150697/rwandas-medical-cannabis-project-at-83-per-cent-completion-government-says)
was read. It describes **off-site infrastructure**, 83% complete, double fencing
outstanding, over 1bn of a 2.2bn-franc public budget spent, and delayed private-project
launch dates. It does not prove the whole plant 83% built or actual patient supply. The
existing entry gets the report's full date and `debate` category for a progress report,
with export plans distinguished from an actual new medical-access rule. The New Times
page supplied its headline/date but no body through the web tool; it was not treated as
an independently read full account.

[Rwanda FDA's September 2026 pharmaceutical register](https://monitoring.rwandafda.gov.rw/register/hmdr/)
was downloaded in full: HTML has 2,614 table rows including the header (2,613 data rows).
No matches for cannabis, cannabidiol, tetrahydrocannabinol, dronabinol, nabilone,
nabiximols, Sativex, Epidiolex or Epidyolex. This supports a limited statement about the
reviewed registration list, **not a claim of no exceptional imports, authorised doctors
or patients anywhere in Rwanda**. Actual patient supply remains unverified. Keep the
current access score pending stronger evidence and mark the medical event explicitly
unscored. The specialist-prescribing rule itself is positively described.

## Scores and boundaries

All six **current** scores and recreational/medical status values are preserved, not
newly validated. The existing 2018 event wrongly moved personal enforcement from 2 to 1
on the strength of a trafficking news report. The original law instead shortened the
user's prison range and offered community service. Remove that unsupported negative
step and reclassify the mixed rewrite as `other`; do not move the drop to another
arbitrary year. The estimated first-ban `before` vector remains 10 in every segment,
but the inherited historical baseline and current enforcement level still require review.
A mechanically clean chain does not validate them.

The planting block's existing licensed/regulated statuses are retained against the 2021
seed and planting-material rules. Remove the clubs block's unsupported claim that no
clubs are known. Keep the cultural work about Helen Codere's 1959–60 study in the atlas
and the earlier research note; the medical article now concentrates on the legal change.
No claim of exclusive Twa use is reinstated. No first-ban date is invented from the
1903 repeal reference. Court case and community-service implementation leads remain
for a later pass; no case is published from a search snippet.
