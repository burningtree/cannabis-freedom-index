# Micronesia state-law follow-up, 15 October 2026

This pass follows the state-law leads left by the preceding Pacific audit. It strengthens one
profile in depth rather than filling a new country with generic national-code text. No research
formula, completion signature or numerical freedom score was changed.

## Read and established

- **Pohnpei:** downloaded the legislature's 2012 advance Code (1,300+ pages); read the relevant
  Title 66 chapter 4 definitions, Schedule I cannabis entries, registration provisions 4-116–117,
  offences 4-123–124 and 4-125, discharge/expungement 4-130, and repeat-offence provision 4-132.
  Read Title 71 section 12-105 on drug-impaired driving. The possession/transfer page was
  visually checked against the rendered PDF (PDF page 1258; printed Title 66 page 35).
  This is a partial reading of the compilation, not a claim to have read the entire Code.
- Pohnpei's small-possession penalty is up to one month, $100 or both. The unpaid-transfer
  exception for no more than an ounce points to that penalty, not the national code's $50 fine.
  Source notes attribute these provisions to State Law 2L-198-91, dated 27 June 1991. The new
  history event records that attribution; it does not claim to have reviewed the original signed
  law, its commencement clause or every later amendment.
- **Yap:** read all of Title 11 chapter 10, including sections 1003–1008. Small possession can
  carry up to 30 days, $100 or both, but a first drug offender is subject only to a fine.
  Production has a separate one-year/$1,000 maximum; delivery can carry three years/$5,000.
  Section 1005 bars prescriptions for the listed hallucinogens, which include marijuana.
  The all-parts definition lacks the national/Pohnpei sterilised-seed exclusions. No equivalent
  small unpaid-gift carve-out appears in the delivery provision. Read code index and edition
  introductions: this is an unofficial edition updated through YSL 5-23, not a 2026 consolidation.
- **Kosrae:** read sections 13.531–532 and the misdemeanor penalty chapter. For an ounce or
  less, category three permits up to six months, $500 or both. Larger categories have their own
  penalties; the smallest band must not be substituted for them. Also read the felony penalty
  chapter, but did not simplify its unusual category-one wording into a precise maximum in
  the profile. Read section 13.710 on driving under the influence. The website describes an
  unofficial legislative-counsel version updated through State Law 7-166.
- **Kosrae v. Paulino, 22 September 1987:** read the full suppression order. Two plants and a
  warrant/plain-view search were at issue. The court said weight affected penalty rather than
  whether possession was an offence. This was not a sentencing judgment or a small-grow licence.
- **Chuuk:** read the retained controlled-substances appendix definitions and sections 291–292,
  together with its introduction. The introduction says the 1980 Trust Territory provisions are
  reproduced for reference and have not been revised. Its prose swaps appendix lettering while
  its actual links and pages identify Appendix B as controlled substances; the relevant drug
  text and status warning are both cited. It cannot verify a current $50 state ceiling.
- **Driving:** read Yap Title 25 sections 704–706, Pohnpei 71 PC 12-105, Kosrae 13.710 and
  Chuuk draft Title 31 section 1305. The latter includes editorial renumbering and inconsistent
  cross-references, so exact repeat-offence revocation rules were not generalised. The new
  context section identifies influence/impairment offences while retaining additional habitual-
  user wording and Yap's separate in-vehicle consumption offences. No numerical THC threshold
  or nationwide uniform penalty was inferred. Blood-alcohol provisions were not relabelled THC.

URLs and locators are retained in `countries/fm.yaml`, history and the new Pohnpei explainer.
Working download, extraction and rendered page are in ignored `tmp/research-progress-pass4/`.

Also read Oneisom’s original 1991 report of an exploratory 1985 Chuuk study. Added explicitly
historical local market and arrest context; the interview sample was not used as a national
prevalence estimate.
