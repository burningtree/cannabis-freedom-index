# Algeria: colonial controls, capital clauses, treatment and personal growing

Research date: 23 October 2026. Previous goal turn: verified progress, adding four Romanian
articles and repairing its medical/legalisation and Senate-vote accounts. This continuation
addresses Algeria's much-compressed history. At the start it had eleven events, two articles
and only two scored steps. The batch leaves fourteen events, nine articles and five scored
steps. The wider history goal remains active.

## Source-led changes

### Colonial date and a missing 1932 control

The entry `algeria-french-decrees-1916` retains its ID for existing links, but its date is
corrected to **9 February 1917** and the title now describes extending French controls to
colonial Algeria. September 1916 is the French metropolitan decree, not the extension date.
A new `algeria-cannabis-controls-1932` entry records the 12 January decree applying the updated
French rules and clarifying the names of controlled cannabis products.

Source: [League of Nations, O.C.1542, 23 May 1934](https://archive.org/details/LoN1934-O.C.1542),
printed pp. 54–55, French government reply of 5 May 1934. Downloaded the Archive-hosted searchable
scan, extracted its text, rendered PDF page 28 and visually read the two-page spread. The
report describes earlier council discussions, small growers' income, local preparations,
seizures and colonial enforcement claims. Its Secretariat footnote questions whether the
metropolitan text expressly covered cultivation. The new article attributes official claims;
it does not repeat near-elimination of cannabis as an independently established result.

The first event's unregulated `before` and immediate post-restriction calibration are retained
from the existing dataset. This is not a fresh reconstruction of every 1917 penal provision.
Original colonial implementing texts and any earlier enacted local controls remain a priority;
1881/1915 discussions in the report do not alone prove an enacted earlier prohibition. The
1932 nomenclature/control entry is **other**, without a new score: the report does not establish
a measurable new personal right or prove a new explicit cultivation prohibition.

### New 1975 entry and article

[Official Gazette 15, 21 February 1975](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/1975/F1975015.pdf),
printed p. 198, Ordinance 75-9 of **17 February 1975**. Downloaded the original scan and visually
read PDF page 2. It has no searchable text layer. The official scan resolves a secondary
reference's conflicting 27 February date in favour of 17 February.

- Article 5: illicit use, two months to one year, 500–5,000 dinars, or either penalty alone;
  courts could order detoxification.
- Article 3: trafficking-related acts, ten to twenty years.
- Article 8: a broad capital clause for offences under the ordinance where their character
  threatened the Algerian people's moral health. Unlike the 1985 replacement, it does not
  restrict its cross-reference to trafficking/facilitation articles; Article 5 use is within
  the referenced ordinance. The description gives the statutory condition and does not claim
  every user automatically received a death sentence.
- Article 9: applies to acts after 1 October 1974 without a final judgment by publication.
  The history date is the ordinance's promulgation, not a claim it was publicly enacted in 1974.

Added `algeria-drug-ordinance-1975`, category **death**, tags **sentencing, treatment**, with an
article and a scored capital-risk step. There is no claim of verified executions under it.

### 1985 narrowing and non-prosecution

[Official Gazette 8, 17 February 1985](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/1985/F1985008.pdf),
Law 85-05 of 16 February. Downloaded and visually read PDF pages 18–20 (printed 138–140).
The scan has no searchable text layer. Article 268 expressly repeals the 1975 ordinance.
Article 245 keeps the same basic use range; Article 248 refers only to Articles 243–244
(trafficking/facilitation), excluding ordinary use Article 245 from its capital clause.
Articles 249–253 provide treatment-linked non-prosecution or possible withholding of the
use penalty, specialist/inpatient or outpatient care, and reports to the judicial authority.

Revised `algeria-health-law-drug-framework-1985` to **death**, because the category includes
limiting capital punishment. It now has an article and a scored step away from the broad
1975 capital exposure. The text describes eligibility and legal alternatives, not observed
programme participation or the frequency with which judges used them.

### 2004 replacement and repeal of the earlier capital clause

[Official Gazette 83, 26 December 2004](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2004/F2004083.pdf),
Law 04-18 of 25 December, downloaded and read in extracted text, printed pp. 3–7.
Article 38 repeals the 1985 drug provisions, including Article 248. The new law provides
life for the cultivation offence (Article 20), organised trafficking and certain other supply
acts; Article 12 provides two months to two years, 5,000–50,000 dinars, or either alone for
use/personal possession. Articles 6–11 retain treatment-linked routes.

The existing entry is now **death** because it removes the drug-law capital clause, with an
article. It remains unscored with an explicit reason: the repealed capital clause concerned
supply/facilitation, while sharing remains at the index's minimum and personal-use penalties
and treatment alternatives remain in the existing bands. This is not a conclusion that all
other Algerian capital offences involving drugs or other crimes disappeared.

### Two 2007 implementing procedures

[Official Gazette 49, 5 August 2007](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2007/F2007049.pdf),
both decrees signed 30 July. Downloaded and read printed pp. 3–5 in extracted text; rendered
page 3 for the authorisation text.

- Decree 07-229, Articles 1–8: specialist assessment, detoxification or supervision, doctors'
  reports, interruption notifications, certificates and a prosecutor's non-prosecution
  decision. Confiscation remains required. Revised the existing treatment entry to publication
  date 5 August and added an article. Kept **other**, since it implements the existing route;
  it does not establish fresh empirical evidence of how often users avoided prison.
- Decree 07-228: health-ministry authorisation for the listed operations, including cannabis
  cultivation, limited to medical/scientific purposes. The application identifies work,
  product, quantity and use, includes a criminal-record extract, and undergoes official checks.
  Import/export permits last three months per operation; records, declarations and secure
  storage are required. Added `algeria-medical-authorisation-rules-2007`, **medical**, tags
  **patients, trade**, explicitly unscored: implements the 2004 exception and does not prove an
  operational cannabis patient-access programme. No dedicated article was added for this
  narrower administrative entry.

### The omitted 2023 personal-growing exception

[Official Gazette 32, 9 May 2023](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2023/F2023032.pdf),
Law 23-05 of 7 May. Downloaded and read the drug-law amendment, printed pp. 12–16, separately
from the human-trafficking law earlier in the same Gazette. Rendered and visually inspected
pp. 15–16, including amended Articles 12 and 20.

Article 20 adds **two to five years and DZD200,000–500,000 for personal-consumption cultivation**.
The original 2004 Article 20 imposed life for cultivation without this exception. The old
entry's reason for leaving this unscored was therefore wrong. Personal growing meets level 1
("a crime, lesser penalties for a few plants") rather than level 0 (treated as production).
The provision's actual test is personal consumption, not a specified number of plants.

Article 12 keeps two months to two years and raises the fine floor from 5,000 to 20,000;
**either penalty alone remains possible**. The previous profile repeatedly made prison and
fine sound mandatory together. The amendment also adds treatment protection for minors who
complete detoxification and higher penalties for specified suppliers.

The entry is now **other** for this mixed reform, tagged **home-growing, sentencing, treatment**,
with an article and a cultivation score step. The profile's cultivation score changes from
0 to 1; other current segment values and recreational/medical classifications remain unchanged.

### 2025 primary text replaces the secondary-only account

[Official Gazette 43, 13 July 2025](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2025/F2025043.pdf),
Law 25-03 of 1 July. Downloaded and read printed pp. 3–6; rendered pp. 4–5 and visually read
page 5. The revised entry and new article use the original law.

Article 21 bis 2 lists offences and aggravating circumstances for capital punishment; Article
16 bis 2 separately concerns recruiting/using minors or specified vulnerable people at listed
locations. Article 27 escalates repeat offences, including life to death. Article 26 bis sets
a maximum-penalty rule when examinations establish drug influence during an offence and
addresses examinations/refusal. Article 5 bis 10 concerns periodic pupil medical examinations: early screening requires consent
from legal representatives or, where appropriate, the juvenile judge. Positive results direct
the pupil to care and are protected from prosecution and other uses. Article 5 bis 9 requires
negative results in recruitment files for listed public/private bodies, with implementing
details left to regulation where needed. The article distinguishes these tests from the
school screening and criminal-investigation examinations.

The existing 2025 enforcement step (2 to 1) is retained, now accompanied by the explicit
maximum-penalty rule rather than relying solely on capital penalties for trafficking. Its
`before.cultivation` changes to 1 to carry forward the 2023 exception. The act dates remain
promulgation dates where previously used, with publication dates stated separately; the batch
does not invent a single nationwide commencement day from the signature alone.
