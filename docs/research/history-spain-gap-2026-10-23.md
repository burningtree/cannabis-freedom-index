# Spain, 1860–1929: the state hemp monopoly of 30 April 1928

The longest post-1850 gap in a large European history was Spain's, from the 1860
hashish poison listing to a year-only "first restrictions" entry for 1929. That
entry rested on the Spanish Wikipedia page. The instrument that names Indian hemp
is the Royal Decree-Law of 30 April 1928.

## What changed

The existing page `spain-first-restrictions-1929` keeps its id. Its date is now
1928-04-30. The category is `tightening`. The score step is unchanged: 100 to 11,
the same drop the old entry carried, because this is still the first scored
restriction. The text now follows the decree: a state monopoly on importing and
distributing Indian hemp, its resin and its extracts, and a fine of 50 to 100
pesetas a gram for illicit holding or use.

An article was added. Spain's other scores were not edited. `node scripts/data-status.cjs chain es`
is the check that the step still meets the 1983 entry.

## What was read

- [Royal Decree-Law of 30 April 1928](https://pnsd.sanidad.gob.es/pnsd/legislacion/pdfestatal/2.pdf),
  the drug-plan reproduction. Base 3 lists "Cáñamo indiano, su resina y extractos"
  and says the import and distribution right belongs to the Restriction from that
  date. Base 14 limits orders to pharmacists, pharmaceutical laboratories and
  teaching or research laboratories. Base 43 sets the per-gram fines and applies
  them to mere illicit holding or use. Base 19 puts the official prescription
  form on the later publication of the regulations, so that form is not dated
  30 April.
- The [provisional regulation of 8 July 1930](https://pnsd.sanidad.gob.es/pnsd/legislacion/pdfestatal/3.pdf)
  repeats "resina de cáñamo indiano" in its packaging rules and points back to
  article 1 of decree-law 2045 of 13 November 1928. That November text was not
  opened, so it is not a separate entry.
- A 1918 toxic-substances regulation was opened. It names opium, coca, cocaine
  and ether. It does not name hemp, so it was not added.

## Left open

China's 1934–1979 gap is the largest by population. The 1950 opium order that
was opened does not name cannabis, and the Chinese Wikipedia history jumps from
the 1934 Xinjiang trade to 1985. No cannabis entry was added there. Pakistan's
1914–1965 gap is the next by population. The 1930 Dangerous Drugs Act page on
the Pakistan code was blocked, so it was not added from a search summary.
