# Australia, Wikipedia narrowed, 24 October 2026

The national profile, the Australian Capital Territory page, and the Northern Territory page were rewritten from statutes and official pages opened for this pass. Scores are unchanged. The recreational status flags are unchanged.

Sentences the opened pages do not state were not carried forward. That includes "federal law still prohibits possession everywhere", a Northern Territory fine of $200, "possession in a public place carries up to two years" as if no infringement notice existed for that quantity, "use is legal at home" in the ACT, "seeds cannot be legally bought" as a national access claim, and "roadside saliva tests in most states" with licence loss regardless of impairment.

## Read this pass

- Drugs of Dependence Act 1989 (ACT), republication 42, effective 16 November 2025. Section 162: cultivating one or two plants is an offence of 1 penalty unit, and does not apply to a person 18 or older who cultivates them in the ACT. For that section, cultivates does not include artificial cultivation, defined as hydroponics or an artificial source of light or heat. Section 171AA: possessing not more than a small quantity is an offence of 1 penalty unit, and does not apply to a person 18 or older who possesses the cannabis in the ACT. More than a small quantity is 50 penalty units, six months, or both. The Act leaves "small quantity" to the regulations and does not itself state 50 g or 150 g. Section 171AAA: more than four plants at premises is 50 penalty units, two years, or both. Section 171AAB: cultivation other than where the person lives, or in an area lawfully accessible to the public, is the same maximum. Section 171AAC: harvested cannabis not stored out of reach of children is the same maximum, with a defence of all reasonable steps. Section 171AB: smoking cannabis in a public place, or exposing a child to the smoke or vapour, is 30 penalty units. Smoke includes vapour and an activated personal vaporiser. https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/a/alt_a1989-11co/current/pdf/alt_a1989-11co.pdf
- ACT Policing, Drugs and the law. Cannabis is not legal. The January 2020 amendments exempt adults over 18 from ACT criminal liability in stated circumstances, and the October 2023 reforms do not change those rules. Adults may possess up to 50 g dried or 150 g fresh, and grow up to two plants at home per person, four per household. Offences include public use, exposing a child, storage where children can reach it, hydroponics or artificial cultivation, growing where the public can access the plants, and selling, sharing or giving cannabis as a gift. Commonwealth law is applicable across Australia, including in the ACT, and officers may use ACT or Commonwealth law. https://police.act.gov.au/community-safety/alcohol-and-drugs/drugs-and-the-law
- Drugs of Dependence (Personal Cannabis Use) Amendment Act 2019, register page. Notified 10 October 2019. Sections 1 and 2 commenced that day. The remainder commenced 31 January 2020. https://legislation.act.gov.au/a/2019-34
- Misuse of Drugs Act 1990 (NT), as in force 1 July 2026. Section 6B: cultivating less than a traffickable quantity of a prohibited plant is 200 penalty units or two years. A cannabis plant is a prohibited plant because it is listed in Schedule 2. That schedule states the traffickable quantity of a cannabis plant as not less than five and not more than 19 plants. Section 7B: possession of a Schedule 2 drug of less than a traffickable quantity is 50 penalty units. Section 7D: the same possession in a public place is 200 penalty units or two years. Cannabis plant material is a Schedule 2 drug, and its traffickable quantity is 50 g, meaning a quantity equal to or exceeding 50 g. Section 20: an infringement notice offence includes section 6B(1) for not more than two cannabis plants, and sections 7B(1) and 7D(1) where the drug is in Schedule 3 and the quantity does not exceed the listed amount. Schedule 3 lists 50 g of cannabis plant material. The prescribed amount is 2 penalty units. The notice may be given only to an adult. Paying within 28 days expiates the offence. A notice is not required, and a prosecution remains available if none is given. Sections 5 to 5D are supply offences. https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=12012
- Drug Misuse and Trafficking Act 1985 (NSW), current compilation. Section 23(1): cultivating, supplying, or possessing a prohibited plant is an offence. A cannabis plant cultivated by any means is a prohibited plant. The penalty for section 23(1) was not read, so no dollar amount is stated. https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1985-226

## Reused from the state pass the same day

These pages were opened while the state profiles were written, and the national sentences repeat only what those notes already record.

- South Australia regulations, version 27 June 2024. A simple cannabis offence includes 100 g of cannabis and one plant. Expiation fees include $250 under 25 g, $400 from 25 g to 100 g, $250 for private smoking or consumption, and $400 for one plant. The Legal Services Commission page says public smoking is outside the notice. https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/_legislation-documents/lz/c/r/controlled-substances-controlled-drugs-precursors-and-plants-regulations-2014/current/2014.236.auth.pdf
- Queensland Police drug diversion program, New South Wales cannabis cautioning guidelines, Victoria Legal Aid drug possession, Tasmania Misuse of Drugs Act section 25, and the Western Australian Cannabis Intervention Requirement page, as already used on those state profiles. Tasmania's "three cautions in ten years" was not re-read and is not repeated on the national page.

## Access lines

The Alcohol and Drug Foundation cannabis page says cannabis for medical or scientific use is legal under federal law, and that patients need a prescription from an authorised doctor. It does not say supply is mostly through telehealth, and it does not say there are no shops. The state and territory access lines now use that wording. The "no shops" and "mostly through telehealth" sentences were dropped because the Wikipedia page that had sat on every state source list was removed. https://adf.org.au/drug-facts/cannabis/

## Left on Wikipedia

The Therapeutic Goods Administration medicinal cannabis hub returned an error when fetched, so the production and medical sections still cite Wikipedia beside that hub. They still say federal licences, hemp licensed by the states, any doctor prescribing with no fixed list of conditions, and tightening of telehealth prescribing. Those sentences were not rewritten. The driving sources already on the profile now carry the consequences section, which no longer describes saliva tests or automatic licence loss.
