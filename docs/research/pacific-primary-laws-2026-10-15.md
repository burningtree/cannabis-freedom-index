# Pacific primary-law audit, 15 October 2026

This pass improves Kiribati, Solomon Islands and Micronesia. It changes no research formula,
sets no completion signatures and leaves all three countries' six freedom scores unchanged.
Medical status becomes `limited` to reflect documented preparation or research frameworks;
none of the legal-access scores increases without evidence of an operating patient route.

## Sources read and limitations

### Kiribati

- Government Trade Portal scan of the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, Cap. 23, Revised Edition
  1977: pages 3, 5–8, 13 and 15–17, plus the cannabis-preparations entry on page 26, were
  visually read. The heading confirms commencement on 20 October 1948. Pages 6 and 17
  establish the cultivation/possession/gift offences and the two modes of trial. Page 26
  expressly includes cannabis extracts and tinctures in Schedule 2 Part I.
- Dangerous Drugs Regulations L.N. 19/80: all 17 regulations read from the government-hosted
  seven-page text. Commencement is 21 February 1980. Regulation 9 expressly authorises
  registered pharmacists to prepare cannabis extracts and tinctures at their shop. Regulations
  6 and 11–12 govern prescription possession and dispensing. No patient stock or dispensing
  service was established. This framework does not authorise ordinary flower sales or growing.
- Act 8 of 1995: operative amendment, explanatory memorandum and certification read. New
  section 8A concerns unexplained suspected drug proceeds, warrantless arrest and forfeiture.
  Passage is certified on 15 August 1995; the event uses only the year because handwritten
  commencement/publication endorsements were not reliably resolved.
- Act 14 of 2001: operative amendments and memorandum reviewed. It adds datura metel to
  existing controls; no cannabis relaxation or penalty revision was identified. Its title year
  is not used as proof of a particular commencement date. No cannabis event was added for it.
- The 2018 PacLII case remained inaccessible and was not reread. The old profile's general
  assertions that few cases reach court and sentences usually fall below maxima were removed.
  The statutory text cannot establish either nationwide pattern.

The full 27-page ordinance was not exhaustively reviewed. A local Vision OCR attempt failed;
the relevant pages were rendered and read visually instead. The readable regulations and
amendment files were extracted with pdftotext. No full contemporary consolidation was claimed.

### Solomon Islands

- Attorney-General's authorised Dangerous Drugs Act reprint, as at 1 October 2009: definitions,
  sections 3–20 and 35–40, and the legislative endnotes reviewed. Section 39(2) was checked
  visually. The current government catalogue entry was also checked.
- Section 39 distinguishes summary conviction (5,000 penalty units, six months, or both)
  from conviction with assessors (100,000 units, ten years, or both). The old summary's dollar
  conversion was removed because the applicable current unit value was not established.
- Sections 7–8 expressly prohibit cannabis cultivation, possession and giving. Broader reports
  about gaps in drug legislation cannot establish that those cannabis offences do not exist.
  Section 12's express use offences concern prepared opium, not cannabis.
- Sections 13–20 include cannabis extracts/tinctures in the pharmaceutical framework. That
  establishes statutory scope, not present patient availability or a production industry.
- The endnotes date constituent Act 4 of 1941 to 14 April 1941 and the 2009 amendments to
  1 October 2009. The original 1941 wording was not obtained, so the first cannabis restriction
  remains estimated. A context event records legislative ancestry without claiming it proves
  every present cannabis prohibition already existed in 1941.
- Parliament's one-page committee call for submissions was read in full. It gives 17 February
  2026 as the deadline and announced inquiry start for Bill 9 of 2025. The existing 20 February
  reporting event now distinguishes that announcement, the later report and enacted law.
  Later assent/commencement was not established. The draft itself was not obtained, so new
  claims about its exact language were not added.
- The 5 November 2025 Gazette rules were checked and concern methylamphetamine specifically;
  they were not added as a cannabis reform or imported into the cannabis penalties.

The old assertions about most personal-use sentences and heavier plantation-size penalties
were replaced with supported statutory descriptions and an explicit practice gap.

### Micronesia

- Full national Code 2014 Title 11 Chapter 11: relevant definitions, Schedule I entries,
  registration provisions 1132–1133, offences 1141–1144 and conditional discharge/expungement
  under 1148 reviewed. The profile's former source URL led only to the narrower definitions
  and Schedule I page; it now points to the complete chapter supporting its penalties.
- The national code permits specific controlled-substance registration and Schedule I research.
  That is not evidence of ordinary cannabis prescribing or an operating patient service.
- FSM v. Ehsa, 24 FSM R. 151 (27 June 2023): marijuana search discussion and jurisdiction
  analysis, pp. 172–175, read along with the order's opening and disposition. It excluded the
  bag evidence, found probable cause concerning approximately seven plants and requested
  briefing on national jurisdiction. It did not decide that jurisdiction question or establish
  a national sentencing pattern. New profile text and a history entry retain that distinction.
- Four state codes were not comprehensively read. Search leads identify Pohnpei Title 66
  Chapter 4, Chuuk's retained controlled-substances appendix, Kosrae section 13.531 and Yap's
  drug-control and driving provisions as useful next work. These are leads, not verified
  national rules or newly added facts.
