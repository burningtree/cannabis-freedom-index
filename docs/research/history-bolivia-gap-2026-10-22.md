# Bolivia: drug penalties, personal-use assessments and medical access

Research and edits: 22 October 2026. Bolivia began this pass with nine history entries and no articles. Added four tagged entries, revised two existing tagged entries and wrote six articles (386–403 words, four sections each). Total project coverage after this pass: 2,818 events and 698 articles.

## Sources read and retained

Research files and baseline YAML copies are in `tmp/history-bolivia-gap-2026-10-22/`. Downloaded HTML was converted to searchable text. Relevant PDF pages were rendered for visual checks. Blank PDF text extraction was not treated as a successful reading.

- [Law 171, 10 January 1962, Justia reproduction](https://docs.bolivia.justia.com/nacionales/leyes/ley-171-1960-1964.pdf): four-page reproduction, text read, first page visually inspected. Article 1 expressly includes Indian hemp and its preparations. Article 2 sets three to ten years **and** a fine for unlawful manufacture/trade/supply; article 3 sets three to five years for possession without justification of lawful origin. Article 11 requires institutional admission for misuse until recovery, with judicial review informed by psychiatric experts. Articles 5, 7–8 and 14 support the aggravations, medical reporting and fine assessment described in the article. Promulgation is 10 January 1962; the congressional date is 18 December 1961. The reprint contains stray markup and apparent transcription errors in unused clauses; historical monetary amounts are omitted. [Lexivox reproduction](https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-L-171.html) was also downloaded and read.
- [Official DGSC text of Law 1008](https://www.dgsc.gob.bo/normativa/leyes/1008.html): relevant clauses and original annex read, cross-checked against [Justia PDF](https://docs.bolivia.justia.com/nacionales/leyes/ley-1008-1985-1989.pdf), whose PDF pages 9–10 were visually inspected. Articles 33(m), 34, 37, 46 and 48–51 support the cultivation, possession, treatment, trafficking and supply accounts. Article 48: ten to twenty-five years **and** a fine. Article 51: eight to twelve years **and** a fine, regardless of supply quantity. Article 49 includes both dependent and occasional consumers; two specialists assess the minimal personal amount. Above it, the trafficking offence applies. No fixed cannabis gram quantity appears there. Article 46 specifies one to two years, two to four for repeat offending; the position of its fine clause is not normalised into a general first-offence fine claim. Promulgation is 19 July 1988.
- [Official DGSC implementing rules, Decree 22099](https://www.dgsc.gob.bo/normativa/decretos/DS22099-88.html): article 26 expressly defines immediate personal possession as equivalent to **48 hours of consumption**, assessed by the two medical experts. Above it trafficking is presumed. Header and closing formula both give **28 December 1988**. Lexivox wrappers for the decree/regulation incorrectly display 1998; use the official text. A separate 48-hour police rule elsewhere in the regulation must not be confused with this personal-use assessment.
- [Official DGSC Law 913 PDF](https://www.dgsc.gob.bo/normativa/leyes/Ley913.pdf): relevant articles 1–8, 11–20, 26, 29–33, repeal clauses, cannabis listing and closing formula read. PDF pages 1 and 10 visually inspected. Article 18(II) requires registration and prior health-ministry authorisation for medicinal controlled substances in Lists I–IV; article 19 provides limited, exceptional scientific/medical research permissions to specified institutions; article 20 regulates authorised manufacture and prescription supply. Cannabis and resin remain in List I. The first repeal clause removes old article 33(a), (q), (r), articles 35, 36, 38–42, 44 and Titles VI–VIII, while retaining the relevant possession/trafficking/supply/cultivation offences. In particular, the old definition of production in article 33(r) must not remain presented as current law in the planting text. Promulgation is **16 March 2017**, despite the Lexivox wrapper displaying 23 March.
- [Official DGSC Decree 3434 PDF](https://www.dgsc.gob.bo/normativa/decretos/DS-3434.PDF) and [Lexivox text dated 13 December 2017](https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-DS-N3434.html): general implementing regulation exists. Official scan begins at printed page 21 and has no extractable text; its first two PDF pages were visually read, including article 1's implementing scope. Lexivox's opening provisions/date were read. No claim that all cannabis-specific medical implementation was complete follows from this general regulation. Conversely, the old blanket statement that implementing rules were never issued is unsupported.
- [Ombudsman report, 23 November 2021](https://www.defensoria.gob.bo/noticias/gestiones-defensoriales-posibilitan-que-el-estado-autorice-la-importacion-de-cannabis-medicinal-para-el-tratamiento-de-una-ninya): full substantive report read. It reports an AGEMED resolution authorising cannabis import exclusively for compassionate treatment of one child, under supervision by a doctor in Bolivia. Application was September 2020; changing requirements complicated it; ombudsman meetings and formal requests clarified them. **23 November is the report date**, not a verified resolution date. The original resolution/number, approved quantity and duration were not recovered. Do not call this the first-ever approval, infer three bottles or three months, name the child, or turn the report's patient-specific improvement claim into general medical advice.
- [Chamber of Deputies medical cannabis bill, PL 376/2025–2026](https://diputados.gob.bo/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/PL-376-2025-2026.pdf): 20 scanned PDF pages; full Vision OCR retained. Page 1 visually confirms sponsor **Sandra Hinojosa Núñez**, letter dated 6 April and receipt dated **7 April 2026**. Operative bill is PDF pages 14–20; pages 2–13 contain explanatory arguments and foreign examples, not additional Bolivian permissions. Proposed articles cover ARCAME regulator, patient register, doctor registration, retained pharmacy prescriptions, cultivation/manufacturing/research/export-surplus licences and plant/batch tracking. The transitional clause would end prosecutions for proven medicinal cannabis-oil possession absent indications of illicit commercialisation. Proposed clinical-protocol duties and state-production priorities are attributed to the bill, not asserted as medical efficacy or existing availability. Page 18 was also visually inspected. No operative home-grow or club permission was found; foreign club examples in the background are not treated as one.
- [Chamber's pending-bills index](https://diputados.gob.bo/proyectos-de-ley-en-tratamiento/): checked 22 October 2026, still lists PL 376 in “Proyecto de Ley en Tratamiento”. All proposed institutions and permissions are conditional in the article. No enacted programme or actual pharmacy sales inferred.
