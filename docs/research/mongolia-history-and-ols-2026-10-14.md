# Mongolia: history and hemp vocabulary, 14 October 2026

Added four history articles: adoption of the 2002 control law, adoption and later commencement of the 2015 Criminal Code, the cabinet's 7 March 2017 national drug-control programme, and the Article 20.7 amendments dated 10 January 2020. The last two events are new unscored timeline context. Added Ols to Culture / Language. No scores changed.

## Sources read

- [Legalinfo, Mongolian Criminal Code](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/11634): current lower-case consolidated provisions 20.7–20.10, amendment annotations, and commencement. Article 20.7(1) adds acquisition in 2020; non-sale conduct includes giving to another person. Paragraph 3.3 now concerns crossing the border; paragraph 4 adds organised criminal groups, twelve to twenty years or life. Providing premises has fine/travel-restriction/prison alternatives. Cultivation retains its separate range.
- [Legalinfo, unofficial English Criminal Code](https://legalinfo.mn/en/edtl/16147365419841): useful for commencement and historical comparison, but the drug provisions omit the 2020 amendments. Do not use the older organised-group range as current law.
- [Legalinfo, amended English 2002 control law](https://legalinfo.mn/en/edtl/16758230735421): articles 3–13, including authorised purposes, manufacture definition, prohibition, education and treatment. The hosted text includes subsequent amendments. Institutional licensing responsibilities in the consolidation are not presented as the exact original 2002 wording.
- [Government cabinet announcement, 7 March 2017](https://www.mongolia.gov.mn/news/view/16381): programme approval, enforcement/prevention/treatment objectives, funding directions, ten-year investigation figures, and convictions for 2014–2015 plus the first ten months of 2016. All figures cover controlled drugs collectively, not cannabis alone. Approval is not proof of successful implementation.
- [Mongolian Great Explanatory Dictionary, ОЛС](https://mongoltoli.mn/dictionary/detail/67735): plant and cord meanings, traditional script and olusu transliteration, seed-oil/textile/tether compounds, and rope/cooperation proverb. These support a vocabulary entry, not a dated origin, clinical efficacy claim or documented religious ritual.

## Corrections and boundaries

Country profile now cites current Mongolian criminal text. Corrected the organised-group range, distinction between sale and unpaid giving, and premises alternatives. Removed the uncited claim that wild cannabis grows in eighteen provinces; the cited statute does not establish that distribution. Consumption prohibition in control-law Article 8 is distinguished from the specific acts criminalised by Article 20.7. The separate administrative penalty for use remains unverified. Generic prescription provisions do not establish an operating medical-cannabis programme.

First domestic prohibition remains unresolved. Kept the existing estimate and scores, but clarified that 1925 is a modelled date rather than a proven national law or accession. No speculative Soviet law was attributed to Mongolia. The 2020 timeline date is the amendment date recorded in the official annotations; a separate effective date was not established.

The original UN-hosted English 2002 law PDF could not be retrieved (web returned 403; direct request returned an empty body). Its existing timeline reference is retained, with the actually read Legalinfo consolidation added. No original-only wording was inferred from the inaccessible document.

The WHO's *Medicinal Plants in Mongolia* (2013) was downloaded and searched. It has **Urtica cannabina**, a nettle, but no Cannabis entry. It therefore supplies no support for cannabis medicinal or ritual claims. The similar botanical name must not be treated as cannabis.

Maren Krings's PhMuseum project *Hemp's Creation of Balance* (2016–2020) was reviewed as modern documentary material. Its ancient Tengri/Genghis Khan claim lacks adequate corroboration and was excluded. No ancient cannabis ceremony was added. No image was added to these legal articles or the lexical entry: a generic scene would not document the subjects. A relevant botanical or textile image can be added when its identity and provenance are established.
