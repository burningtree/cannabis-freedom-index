## Historical interpretation

The museum supplies the object's date and description. Its record does not identify
cannabis residue. The entry explicitly says its original contents are unknown and
distinguishes the Han object from later Shangqing traditions.

[Brand and Zhao (2017)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2017.00108/full)
provides textual analysis of early medical sources. Corrected the Tao Hongjing row
to *Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu* and *mabo*, with a note about uncertain plant-part terminology.
The future-knowledge claim is reported as historical belief, not established fact.

[Censer — Chinese use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censer) and
[Cannabis in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_China) reproduce
Needham's interpretation of *Wushang Biyao* (about 570) and the proposed connection
between cannabis and Shangqing visions. The encyclopedia itself was not translated
or independently checked here; the entry attributes its interpretation to Needham.
His psychoactive-incense argument is explicitly a scholarly hypothesis. Archaeology
at Jirzankal is separate evidence, not proof of Shangqing use or this vessel's contents.
