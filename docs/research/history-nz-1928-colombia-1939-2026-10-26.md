## New Zealand 1928: Dangerous Drugs Act commencement (pop 5M)

New `articles/new-zealand-dangerous-drugs-commencement-1928.md` (416 words,
per-sentence citations, all three sources cited; history:test 40/40,
answers:test 37/37, build 4273 pages).

Sources actually read:

- The as-enacted Dangerous Drugs Act 1927, No. 18, via the ENZS Auckland
  reproduction on the Wayback Machine
  (`web.archive.org/web/2019/http://www.enzs.auckland.ac.nz/docs/1927/1927A018.pdf`,
  added as a second law source): assent 11 November 1927; section 1
  commencement 1 January 1928; section 9 licensing rule (licence from the
  Director-General or other lawful authority, prior offenders need ministerial
  approval); Schedule definition of Indian hemp (dried flowering/fruiting tops
  of the pistillate plant with resin unextracted, plus resin, resin-based
  preparations, extracts and tinctures). The official legislation.govt.nz URL
  from the entry returns an empty 202 to non-browser fetchers, and NZLII
  returns 403, so the archived print is the read copy; both URLs are cited.
- NZLC R122, Controlling and Regulating Drugs (2011): chapter 3 on the 1925
  Geneva Convention adding cannabis/Indian hemp and NZ complying through the
  1927 Act's licensing scheme; foreword on the Misuse of Drugs Act 1975 as the
  later governing statute (used only for that framing, not for repeal
  mechanics, which were not verified).

## Colombia 1939: absolute cultivation ban (pop 53M)

Entry `colombia-restrictions-1939` upgraded from Wiki-only: enriched
description with per-sentence citations, added `related` links to the 1928
first law and the 1949 decree, and new `articles/colombia-restrictions-1939.md`
(377 words; both sources cited; rendered page shows both reference marks).

Source actually read:

- Eduardo Sáenz Rovner, "The 'prehistory' of marihuana consumption and
  growing in Colombia between 1930 and 1960", Cuadernos de Economía, vol. XXVI
  no. 47, Bogotá, 2007 (SciELO English version). Verified: authorities
  noticed growing in 1925 (ports); measures since the 1920s; US 1937 Tax Act
  and Anslinger/Kolb campaign as the pressure; Kolb's "Marihuana: the weed
  which drives you mad" in the Revista de Higiene, September 1939; the
  absolute prohibition (destroy plantations, violators punished as traffickers
  in "drastic drugs"); Resolución 645 of 18 September 1939 (Diario Oficial, 6
  October 1939); Colombia's 1939 drug-traffic report (AGN-FMRE box 120, folder
  940) as the archival footprint. The 1949 decree's recall of the 1939
  resolution is taken from that (checked, UN-sourced) entry per WRITING.md.
