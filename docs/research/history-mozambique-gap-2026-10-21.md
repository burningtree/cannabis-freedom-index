## Changes and distinctions

- Added the 1970 extension, category tightening, tags colonial, sentencing,
  trafficking and visitors. Personal acquisition/possession and non-profit
  supply: up to two years **and** a fine; ordinary unauthorised dealings:
  two to eight years **and** a fine. Article 4 separately punishes harmful
  habitual use/public scandal and encouraging use by consuming before
  others. Do not collapse all consumption into one identical offence.
  Foreign offenders face expulsion without a time limit. Authorised
  industrial, scientific and therapeutic activities are excepted.
- Corrected the 1997 entry and wrote an article. Category **other**, because
  it combines increased ordinary trafficking terms with a much shorter
  specific cannabis cultivation offence and treatment/waiver provisions.
  Tags trafficking, sentencing, thresholds, home-growing and treatment.
  Ordinary trafficking in Article 33 is **16–20 years**, not the former
  entry's blanket 8–12. Small-scale/less serious cases under Article 36
  can be **2–8 years plus a fine**; avoid a universal cannabis gram cutoff.
  Article 36(2) defines minute quantities by one day's personal consumption,
  but 36(1) also addresses substantially reduced seriousness. Its printed
  cross-reference to Articles 27/29 looks inconsistent; the 2024 court
  expressly applies Article 36 to the cannabis-selling facts. Do not
  silently rewrite the law's cross-reference or invent a fixed kilogram rule.
- Cannabis cultivation under Article 34: **3 days–1 year**. Personal
  cultivation under 55(3): **up to one year OR a fine**. Personal use/holding
  under 55(1): **up to two years AND a fine**. Above three days' average
  personal supply 55(2) says **not less than one year**, not "up to one year"
  as the country profile had claimed. The statute supplies no grams in
  this provision; the court discusses individual circumstances.
- Occasional-user waiver under 55(4) has no express prior-record/treatment
  condition. The distinct 55(6) exemption requires minority, no recidivism,
  a promise before a magistrate, and voluntary treatment if dependent.
  Corrected the profile's conflation of these provisions. Article 56
  promises anonymity for voluntary treatment; Article 59 permits suspended
  sentences tied to treatment. Do not infer actual treatment capacity or
  a medical-cannabis programme from these clauses.
- Added the 2024 individual sentencing case as **enforcement**, with court,
  sentencing, trafficking and thresholds tags. The reduced sentence applies
  the existing lesser offence; it is not nationwide decriminalisation or
  a new statutory threshold. The court discusses police-officer status as
  an aggravating factor; do not infer a national police-corruption trend.

## Scores and remaining gaps

All baseline `before` vectors and the current profile scores are preserved.
The existing chart compresses all later penalties into a 1914 drop from
98 to 11. The 1970 increase is explicitly unscored pending reconstruction
of the intervening colonial penalty sequence, including the 1941 rules.
Preservation does not validate that inherited scoring. The mixed 1997 law
needs a fuller sentencing comparison before separate segment changes can
be assigned honestly. The 2024 case is application of existing law.

The 1970 law's continued relevance after colonial rule is directly shown by
its express repeal in Article 93 in 1997. Do not claim every intervening
rule or enforcement practice remained identical. Article 94 gives immediate
entry into force; the promulgation and gazette date is 13 March 1997,
while parliamentary approval was 27 December 1996.

Earlier 1914/1941 original texts and Mozambique publication of the 1970
extension remain research targets. A 2001 Supreme Court appeal invoking
420/70 was located on SAFLII but direct access returned 403; no new event
was derived from that failed retrieval. Search leads to a 1989 fine
revaluation and 1974 amnesties need complete local-scope verification.
An October 2026 law-review announcement is a lead, not an enacted change.
