## Sources read

**RSFSR Presidium decree, 15 July 1974**, complete primary-text transcription: https://www.economics.kiev.ua/download/ZakonySSSR/data03/tex14496.htm . Read the preamble, the rewritten articles 224–225 and new 224.1, 224.2, 226.1, 226.2, and sections 2–7. It expressly implements the USSR decree of 25 April 1974. Article 224 distinguishes possession/manufacture/acquisition/transport/dispatch without intent to supply (up to three years OR one year corrective labour; specified repeat offences up to five years) from supply/intended-supply activities (up to ten years; aggravated cases six to fifteen with confiscation). Article 225 names Indian, southern Manchurian and southern Chuy hemp; ordinary growing maximum five years, aggravated three to eight. Article 226.1 premises offence is five to ten years with possible confiscation. Sections 4–5 amend health legislation and a 1972 compulsory-treatment decree: drug-dependent people evading treatment may be confined by district/city people's court for six months to two years. This is not evidence that compulsory treatment first began in 1974. Section 7 also mentions non-prescribed drug consumption in the administrative-fine framework.

The document was read as a transcription on a legal archive, not a visually verified original gazette. No separate commencement clause was established, so the dates identify the decrees, not a separately proven effective day. A later Meganorm consolidation of the Union decree incorporates 1982–1991 amendments and is not used to project its small-quantity or later corrective-labour rules back into 1974.

**Human Rights Watch, Rehabilitation Required (2007), background:** https://www.hrw.org/reports/2007/russia1107/2.htm . Read the sections on Soviet treatment institutions and changes in the early 1990s. Supports the later account of simplified commitment proceedings, no appeal, prison-like conditions and the 1990 constitutional-supervision criticism. It does not establish cannabis-specific patient counts; none are inferred.
