## Evidence actually read

1. **Trust Territory v Bermudes, 7 TTR 80–90, 23 July 1974**, High Court Trial Division, Mariana Islands District: https://cnmilaw.gov/docs/ttr/vol7/7-TTR-080.pdf . Read the case through the dismissal. Printed pp. 82, 84–87 and footnote 1 on p. 85 identify the challenged law as Title 63 chapter 7, subchapter II, sections 301–303, with health-director regulations. Section 301 used broad effects/abuse language; section 302 let the director identify covered substances; section 303 prohibited unauthorised supply and possession/use outside prescribed treatment. The court found insufficient statutory guidance, equal-protection defects and an unjustified harsher marijuana fine comparison. It did not decide the private-use question. This is the predecessor scheme, not the schedule/recommendation scheme attributed to P.L. 5-110 in the later code. No first-enactment date is supplied by the judgment.
2. **Congress of Micronesia Library, Congress of Micronesia Bibliography**, official library publication hosted by Habele: https://habeleinstitute.org/w/img_auth.php/6/65/6EKWQ8YC.pdf . Downloaded the complete 7,456,972-byte PDF, extracted text and visually checked printed p. 5 (PDF p. 9). It lists Laws and Resolutions, Fifth Congress First Special Session, 23 July–9 August 1974, containing P.L. 5-90 through 5-110. This establishes the session associated with the Act. It supplies neither a precise passage/assent day nor a commencement day. A first partial download timed out; the complete second download is the one used.
3. **Trust Territory Code 1980, Title 63**, archived original scan already recovered in the earlier Marshall review: https://web.archive.org/web/20241223000000id_/https://www.cnmilaw.org/pdf/ttc/t63.pdf . Re-read printed pp. 127–142 from the retained full text and visually inspected pp. 140–141. Sections 251–252, 256, 259, 291–292 and 298 attribute the controlled-substances chapter to P.L. 5-110. Section 256 provides hearings/recommendations and reserves scheduling to Congress, unlike the predecessor law in Bermudes. Section 252 excludes specified mature-stalk products and non-germinating sterilised seed. Section 292 uses one ounce or less, more than one ounce but less than 2.2 pounds, and 2.2 pounds or more: the last category is inclusive and creates a rebuttable trafficking presumption. It must not be paraphrased as only more than a kilo. Section 291(3) treats an unpaid gift of no more than one ounce as small possession. Section 298 allows once-only conditional discharge, subsequent expungement application and a retained nonpublic eligibility record. These are statements about the published edition, without assigning every clause to its original 1974 commencement.
4. **Trust Territory Code 1980, Title 1**, archived original scan: https://web.archive.org/web/20241223000000id_/https://www.cnmilaw.org/pdf/ttc/t1.pdf . Re-read section 151, printed p. 75: coverage is the Mariana Islands other than Guam, and the Marshall and Caroline Islands. Its credits refer to 1966 section 35 and 1970 section 151. This establishes territorial scope, not the date cannabis first came under it.
5. **Innocente I. Oneisom, Marijuana in Chuuk**, Micronesian Counselor 3, June 1991: https://micsem.org/micronesian-counselo/marijuana-in-chuuk/ . Re-read the introduction and law/enforcement sections. The report describes exploratory fieldwork in 1985 and Chuuk's use of former territorial rules. Its rounded ounce/kilo boundaries are overridden by the primary 2.2-pound wording. It reports daily/monthly police discrepancies: 36 daily-report cases versus 56 monthly-report cases and 40 arrests in 1984. These are local records, not national prevalence or complete comparable enforcement series.
