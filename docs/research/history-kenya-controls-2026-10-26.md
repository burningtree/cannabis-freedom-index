## Documents recovered and read

- [Original 1932 annual ordinance volume, Parliament of Kenya](https://libraryir.parliament.go.ke/bitstreams/51c4073c-5e8e-4b77-b74e-0a4731dacf75/download). Downloaded the full volume, extracted the relevant text and read the ordinance on printed pp. 86–97. Visually inspected printed pp. 89 and 95 (PDF pp. 104 and 110). The original cannabis provisions prohibit cultivation and plant possession/dealing, with the stated medicinal-preparation exclusions. Extracts and tinctures are separately controlled under the medicines provisions. Original section 19 allows a Supreme Court maximum of ten years or £1,000 or both, and a first-/second-class subordinate-court maximum of one year or £250 or both. Original section 24 repeals the prior opiates ordinance and its amendments. Do not substitute the later consolidated shilling figures for the original pound amounts.
- [Kenyan government communication to the UN, E/NL.1970/65–71](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1297062/files/e-nl-1970-65-71-e.pdf), issued 10 January 1972. Download succeeded through the `?download=1` URL even though the web reader returned 403. Read the commencement annotation, cannabis and medicinal-extract provisions, penalties and relevant prescription/supply rules; visually inspected the first page. It explicitly records **1 February 1933** commencement. The rest is a consolidated text containing later amendments, including institutional wording from 1956–1961; those later rules are not backdated to commencement.
- [Samson Ndanyi, *Cannabis in Kenya*](https://journals.ufs.ac.za/index.php/jch/article/download/5623/4374/14961), Southern Journal for Contemporary History 46(2), 2021, pp. 4–23. The 1961 date describes the reporting period, not a newly dated criminal statute.
- [DCI official Anti-Narcotics Unit presentation](https://www.dci.go.ke/sites/default/files/2023-11/ANU%20WEBSITE%20PRESENTATION%20NOV%202023.pdf), pp. 1–2, opened and read through the PDF reader. The unit's general webpage had certificate/availability problems; no security warning was bypassed.
- [Original-text reproduction of the 1994 Act, UNODC text mirrored by UNHCR/ecoi.net](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/1184376/1226_1279195588_4c36fb212.pdf). Downloaded all fifteen pages; read definitions, sections 3–7, 16, 58 and 88. Visually inspected PDF p. 6. Cannabis possession shown to be solely for one's own use is liable to ten years; other cannabis possession twenty years. These are the statute's penalty terms, not a claim that every defendant received the same sentence. Trafficking is life plus the greater of one million shillings or three times market value. Use, certain premises and utensils offences: 250,000-shilling fine or up to ten years or both. Growing: up to twenty years or the specified fine or both. Land forfeiture depends on ownership or the owner's involvement; it is not automatic for an uninvolved owner. Medical/professional and licensing exceptions remain explicit. The Act repeals the Dangerous Drugs Act in section 88.
- [KELIN, *Punitive laws and practices affecting HIV responses in Kenya*](https://kelinkenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/punitive-laws-practices-affecting-HIV-responses-in-Kenya.pdf), 2014, section 3.4, printed p. 29 (PDF p. 29). ## Amendment slip and other limits

The scanned 1932 volume includes an inserted, incompletely headed amendment slip ahead of printed p. 89, with handwritten corrections on the original page. That slip expands the hemp/resin wording and adds transit provisions. Its own date was not established. It must not be treated as original wording operative on 1 February 1933. The article therefore uses the original plant, medicinal-preparation, extract and penalty provisions, and cites the later UN text only for commencement.

Downloaded the [1933 annual ordinance volume](https://libraryir.parliament.go.ke/bitstreams/812586c6-31a7-4b5a-a21a-d27ebbd737f9/download) to investigate the slip. Its index and searchable text did not locate the needed cannabis amendment. No amendment event is manufactured from the insertion. A search lead for a Dangerous Drugs amendment numbered 31 in 1933 concerned Northern Rhodesia, not Kenya, and was rejected.

The UN collection's 1968 poisons-list and dangerous-drug rule amendments also do not by themselves prove a new first cannabis restriction. The broad listing and later consolidated rules were not converted into speculative dated reforms.

The earlier Uganda research lead concerns an unrecovered original 1970/1971 narcotics regime; its later surviving professional-law consolidation remains insufficient to reconstruct deleted drug offences. No Uganda event was added in this pass.
