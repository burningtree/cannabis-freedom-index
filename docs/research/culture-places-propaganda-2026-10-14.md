## Research decisions

- Compact lexical entries avoid padding beyond the evidence. Grass's 1932 American
  attestation comes from Etymonline. Dope's broad drug meanings and British cannabis
  usage come from Oxford; its earlier history comes from Etymonline. Wiet's Dutch
  borrowing is supported by the dictionary; product and statutory terminology are
  checked against the Dutch government's own explanation.
- Three Kathmandu Post accounts supply local business history and recollections.
  The entry distinguishes licensed retail from prohibited exports and the 1973
  closure from the 1976 national ban. The 2019 café account is dated, not presented
  as a check of today's opening status.
- The Sorbonne notice for Pointeau-Lagadec's history distinguishes the documented
  Paris gatherings of 1845–1849 from the conventional 1844 starting date. Gautier's
  narrative is literary evidence, not a clinical transcript or reliable membership
  register. The pictured room is not claimed to be the exact meeting room.
- AFI and BFI catalogue *Tell Your Children / Reefer Madness* as 1938; Cornell uses
  1936. The context event follows AFI and openly records the discrepancy. Removed
  conflicting hard dates from the existing Reefer article. No claim that the film
  was a federal production or caused the 1937 Tax Act.
- The magazine transcription and museum artifact support the account of Anslinger's
  propaganda. Allegations are attributed and analysed, not repeated as medical or
  criminological facts. The article is distinguished from the similarly named film.
- Two new events are context entries. No country scores change.
