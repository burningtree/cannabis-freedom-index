## Primary government report, published in 2010

https://www.sgg.cg/JO/2010/congo-jo-2010-03-sp.pdf

Downloaded the full official **special edition 3 of 10 November 2010**; extracted/read the
complete drug-policy section, printed pp. 75–76; visually inspected page 76. The same report
was later circulated as CRC/C/COG/2-4, submitted 11 November 2010 and circulated 18 September
2012. The local gazette is the retained source. An attempted UN PDF download returned an
empty file; no successful PDF retrieval from that URL is claimed.

The report provides retrospective institutional dates:

- Judicial-police drug service created in **1992**, staffed by **two officers at the time of
  the report**, not necessarily two at creation. It updated a register of drug users and
  their addresses between **1998 and 2003** and lacked operational resources.
- Interministerial technical committee created by **94-578 of 25 October 1994**. Objectives:
  prevention, care, social integration, information and research; support government decisions.
  In 2010 it faced major operating difficulties, the national anti-drug project was no longer
  operational, and police/customs lacked training/detection equipment. The report calls for
  reactivation and stronger capacity. The original 1994 decree was not recovered; event date
  and remit rely on the government's full later report, not an asserted reading of the decree.
- **2004 survey** in **Brazzaville, Dolisie and Ouesso**, **239 people** encountered through
  police, customs, prisons and psychiatric services. Bibliography gives the underlying survey
  report as November 2005. The event uses the stated data-collection year, not report publication.
  This is **not a representative national prevalence survey**. No cannabis share, national
  rate, youth-wide statistic or extrapolated headcount is claimed. The report identifies
  cannabis, cocaine and heroin as main accessible drugs and discusses street-connected
  children, young people and former combatants. It gives some broad age percentages, but
  these are not used to claim the sample represents all consumers or children.
- Health ministry/Caritas work, anti-drug clubs, UN-agency support and local associations are
  described. No dated founding event is invented from undated activity descriptions.
- The report calls the older legal protection inadequate and records an unadopted general
  narcotics bill. This supplies policy context, not proof that all drug activity was legal.

The source traces poisonous-substance rules to **12 July 1916**, implementing decrees
**30 December 1916 / 5 March 1918**, and a **29 August 1926** extension to cultivation. It
also cites Penal Code Articles 274–276 for cannabis possession/use. Those original texts
were not recovered in full. The inherited 1926 first-ban score anchor remains a historical
simplification needing original instruments and schedules before a defensible rebase. No
new score vectors or date precision were invented from the retrospective account.

## Child-protection law of 2010

Official scanned text downloaded:
https://www.sgg.cg/textes-officiels/lois/2010/congo-loi-2010-04.pdf

Readable government/UNICEF legal edition held by WHO, downloaded/extracted/read:
https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/law/COG-CC-46-01-LAW-2010-fra-Law-Protection-of-the-Child.pdf

Visually inspected Articles **69 and 123**, PDF/printed pp. **48 and 78**. Read opening
adoption/definition and final signature clauses: signed **14 June 2010**, **Denis
Sassou-N'Guesso**, after National Assembly and Senate adoption. Official gazette reproduction
also identified as journal of 17 June 2010, but the event is explicitly dated to signing,
without asserting a separately verified commencement calculation.

- Article 1: child under eighteen who has not already reached majority by special rule.
- Article 68: worst forms of labour include use/recruitment/offering of children for illicit
  activities, particularly production and trafficking of narcotics.
- Article 69: prohibits inciting a child to consume drugs/narcotics/alcohol and using a child
  in production/distribution.
- Article 123: listed penalty **1–5 years AND 1–10 million CFA francs** for incitement to use
  or using a child in production/trafficking. It does not itself state a fine-only alternative.
  This is the listed offence penalty, not a reconstruction of every general judicial
  sentencing discretion or a claim that a named person received it.
- Article 131: repeals contrary earlier provisions, publication/execution as state law.

Category `other`, because earlier youth-related drug penalties remain unreconstructed;
there is no claim that 1–5 years is necessarily harsher than every former applicable rule.
The article follows the law through the 2025 statute's explicit reference to Article 123.

## Actual pre-2025 enforcement

Full relevant official ACI article body read:
https://www.aci.cg/cuvette-societe-lattache-des-services-administratifs-du-ministere-en-charge-de-lagriculture-interpelle-pour-detention-de-pointe-divoire/

Report **20 November 2024** describes **recent** arrests in **Owando**, not an independently
established arrest on 20 November. A young adult allegedly held **52 wrapped cannabis
portions**; investigators then searched an agriculture official's home and found more
cannabis and an elephant tusk. Suspects were presented to the prosecutor. No weight,
conviction or particular sentence imposed is supplied. Do not combine the wildlife offence
and cannabis allegation into a confidently reconstructed general cannabis penalty. The
report cites **Article 2 of the 12 July 1916 law** for cannabis enforcement, which counters
any claim that the 2025 law created prohibition from an entirely unregulated state.

## Senate debate in April 2025

Read complete relevant official ACI body:
https://www.aci.cg/congo-sante-les-senateurs-approuvent-la-loi-sur-le-controle-des-stupefiants-et-des-substances-psychotropes/

Published **12 April**, reports Senate vote **7 April 2025**. Sponsor **Yves Fortuné
Moundele-Ngollo Ehourossia** links illicit drugs to organised crime, laundering, corruption,
violence and other harms. The report says Congo needed a legal framework following its
convention approvals under laws 21/22-2003 of 31 July 2003. Do not repeat the loose claim
that there was no drug law at all: the 2010 government report and 2024 enforcement account
show continued reference to older law. The entry records a parliamentary stage and is
`debate`, with trafficking tag, not a national commencement or scored law change.

## Full 2025 law and profile corrections

https://www.sgg.cg/textes-officiels/lois/2025/congo-loi-2025-30.pdf

Downloaded ten-page official extract; read relevant full clauses and visually inspected
PDF pp. **6–7**, printed **1336–1337**, including the split Article 53 and Article 61.
Signed **22 August 2025**, **Denis Sassou-N'Guesso**; published **25 September 2025**.
Article 86 provides publication and execution, repealing contrary provisions without naming
an immediate predecessor statute. No independently verified earlier/later commencement
calculation is claimed.

- Articles 3–5: ministerial domestic schedules, linked to international classifications.
  No annex lists a particular cannabis preparation's domestic table in this extract.
- Article 7: narcotic-plant cultivation ban and landowner/operator/occupier duty to destroy.
- Article 37: high-risk cultivation/production **5–10 years OR 5–20m CFA OR both**.
- Article 38: import/export/international transport **5–10 years OR 5–50m OR both**.
- Article 39: general high-risk supply/buying/possession **2–10 years OR 5–20m OR both**.
- Article 40: personal-consumption employment/possession **1–12 months OR 1–2m OR both**.
  Article 2 oddly defines `emploi` exclusively as industrial employment while Article 40
  uses it with personal consumption. This drafting inconsistency is not silently repaired
  into a separately proved universal smoking offence; Article 52 explicitly bans use.
- Article 41: facilitating high-risk use, including premises, **5–10 years OR fine OR both**.
- Article 43: direct high-risk supply to a consumer **1–2 years OR 500,000–1m OR both**.
- Article 52: use outside prescriptions prohibited; seizure/confiscation can occur without prosecution.
- **Article 53**: small personal buy/possession/growing; **2 months–1 year OR 100,000–500,000
  OR both** for high-risk plants/substances including cannabis oil; cannabis derivatives
  other than oil **1–6 months OR 50,000–250,000 OR both**. No gram/plant threshold. A
  separate lower-risk tier is 15 days–3 months OR fine OR both. Its discretionary waiver
  is expressly for people **below criminal majority**, not a universal adult first-offender
  waiver. It overrides **40–46**; despite expressly mentioning growing it does not name
  ordinary cultivation Article 37 in that override. No judicial resolution of this overlap
  was recovered. The article accurately reports its express personal-growing wording.
- Article 54: drug-impaired driving and refusal testing **3 months–5 years OR fine OR both**.
- Article 55: knowing supply to minors **5–10 years OR 1–10m OR both**, without prejudice to
  Article 123 of the 2010 law. No assumption of automatic cumulative punishment is made.
- Article 56 doubles maxima **37–51** for listed aggravations; do not automatically double
  the personal tier at 53 through that provision.
- Article 61: optional foreigner expulsion for **40–54**, including small personal quantities,
  plus residence/profession/driving/property consequences. It is not an automatic mandatory
  expulsion clause for every charge.
- Article 63 allows treatment instead of or alongside punishment for specified cases, but
  has peculiar references **37, 51, 55–63** rather than clearly including 53. No blanket
  treatment alternative for every small-quantity defendant is asserted.
- Articles 65–66: additional 48-hour police custody may be authorised for **37–55**; medical
  checks; night searches restricted to specified drug offences. These support police-powers tag.
- Article 74: convictions **40–54**, prison term **at least one year**: specified early
  release/adjustment options restricted until two-thirds served. No claim every cannabis
  defendant has to serve a year or two-thirds of a statutory maximum is made.
- Other internal errors include 62's reference to 68 and 72's reference to 64. They are not
  silently corrected or used to extend expulsion beyond Article 61's express scope.

The old profile omitted the `or either alone` alternatives across several clauses, even
though the old article partly mentioned them. Corrected the profile and rewrote the article
around practical sentencing distinctions and sourced political context, removing the old
closing discussion of index bookkeeping.
