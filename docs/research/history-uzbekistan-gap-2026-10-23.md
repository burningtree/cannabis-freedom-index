# Uzbekistan: prison terms for hemp inside the 1934–1995 gap

23 October 2026. The longest post-1850 hole on the Uzbekistan timeline ran from the
1934 Soviet cultivation ban to accession to the drug conventions on 24 August 1995
(61 years).

## What was read

The Meganorm reprint of the edict of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the
USSR of 22 June 1987, No. 7226-XI, which rewrites the edict of 25 April 1974
"On strengthening the fight against drug addiction" (Vedomosti 1974, No. 18,
item 275, cited on that page). Article 3, set out as the new wording, punishes
sowing or growing opium poppy banned from cultivation, and Indian, southern
Manchurian, southern Chui, southern Arkhonskaya and southern Krasnodar hemp,
with up to five years, and up to eight years on a repeat or after listed earlier
drug crimes. New article 10.1 punishes other hemp (and oil-seed poppy) with a
warning or a fine of 20 to 100 rubles, then up to three years or up to two years
of corrective labour if the person is punished again within a year, and up to
eight years after a prior conviction for that crime or for articles 1 to 5.
Articles 6 and 10, also rewritten that day, deal with narcotic drugs as a class.
They do not name hemp. This pass does not treat them as a cannabis possession
or use rule.

The Bestpravo reprint of the 1974 edict as amended to 2 July 1991. Article 14,
which carries no amendment note on that page, tells the presidiums of the union
republics to amend republic legislation to match. The hemp sentences in that
reprint are marked as the wording introduced on 22 June 1987, so they are not
dated to 1974.

## What was added

`uzbekistan-hemp-growing-penalties-1987`, 22 June 1987, tightening, tags
sentencing and hemp, unscored. The 1934 entry remains the only scored step
(100 to 6). No Uzbek SSR decree implementing the edict was found, so the entry
states the union text and the 1974 instruction to the republics. It does not
say the Uzbek criminal code was amended on that day. The same edict was not
copied onto other republics in this pass. Russia, Armenia, Belarus and Latvia
already have 1974 possession entries sourced to Wikipedia; those were left as
they are.
