# India: hemp measures in the 1939 League return

23 October 2026. Dated entries jumped from the Dangerous Drugs Act (1930) to the
1946 opium-convention protocol. The Government of India's own report for 1939,
sent from New Delhi on 12 December 1940 and circulated by the League as
C.32.M.32.1942.XI, was read in full for the laws section.

## Added, from that report

- Notifications 5 and 8 (1 July and 26 August 1939) let provincial governments
  and collectors of customs regulate import of hemp in transit. The amended row
  was already "(4) Hemp (excluding charas imported by land)". Recorded on
  `india-hemp-extract-preparations-1939`.
- Notification 10 (28 October 1939) brought preparations made from the extract
  or tincture of Indian hemp under import, export and the other dealings the
  amendment covered, except preparations capable only of external use. It
  amended notification No. 2-Dangerous Drugs of 10 January 1931. The 10 January
  1931 text was not opened.
- Bombay, from 1 August 1939: transport, possession and sale of charas
  prohibited in Bombay and Ahmedabad. In parts of Ahmednagar and Kanara, the
  permit-free limit became half a tola of ganja (5.85 grams) and two tolas of
  bhang (23.4 grams). Hemp-drug users could keep a limited amount on a medical
  permit. The maxima are not printed.
- Bihar, during 1939: transport and possession of ganja and bhang prohibited in
  the Hajipur subdivision and parts of Ranchi, Hazaribagh and Manbhum. Duty per
  seer: ganja 35 to 50 rupees, bhang 3 to 4. No day inside the year.
- United Provinces, during 1939: charas duty up 20 rupees a seer; opium and
  hemp-drug shops cut by about 20 per cent; state management of hemp-drug shops
  in selected areas of eleven named districts. The 1 April date in that
  paragraph is the opium issue price, not the charas duty.

All four are unscored. The chain stays 1896 (96 to 40) until the 1985 Act
(40 to 11).

## Read and not used as cannabis events

- The same report's opium-only items: Ghazipur factory limit, section 31
  magistrates, Chittagong Hill Tracts opium, heroin export draft, United
  Provinces poppy tracts, Madras prohibition extension, Orissa opium rules,
  Central Provinces opium possession and shop closures.
- Bengal Dangerous Drugs Rules, 1939, described as rules for manufactured drugs
  other than prepared opium. The paragraph does not name hemp, ganja, bhang or
  charas, so no Bengal entry was made from it. The Bengal Statutory Rules and
  Orders, 1940, volume 2, was opened and does not contain those rules.
- A commercial reprint of the Assam Dangerous Drugs Rules, 1937, was read. It
  treats medicinal hemp as a dangerous medicinal drug and forbids sale without
  a dealer's or chemist's licence. The page dates the rules "Mar, 11 1939" and
  leaves the notification number blank. No gazette was opened, so no Assam
  entry was dated from that page.
- A 2008 survey of Chinese drug legislation from 1949 to 1998, read the same
  day, lists the 1950, 1952, 1963 and 1973 instruments as opium, morphine and
  poppy measures. It does not name cannabis. No China entry was added between
  1934 and 1979.

## Punjab orders, read 23 October 2026

India Code on nic.in timed out. The same files were then read from
`indiacode.gov.in` bitstreams. These are Indian Punjab, not Pakistan. Both
ganja and charas orders are republications of 9 August 1962. Neither says
"as amended".

Added, unscored. The chain stays 1896 (96 to 40) until 1985 (40 to 11).

- `india-punjab-ganja-prohibited-1954`, dated 23 December 1954, the date on
  notification No. 2525-E&T-54/2606. The Governor, under sections 17(a) and
  24(4) of the Punjab Excise Act, 1914, prohibited import into the State of
  Punjab and possession of any quantity of ganja, "or any preparation of
  admixture thereof". The reprint names no commencement day and does not name
  the notifications it supersedes. Republished as GSR 139.
- `india-punjab-charas-order-1955`, dated 14 July 1955, because the order
  "shall come into force at once" and the notification is No. 1639-E&T-55/1839
  of that day. It cancels notifications 4870-EX (13 November 1935), 4305-EX
  (16 October 1937), 214-EX (13 January 1939) and 266-E&T-50/559 (10 February
  1950). Those four texts were not opened. The prohibition line, checked on
  the page image as well as in pdftotext, prints "classes", then "any
  preparation or admixture thereof", then "any substance obtained from the
  Indian hemp plant and used as a substitute for charas". The word was not
  rewritten as charas. The gazette of 1955 was not opened. Republished as
  GSR 138. The body does not name the Governor.

Read and not entered, because the only text is a later amended republication:

- Punjab Bhang Import, Export, Transport and Possession Orders, notification
  No. 4008-E&T-55/2362 of 3 September 1955, republished 21 August 1962 as
  G.S.R. 156 "as amended upto the 1st January, 1962". Bracketed words include
  "place", "Union Territory or State", and "thirty seven kilograms". Unbracketed
  rules still allow a licensed wholesale or retail seller to possess bhang in
  any quantity, exempt private consumption up to the retail maximum in the
  Punjab Intoxicants, License and Sale Orders, and require a Financial
  Commissioner pass for other import. Not dated to 1955 from this print.
- Punjab Hemp Cultivation and Bhang Permit and Pass Rules, notification
  No. 194-OSD(R) of 23 November 1955, republished 19 October 1962 as G.S.R. 248
  "as amended upto the 31st August, 1962". Districts (1) to (4) are marked
  omitted. The area cap "[0.10 Hectares (one quarter of an acre)]" is in
  brackets. A special licence in form H.17 may go to the head of a temple,
  dharamsala or similar institution where cultivation has hitherto been usual,
  in Ludhiana, Ferozepore and Amritsar. Produce is for the licensee, household,
  and attendants or visitors, and is not to be bartered or sold. Not dated to
  1955 from this print.
