## Sources read and dating limits

### Wooding Commission

https://decouto.bm/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Wooding-report-1969.pdf
Bermuda Civil Disorders 1968 — Report of Commission, by H. O. B. Wooding,
Hugh W. Springer and L. P. R. Browning. Read paragraphs 58–62, printed pp.38–42,
and paragraph 228, p.172. Visually checked these spreads and signature p.5.
The report was signed **24 January 1969**; the government publication statement
is March 1969. The timeline uses the signature date, not a claimed release date.

Paragraph 58 retrospectively dates the narcotics squad to 1961 and the search
amendment to 10 March 1966. Its no-more-than-22 arrests in 1961–1965 are drug
arrests, not a cannabis-only count. The 103 searches from the beginning of 1968
to 25 April found prosecution quantities in 11 cases, traces in seven and nothing
in 85. Four successful searches were at the airport. Of the other 99 searches,
49 were in or near Court Street and five elsewhere in Pembroke. These are also
drug-search figures, not a cannabis-only dataset.

The inquiry criticised searches justified by appearance, reputation or associates
without reasonable grounds concerning current possession by the individual.
It did not call for legalisation: paragraph 228 supported prohibition and proper
application of the search limits. No claim is made that its recommendation was
binding or that subsequent police practice changed. Historical descriptions of
young people are attributed to witnesses or the commission, not adopted as fact.

### Original 1966 search amendment

https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1296275/files/e-nl-1967-08-09-e.pdf
Read E/NL.1967/9, pp.2–3, including new section 25A and assent 10 March 1966;
visually checked p.2. Warrantless searches cover persons reasonably suspected
of illicit possession and vehicles, vessels or aircraft suspected of carrying
drugs. Officers must justify reasonableness. The entry is enactment-dated:
the reproduction supplies assent, not a separate commencement instrument;
the commission independently dates and discusses the amendment's application.

The accompanying 1964 amendment was read but not added. Its penalty ceiling
cannot safely be described as an increase without recovering the previous text.

### Original 1972 Act and 1973 instruments

https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/7640/files/e-nl-1977-10-14-EN.pdf
Read definitions, sections 4–13 and 41–43, regulations on licensing and ordinary
professional permissions, regulation 21, designation order 60/1973 and commencement
order 61/1973. Visually checked printed pp.42–43. The UN circulation in 1978 is
not the date of the instruments. The Act received assent in December 1972 and
commenced in stages; the first order, 32/1973, has not been recovered here.

The designation and regulations start **8 September 1973**. Section 12(4) allows
the designation to remove cannabis and resin from ordinary professional permissions
required under 12(3). Regulation 4 permits ministerial licences. The same day's
commencement order brings remaining provisions into operation except 27(6) and
27(7). Do not claim all possession offences first began on this date, or infer
actual pharmacy availability from a licensing power. No modern seven-gram exception
has been projected back into the original law.

### Original 2005 supply presumption

https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Annual%20Law/Acts/2005/Misuse%20of%20Drugs%20Amendment%20Act%202005
Read all seven pages; visually checked Schedule 7, printed p.6. Cover gives assent
and operation **4 August 2005**. Section 8 inserts 27D: for an offence involving
supply, possession at or above the scheduled quantity raises a rebuttable intent
to supply presumption. Cannabis threshold: 20 grams. Section 11 applies the rule
to cases appearing for trial or sentencing after operation. This is not an
irrebuttable trafficking conviction or a change to the legal status of every
small personal possession. Other penalty provisions have distinct scopes;
no cannabis-specific 50% uplift or universal life sentence is claimed.

A previously used government 2017 reform factsheet could not be retrieved during
final verification (page shell; old PDF returned 404). Removed that ancillary
citation and comparison from the new 2005 entry; its claim rests on the original Act.
