- **Early national Criminal Code**, PRG document 3008032, public page 9:
  complete articles 259–262 read. The edition incorporates July 1998 changes
  and precedes July 1999 changes. Personal acquisition/keeping in article
  259(1) requires a **large quantity** and carries up to three years; other
  manufacture, transport and supply are separately covered. Ordinary growing
  offers a 500–700 calculation-unit or income-based fine or up to two years;
  aggravated growing remains three to eight years. The old repeat-small-
  possession crime has no counterpart in this replacement drug chapter.
- **Commencement Law 168/1997**, Uchet `Z970000168_`: read articles 1–3. It
  expressly starts the new code and repeals the old code on **1 January 1998**,
  requires retrospective review of beneficial changes and closes cases for
  decriminalised conduct. Article 4’s later consolidated dates for deferred
  punishments are not presented as the original commencement schedule.
- **2006 Law 123**, complete CDB reproduction: read article 1(221), inserting
  public-use article 336-2, and article 2. Original penalties are **one to two
  calculation units**, then **five to ten** for repetition within a year.
  There is no detention tier. The law was signed **20 January 2006** but
  declares commencement from **1 January 2006**. The event uses year precision
  and states both facts. This is not evidence that new punitive liability was
  lawfully enforced retroactively before signature/publication.
- **2010 Law 372-IV**, PRG public pages 1–2, document 30912334: read article
  1(3)(4), the replacement public-use offence, and article 2’s commencement.
  First fine **five units**, repeat **ten**, then **fifteen days’ detention**

  publication record 30912337 lists *Kazakhstanskaya Pravda*, **11 January

  The event uses **2011-01**, distinguishing commencement from the December

- **Repealed national Administrative Code**, Uchet `K010000155_`: complete

- **Current Criminal Code**, kodeksy-kz.com articles **10, 79 and 296**:

## What the 1991 reform actually abolished

The law ordered closure of the Interior Ministry’s treatment-and-labour and
youth institutions, release of people held there, release from punishment for
escape or non-prescribed use, clearance of their conviction status and closure
of pending cases. It expressly followed the Soviet constitutional oversight
committee’s October 1990 review. General drug-use penalties were removed from
both criminal and administrative legislation; repeat small possession remained.

Do not describe this as abolishing every form of compulsory treatment. Section
II also preserves a separate judicial treatment power alongside a criminal
sentence. The article states that distinction. No completion date, actual
release count or claim that every cannabis prisoner was freed is supplied.
