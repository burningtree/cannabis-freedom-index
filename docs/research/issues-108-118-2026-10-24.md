## 108 — Nigeria: reconcile the compilation with the amendments

**Resolution: keep `recreational: illegal`. The original amendment texts replace the capital cultivation alternative with imprisonment.** The online 2004 Indian Hemp Act compilation is not a reliable consolidated rendering of section 2(1). The profile now explicitly explains its conflicting death wording and cites the amendments.

- [1975 Decree 34, communicated by the Nigerian government to the UN](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/131681/files/e-nl-1980-91-e.pdf), E/NL.1980/91, section 1(a), replaces everything from “to be sentenced” to the end of section 2(1) with imprisonment **not less than ten years**. Section 1(b) also replaces the import/sale death alternative with at least ten years. Commencement: 24 October 1975. The explanatory note says “not exceeding”; that contradicts the operative text. Use the operative minimum, which is also the wording quoted by the subsequent amendment. The scan was inspected visually.
- [1984 Decree 27, original Official Gazette 55](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ng/1984/ng-government-gazette-supplement-dated-1984-09-20-no-55-part-a.pdf), A553–554, section 1(a), expressly amends section 2(1) **as amended in 1975**. It substitutes imprisonment **not less than 21 years** for the ten-year prison term. It does not insert a death alternative. The scan was inspected visually. It was made 14 September, published 20 September 1984, and deemed effective 31 December 1983 (sections 2–3).
- Section 3(2) repeals the 1975 amending Act. That does not silently restore the original capital language: [Interpretation Act](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=218) sections 4(1) and 6(1)(a) direct that amended laws be read as amended and that repeal does not revive something already out of force. The 1984 operative cultivation amendment independently specifies the new prison wording.
- The [online compilation](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/print.php?sn=202) still prints death or at least 21 years. Its text therefore cannot displace the original sequential substitutions. The profile's death-penalty context acknowledges the discrepancy instead of using the reprint to classify Nigeria as currently capital for cannabis cultivation.
- [Mohammed v FRN (2018)](https://lawcarenigeria.com/abubakar-mohammed-v-federal-republic-of-nigeria2018/) concerns implied repeal of the magistrates' jurisdiction provision by the NDLEA Act's Federal High Court jurisdiction. It does not decide the section 2 death question. The conclusion above rests on the actual sentencing amendments, not an invented wholesale implied repeal.
- The [October 2026 executive bill](https://naltf.gov.ng/senate-receives-executive-bills-to-overhaul-drug-enforcement-framework-and-expand-transport-safety-mandate/) is a proposal to replace both Acts. It is recorded as debate, not an enacted repeal. No subsequent enactment was established in this review.

Also removed the profile’s separate pipe-possession penalty: the 1975 decree section 1(e) deleted section 6, and the 1984 decree does not restore it. The consumption paragraph now cites the gazetted smoking/possession amendment directly.

Corrected the 1975 history entry to its commencement date and primary text, added the 1984 prison amendment, and reclassified the 2024 Senate proposal as debate. The current practical scores remain unchanged: resolving the reprint does not establish new current rights. The old 1966 national score step is inherited and is not asserted to be a fully audited historical vector. Removing death while retaining at least ten years stays in the rubric's severe-penalty range; the 1975 and 1984 amendments carry explicit unscored reasons.

## 109–110 — Indonesia: read the actual 2026 annex

The issue's Kemenko PDF URL returns 404. A complete 260-page gazetted copy is available from the [Bandung regional legislature's legal documentation service](https://jdihdprd.bandungkab.go.id/asset/file_hukum/No_1_Tahun_2026__69df0feee34a76.73084363.pdf). The [BPK registry](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/337869/uu-no-1-tahun-2026) confirms promulgation and commencement on 2 January 2026. The [Supreme Court's analysis](https://marinews.mahkamahagung.go.id/artikel/implikasi-uu-penyesuaian-pidana-terhadap-perbuatan-pidana-0NL) corroborates the amendments; the enactment is the controlling source.

Annex II pp. 1–2: article 111(1), up to twelve years and/or category VI fine; 111(2), life or up to twenty years and/or increased fine; 114(1), life or up to twenty years and/or category VI fine; 114(2), death, life or up to twenty years and/or increased fine; 116(1), up to fifteen years and/or category VI fine; 116(2), death, life or up to twenty years and/or increased fine. There are no four-, five- or six-year special statutory prison floors in these amended penalties.

The [official 2009 BNN text](https://jdih.bnn.go.id/internal/assets/assets/produk/UU/2009/10/UU_Nomor_35_tahun_2009.pdf) supplies the unchanged offence elements: articles 111(2) and 114(2) say **more than 1 kg OR more than five plants**, rendered as at least six plants. Five plants can still meet the independent weight criterion. Article 111 has no death alternative. Article 116(2)'s capital aggravation depends on death or permanent disability, not the plant-count test.

The 2026 law's recast Criminal Code article 622 repeals article 113. Its recast article 610 covers production, import, export and distribution: basic Class I maximum fifteen years and/or category V fine; aggravated plant-form Class I **more than 1 kg OR more than five plants**, or non-plant Class I **more than 5 g**, permits death/life/up to twenty years and/or category VI fine. Profile text now distinguishes 610 from 111/114 instead of citing repealed 113 as current.

Revised possession, cultivation, sharing, capital penalties, seeds and visitor guidance with inline citations. Added the 2 January history event. All six scores remain zero: long prison maximums, criminal use, prohibited growing and supply, and capital trafficking remain within their existing rubric levels. No mechanical score rise follows from deleting a mandatory floor.

## 111 — Georgia: judgment narrower than a current blanket resin exemption

Read the [full Georgian judgment 1/2/882](https://constcourt.ge/ka/judicial-acts?legal=20023) as well as the [court's English release](https://www.constcourt.ge/en/media/news/the-first-board-of-the-constit.html). The operative part explicitly addresses **article 260(1), (3) as in force before 1 May 2025**, for buying/possessing **0.1315 g resin** for personal use, including a prior drug conviction. It strikes imprisonment, leaves criminal liability intact, and takes effect on publication, 24 June 2026. The repeat-possession branch had already been addressed in 2022; the judgment terminates that part of the claim.

The previously added profile text was too broad about which code was invalidated. The profile and new history entry now identify the pre-May-2025 code. Do not treat the ruling as full legalisation, an exemption for all resin quantities, or an amendment of the dried-herb five-gram threshold. Scores and partial status remain unchanged.

## 114 — Mississippi

Read the [2026 state medical board's statutory compilation](https://www.msbml.ms.gov/sites/default/files/2026%20Law%20Documents/Mississippi_Medical_Practice_Act.pdf), printed pp. 88–89, section 41-29-139(c)(2)(A)–(B). First ordinary <=30 g: $100–250; second conviction within two years: up to sixty days (the previous five-day floor was also wrong). The additional vehicle offence applies to an **operator**, for **>1 g to <=30 g** on their person or knowingly kept/allowed in the passenger area, including the glove compartment, with up to ninety days and/or $1,000. The trunk and non-passenger area of a trunkless vehicle are excluded from that additional offence.

For >30 g to <250 g, state the alternatives directly: up to one year county jail and/or $1,000, or up to three years state custody and/or $3,000. Removed the categorical “all above thirty grams is felony” shortcut.

**Enforcement four → two**, a correction to existing exposure: “fines or diversion only” cannot describe the first-offence vehicle imprisonment option. The generated quick answer now permits prison. Possession three stays for the ordinary fine-only small-possession rule. The state's total falls from 24 to 20; no national score changes or invented 2026 legal-change date.

## 115–117 — Michigan, Pennsylvania, Kansas

- **Michigan:** [state judiciary benchbook](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a8168/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/csbb/csbbresponsivehtml5.zip/CSBB/Ch_8_Marijuana/Scope_of_Michigan_Regulation_and_Taxation_of_Marihuana_Act_(MRTMA).htm), quoting 333.27955(1)(b) and 333.27954(1)(f), (i). Residence allowance is ten ounces **plus** homegrown harvest; twelve plants per residence, secured/out of public view; excess above 2.5 ounces secured. Carry limit 2.5 ounces, at most fifteen grams concentrate. No inferred unrestricted off-premises possession. Scores remain within existing legal capped-possession/grow levels.
- **Pennsylvania:** [official Drug Act](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/PDF/1972/0/0064..PDF) section 13(a)(30), (f)(2), ordinary manufacture maximum five years and/or $15,000, with no 2½-year floor. [Van Aulen (2008)](https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2008/s29044-08.html) confirms personal-use growing can constitute manufacture. Its actual sentence was below the supposed 2½-year mandatory floor. The ordinary cap is distinguished from higher bulk/repeat penalties; score one retained, as small manufacture has a lower ceiling than the >1,000-pound class in (f)(1.1). Medical home growing remains prohibited, supported by the existing programme/penalty source, not misattributed to a 2008 case.
- **Kansas:** read official [2-3901](https://kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/002_000_0000_chapter/002_039_0000_article/002_039_0001_section/002_039_0001_k/), [2-3908](https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/002_000_0000_chapter/002_039_0000_article/002_039_0008_section/002_039_0008_k/), [65-6235](https://kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/065_000_0000_chapter/065_062_0000_article/065_062_0035_section/065_062_0035_k/) and [21-5706(d)](https://www.ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch21/021_057_0006.html). Final compliant hemp products <=0.3% THC by weight, subject to prohibited product types. Medical oil defence is THC <=5% **of CBD concentration**, independent laboratory verification, qualifying condition and carried Kansas physician letter within fifteen months. It is an affirmative defence, not a dispensary licence or blanket possession authorisation. Medical none/access zero retained.
