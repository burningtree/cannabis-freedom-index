# Hong Kong: initial legal-history coverage

The timeline had no Hong Kong entries before this pass. Added four checked,

## Original sources examined

### Legislative passage in 1928

Downloaded the Legislative Council's official proceedings for **29 March** and
**5 April 1928**. Read the cannabis-related introduction and objects at printed
**pp. 15–16**, and the later second reading, committee and third-reading passage
at **p. 24**. Visually inspected p. 15 and p. 24 against the extracted text.

The Attorney General expressly said the bill implemented the 1925 Geneva
Convention, would prohibit possession of the Indian-hemp plant and its parts,
and would place medicinal tincture in the drug-control framework. His harm
claims are attributed period arguments. The later proceedings record passage
without committee amendment. Added `hong-kong-indian-hemp-amendment-1928`
using **5 April 1928 legislative passage**, not independently verified assent
or commencement. The actual enacted ordinance and regulations still need
separate inspection; no penalty scale was inferred from the speeches.

### Replacement ordinance in 1968

Downloaded the original scan of **Ordinance No. 41 of 1968**, hosted by Wikimedia
Commons. It is an image-only PDF; empty pdftotext output was not treated as
successful reading. Visually inspected:

- **A207 (PDF p. 4):** assent on **7 November 1968**, replacement purpose,
  commencement by later gazette appointment and the original cannabis definition.
- **A208 (PDF p. 5):** dangerous-drug definition and treaty references.
- **A213–A214 (PDF pp. 10–11):** sections 7–9, trafficking-intent possession,
  personal possession/consumption, plant offences, distinct penalty scales and
  transit provisions.
- **A243–A244 (PDF pp. 40–41):** First Schedule, including cannabis extracts,
  tinctures, cannabis, resin and preparations.

Also downloaded the **9 October 1968** official Legislative Council proceedings
and read the relevant second-reading explanation at **pp. 481–485**. It explains
the objectives of separating traffickers from consumers and strengthening
evidentiary powers while preserving much existing law.

Added `hong-kong-dangerous-drugs-ordinance-1968` using the original scan's
assent date, and a compact article. A later consolidated-law search result
reports commencement on 17 January 1969, but the appointment notice was not
independently inspected. It was not substituted for the verified assent date.
The article does not backdate today's penalty levels to 1968.

### Definition amendment in 1978

Downloaded the official **7 June** and **21 June 1978** proceedings. Read the
introduction and government explanation at **pp. 999–1000**, then the clause
approval and third-reading passage at **pp. 1071–1072**. Visually inspected
p. 999 and pp. 1071–1072.

The Secretary for Security described prosecution difficulties under the previous
definition, coverage of the whole plant and synthetic active ingredients, and
separate transit-penalty changes. These statements establish the government's
stated legislative purpose, not a measured increase in cannabis use. The later
proceedings record passage without committee amendment.

Added `hong-kong-cannabis-definition-1978` with **21 June legislative passage**.
The final enacted text and its assent/commencement were not retrieved, so the
entry does not purport to reproduce the complete replacement definition.

### CBD commencement in 2023

Opened and read the government's 31 January 2023 commencement announcement and
the Police Force's subsequent THC/CBD legal-status guidance. These establish
**1 February 2023** as the CBD extension's operative date. Added
`hong-kong-cbd-controlled-2023`, distinguishing statutory penalty maxima from
mandatory or typical sentences. Did not adopt medical claims from the police
page or infer that THC cannabis was first controlled then. This is unscored
context for CBD, which the current profile already mentions. The full 2022
gazette amendment was not independently inspected in this pass.
