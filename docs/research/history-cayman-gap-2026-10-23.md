# Cayman Islands history gap — 23 October 2026

Seven events fill the gap between the estimated first restriction and the 2025
referendum: the 1973 replacement law, 1977 penalties, 1978 forfeiture changes,
2016 medical-extract reform, a producer's 2017 oil shipment, the June 2025 reform
referral and July 2026 consultation. Two existing referendum/timetable entries
were revised. Two articles explain the 1977 and 2016 changes. Six segment texts
and six context blocks now use inline citations against the current statute and
primary parliamentary or consultation materials.

## Evidence actually read

Downloads, full extractions, source metadata and scripts are retained locally in
`tmp/history-cayman-gap-2026-10-23/`; exact URLs are in `sources.json`.

- The official *Misuse of Drugs Act (2026 Revision)* was downloaded from GOV.KY
  (`law.pdf/.txt`). Definitions, medical section 2A, offences in section 3,
  penalties in section 16, relief/alternatives in sections 37–44, cannabis
  schedule entries and the complete legislative-history table were examined.
  The Act distinguishes statutory maxima from community service/attendance
  options in specified cases. No claim that maximum prison terms describe
  typical sentencing is made. Existing numeric enforcement calibration remains
  a question for a broader sentencing-practice review.
- UN document E/NL.1979/42–45 was recovered from the Digital Library's direct
  download endpoint (`un-direct.pdf/.txt`). The alternative UN document API did
  not return a PDF. The original 1973 law, including offences, sentencing and
  cannabis definition, and the full 1977 and 1978 amendments were read. The
  scanned 1977 penalty/mitigation page (PDF page 9) was visually checked.
- The 1973 text supplies the original five-year first-possession ceiling and
  ten-year repeat ceiling with hard labour. It replaces an earlier Dangerous
  Drugs Law, so it is not evidence of Cayman's first cannabis restriction.
  The earlier law itself was not recovered; the first-ban estimate stays.
- In 1977, offences including cannabis possession below one pound carried up to
  seven years for a first conviction; a repeat conviction carried five months
  to seven years and a CI$5,000–15,000 fine. At one pound or more the first
  conviction minimum was five months, with a repeat range of two to ten years.
  The restriction on specified probation/sentencing powers was read in context.
  The separate hard-drug schedule must not be applied to ordinary ganja.
- The 1978 change concerns forfeiture, including vehicles and aircraft within
  the original definition of vessel. It replaces the first-conviction discretion
  and fixed CI$6,000 redemption provision with mandatory forfeiture on specified
  findings and conditional restoration. An innocent-owner route and a separate
  value-based payment route must not be collapsed into an automatic right to
  reclaim the property.
- Commencement dates for these laws come from the official 2026 legislative
  history: 12 December 1973, 12 April 1977 and 4 July 1978. They are distinct
  from the earlier assent dates printed on the 1977 and 1978 originals.
- The published August 2016 medical-extract bill was downloaded from Parliament
  (`bill.pdf/.txt`); its memorandum and all clauses were read. It is labelled a
  proposal, not passed off as the original final Act. The final permission is
  checked against the current Act, whose legislation history records enactment
  on 24 October and commencement on 18 November 2016. The original final
  amendment PDF could not be recovered from the old legislation server, which
  returned errors. No bill-only additional condition is represented as enacted.
- Cronos Group's full 29 August 2017 release was downloaded from PR Newswire
  (`cronos.html/.txt`). This is a primary company account of its own completed
  Peace Naturals shipment, buyer and pharmacy partner. It establishes neither
  Cayman's first-ever medical import nor patient numbers, benefits or typical
  access. Distributor pages were blocked; their snippets were not used.
- Premier André Ebanks's complete written answer to Parliamentary Question 19,
  dated 27 June 2025, was downloaded (`answer.pdf/.txt`). Its reported 55.64%
  referendum support and promised reform referral replace the old Wikipedia
  citation. The July 2026 paper separately dates Cabinet's referral to that day.
- The election-office results PDF was downloaded but its district tables were
  not fully reconciled. No independently calculated national vote totals or
  denominator claims are added. The percentage is attributed to the premier.
- The Law Reform Commission's full relevant sections 1–2, 4.2–4.3 and section
  10 of its 1 July 2026 paper were read. Its 30 grams dried / 150 grams wet,
  four plants per household and expungement proposals are consultation options,
  not enacted permissions. Reform rationale is attributed to the commission.
- GOV.KY's complete 1 July consultation announcement and the relevant passage
  in its 5 October report of the 2 October parliamentary sitting were read.
  The latter expects a report and supporting legislation for government review
  in November/December, not automatic legalisation at that time.

## Scores, categories and unresolved history

Current scores and classifications remain unchanged: possession 1, cultivation 1,
enforcement 2, sharing 1, consumption 0, access 1; medical `limited`. The 2016
medical event now carries access 0 → 1, reflecting the statutory prescription
and authorised supply route. This removes its projection back into the estimated
first restriction. The first estimated step becomes 100 → 10; the 2016 step is
10 → 11. The 2017 shipment is a trade milestone, filed as culture with patients
and trade tags, not a second score increase.

The 1977 penalties refine an existing custodial regime; a separate numeric
change would require more historical sentencing evidence and a reconstruction
of intervening relief reforms. Its explicit unscored reason records this limit.
The 1978 forfeiture amendment has an unscored reason because collateral property
consequences do not change a whole personal-freedom segment. The 1973 replacement
law is `other`, without treating it as a newly discovered first ban.

Outstanding work includes the earlier Dangerous Drugs Law, the original final
2016 amendment, intervening sentencing-relief amendments and sentencing practice.
The 1977 article links those historical restrictions to the present alternatives
without assigning an unverified introduction date to those alternatives.
