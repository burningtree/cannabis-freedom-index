# The hemp dance: museum illustration and source notes

Installed the original museum photograph of Stanisław Grzegorzak's carved wooden
relief Zapusty w karczmie (Shrovetide in the Tavern), 1984. Collection: Muzeum
Narodowe Ziemi Przemyskiej, Przemyśl, Poland. Photograph: Paweł Husarz.

Museum article by Małgorzata Dziura, 12 February 2025:
https://mnzp.pl/zapomniane-zwyczaje-zapustne/
Original supplied image:
https://mnzp.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Zapusty-w-karczmie-1024x664.jpg
Local asset: src/assets/culture/zapusty-w-karczmie-grzegorzak-1984.jpg

The JPEG is saved unchanged (1024 × 664). No crop, recolouring or generative
processing was applied. `fit: contain` and no cover aspect ratio preserve the
full composition, warm wood and relief details. The caption and alt text match
the requested wording. Credit links to the museum's article. Reuse terms have
not been confirmed; no open licence is claimed.

The artwork illustrates the Shrovetide tavern setting, not a documented
hemp-specific performance. The museum article supplies the Polish chant and
explains agricultural fertility symbolism. The Radom museum supports the
regional Ash Wednesday variation and married women's high jumps:
https://muzeum-radom.pl/wielkanoc/popielec-w-tradycji-ludowej/4059

Frazer's supplied Gutenberg text supports the European parallels on pp. 137–139:
https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59607/pg59607-images.html
Its publication history lists the third edition in March 1911 and reprints in
July 1911, 1913 and 1917; the linked text is not labelled 1920. The entry cites
1911 and notes the 1917 reprint rather than assigning an unverified 1920 date.
It distinguishes hemp-growth beliefs (France, Swabia, Transylvanian Saxons,
Baden) from flax-growth beliefs (Germany and Austria). Existing Benet and
abridged Frazer citations remain for the other customs, including Ukrainian
St Andrew's Eve courtship divination. These are fibre-crop and courtship
traditions, not evidence of recreational or psychoactive use.
