## Dates and factual boundaries

The 1991 and public-use 1995 events use **year precision**. Their descriptions
identify issue/adoption dates, but exact commencement/publication dates were not
verified. The 1995 resolution explicitly says publication is commencement. The
release-order entry uses **15 February 1995**, the verified adoption of the
order, and consistently says parliament ordered release, not that release had
already occurred that day. No publication-date guess becomes a full-date event.

The 1991 decree retains a two-year total ceiling after a court extension for
systematic misconduct inside an institution; its six-to-twelve-month ordinary
period is not an absolute ceiling. It requires public-order/others'-rights
violations in the revised admission wording. The article does not claim every
person who used cannabis was eligible for compulsory treatment or that treatment
institutions disappeared. The general use offence was removed; separate drug
possession and supply offences remained.

The 1995 grow reform is more complicated than a blanket pardon. The new law
retained criminal repetition and aggravated cultivation, including the 500-plant
band and supply-related circumstances. The release resolution covers old
article 229-3(1) and its repeat-offence form in part 2, while expressly excluding
old article 229-9 and specified other article 229-3(2) convictions. Public prose
keeps these separate and avoids calling all growing legal or all growers freed.
No fixed cannabis gram threshold is inferred from this law.

## Partial consumption-chain reconstruction

Before this pass, the chart jumped directly from 1934 to 2024 and projected
modern consumption score 2 across the entire period. The directly compared
statutes now support three intervening consumption snapshots:

| Period / event | Consumption | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before 1987 decree | 1 | Non-prescribed use was an administrative offence; the 1974 text already assigned authority over it. This is a legal classification, not evidence about prosecution frequency. |
| 1987 decree | 0 | Repeated use after an administrative penalty became a crime. |
| 1991 repeal | 3 | Use itself removed from both offences; possession remains restricted. No unrestricted public-use score is assumed. |
| 1995 public-use offence | 2 | Prison exposure for the specifically defined public/group conduct; private use remains distinct. This is the retained modern score, between illegal-use and private-use-only levels. |

The revised 1987 `before` carries consumption 1. The new 1991 `before` carries
0, and the new 1995 public-use `before` carries 3. All other components in those
snapshots preserve the inherited pre-medical vector. Existing 1934 and 2024
`before` objects and the current profile's six scores are unchanged.
Exact totals now read **100→20.5 (1934), →19.5 (1987), →22.5 (1991), →21.5
(1995), →22.5 (2024)**. The chart rounds them to 100→21→20→23→22→23.
The 1987 `unscored` reason is removed because consumption is now scored.

This is a **partial** reconstruction, not certification of the other five
historical components. The initial 1934 step still carries later restrictions.
The 1974 possession offence and the 1995 grow easing remain explicitly pending
in their `unscored` fields. In particular, do not project the 1995 administrative
cultivation tier through the 2001 replacement code and claim it explains the
2009 plant threshold without reading those original rules together.

The current consumption profile's old claim that public use was merely an
administrative offence is corrected to the defined criminal offence and actual
prison ranges. The other profile fields, sources and current scores are preserved.
The previous note's current possession fine, ordinary article 309 penalties and
small-grow boundary issues remain separate audit tasks.
