# UAE 1955–1971 — replacing the 1925 estimate

Replaces `ae-first-restriction-estimate` (1925 Geneva window) with three dated,
scored entries from a read primary-season source.

## Source read

Hashim Abdullah Sulaiman Sarhan, *Drugs Abuse in the United Arab Emirates*,
PhD thesis, Department of Social Policy, University of Newcastle upon Tyne,
1995, Chapter 4, pp.108–109. Local file `/tmp/sarhan95.txt` (PDF at
https://theses.ncl.ac.uk/jspui/bitstream/10443/173/1/sarhan95.pdf).
The chapter cites the British Public Record Office file
`F0371/114755` (Dangerous Drugs Legislation) and the Abu Dhabi Gazette
No.20, 1973 — neither read directly; they are the thesis's citations.

## What the thesis states

- 1950s: drugs trade in the Trucial States expands; Britain issues a special
  Dangerous Drugs Regulation in 1955 (PRO F0371/114755), ten articles.
  Art.1 bans sale/supply except al-Maktum Hospital (Dubai) and American
  Mission Hospital (Sharjah) or under Political Agent licence; Art.2 access via
  approved practitioners; Art.3 no prescription without treatment; Art.4 no
  import/export without permission; Art.5 aiding foreign offences; Art.6
  attempts; Art.7 penalties (max 3 years, 5,000 Indian rupees fine, forfeiture);
  Art.8 drug list: raw opium + derivatives, coca leaves + preparations,
  Indian hemp + preparations.
- The rulers did NOT apply the regulation and were not asked to enact
  ordinances; they preferred Islamic law. The Political Agent and the rulers
  alike held the regulation plus Islamic law adequate.
- 1960s: opium, hashish and qat on sale, especially Dubai and Sharjah,
  "without restriction from the Rulers". Opium and hashish the two most-used
  drugs. A dealer was punished by the Ruler of Sharjah for importing opium
  and hashish from Iran (interview, 17 Jan 1992).
- 1970: Abu Dhabi government issues the first Dangerous Drugs Act — max 5
  years or 10,000 Bahraini dinars fine; qat added 16 July 1973. Applied only
  in Abu Dhabi.
- 1971: Dubai issues its dangerous drugs law; remaining emirates have none
  but most apply Dubai's law (same penalties).

## Scoring rationale

1955 (ban, 100→62): first written prohibition incl. Indian hemp with real
penalties, but no secular enforcement machinery — rulers stood aside and the
1960s market stayed open. A large first-ban drop, moderated for
non-application. 1970 (tightening, 62→44): first locally made and applied
law, but Abu Dhabi only. 1971 (tightening, 44→0): shared prohibition across
the emirates months before federation, with 5-year maxima — the maximal
state the 1995/2016/2021 entries already assumed.
