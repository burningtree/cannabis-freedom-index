## #215 — New Mexico public product limits

Read §§26-2C-25 and 26-2C-30 (2025 Justia reproductions) and both pages of the official [RLD cannabis guide](https://www.rld.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Guide_to_Cannabis.pdf). Ordinary public amounts are 2 oz cannabis, 16 g extract and 800 mg edible cannabis; the profile uses the regulator's alternative-product presentation without asserting additive mixed maxima. Excess private-residence storage must be out of public view. Retained registered-medical and lawful-harvest/moving exceptions.

Section 30(B)(1) names the misdemeanor overages; (B)(2) lists “more than eight ounces of cannabis, sixty-four grams of cannabis extract or three thousand two hundred milligrams of edible cannabis”. The modifier's scope at exactly 64 g / 3,200 mg is unclear. No authoritative judicial clarification was established, so the profile explicitly preserves that boundary uncertainty instead of claiming an inclusive threshold. Amounts above it carry fourth-degree felony exposure. Gifts require adult recipients, lawful acquisition and no financial consideration, with the regulator applying possession limits. Quick carry/caught answers updated; no scoring/history change to standing law.

## #216 — Massachusetts equivalents after April 2026

Read current [G.L. c.94G §7](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94G/Section7), operative clauses effective 19 April 2026, Administrative Order 6 of that date, and Commission [935 CMR 500 effective 11 September 2026](https://masscannabiscontrol.com/document/935-cmr-500-000-adult-use-of-marijuana-effective-09-11-26-pdf/), §§500.140(3)–(4), 500.146(2), printed pp.103–104,127. The current regulations retain a two-ounce combined retail allowance and per-ounce conversion to five grams active THC in concentrate or 500 mg active THC in edibles, yielding 10 g / 1,000 mg at two ounces. Concentrate gross weight is not THC content; mixed purchases share the equivalent allowance. Topicals/ointments are exempt from the daily retail limit. Medical-use rules are separate.

The statute applies Commission product equivalence to ordinary possession and gifts. The numerical conversion reviewed directly governs retail licensees, so the text scopes it to retail rather than inventing an independently verified private-gift formula. Gifts require adult 21+ parties, no remuneration and no public advertising/promotion. Retained ten ounces at home plus lawful on-premises harvest. No existing April-2026 Massachusetts history entry was found to correct; no new score event is inferred.

## #217 — Oregon extract source and product categories

Read ORS §§475C.009, .305, .337 and .345 in the official chapter/Justia reproductions. Checked the Legislature's [2026 amendment/repeal table](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026OrLawAR.pdf), printed T4: it lists other chapter 475C amendments but none to these four sections. Section .305 household exemptions distinguish 16 oz concentrates from 1 oz extracts, with licensed-retailer or authorised medical-dispensary provenance for extracts. Extraction method controls statutory category; product nicknames alone are insufficient. Solid products 16 oz and liquids 72 oz are also included.

Section .337(1)(g) separately restricts extract not bought from a licensed retailer; (3)(c)(C) expressly lists more than one-quarter ounce of such extract as Class C felony. Preserve medical/other authorisations and do not present this as a universal hash/rosin penalty. Ordinary .305 noncommercial gift permissions list concentrates but no corresponding extract allowance; .345 supplies the delivery offence exceptions. Preserved the previous correction: a usable-marijuana gift above 1 oz through 2 oz is Class B misdemeanor. Carry/caught answers corrected; scores and history unchanged.
