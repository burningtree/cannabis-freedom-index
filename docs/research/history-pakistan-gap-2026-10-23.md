# Pakistan: medicinal-hemp rules inside 1931–1965

23 October 2026. The hole ran from the Dangerous Drugs Act in force on
1 February 1931 to accession to the Single Convention on 9 July 1965.

## Read

The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa code reprint of the West Pakistan Dangerous Drugs
Rules, 1958. The heading is notification No. Exp. V/1/1-58, dated 21 March
1959, gazette 17 April 1959, referring to a draft notification of 20 November
1958 published on 12 December 1958. The Governor made the rules under section
8(2) of the Dangerous Drugs Act, 1930.

Rules 3 to 6: a druggist's licence may authorise sale of medicinal hemp; sale
only for a medicinal purpose; possession only up to the quantity on the
licence; import by permit and export or transport by pass. Rule 13: a medical
practitioner may possess three ounces of medicinal hemp for use in the practice
and not for sale; the collector may allow more. "Use in his practice" is direct
administration in an injection, an operation, or another emergency, by the
practitioner or in the practitioner's presence. Other issues from the
dispensary count as sale. Rule 14 keeps a register heading for extracts or
tinctures of Indian hemp, requires lock and key, and a report of theft to the
nearest excise or police official. Rule 18 lets a person possess the quantity
a licensed druggist sold for medicinal purposes. The rules do not define
"medicinal hemp" in the definitions that were read.

Rule 1(2) in this reprint reads only "They shall come into force." No further
day is printed. The entry is dated to the gazette day in the heading,
17 April 1959, and the description also gives the making day, 21 March 1959.

## Not used

The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Prohibition Act, 1938, on the provincial excise site.
That page is the Act as amended by Ordinance X of 1972 and uses the later
province name. It defines an intoxicating drug to include bhang, siddhi, ganja
and charas, and it punishes unlicensed cultivation of the hemp plant. Because
the page is the amended text, those penalties were not dated to 1938.

## West Punjab Excise (Amendment) Act, 1949

Read in the CityLaws compilation of the Punjab Excise Act, 1914
(https://citylaws.pk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Punjab-Excise-Act-1914.pdf).
The footnotes attribute Act XX of 1949, notification 579-Leg., to the West
Punjab Gazette Extraordinary of 14 August 1949, pages 189–190. The gazette
itself was not opened, and the compilation does not print a commencement
clause or the words the new subsection replaced.

Section 3 substitutes the power to prohibit or restrict possession or
consumption of any intoxicant for any person or class. Section 5 inserts the
matching rule-making clause. Section 6 inserts "or consumes" into the offence
that carries up to two years or a fine of up to 2,000 rupees; those maxima are
marked as substituted in 1925, not in 1949. The ganja and charas definition is
marked as substituted by the Dangerous Drugs Act, 1930. Added as
`pakistan-west-punjab-excise-amendment-1949`, tightening, unscored. The 1914
score still stands until 1979.

The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Prohibition Rules, 1949, are described on the same
excise site as notification 895-Exc. of 25 October 1949, with later
amendments. The rules text itself was not printed there, so no entry was made.
