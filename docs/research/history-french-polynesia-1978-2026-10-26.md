## Gap and changes

The largest interval in the existing post-1800 French Polynesia timeline ran from
its unresolved 1925 first-restriction estimate to the January 2023 medical-law entry:
98 calendar years. Adding the 1978 adoption reduces that recorded interval to 53
calendar years, with a further 44-year interval to the 2022 research permission.
These are distances between recorded events, not proof of uninterrupted legal regimes.

Added three checked, unscored events and one 396-word article:

## Primary text recovered and read

The pharmacists' professional council links to Lexpol's record for deliberation
78-137. That index incorrectly displays 13 August; the gazette and Lexpol's own
record establish 18 August. Lexpol failed in the web reader but downloaded directly.

- Record: https://lexpol.cloud.pf/LexpolAfficheTexte.php?texte=201417
- Original gazette: https://lexpol.cloud.pf/document.php?document=189651&deb=861&fin=870
- JOPF 28, 15 September 1978, pp.861–870 (ten PDF pages).
- Read preamble, executable order, articles 1, 17–18, 21–24, 41–43, 65–67;
  visually checked printed pp.861, 863, 866 and 870 against the scan.

Article 18 prohibits import/export, production, commerce, use and possession of
Indian hemp and preparations; its botanical definition refers to female flowering
and fruiting tops. Its research exception requires a government-council order on
the public-health director's proposal. Article 42 in the original medical chapter
already prohibits THC isomers and derivatives, with a research exception. This
claim is therefore not inferred backwards from the 2024 consolidation. Article 66
requires destruction of cannabis seized over an article-18 offence.

The date is explicitly **adoption**, not asserted commencement: 18 August adoption,
4 September executable order, 15 September publication. Lexpol's earliest available
consolidated version is dated 15 September. Neither an adoption nor an executable
order alone proves when each prohibition first became enforceable against a person.
No new score step is inferred from this sequence.

## Remaining larger-gap leads

The 1978 preamble names the **4 January 1924** decree regulating poisonous
substances in the French Establishments in Oceania, a 19 April 1926 pharmacy
decree, a 9 November 1937 substances decree, the 1954/1955 extension of health
legislation (local promulgation 30 September 1955), and the 1970 drug law (local
promulgation order 407 AA, 5 February 1971). These are primary-text leads, not
proof that the 1924 decree was the first cannabis restriction. Recover their
original schedules, amendments and local publication before replacing the estimate.
The 1924 and 1937 instruments are the best next leads for the remaining 53-year gap.
