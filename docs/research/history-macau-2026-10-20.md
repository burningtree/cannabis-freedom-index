# Macau: first history coverage, 1991–2016

Added three checked, unscored events and an article on the 2016 amendment.
Macau previously had no timeline records. The previous goal turn made verified
progress; this pass continues the broader history-coverage goal.

## Sources examined

Used original Portuguese/Chinese official-gazette PDFs. Downloads, extracted
text and rendered pages remain in ignored `tmp/pdfs/macau-2026-10-20/`.
The original images were especially important for 1991, whose PDF supplied
no usable extracted text. The HTML legislation is annotated with later changes;
original PDFs were used to avoid attributing those changes to earlier dates.

### Decree-Law 5/91/M

Read and visually inspected printed **pp. 248–249**, the beginning and preamble;
**p. 253**, Articles 23–25; **p. 256**, final repeal provision and approval;
and **p. 261**, Table I-C. Additional pages were inspected while locating these
passages but were not used to claim a complete reading of the 25-page PDF.

Article 23 covers acquisition/possession for personal use outside Article 11,
with up to three months' imprisonment or a 500–10,000 pataca fine, plus a
separate therapeutic-purpose fine limb. Table I-C expressly names cannabis
leaves/tops, resin and oil. The preamble and repeal clause establish that this
revised an earlier framework; it is not evidence of Macau's first ban.

Added `macau-drug-law-1991` at **28 January 1991 publication**. The PDF contains
no separate commencement clause in the final provisions inspected; no date
was inferred from the preceding, unrelated decree's entry-into-force clause
on p. 248. That earlier clause belongs to Decree-Law 4/91/M, not this drug law.

### Original Law 17/2009

Read the extracted Portuguese text for Articles **7–8, 11, 14, 39–42**, Table
I-C and the reference-quantity annex. Visually inspected **p. 1161** (Article
14), **p. 1171** (repeal, commencement and signatures). The original article
provides up to three months' imprisonment or sixty days' fine, unlike the
later amended text displayed on the consolidated HTML page.

Added `macau-drug-law-2009` at **10 August 2009 publication**, explicitly stating
Article 42's thirty-day commencement delay. Approval on 30 July, signature on
3 August and gazette publication are distinct. No unverified exact Gregorian
commencement day was substituted for the known publication date.

### Law 10/2016

Read the amendment's operative Articles **1–4**, including replacement Articles
**7, 8, 14 and 15**, and the commencement clause. Compared against the original
2009 provisions rather than relying only on the current consolidation. Visually
inspected **p. 3061** (Article 14) and **p. 3065** (Article 4), plus the gazette
publication heading in the original PDF. HTML metadata independently identifies
publication on **28 December 2016**.

Added `macau-personal-use-penalties-2016` at that publication date and an article.
The text distinguishes the thirty-day delay for the penalty/charging changes
from Article 2's next-day schedule amendment. No precise January commencement
date is asserted without separately establishing the local counting rule.

The amendment raises Article 14 from a three-month maximum to three months–one
year, or a sixty–240-day fine. Above five reference quantities, it refers to
Articles 7, 8 or 11 **as applicable**; it is not an unconditional direction to
impose the general five–fifteen-year trafficking range in every case. Personal
quantities are included in that calculation. The article also distinguishes
the original lesser-gravity quantity assessment from a lawful possession allowance.
