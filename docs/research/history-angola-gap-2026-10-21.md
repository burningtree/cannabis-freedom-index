## Date and substance corrections

Law 3/99 was published **6 August 1999**, but Article 46 gives effect
**ninety days after publication**, calculated as **4 November 1999**.
Law 4/99 Article 50 has the same delay. Both record parliamentary approval
on 11 May 1999; neither that approval nor the publication date is used as
the effective-date event. Python date arithmetic checked the ninety days.

The criminal law combines lighter personal-use terms with heavier ordinary
trafficking terms relative to 420/70. Changed the existing 1999 entry from
**tightening** to **other** (mixed reform), added sentencing, trafficking,
thresholds, home-growing and treatment tags, and replaced its general title
and thin description. The ordinary personal-use offence in Article 23
covers consumption, possession, acquisition **and cultivation**: up to
three months, or one year above three days' average individual supply.
It specifies prison, without the fine alternative seen in other countries.
Article 44 delegates daily-dose quantities; the statute itself gives no
cannabis gram cutoff here.

Ordinary trafficking under Article 4 is **8–12 years**; less serious cases
under Article 8 can receive **2–8 years**. Article 9's dealing solely to
obtain one's own drugs has a separate shorter term, unavailable above
five days' supply. Do not conflate that rule with simple personal possession.
The authorised operator who diverts drugs faces a different **12–16-year**
range; 8–12 is not a universal maximum. Article 26 allows suspension with
voluntary treatment/admission and proof of compliance. Articles 24–25
promise anonymity and care for people presenting voluntarily; they do not
show how many treatment places actually existed.

## Added events

- **1970 extension:** category other; colonial, sentencing, trafficking,
  visitors tags. Date is the Portuguese extension-order date. Mozambique's
  or Angola's local bulletin commencement was not recovered; do not claim
  local enforcement began precisely on 26 October. The earlier Angolan
  1940 text's full penalty schedule remains unrecovered, so no directional
  tightening/easing claim is made against it. Under 420/70 Article 2,
  personal possession/non-profit supply had up to two years **and a fine**;
  other unauthorised dealings had two to eight years **and a fine**. Article
  4 separately qualified consumption offences. Article 12 required expulsion
  of foreign offenders after sentence without a time limit. Authorised
  industrial, scientific and therapeutic acts were exempt.
- **1999 licensing:** category other; trade, science, retail tags. This
  regulates already controlled substances and authorised professional
  handling, not evidence that a cannabis treatment programme opened or
  particular growers received licences. Cannabis/resin/oil are in Table
  I-C, so the general Table I controls apply. Read cultivation applications,
  non-transferable licences, separate premises, imports/exports, wholesale
  pharmacists and prescription checks. Article 24 requires refusal of a
  special prescription more than ten days old or already filled. Actual
  cannabis licences, medicine approvals and dispensing data remain needed.
- **2023 case:** category enforcement; court, sentencing, trafficking,
  amnesty tags. A first search found 2 kg; the later discovery brought the
  **total to 52 kg**, not 54. The defendant's inconsistent accounts included
  different quantities; keep allegations distinct from the court's findings.
  Five-year sentence below the ordinary minimum was special mitigation,
  not a new statutory range. Family responsibilities and partial confession
  counted in his favour; no aggravating circumstances were found. The
  final order confirms the sentence and grants a **quarter pardon** under
  the 2022 amnesty law. Do not claim immediate release, an expunged
  conviction or that every cannabis offender qualified for the same relief.
