## Sources actually read

- **NDPS (Amendment) Act 2001, Act 9**, Indian Kanoon primary-text transcription: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1997854/ . Read sections 1, 3, 7, 10, 17, 30 and 41. Section 7 rewrites cannabis penalties: small quantity up to six months OR 10,000 rupees OR both; intermediate up to ten years AND up to 100,000 rupees; commercial ten–twenty years AND 100,000–200,000 rupees, with a reasoned higher-fine power. Cultivation remains separately punishable up to ten years and 100,000 rupees. Section 17 retains named serious offences alongside commercial quantities in the strict bail provision. Section 30 replaces treatment-linked immunity; completion of recognised treatment is required. Section 41 extends beneficial penalties to pending investigations/trials but excludes pending appeals. The site's automatically generated January publication/commencement fields are inconsistent with the Act's May heading and are not adopted.
- **NCB Annual Report 2001:** https://narcoticsindia.nic.in/Publication/ncb-annual-report-2001.pdf . Downloaded the complete 94-page scan. Inspected printed pp. 12–13 (PDF pp. 13–14) directly as images: commencement 2 October 2001, graded sentences, asset freezing, controlled delivery and enlarged search scope. The contemporaneous report verifies the effective day independently of the earlier MyNation footnote. Do not infer the package was uniformly lenient: the article focuses on sentencing, bail and treatment while the report also describes stronger investigative powers.
- **Government ATFP annotated NDPS compilation:** https://atfp.gov.in/assets/pdf/Act/Narcotic-Drugs-and-Psychotropic-Substances-Act1985.pdf . Downloaded/extracted; read section 1 footnote (S.O. 957(E), 27 September 2001, commencing on 2 October), definitions and section 20. This compilation retains the six-month wording; it is not used as a statement of the current small-quantity maximum.
- **NCB Drug Law Enforcement handbook:** https://narcoticsindia.nic.in/DLEA/1.pdf . Downloaded/extracted; read annexure IX, printed/PDF pp. 138–140. Visually checked entry 23 (resin: 100 g/1 kg) and 55 (ganja: 1 kg/20 kg), alongside the table heading identifying S.O. 1055(E), 19 October 2001. The handbook is a later official reproduction, not an original gazette scan. It calls the final column large quantity; the statutory definitions establish the legal commercial-quantity meaning. Those definitions use less than/greater than the specified quantities. No inclusive-boundary or legal-possession permission is inferred from the table.
- **Supreme Court, Basheer alias N.P. Basheer v State of Kerala, 9 February 2004**, complete judgment transcription: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1517833/ . Read the quoted legislative objects, section 41 exception, equality challenge, reasoning, overruling and final directions. It upholds exclusion of cases whose trials had concluded and appeals were pending on 2 October 2001. It expressly discusses the different situation of an appeal filed after that day; the event does not expand the exclusion to every later appeal or claim every defendant's merits were finally disposed of. No cannabis-specific appellant count is stated. The transcription says 9 October for the quantity notification; the official reproduced notification/table instead identifies 19 October, which is retained.
- **Department of Revenue NDPS compilation incorporating 2014:** https://dor.gov.in/files/acts_files/Narcotic-Drugs-and-Psychotropic-Substances-Act-1985_0.pdf . Downloaded/extracted and inspected printed/PDF pp. 19 and 24 as images. Section 20's small maximum becomes one year, retaining the fine alternative; the footnote dates the change to 1 May 2014 under S.O. 1183(E), 30 April. Section 31A replaces mandatory death with a prison alternative or death on that same date; its table includes qualifying hashish cases. Neither change is dated to the March assent. Also read section 9(1)(a)(va), 9(2)(ha) and their 2014 amendment notes: the central government gains rule-making powers over essential narcotic drugs. This preserves the broader medicine-regulation context without claiming that access improved immediately.

For the existing 1985 companion, also revisited its existing Wikipedia pages and the TNI/GDPO 2014 report (downloaded; treaty discussion on printed p. 25). The 2001/2014 law texts now support the revised penalty paragraph, and the federal leaf/seed definition is qualified by state law. Its older political and survey background remains sourced as before; this is not a new comprehensive verification of every 1985 legislative debate or every state excise instrument.
