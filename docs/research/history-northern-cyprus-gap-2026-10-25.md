## Evidence actually read

Research PDFs, extracted text and page renders are retained in `tmp/history-northern-cyprus-gap-2026-10-25/`. Read `docs/WRITING.md` and the relevant maintenance rules in the ongoing task. Applied the PDF skill for reading the gazette and the ministry report; this is read-only PDF work.

1. **Original law:** [Official Gazette 68, 27 May 2016](https://basimevi.gov.ct.tr/Portals/6/2016/68.pdf?ver=2021-04-03-144848-443), printed pages 361–368, PDF pages 21–28. Downloaded successfully even though the web fetch timed out. Read sections 2–7, 12, 14, the temporary provision and the First Schedule; visually inspected printed pages 363 and 368 to check the option and amount table. The assembly adopted Law 28/2016 unanimously (28 votes) on 16 May. Section 14 commences it on gazette publication, **27 May**, not on the vote. The law covers eligible convicted users/possessors before sentence, with a personal-import route and a cannabis/resin schedule figure of twelve grams. It excludes sales/trade, manufacture, export and growing; certain repeat beneficiaries; and specified non-citizens. Treatment lasts one to three years following psychiatric assessment, with testing and supervision. Successful completion means no sentence and no conviction entry. Breach or refusal returns the matter to the ordinary process. Eligible existing prisoners and people with pending cases could apply under the temporary provision.
2. **2017 implementation interview:** [Nadire Bahadi’s Havadis report, republished by Haber Kıbrıs, 20 January 2017](https://haberkibris.com/denetimli-serbestlik-yasasi-hayal-kirikligi-oldu---2017-01-20.html). Opened and read the full relevant interview. Lawyer and Eastern Mediterranean University lecturer Ramadan Sanıvar describes psychiatric/rehabilitation shortages, disruption after a laboratory fire, delayed paperwork and staffing/procedure concerns. These are attributed contemporary criticisms, not findings that the law was repealed or universally unavailable. Filed as debate because it is criticism and proposed improvements, not a verified new legal change. Public coverage of this source is kept compact; the article also explains the actual statutory obligations and later official reporting.
3. **2019 implementation:** [Labour and Social Security Ministry, October 2018–September 2019 activity report](https://csgb.gov.ct.tr/Portals/28/CSGB%202019.pdf), PDF page 60. Downloaded and text-extracted; rendered and visually checked the complete page. It reports regular interviews and psychosocial support for **98** people benefiting from the law. That is a count receiving interviews, not a number who completed treatment, and no separate cannabis count is given. The report refers to 16 May 2016 as commencement; the original gazette overrides that shorthand, so this batch uses 27 May for the legal step. The entry uses 2019 because an exact report-publication day or interview-snapshot day was not verified.
4. **Continued operation in 2025:** [Kamalı Haber via MYKıbrıs, 30 June 2025](https://www.mykibris.com/kktc/haber/merkezi-cezaevindeki-776-kisinin-729u-erkek-57637). Opened the full page and read its supervised-release section: board member Mustafa Hidayet reported **42 beneficiaries** from 18 September 2023 to 30 June 2025, and **36** processes continuing. The report concerns the Nicosia judicial-year closing session; neither national coverage nor a cannabis-only count is asserted. The two numbers are not summed, and “benefited” is not reworded as successful completion. This evidence also supports keeping the treatment route in the present profile.

The North Cyprus UK and Giynik versions of the 2025 court report were located but subsequent fetches failed before the relevant section was returned. The final citation therefore uses the MYKıbrıs full page actually read, not search snippets. Several news/official court pages return 403 to scripted retrieval; successful web reads and downloaded official PDFs are distinguished from failed fetches.

## Limits and next work

A **2017 appeal** (Yargıtay/Ceza 40/2017, D.9/2017, 21 June) was located in a commercial republication. Its accessible part describes a synthetic-cannabinoid import case; later text is subscription-locked, while the official court site was blocked. Search snippets discuss how the schedule interacts with possession and use, but are not a sufficient basis to claim the court’s holding verified. No new court entry or assertion of that holding is added. Retrieve the full authoritative judgment before presenting twelve grams as a universal eligibility cap. In this batch, twelve grams is specifically the statute’s scheduled figure in the personal-import provisions, with further assessment above it; it is never described as a lawful allowance.

The original **1972 drug legislation**, later penalty amendments and the exact present prison maxima still need a linked audit. The 2016 law proves the criminal categories and the treatment route, but does not by itself provide all underlying penalty ranges. The inherited **1925 first-ban allocation** also needs segment-by-segment historical reconstruction. A structurally continuous 1925 → 2016 graph is not evidence that the present pre-reform vector began in full in 1925. None of those earlier sources is newly certified by this batch.

The ministry’s 98 and the court-session 42/36 describe different periods, institutions and measures. They are not a trend series or a treatment success rate. The 2017 criticism is retained as a contemporary report, without using it to negate the later evidence that people received support.
