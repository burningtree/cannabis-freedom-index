# Afghanistan: fill the republic-era legal gap

21 October 2026. Rechecked writing rules, categories and tags. Baseline:
`tmp/history-afghanistan-gap-2026-10-21/changes-before.yaml`. The timeline
had no domestic legal entries between the 1973 crackdown and the 2023

## 2005 government text separates consumption, trafficking and growing

Downloaded UNODC's actual English reproduction, E/NL.2009/20, of the
government-communicated **Counter Narcotics Law, 17 December 2005**.
Retained `law-2005.pdf` and text. Read articles 1–3, 15–16, 25–27,
58 and the schedules. Visually inspected printed/PDF 19–20, including
the personal-consumption threshold and medical exemption.

Article 27(1)(c): unauthorised personal use/possession of the residual
controlled-drug group, including cannabis, brings one to three months
AND 5,000–10,000 afghanis. Article 27(1)(d) sends possession of **more
than ten grams of hashish** to article 16. Article 16(3)'s residual
trafficking scale includes up to three months AND a fine below 250 grams,
one to three years AND a fine at one to five kilograms, and ten to
fifteen years AND a fine above ten kilograms.

Article 26(3): less than one jerib of cannabis growing brings three to
nine months AND 5,000–20,000 afghanis. Larger areas add punishment.
Illicit crops must be destroyed without compensation; financing or
inciting cultivation doubles the farmer's penalty. Article 27(2) lets a
court exempt a medically certified dependent person from imprisonment
and fine, and require treatment. Do not call ten grams a legal allowance.

Article 58 commences on presidential signature. The communicated text
is dated **17 December 2005**, used for the new event. Official Gazette
875 is also described elsewhere as dated 15.11.1384 (February 2006).
That publication reference must not be confused with the express
signature-based commencement. The 2010 law independently identifies
Gazette 875 as its repealed predecessor.

Added an `other` event and article: the source establishes the replacement
law's substance but not a net direction relative to all earlier rules.
Tags sentencing, thresholds, treatment and eradication.

## 2010 gives hashish a lower ordinary trafficking scale

Recovered both the WTO-hosted Dari gazette and UNODC's actual English
translation, `law-2010-dari.pdf`, `law-2010-english.pdf` and text. The
English footer identifies Official Gazette **1025, 14 June 2010** and
its translation from the approved Dari text. Read articles 41, 46–47,
57–58 and 67; visually inspected PDF 18 and 22. Article 67 commences
on publication and repeals the Gazette 875 law.

Article 46 expressly separates hashish from other drugs. Less than ten
grams: up to a month; over ten to 100 grams: over one to two months;
over 100 to 500 grams: over two to three months; over 500 grams to one
kilogram: over three to six months; over one to five kilograms: over
six months to a year. Above five kilograms adds three months per extra
500 grams, up to ten years for this ordinary offence. Article 58 doubles
maximum punishment for directing more than two people, subject to a
twenty-year ceiling. Do not describe ten years as the ceiling on every
aggravated hashish case.

Compared directly with article 16(3) of the earlier law, a two-kilogram
ordinary case falls from one to three years plus a fine to over six
months to one year. Added an easing event and article, tags sentencing,
thresholds and treatment. Numerical scoring remains explicitly pending:
the existing 1973-to-Taliban score sequence does not document the republic
period adequately. No invented six-segment vector was assigned.

Article 57(1) exempts a user/holder of up to fifty grams of hashish for
personal consumption from prosecution if a medical examiner certifies
dependence, and directs the person to a treatment centre. Above the
quantity, certified dependence can lead to treatment detention while
punishment remains available. Reports every fifteen days inform court
decisions; detention time counts against later custody. Do not present
this as an exemption for every consumer or wholly voluntary care.

The English cultivation clauses mix 'acre', 'jerib' and a contradictory
beswa conversion. No comparison of exact plot sizes was published from
that translation; reconcile the original before asserting hectares or
acres. The article focuses on the clear gram/kilogram provisions.

## Correct the survey's product and publication date

Downloaded the actual UNODC/Afghan Ministry of Counter Narcotics
*Afghanistan Cannabis Survey 2009*, retained as `survey-2009.pdf` and
text. Read the preface, key findings, production and cultivation-reasons
sections; visually inspected PDF 11, printed p. 7, the key findings.

It estimates **10,000–24,000 hectares** and **1,500–3,500 tonnes of resin**
for 2009 and calls Afghanistan the largest cannabis-resin producer. This
is not a ranking of all cannabis herb. High prices and low input costs
are reported reasons for cultivation. Replaced the old Wikipedia summary
and removed its unsupported $47-million/1973 historical story. Set the
entry to **April 2010**, the report's printed publication month; the
previous exact 31 March announcement date was not independently recovered.
Tag science replaces military. No article added from this report yet.

## Further gaps and limits

The 2003 classification/drug law is catalogued by SHERLOC, but the
original was not recovered in this pass. The 2005 repeal refers to an
earlier Gazette 813 law. Do not merge calendar references or assign a
precise 2003/2004 date without the actual text.

The 1973 entry still relies on a weak claim that the king first outlawed
production and on unsupported funding detail repeated in secondary
accounts. Earlier restrictions already exist in the timeline; that
entry and the 1972 crackdown's category need a dedicated primary-source
pass. FRUS documents 350/372 establish older prohibitions and growing
enforcement commitments, but alone do not establish an exact 1973 law.
The 2017 Penal Code, post-2021 legal practice and Taliban decrees also
need a coherent chronology before historical score reconstruction.
Current country scores and every baseline `before` vector are preserved,
without claiming those older vectors have been validated.
