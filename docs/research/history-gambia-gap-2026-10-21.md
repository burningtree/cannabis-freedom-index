## Important correction: the 2014 enacted scale differs from news reports

Recovered the **complete four-page enacted amendment**, Act 9/2014, from
the government's security-legislation archive. Read all four pages using
local Vision OCR, then visually checked PDF pp. **2–3**, containing the
complete scale and certification. This is the printed enacted text,
not an August bill summary. Assent/bracket date **9 October 2014**;
Gazette supplement **16 October**; parliamentary passage **25 August**.

For first-time cannabis possession, section 5 inserts a new section 35(2):

| Quantity, literally printed | Fine (GMD) | Prison |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1–150 g | 50,000–100,000 | **1–2 years** |
| 151–500 g | 100,000–150,000 | **2–5 years** |
| 501 g and above | 150,000–250,000 | **3–10 years** |

**Each band allows a fine OR prison OR both.** Do not publish the former
six-month–one-year / one–two-year / two–three-year scale as enacted law.
Those figures occur in The Point and JollofNews's **27 August 2014** reports
of passage. Both reports were read in full. They are retained as evidence
of Ousman Sonko's reasons: reducing youth imprisonment, burdensome fines,
prison overcrowding and public expense. No explanation for the tariff
conflict was recovered; the enacted text takes priority. Do not speculate
that a particular drafting stage or amendment caused the discrepancy.
No fractional-gap interpretation is invented between the literally printed
150/151 g and 500/501 g bands.

The Act also renames the agency, changes Board meeting frequency, extends

amendment as a lighter penalty.

## Primary 2003/2005 consolidated law

Recovered **90 scanned pages**, *Drug Control Act*, Chapter 13:05,
**Issue 1/2009**, Act 15/2003 amended by Act 11/2005. Native extraction is
empty; ran English Vision OCR. Read the relevant commencement, definitions,
agency-establishment provisions, sections 31, 33–37, 43–44 and 139, and
First Schedule passages. Visually checked PDF **29** / printed **31**
(possession and shared premises) and PDF **35** / printed **37** (quantity
presumption). This is **not an unamended 2003 original**. Footnoted 2005
changes are not silently attributed to initial enactment.

https://ons.gov.gm/security-legislation/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/DROG-CONTROL-ACT.pdf

Commencement **3 October 2003**; section 139 expressly repeals Decree
80/1996 and has transitional prosecution rules. The article dates the
replacement law, then explicitly labels detailed tariffs as the published
law incorporating the 2005 amendments. Category other replaces the vague
unsupported tightening claim because the predecessor's precise penalty
comparison was not recovered.

Cannabis/resin are prohibited drugs in the First Schedule. Definitions
cover plant, resin, extracts and tinctures. Section 33 broadly prohibits
unauthorised production, growing, supply, transport, acquisition, use and
possession, with tightly limited authorisation for approved persons in
medical/scientific establishments. These general authorisations do not
establish an operating patient programme.

Section 35, published text: First/Second Schedule possession without valid
licence **fine ≥250,000 OR prison ≥3 years OR both**. Shared-premises rule
presumes joint possession unless knowledge/consent is disproved. Do not
convert a statutory minimum into a fixed three-year sentence.

Section 36 cultivation: Second Schedule tariff **fine ≥50,000 OR prison
≥3 years OR both**. The 2014 addition of First Schedule and prohibited-drug
wording makes this clause expressly cover cannabis. **Section 33 separately
covers growing prohibited drugs with a ≥250,000 fine / ≥3-year prison
alternative.** Their overlap and actual charging practice require cases.
The profile identifies the tariff as the cultivation provision's penalty,
not a guarantee that no other provision may apply. No home-growing right,
commercial hemp exemption, viable-seed status or new medical programme is
inferred. The research did not establish later changes beyond the recovered
amendments; avoid asserting a complete 2026 consolidation.

## 2010/2011: cannabis fine increase, separate cocaine/heroin capital clause

Recovered complete four-page **Act 16/2010**, assented **29 October**,
passed **4 October**, and complete two-page **Act 2/2011**, assented/bracket
dated **29 April**, passed **4 April**, gazetted **24 May**. Read both
complete OCR texts and visually checked PDF p. **2 of each**.

https://ons.gov.gm/security-legislation/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Drug-Control-Amendment-Act-2010.pdf
https://ons.gov.gm/security-legislation/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Drug-Control-Amendment-Act-2011.pdf

2010 inserts “pursuant to subsection (4)” in general trafficking section
43(1), and creates section 43A. **43A(1) concerns >250 g cocaine/heroin**, not
cannabis. It originally required death. **43A(2) concerns 100–250 g cocaine/
heroin**: one-million fine with a 15-year default AND 25–75 years without
fine option. The 2011 amendment changes 43A(1) death to **life** and
43A(2)(a) fine to **fifty million** (not ten million).

The cannabis-relevant change is section 43(1)'s **minimum fine one million
→ ten million**, alongside the existing **≥10-year prison term**, using
section 43(4)'s threshold of **>10 g resin OR >2 kg cannabis**, rebuttable
by the accused. The 2005 revision marks the threshold subsection amended.
Do not describe the 2011 change as abolishing a cannabis death penalty.
Do not claim 2 kg is a lawful personal allowance or that smaller holdings
can never trigger a separate supply or other offence.

Reframed the existing 2011 entry as **tightening**, with title about the
cannabis-relevant fine. Stable ID `gambia-death-penalty-abolished-2011`
retained for existing links. Date now exact assent/bracket **29 April**.
The same amendment's cocaine/heroin change remains attributed context.
An explicit unscored reason avoids inventing a personal-user score effect.
The old pre-2014 enforcement 0 can denote long sentences, not only death;
no inference of a cannabis death sentence is derived from that score.

Read The Point's **6 April 2011** direct parliamentary report for Gomez's
deterrence argument and constitutional context; read the Library of Congress
article in full. The latter relies on contemporaneous reporting and
conflates the fine increase when describing the cocaine/heroin offence:
use the enacted text for the distinct **10 million / 50 million** clauses.

## Institutional gaps and the later reform proposal

Downloaded the agency's *Five-Year Strategy Plan 2019–2023*, copyright
**2019**. Read the relevant passages on printed pp. **13–17**, **33–34**, **37–38**
and **44**, using native extraction. It dates the police Drug Squad to
late **1980**, replacement by National Drug Squad in **1991**, 1993 law,
1996 decree, **2005 autonomous NDEA**, and later renaming. The 2005 agency
is also corroborated by the consolidated Act's amendments/sections 4–5.
The new context entries are unscored: 1980 squad, 1996 decree, 2005 agency.
Do not repeat the report's “first ever legal framework” wording for 1993:
it conflicts with the already documented reference to the 1935 ordinance.
The original 1993 and 1996 texts remain unread; no new penalty is invented.

The 2019 plan proposes community service, counselling, skills and
rehabilitation facilities, and reports limited help during/after prison.
A new **debate** entry records those proposals, with treatment/youth/
sentencing tags. It is not evidence that community service or a treatment
programme actually started that year.
