# Hong Kong: the gap after 1978

Research date: 23 October 2026. Before this pass, Hong Kong had four history
entries, with no event between the 1978 definition amendment and the 2023 CBD
listing. This batch adds nine events, revises three existing descriptions and
creates four articles. It expands the profile's sentencing account, corrects the
description of the first CBD imprisonment and converts revised blocks to inline
citations. Current scores and status classifications are preserved. All twelve profile text
blocks now use inline citations.

## Primary sources read

- **Legislative Council, 3 June 1987**, printed pp. 1696–1697: written answer
  announcing more cannabis/mandrax publicity and wider secondary-school talks.
  The 600 talks and 200,000 pupils concern the whole first cycle of school talks,
  not a cannabis-only programme. The event records an announcement, category
  `debate`, not a new criminal rule or completed rollout of every proposal.
- **Legislative Council, 26 February 1992**, relevant drug-amendment introduction
  at PDF pp. 55–57: rights rulings, removal/narrowing of presumptions and proposed
  possession penalties.
- **Legislative Council, 24 June 1992**, relevant second-reading discussion at
  PDF pp. 80–84, committee amendments at pp. 111–112 and third-reading passage
  at p. 130. Read the government's original proposal, Professor Felice Lieh Mak's
  committee account, the adopted changes to clauses 2 and 4 and final passage.
  The summary-case fine rose to **HK$100,000**, rather than the government's
  initial plan to retain HK$10,000. The indictment maximum became seven years
  and HK$1 million; summary custody remained three years. Removing the separate
  trafficking-intent possession section did not legalise possession for supply:
  that conduct was moved into the trafficking definition.
- **Legislative Council, 20 June 2001**, bilingual printed p. 199: an official
  commencement table gives **26 June 1992** for the amendment. The table and
  the penalty discussion at PDF p. 82 of the 1992 sitting were rendered and
  visually checked. The original gazetted 1992 amendment was not recovered;
  the entry combines final parliamentary proceedings with the government's
  later commencement record. The 1994 sitting was downloaded as corroboration
  but was not needed as a public source.
- **R v Tuen Shui Ming and another**, 9 June 1995, full judgment read, especially
  paragraphs 1–4 and 10–20. More than **51 kg**, not 5 kg: the judgment itself
  discusses the original judge's mistaken wording. The two sentences were raised
  from two years to four. Weight was the initial basis, with limited potency
  adjustment; the lowest bands became one band below 2 kg. The court retained
  different treatment for herbal cannabis, resin and oil and refused a wholesale
  upward recasting of the tariff.
- **HKSAR v Phung Hai**, 17 February 2000, full short judgment read: personal
  possession of 1.18 g; two earlier drug convictions; six months reduced to two;
  prosecution did not defend the original term. A single outcome does not
  establish the typical sentence for all repeat offenders.
- **HKSAR v Chor Lui**, 3 August 2001, full judgment read: two cigarettes,
  0.24 g, HK$30 undercover purchase, previous convictions, commercial context,
  mitigation, plea reduction and dismissed appeal. The four-month herbal
  benchmark was an explanation of the then-current approach, not a statutory
  maximum or a guarantee of leniency.
- **HKSAR v Nguyen Thang Loi and Dang Hung Ngoc**, 20 January 2023: read case
  facts and sentencing reasoning at paragraphs 1–29 and 94–109, with relevant
  historical/submission passages. The full judgment was recovered, not merely
  the official three-page summary. The Department of Justice summary was also
  read in full and its cultivation table visually checked. The guideline bands
  concern starting points and individual culpability; they are not mandatory
  sentences, possession allowances or plant limits. Estimates for growing
  operations concern annual production, not just the cannabis present on the day.
- **Security Bureau legislative brief, 20 October 2022**, paragraphs 6–11:
  CBD market, the government's contamination/conversion rationale, prescription
  rules since **13 December 2019**, and absence of registered CBD pharmaceutical
  products at the date of the brief. Chemical/medical statements are attributed
  to the government's explanation, not adopted as independent efficacy findings.
- **Government announcements, 30 November 2021 and 31 January 2023**, read in
  full: THC prohibited regardless of concentration before CBD itself was listed;
  1 February 2023 commencement; about 77,400 surrendered items by 29 January.
- **Information Services Department, 7 July 2023**, read in full: first CBD-related
  imprisonment, **two months overall for CBD, ketamine and equipment counts**.
  The report supplies no separate CBD-only sentence. March 29 is arrival/arrest,
  not the sentencing date.
- **Hong Kong Police laws/penalties page**, **HKU CLIC possession sentencing
  page**, and **17 March 2026 government statistics release**, read in full for
  the revised profile blocks. The 4,108 arrests and 86% conviction rate cover
  drugs generally, not cannabis alone. Registry reports are not a population
  prevalence survey.

## Retrieval and provenance

HKLII judgment URLs initially return a JavaScript shell to a simple reader.
The site's own application bundle documents its public `getjudgment` API;
that returned the original judgment body and date for all four cases. Both raw
JSON and extracted text are retained locally. Public citations point to the
normal HKLII case pages. A vLex reproduction of the 1995 judgment was retrieved
as a lead, then superseded by the HKLII original. Direct Judiciary retrieval
timed out; it was not mistaken for a read source.

LegCo downloads failed ordinary local certificate validation, then succeeded
for these public documents using an unverified SSL context. Their domain,
document titles, contents and relevant page images were checked. Browser
search snippets were used as leads, not as substitutes for reading the PDFs.

The same public API supplied the current ordinance consolidation dated 12 June
2026. Read sections 4, 8, 9, 22, 24, 25 and 54A and the relevant First Schedule
Part I entries. Section 22(6) excludes paragraphs 8–11 from ordinary professional
and pharmacy authorities; paragraph 11 expressly lists cannabis and resin. The
profile therefore replaces a broad professional-exemption description with the
actual exclusion and licence requirement. The CBD pharmaceutical text is explicitly
dated to the 2022 report; no operating 2026 patient programme is inferred from
general controlled-medicine powers. The original current status flag is preserved
pending specific patient-product/availability evidence. Legacy commentary and
VOA references were replaced by read statutory and government sources.

The API also exposes earlier ordinance versions, a useful lead for reconstructing
treatment-report rules and the older score chain in a later pass. Only the sections
and versions actually inspected in this batch are treated as read.

Research files live under `tmp/history-hong-kong-gap-2026-10-23/`. No complete
judgment or parliamentary scan was copied into public article assets.

## Classification and score decisions

- **1992: `other`**, because higher maxima and weaker prosecution presumptions
  are a mixed reform. The explicit unscored reason preserves the criminal
  possession/personal-enforcement segment levels; the sources do not establish
  a new typical personal-use disposal deserving a whole-point movement.
- **1995: `other`**, because the review combined full-weight sentencing, limited
  low-potency adjustment and wider discretion in the lowest band. It doubled
  these defendants' sentences but did not simply increase every cannabis tariff.
- **2000 and 2001: `enforcement`**, individual sentencing outcomes under existing
  rules; no general decriminalisation or new possession offence is claimed.
- **2019: `medical`**, a CBD pharmaceutical classification, with an explicit
  unscored reason. The brief reports no registered CBD medicine in October 2022
  and establishes no operating THC-cannabis supply route. Current `medical: none`
  and access zero are preserved; this entry does not imply a functioning patient
  programme or certify availability in 2026.
- **2021: `debate`**, a government warning about the existing THC rule while CBD
  remained outside dangerous-drug controls; no CBD ban is backdated to it.
- **January 2023: `tightening`**, ending the lower herbal trafficking scale and
  setting cultivation bands. Sharing and cultivation are already zero; the
  ordinary possession segment is not changed by these tariffs.
- **July 2023: `enforcement`**, a combined-offence sentence, not a further legal
  tightening or proof that every CBD possession case receives custody.

All pre-existing history score vectors and current profile scores are unchanged.
The old 1928 first step remains a coarse historical baseline requiring further
penalty reconstruction; this pass does not claim to reconstruct every score
between 1928 and today. The 1968 and 1978 dates remain verified assent and
legislative passage respectively, rather than invented commencement dates.
