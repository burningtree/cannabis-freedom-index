## Corrections and the score timeline

The 1934 event now uses **1935**, the decree’s express commencement year. Its
title and account identify Indian hemp and the medical/scientific exception.
Removed the false assertion that Kyrgyzstan was already a Soviet union republic
in 1934; it was then autonomous within the RSFSR. Its existing unregulated
`before` vector is unchanged. The old broad allocation of other segment drops
to this first step still needs reconstruction from Kyrgyz republican codes and
earlier drug legislation; this pass does not certify that all modern restrictions
began with the Indian-hemp decree.

The former 2019 description incorrectly presented the **old** first/second/third
offence sequence from AFEW’s pre-reform interview as the new code’s rules. It
also omitted the actual misdemeanour code. The corrected event follows articles
123–124: category-II fine and category-I restriction of liberty are listed for
small personal possession and small growing. For adults, category-I restriction
means three to six months with probation reporting and travel conditions. General
articles 42–43 distinguish these penalties from a criminal record and describe
the court’s sentencing choices; no claim about a universal sentence imposed in
practice is made. The article’s descriptions of available restrictions are
statutory, not an estimate of how often probation was used.

The 2021 replacement code places small possession and small cultivation in the
administrative offences system. Possession carries a 200-calculated-unit fine
or 40 hours of community work; an individual’s small grow carries 125 units.
Large or group cultivation remains criminal under article 288.

Current **cultivation 1→2** corrects the previous blanket criminal classification.
Possession’s current 3 is retained, but its history now distinguishes the 2019
penal-misdemeanour period (score 1, including liberty restrictions) from the
2021 administrative response (score 3). The 2019 `before` snapshot uses the
former profile vector; the new 2021 `before` changes only possession to 1.
The reconstructed recent steps are **18→13 in 2019**, then **13→20.5 in 2021**.
The current exact score is **20.5**, previously 18; the displayed rounded score
is 21. Other current segment scores and both legal-status labels are preserved.
In particular, the retained sharing/practice score is not independently verified
as evidence of present tolerance. The public explanation now states the actual
gift/supply rule and prison range; further practice evidence remains a task.

The profile’s approximately **5 g** cannabis claim was unsupported and is removed.
The later quantity annex specifies **up to and including 20 g** dried marijuana
as small, over 20 through 600 g as large, and over 600 g as especially large.
Dried resin has 3/90 g boundaries; oil preparation 2/60 g; extracts/tinctures
3/90 g as dry residue. These are preparation-specific legal bands, not permission
to possess or supply cannabis. The current table is not backdated as proof that
the 2025 resolution introduced these exact cannabis amounts or increased a prior
limit. The partially accessible August 2026 amendment could not establish such
a comparison.

Medical `none` now has direct statutory support: cannabis preparations appear
in narcotic list IV, and the law/rules prohibit their ordinary medical use.
Narrow research and official-use exceptions do not establish patient access.
The seed exclusions in the cannabis-material definitions replace the earlier
claim that only an unread list could address seeds. Import, seed trade and the
complete living-organisms list still require review; seed/cloning statuses are
not changed. No plant-count limit is invented.

Existing seizure and all-drug prosecution statistics are preserved verbatim,
including their source lists. They do not prove cannabis-specific arrest totals.

## Remaining historical and legal retrieval tasks

The 1965 crop decision is a valuable gap-filler, but original enactments are still
needed for exact penalties and a fuller distinction between industrial southern
Chuy hemp, Indian hemp and other cannabis varieties. Its explicit `unscored`
reason reflects that limitation and the existing prohibited-growing score level.

The 1974 USSR decree located in this search was a **1991 consolidation** containing
1982 and 1987 amendments. It was not backdated into a new Kyrgyz event or used
to claim that small-amount administrative rules existed in the original 1974
text. A separate lead suggests a Kyrgyz drug-possession decree already existed
in December 1962; obtain it and the republican code before adding a supposed
first personal-possession offence in 1974. A KRSU historical-document compilation
returned 403 and was not treated as read.

Nazaraliev’s 2014 event now concerns his own proposal; the old composite sentence
combining the 1990s, 2014 and 2017 was removed. The separate pre-existing December
2024/February 2026 Bekeshev entry was not revised in this pass. It remains a
candidate for splitting after its original parliamentary sources are retrieved.

The July 2026 case involved multiple product types and a supply investigation.
Four grams of marijuana and seeds found in one search do not prove that small
personal possession or seeds alone led to the arrests. No convictions or product
THC percentages are supplied. The profile avoids presenting the report as a new
law or as a general ban on every hemp-labelled food.
