# Burma, 1891–1939: the Dangerous Drugs Act of 1 February 1931

The long hole between the 1891 note and the 1939 ganja rules now has the
Dangerous Drugs Act. An earlier pass recovered the 1939 sales rule from a 1946
UN report and did not have this Act.

## What was added

`myanmar-dangerous-drugs-act-1931`, date 1931-02-01, category tightening,
unscored. The 1874 prohibition still carries the score of 11 until the 1939
entry raises it. The Act requires a licence to make an extract or tincture of
hemp and to import or export it, with up to two years for a breach. Its
cultivation sections name the poppy and coca, not hemp.

An article was added. No profile or score was edited.

## What was read

- The Burma code reprint at burmalibrary.org, headed "[INDIA ACT II, 1930.]
  (1st February, 1931.)". Sections 2(c) and 2(d), 6, 7, 12 and 13. The reprint
  uses "President of the Union" and "Union of Burma", so it is a later copy of
  the Indian Act, not the 1931 gazette. The heading is the date used here.
- The India Code text of Act II of 1930, already read for Bengal and Punjab.
  The hemp definition and the two-year penalties for manufacture and for import
  or export match the Burma reprint. The Indian text does not name Burma.

## Left as it was

The 1939 rules themselves are still unrecovered. That entry stays unchecked.
The Gazette of India page for 1 February 1931 was not opened; the Burma code
heading is the date.

## Excise Act, 1896, partly read, not added

A Burma reprint of Act XII of 1896 was read from
https://www.burmalibrary.org/sites/burmalibrary.org/files/obl/1896-03-19-GB-No.12-tu-en_0.pdf
The direct download returned 403; the text used is the extract of that PDF.
The act is dated 19 March 1896 and section 1(3) says it comes into force at
once. Section 1(2) extends it to the North-Western Provinces and Oudh, the
Punjab, the Central Provinces, Burma, Coorg, and Ajmere and Merwara. It does
not extend to Bengal. "Intoxicating drugs" means ganja, bhang, charas and
every preparation and admixture of them. A later bracket adds other drugs a
local government may declare. "Hemp" means any variety of the hemp plant from
which intoxicating drugs can be produced. Retail sale is one ser of bhang and
five tolas of ganja or charas.

The bodies of section 12 (cultivation) and section 48 (the penalty) were not
in the extract. No Myanmar entry was added from the contents list alone. The
reprint's footnotes include the Burma Laws Act, 1898, so it is not a clean
19 March 1896 gazette.
