## Profile corrections and retained limits

Australia's driving context now distinguishes NSW assent from implementation.
The government announcement gives a **$722** fine for the third qualifying
detection, replacing the profile's $704 figure. The positive-roadside-test
24-hour ban remains; the 50 ng/ml laboratory criterion is not a change to the
roadside detection threshold. Learner/provisional and commercial drivers are
excluded. The medical scheme still requires registration and education;
impairment and mixed-drug offences remain. Implementing instruments, registration
availability and actual commencement should be checked before presenting the
scheme as available to drivers.

### Source and legal-stage checks

Manitoba's [Bill 56 text](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/bills/43-3/b056e.php)
changes section 101.4(3), item 2 and adds regulation-making power under section
157(1). It does not prescribe a staffing number. The
[6 October debate](https://www.gov.mb.ca/legislature/hansard/43rd_3rd/vol_68b/h68b.html)
explicitly preserves federal product-visibility restrictions: being able to see
staff and the interior is different from displaying cannabis to outsiders.
The [8 October committee record](https://www.gov.mb.ca/legislature/hansard/43rd_3rd/ju_03/ju_03.html)
and [current catalogue](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/bills/43-3/index.php) were also
reviewed. The Manitoba profile now records the proposal with scores unchanged.

Cayman's [proceedings summary](https://gov.ky/w/summary-of-parliamentary-proceedings-2-october-2026)
was published on 5 October but describes the 2 October sitting; the event uses
the sitting date. Its [July consultation](https://gov.ky/w/public-comments-cannabis-reform)
considered possession/use, home cultivation, administrative penalties and historic
convictions. The October timetable concerns submission for consideration, not
a parliamentary passage deadline. Cayman has no standalone index profile, so
this is a context event without a country assignment; it is not attributed to
the UK's domestic law or scores.

Queensland's [final Act](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/act-2026-023)
and [legislative history](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/act-2026-023/lh)
are stronger evidence than the June ministerial announcement. Section 2(1)
commences Part 10 Division 3 on 1 December 2026. New section 78E applies minimum
fine rules when a court chooses a fine-only sentence, with exceptions and rules
for sentencing several offences. The amended section 79(2AA) specifies a
7.5-penalty-unit minimum; it retains a 28-unit or three-month imprisonment maximum.
Do not repeat the announcement's broad “doubled penalties” claim as though every
THC-presence maximum doubled. New section 79(2AB) concerns drug presence together
with an alcohol-limit contravention. Division 4's testing and suspension changes
require proclamation and were listed as uncommenced in the reviewed history.
Australia's profile now states the future date rather than suggesting the whole
penalty package already operates.

A related Queensland diversion amendment in Act 7/2026 was found during the scan.
The [Drugs Misuse Act legislative history](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/asmade/act-1986-036/lh)
identifies Part 2 as not yet proclaimed. An indicative reprint showing a proposed
single-diversion model is not a current-law consolidation. A complete check of
the police-powers provisions and their commencement is still needed before
altering the Queensland possession/diversion scores.

DC's [final Emergency Act 26-391](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/acts/26-391)
confirms the effective date, conditional-licence changes and limited emergency
life. The [regulator's August announcement](https://abca.dc.gov/release/mayor-signs-medical-cannabis-licensing-and-unlicensed-establishment-enforcement)
gives the specific October expiry. The three-year conditional licence is a
licensing-process allowance; it does not itself authorise possession, production
or sales. Summary closure provisions include notice and a right to request a
hearing. The event records both licensing and enforcement changes without
claiming licensed recreational retail or permanent reform. No successor measure
was established, so the expiry must be checked before future reuse.
