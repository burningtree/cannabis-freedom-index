## The earlier rules

**1915:** preparation, peddling, sale and consumption of smoking cannabis
were prohibited throughout the colony and dependencies. Penalty **six days
to twelve months**, **100–3,000 francs**, **either or both**. Article 463's
mitigation rule remains applicable. Seized cannabis must be confiscated and
destroyed. Date used is the **French decree date, 9 October**. The 1960
memorandum reports local promulgation on **3 December** and colonial
publication on **11 December**, p. 1222. Do not claim local enforcement
began on the French signing date. Category ban, tags colonial/sentencing/retail.

**1958:** the memorandum records order **145-CG of 23 May 1958** making
assembly deliberation **58-34/AR of 17 April** enforceable. It added
cultivation and possession to earlier offences. The memorandum gives
**eleven days–one month and a fine**, with larger penalties for recidivism;
the exact original text was not recovered. One OCR occurrence of "1938"
in the later repeal is contradicted by the surrounding 1958 dates and the
catalogue, so it is not treated as an event in 1938. Category tightening,
colonial/sentencing tags; explicitly unchecked and unscored.

**1960:** signed by **Philibert Tsiranana on 28 July**, official publication
**6 August 1960**, no. 113, p. 1372. This entry uses the signature date and
the article says signed, not that it first became enforceable that day.
Articles 1–2 cover growing, preparation, transport, sale, possession and
consumption; Article 3 sets **six months–five years and 10,000–1,000,000
francs, either or both**. Article 4 covers facilitation, including lending a
vehicle or premises with or without payment, and requires confiscation or
closure plus interdiction de séjour. The English "residence ban" describes
that consequence without inventing its territorial details. The memorandum
argues existing penalties are inadequate, citing police reports of spreading
use and cannabis's low price relative to opium. Its claim that health effects
are equivalent to opium is not repeated as medical fact. Category tightening,
sentencing/trafficking tags; unchecked and unscored pending scan recovery.

## Corrections to 1997 and the profile

The former blanket **2–10 years for use/possession** description was not
supported by the promulgated provisions. Article 141 separates small
personal quantities (possession, purchase **or growing**) from the general
production/trafficking offences. It gives **2–6 months** for a cannabis
**derivative other than oil**, **3 months–1 year** for high-risk plants or
substances including cannabis oil, in both cases **prison, a fine or both**.
There is no fixed gram threshold in that article. Do not silently replace
"derivative" with "all plant material" or "oil" with "all stronger
preparations" as the profile did. How courts classify fresh/dried flower
under the wording needs case law; the revised profile keeps the text's
distinction. Waiver language refers to age and recidivism; the article
avoids asserting an unverified alternative/cumulative interpretation.

Articles 95–97 allow temporary forced labour **or fines or both**, rather
than mandatory forced labour in every case. Article 99 separately addresses
offering/supplying for another person's own consumption: **1–5 years,
a fine or both**, with aggravated cases possible. Article 8 requires
landholders to destroy cannabis crops. No new national cannabis programme
or lawful-access claim was derived from pharmaceutical provisions.

Changed 1997 to **other**, reflecting the mixed replacement: shorter
personal-quantity ranges with severe broader production/trafficking rules.
Added sentencing, trafficking, thresholds, home-growing and eradication tags.
Kept **4 November** as the existing promulgation-date event; do not call it
the verified nationwide commencement day. Replaced the parliamentary source
with the government-hosted promulgated reproduction and corrected related
profile penalty prose without changing scores/statuses.

Changed the 2020 seizure entry from tightening to **enforcement**, with
enforcement/eradication tags and a focused three-sentence description. The
**53 tonnes** is the gendarmerie's total, not all Malagasy agencies' seizures.
May–June: more than 16 tonnes, over 80,000 plants, 80 arrests. July: over
100 hectares burned. Removed unrelated price/production estimates from
that event; they remain in their separately sourced profile context.
