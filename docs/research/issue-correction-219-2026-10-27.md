# Argentina driving-map classification — #219

Reviewed 27 October 2026. Correction to the description of standing law; no six-segment score or history change.

Read the official [consolidated Law 24.449](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/818/actualizacion), articles 1–2, 48(a) and 73, and [Law 27.714](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-27714-383043/texto), article 1 (enacted 13 April 2023, published 3 May). Article 48(a) uses `habiendo consumido estupefacientes o medicamentos que disminuyan la aptitud para conducir`. Its separate alcohol sentence expressly uses a blood concentration above zero. Neither that alcohol rule nor the absence of a numerical THC limit establishes that any residual detectable THC is an independent offence.

Also checked the government's consolidated [Decree 779/1995 Annex 1](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/recurso/30389/texactdto779-1995-anexo1/htm), articles 48 and 73, and [Annex 2](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/recurso/30389/texactdto779-1995-anexo2/htm), article 67(5). The implementing language addresses effects on safe driving, tests and sanctions. It does not establish an independent any-detectable-THC offence. The annexes retain older alcohol wording; the profile uses the amended statute for the current zero-alcohol rule. No nationwide THC cutoff or controlling judgment imposing a residual-detection offence was established in this review.

Changed `context.driving.status` from `zero` to `consumption`, matching the rubric's after-consumption category. Retained the drug/medicine wording without resolving the grammatical reach of the ability-to-drive qualifier into a universal impairment-only standard. Article 73 requires authorised intoxication tests and makes refusal an offence plus a presumption under article 48(a); testing is evidence, not itself a separate THC limit. Articles 1–2 establish federal scope, adoption and local jurisdictional powers, so the profile qualifies its national baseline rather than claiming uniform provincial testing practice.

Updated profile citations and maintenance guidance: zero labels require a cited detectable-THC legal trigger, not inference from a testing power or an alcohol rule. The shared driving vocabulary already distinguishes `consumption` and `zero`; no global label change is needed. The country chip, map/table data and API derive this classification from the same profile. A regression protects the corrected status, evidential distinctions, jurisdiction caveat and unchanged scores.
