# Mexico, 1940–1967

23 October 2026. The dated hole ran from the July 1940 suspension of the
drug-addiction regulation to accession to the Single Convention on
18 April 1967.

## Read

Diario Oficial de la Federación, morning edition, 14 November 1947, pages 2
and 3 (image PDFs of the gazette, pages rendered and read). Decree of the
Secretaría de Gobernación reforming articles 193, 194 and 197 of the Penal
Code for the Federal District and territories in local matters and for the
whole republic in federal matters. Signed by Miguel Alemán; the compliance
clause is dated 12 November 1947. In force the day after publication, so
15 November 1947.

Article 193 defines enervating drugs as those determined by the sanitary
code, the regulations and other provisions in force or later issued under
article 73, fraction XVI, of the Constitution, and those indicated by
international conventions Mexico has joined or later joins. The decree does
not print marijuana, cannabis or hashish.

Article 194: one to ten years and a fine of 100 to 10,000 pesos for
unauthorised trade, making, possession, buying, free supply and the other
dealing the article lists; the same range, when done against the sanitary
laws, for cultivating or trafficking seeds or plants classed as enervating
drugs. A further clause names raw opium, cocaine and prepared smoking
substances, with language about poisoning and degenerating the race. That
clause was not treated as a cannabis rule. Inducing use, where the other
person is a minor or incapacitated, or the inducer uses ascendancy or
authority: three to twelve years plus the fine. No conditional sentence,
even below two years, for those who cultivate, make or traffic the drugs
or such seeds or plants.

Article 197: illegal import or export, six to twelve years and a fine of
500 to 20,000 pesos, plus disqualification where the previous article
applies. Articles 195, 196, 198 and 199 are printed only as dotted lines.
