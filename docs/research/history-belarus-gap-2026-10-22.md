## Legal sources read and their limits

Research downloads, extracted text, the visually inspected environmental gazette page,
baseline snapshots, source metadata, apply script and verifier are retained in
`tmp/history-belarus-gap-2026-10-22/`. The build output has been removed.

- **2019 Criminal Code:** WIPO Lex record 20145 and its full statutory text. Read articles
  54–55, 328, 328² and 329. This is a historical consolidation, not a complete 2026 code.
  It distinguishes short custody, restriction of liberty, personal possession, supply,
  repeat public-use offences and cultivation. The cultivation clause was also read in the
  public AVCD reproduction and agrees on the basic fine/short-custody/up-to-three-year
  alternatives. AVCD’s separate drug-possession page is stale and was rejected as a source
  for the 2025 changes despite its current-year page title.
- **2012 cultivation clause:** Read article 11 in Zakony-by’s statutory reproduction.
  The original Etalon page was region-blocked. Alta’s full reproduction was partly readable
  earlier but later timed out; no article relying on unread sections was added. The entry
  uses the signed date, not an assumed commencement date.
- **2014 decree:** The President’s site returned its caption without operative body.
  SPInform’s free preview supplied the preamble, early paragraphs and commencement metadata.
  The detailed policy discussion comes from printed page 8 onwards of the 2021 Institute
  of Sociology assessment hosted by UN Belarus. The title page identifies ministries, the
  academy institute and OHCHR; its disclaimer does not make every research conclusion a UN
  institutional position. Later code wording supplies the precisely qualified sentence
  ranges. The free preview was not treated as the full decree.
- **2016 environment rule:** Downloaded the original two-page gazette PDF from FAOLEX,
  extracted it and visually inspected page 1. Resolution 35 amends the 2008 rule, removes
  its wild-growing qualifier, replaces the plant list and specifies commencement. The
  heading concerns introduction/acclimatisation. The text does not itself establish all
  the crop-growing, sale, destruction and scientific-exception claims repeated in news
  reports. Those broader claims were omitted. It does not prove that every listed species
  was newly prohibited in 2016.
- **2019 amendment:** Read the actual drug amendment in SPInform’s public preview of law
  220-Z. Article 1(2) lowers the two minima; metadata gives 27 October commencement.
  Read the Current Time vote report for campaign reaction. Its June vote date is not the
  final operative date, and its description of the group tier is less precise than the law.
- **2025 reform:** Read the public amendment metadata for law 61-Z, the National Legal
  Portal explanation reproduced by Seldon, the updated statutory reproduction at Legalize
  Belarus, and the official prosecutor’s public post. The original national legal-register
  sites were region-blocked; no access restriction or paid preview was bypassed. The
  legal-portal explanation supplies the operative date and rationale. The three independent
  accessible texts agree on the drug-sentencing changes.
- **2025 amnesty:** Read law 70-Z’s public opening articles and commencement metadata,
  LawPike’s reproduced exclusions, and Minsk lawyer Kristina Asryan’s explanation of the
  relief routes and implementation. Eligibility is conditional; the first-conviction
  exception for the principal drug offence’s first four parts does not also appear in
  part 5. Other eligibility exceptions exist, so no blanket claim that every death-related
  case was excluded was made. Later clauses outside the legal database’s preview were
  not represented as personally read in the original act.
- **2025 controlled-drug list:** Downloaded the Health Ministry consolidation hosted by
  the Eurasian Economic Commission. Read its title/amendment history and list 5 entries
  5.1, 5.4 and 5.5. The explicit whole-seed exclusion replaces the profile’s unsupported
  claim that the narcotics legislation says nothing about seeds. Holding seed status and
  trade status were preserved; no permission to sow was inferred.

## Reporting and attribution

The 2018 Krytyka Polityczna interview was read for the campaign and participant’s account.
Its synthetic-drug family case is identified as such. Its numerical mapping of proposed
penalties conflicts with the enacted law and was not used to describe the offences.
Advocacy prisoner totals and claims about typical marijuana sentences were removed from
the current profile rather than repeated as official statistics.

The 2026 sentencing figures come from the Prosecutor General’s own public Telegram post
13713, timestamped 23 June. They cover drug offences generally. The article and profile do
not relabel restriction of liberty as probation or assume it is always served at home.
Secondary first-half-2026 court statistics were left out because the underlying court data
were not recovered. A separate Supreme Court joint-purchase interpretation remains a lead;
it was not turned into a new sharing exception or score change without the ruling.

## Profile and score treatment

Replaced the profile’s stale possession and supply ranges with the 2025 alternatives.
Explained the difference between short custody and restriction of liberty, corrected the
separate cultivation offence, and included the repeated-public-use criminal rule. Removed
unsupported claims about typical prison sentences, uniforms and club nonexistence.
Preserved recreational and medical classifications, planting and club/visitor statuses,
and the independent UNODC seizure block verbatim.

Current cultivation is corrected from 0 to 1 because the separate basic offence includes a
fine and shorter alternatives. This is a calibration correction, not a claim that growing
was newly liberalised in 2025: the 2015 step no longer invents a cultivation decline.
The March 2025 event raises enforcement from 0 to 1 for the new shorter sentencing options,
supported by later official practice. Possession, sharing, consumption and access stay 0.

The resulting rounded chain is 1934: 100→19; 1974: 19→8; January 2015: 8→3;
March 2025: 3→5. The chain checker flags no discontinuities. The inherited 1934 and 1974
entries still need stronger primary sourcing; this batch did not independently authenticate
those first-restriction claims. The inherited personal-possession component of the 2015
scoring also needs a separate historical review: the WIPO 2010 consolidation already
contains the two-to-five-year personal-possession prison range. This batch does not claim
that the decree first created that range.
