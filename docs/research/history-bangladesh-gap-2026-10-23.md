# Bangladesh, 1917–1974: the Dangerous Drugs Act in Bengal

The longest post-1850 hole in Bangladesh's dated history ran from the 1917
Naogaon cooperative to a 1974 international agreement. The Dangerous Drugs Act
1930 sits in that hole. It applied to Bengal, and it names ganja.

## What was added

`bangladesh-dangerous-drugs-act-1931`, date 1931-02-01, category tightening,
unscored. The 1876 score (32) still stands until 1989. The Act licences import
and export of hemp and the making of extract and tincture, with up to two years
for making extract without a licence. Schedule II keeps bhang, siddhi, ganja
and charas inside the Bengal Excise Act 1909 and takes extract and tincture
out of that definition.

An article was added. No profile or score was edited.

## What was read

- India Code text of Act II of 1930, assent 1 March 1930:
  https://www.indiacode.nic.in/repealedfileopen?rfilename=A1930-2.pdf
  Sections 2 (hemp, medicinal hemp, manufactured drug, dangerous drug), 6, 7,
  12 and 13, section 39 (stricter local laws on consumption and traffic stay
  valid), and Schedule II (Bengal Excise Act 1909). Commencement is "such date
  as the Governor General in Council may appoint". The PDF does not give that
  date.
- SooperKanoon bare-act page, read in full. A footnote says the Act came into
  force on 1 February 1931 and cites Gazette of India, 1931, Part I, page 35.
  That gazette page was not opened.

## Not used

A Burma code reprint of "India Act II, 1930" also prints 1 February 1931, but
its operative text is the Burma adaptation ("President of the Union"). It was
not used for the Bengal rules. The Narcotics Control Act pages on bdlaws are
the modern statute, outside this gap.

## 1950: Sylhet (read 23 October 2026)

The Government of East Pakistan's collection of East Bengal and East Pakistan
Acts, Regulations and Ordinances, 1947–1959, was downloaded and searched.
East Bengal Act XII of 1950, dated 29 March 1950, extends the Bengal Excise
Act, 1909, to Sylhet from 1 April 1950 and repeals, for that district, the
Eastern Bengal and Assam Excise Act, 1910, and the Assam Temperance Act, 1926.
Sections 2 to 9, the amendments to the 1909 Act, are marked repealed by East
Pakistan Ordinance XXVIII of 1960 and are not printed. Added as
`bangladesh-sylhet-excise-act-1950`, category other, so the 1876 score is not
moved. A search of the same volume for ganja, bhang, charas, cannabis and
siddhi found no other cannabis statute. The one "hemp" hit is a commodity in
a trade list. The opium-smoking amendments in the volume name opium, not
ganja, and were not added.

## Bengal Excise Act, 1878, and the 1893 wild-hemp order

Read in the Indian Hemp Drugs Commission report, 1893-94, Volume I, chapter XV,
paragraphs 592–601, in the PDF at
https://newrevenue.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/ihd-vol-1-pages-283-303-or-so74464868_53_72.pdf
The Act's own text was not opened. The report gives no day for Act VII of 1878.

Added `bangladesh-bengal-excise-act-1878`, year 1878, tightening, unscored.
Section 5: no manufacture of ganja, charas or bhang, and no cultivation of the
plants, without the Collector's licence. Section 11: no sale without a licence.
Retail limit, sections 15 and 60: a quarter of a ser of ganja or bhang, five
tolas of charas. Section 17: possession above that limit, with the section 62
exemption for authorised cultivators. Section 16: a cultivator sells only to a
person authorised by pass or licence. The five-tola limit on Garhjat ganja is
a Board declaration under section 17-A, not the Act's general limit. The 64
square miles, the free cultivator's licence and the shop numbers are the
system the Commission found, not dated to 1878 in the entry. The 1876 score
stays at 32 until 1989.

Added `bangladesh-wild-hemp-order-cancelled-1893`, June 1893, easing, unscored.
Paragraph 598: January 1893 order to extirpate spontaneous growth, cancelled
the following June. Collecting leaves still needed no licence. The named
wild-growth districts are Bhagalpur, Monghyr and Purnea.

## Narcotics Control Act, 1990

Read on the Laws of Bangladesh compilation of Act No. 20 of 1990
(http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-736.html). The act is dated
1 February 1990 and section 1(2) deems it in force from 19 Poush 1396,
2 January 1990. Added as `bangladesh-narcotics-control-act-1990`,
category death, unscored. The chain stays 1989 (32 to 5) until 2018
(5 to 3). Today 3.

Section 9 prohibits cultivation, production, processing, carrying,
transport, import, export, supply, buying, selling, keeping, storing,
displaying and use of a narcotic other than alcohol. Words about
attempts, investment and institutions, and a separate use penalty,
are marked as inserted by the Narcotics Control (Amendment) Act, 2000
(Act No. 39 of 2000) and were not dated to 1990. A licence for
cultivation, and a permit for use, are likewise marked as 2000
insertions. The 2000 act's own text was not opened.

Section 19's table, which does not apply to cultivation: ganja or any
herbal cannabis, six months to three years up to 5 kilograms and three
to fifteen years above that; any cannabis plant, six months to three
years up to 25 plants and three to fifteen years above that; cannabis
resin, with opium, two to ten years up to 2 kilograms and death or life
above that; tetrahydrocannabinol, with pethidine and morphine, two to
ten years up to 10 grams and death or life above that. A fine is added.
A repeat that is not already death or life is twice the maximum.

Footnote 29 says serial 6 of that table, and the entries against it,
were deleted by the 2000 act. An English translation on CommonLII shows
serial 6 as alcohol and the ganja rows as serials 7 and 8, with the
same quantities. The ganja rows were therefore not treated as that
deletion. The first schedule's class list was not on the Bengali page
that was read. The direct download of the same page later timed out;
the text used is the compilation already fetched from that URL.
