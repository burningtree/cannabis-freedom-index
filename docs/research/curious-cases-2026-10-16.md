## Curious traditions (same day)

Three new atlas entries in `culture/traditions/`, all from books in `books/`:

- `the-spit-bubble-game`: Duvall, *The African Roots of Marijuana* (2019), pp. 104–105, fig. 5.3,
  notes 88–89; du Toit in *Cannabis and Culture* (1975), pp. 96–98 and note 5 (Braatvedt 1949,
  Samuelson). Lead image: Rijksmuseum RP-F-2001-7-1128-47 (CC0), cropped to the upper photograph of
  the album page. The left man bends over a long reed towards the floor; the caption does not claim
  he is playing the game. Second image: Wellcome M0005729 (CC BY 4.0).
- `the-earth-pipe`: du Toit pp. 97, 104–105 (Campbell 1822; Moszcik 1910 via Balfour 1922);
  Duvall pp. 58–59, 67–68, notes 90–92. No suitable open image was found.
- `the-hemp-dance`: Benet in *Cannabis and Culture*, pp. 43–44; Frazer, *The Golden Bough* (1922
  abridged edition, Gutenberg #3623), ch. 3 §2, ch. 62 §5, ch. 63. No image: Commons has only modern
  Kupala-night photos, which do not show the hemp custom.

Further leads: Benet p. 43 (hemp seed thrown in the fire for the dead at harvest; Estonian bride
"showered" with hemp, used here only briefly); Duvall p. 105 on coughing as a performance among
Nyamwezi smokers ("renowning it", Burton).

## Third batch (same day)

- `egypt-camel-stomach-smuggling-1939` (history entry + article): Ram, *Intoxicating Zion*, ch. 2
  (print pp. 48–49 per index; the EPUB has no page map) and ch. 5 (p. 115); Booth, *Cannabis: A
  History*, ch. 11. Ram says X-ray machines at Qantara by the early 1940s (and mine detectors, p.
  115); Booth says a mine detector, developed into a camel-sized metal detector. The article gives
  both.
- `mozambique-delagoa-bay-bang-1798` (history entry): William White's journal (1800), pp. 33–35,
  read from the Wellcome/Internet Archive scan on Commons. The plate facing p. 34 is PDF page 45,
  rotated and cropped into `src/assets/culture/white-1800-smoking-bang-delagoa-bay.jpg`.
- `traditions/the-smokers-cough` (atlas): White; Duvall pp. 105–106 and notes 96–104 (Burton,
  Monteiro, Wauters, Sparrman, Johnson's Lake Malawi story, the Black Sabbath footnote).

More leads in Booth ch. 11: the 1932 camel-hair case could be its own dated entry; hashish in
hollow Suez Canal buoys and in dead livestock thrown overboard (undated in Booth).

## Fourth batch (same day)

- `traditions/the-diamba-circles`: França, *História da maconha no Brasil*, ch. 2 (Iglésias 1918
  quotations and verses, Dória 1915, Deodato 1922) and ch. 3 (1921 law silent on cannabis);
  Hutchinson in *Cannabis and Culture*, p. 179 ("Club de Diambistas", Pereira 1958). The verses are
  a 1918 folk text reprinted by França. No open image found.
- `traditions/ganja-tea-in-jamaica`: Rubin and Comitas, *Ganja in Jamaica* (Anchor 1976), ch. IV,
  pp. 50–53, tables 2–3. The tables were extracted from a PDF with scrambled columns; only rows
  whose informant, amount and comment could be matched with confidence were used.
