## Laws compared

- [Official Bulletin 6/81](https://amategeko.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/BOB_No6-1981.pdf):
  read the explanatory statement at printed pp. 240 and 246, including paragraph 64;
  Articles 324–330 at pp. 283–284; and the repeal/commencement and signatures at pp. 297–298.
  Printed p. 284 was rendered and visually checked. The drug-policy explanation expressly
  invokes public health and concern about trafficking; the broader explanation describes
  replacement of inherited colonial criminal law and consolidation of scattered laws.
  Signed 4 April, expressly operative 1 May 1981 despite the June bulletin issue.
  General offence: 3 months–5 years AND fine up to 250,000 francs OR either alone.
  Production/import/manufacture/export could attract doubled penalties. Use: 3–5 years
  AND 50,000–100,000 francs OR either alone. Facilitating use, including free premises:
  3–5 years AND 5,000–500,000 francs OR either alone; up to 10 years when use/supply
  involved under-18s. Confiscation and crop destruction were required.
- [2009 reproduction](https://www.burundinet.com/download/codes_lois/code_penal_2009.pdf):
  read heading, Articles 78–81, 489–497 and final repeal/commencement clauses. PDF p. 139
  was rendered and visually checked. The title specifies 22 April 2009; the final signature
  date is unfilled. Earlier research independently checked the Refworld date record (see
  `history-1980s-2000s-2026-10-17.md`, twenty-ninth pass). Refworld did not load during
  this pass. The heading and prior independently recorded date are retained; no new
  Gazette publication date is asserted.
  Possession/use: 1–5 years AND 50,000–100,000 francs OR either alone; production/import/
  manufacture/export: 10–15 years AND up to 1 million, doubled for organised groups.
  Facilitating use: 1–5 years AND 50,000–1 million; group penalties doubled; under-18
  use/supply 10–20 years. Article 497 requires additional supervision for consumption.
- [Original Official Bulletin 12TER/2017](https://amategeko.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/BOB_No12TER-2017.pdf):
  replaces the profile's partial reprint citation. Read Articles 78–81, 505–513 and
  647–648, including signature. Printed p. 2100 (PDF p. 70) was rendered and visually
  checked. Signed by Pierre Nkurunziza on 29 December; Article 648 makes promulgation
  the commencement trigger. The central narcotics tariffs and compulsory supervision
  match 2009, with renumbering. No new overall legal direction is assigned to this code.

**Important correction:** the general regulatory offence (2017 Article 507) has the
100,000–250,000-franc fine; the specific possession/consumption clause (Article 510) has
**50,000–100,000 francs**. Both allow a fine alone or prison alone, as well as both. The old
profile applied the general fine to possession/use and claimed no heavier trafficking tier
had been found. Article 508 expressly supplies a 10–15-year production/import/manufacture/
export tier. Facilitation under Article 509 includes unpaid help. Its wording uses prison
AND fine, without the express fine-alone alternative in Article 510.

The code separates cultivation's prohibition, the general regulatory offence and illicit
production without defining their boundary for a few home plants in these articles. The
profile describes both penalty clauses and does not promise a fine-only outcome for home
growing. No verified gram/plant threshold is added.

## Assistance and the Mainline briefing

[Mainline's PDF](https://mainline.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Burundi.pdf) was downloaded
and the Context, Policies, Peer Involvement, Human Rights and Prison sections were read.
Its own key-informant interviews describe rarely implemented supervision, institutional
capacity shortages, stigma, fear of arrest and BAPUD advocacy. Those are explicitly
attributed accounts covering people who use drugs generally, not a national cannabis-only
enforcement survey. The briefing describes a 2020 ANSS partnership; no separate event is
added for it without checking its original dated record.

The briefing combines older research/interviews (including a meeting still described as
expected in February 2022) with updates through mid-2025. The old entry's August date
came from the hosting path; the document has no explicit August publication date. Reduce
the entry's date to **2025**, retain its stable ID, and use the current `debate` category
for a policy report. Avoid treating every statement as a fresh 2025 observation or saying
a second study occurred in 2019: its discussion of 127 participants refers to the earlier
2017 assessment and its later publication.

The briefing says the code does not provide therapeutic injunctions. **Articles 79 of
both codes expressly allow a treatment order after medical assessment**, backed by
imprisonment for refusal. The legal account therefore follows the statutes and uses the
interviews only for implementation. This is additional supervision after conviction,
not a verified dismissal/diversion route or medical-cannabis permission.
