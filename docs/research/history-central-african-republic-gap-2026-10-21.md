# Central African Republic: recovering the drug law, 21 October 2026

## Coverage gained

The baseline had three events and no article for the main drug law. Recovered the complete
2001 law, rewrote that entry around its actual personal-quantity rules, and added a 418-word
article. Added the 2010 Penal Code's express continuation of the drug law, the February 2022
Ouandago operation and the November 2024 destruction of seized products in Bangui. All six
country entries now have topic tags, including the inherited colonial entry.

The profile no longer says the drug law could not be found. It now describes possession,
personal growing, ordinary production/trafficking, direct supply, prohibited use, treatment,
research authorisations and additional penalties using the primary text. Removed unsupported
Wikipedia-based assertions that there was no licensed production or any legal product.

## Text recovered and read

Complete UNODC-hosted text, 128 PDF pages:
https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/caf/loi-01011_html/CAF-_loi_01011-fr.pdf

This is the national harmonised drug-control, extradition and mutual-assistance law, **01.011**.
Signature page, PDF p. 127, says **Bangui, 18 July 2001**, President **Ange Félix Patassé**.
Do not confuse a file upload/publication date or the UN's later communication with enactment.
Article 187 requires registration/publication; the original gazette and commencement were not
recovered. The entry uses the signing date without claiming it was commencement.

Relevant complete clauses read in the text extraction:

- Article 8: cannabis growing prohibited; landowners, operators and occupiers must destroy
  plantations growing there. This is an explicit cannabis provision.
- Articles 9–12: controlled schedules and licensed medical activities; Article 86 permits
  specifically authorised quantities for medical/scientific research, teaching and forensic
  work. These are not proof of an operating cannabis patient-access programme.
- Articles 95–97: ordinary high-risk production, international trafficking and local trafficking
  ten–twenty years **OR** 250,000–125 million CFA **OR both**. Never omit the fine-only option.
- Article 99: giving/offering a high-risk drug directly for another person's consumption
  two–five years **OR** 25,000–2.5 million CFA **OR both**.
- Article 109: controlled-drug use outside prescription prohibited; confiscation possible
  without prosecution. Article 97 also lists use among its high-risk offences. Do not apply
  the small-possession clause to use itself without qualification.
- **Article 110**, starts PDF p. 67 and continues on p. 68: notwithstanding Articles 95 and 97,
  small quantities possessed, bought or grown for personal consumption have a separate tier.
  High-risk plants/substances **including cannabis oil** two months–one year OR
  25,000–1.25 million CFA OR both. Cannabis derivative **other than oil** one–six months OR
  25,000–500,000 CFA OR both. Lower-risk plants/substances fifteen days–three months OR
  25,000–500,000 CFA OR both. No gram/plant threshold. The waiver conditions are listed as
  bullets (minority, no recidivism, solemn declaration); their cumulative/alternative
  relationship is not clarified here, so no general adult waiver is claimed.
- Article 111: drug-impaired driving punished like alcohol-impaired driving; refusal same.
  Exact alcohol-offence penalties not reconstructed; no driving profile section added.
- Articles 119 and 142: optional foreigner exclusion permanent or two–ten years for
  **Articles 95–103**, removal after prison. Do not claim automatic expulsion for every
  small-personal-quantity case under Article 110. Other optional restrictions include civic
  rights, profession, travel and driving.
- Article 121: court may invite a dependent person convicted under specified offences to
  undergo care instead of/additional to punishment. Evading the measures carries two–five
  years OR 25,000–2.5 million CFA OR both. Do not describe this as unconditional voluntary
  diversion or evidence that treatment was available in practice.
- Article 125: night searches of specified drug premises; written judicial authorisation
  required for dwelling/apartment searches. Article 144: first two thirds of prison term
  restrictions for specified 95–103 convictions, not automatically Article 110.

Rendered and visually inspected PDF pp. 5, 58, 67–68, 77 and 127 alongside the extracted text.
Relevant retained files and contact sheet are in the batch's `tmp/` research directory.

## Continuation and practice sources

The **6 January 2010** Penal Code was downloaded in full from
https://bwcimplementation.org/sites/default/files/resource/CAR_PenalCode.pdf.
Read and rendered PDF p. 42: Article 246 directs drug offences to the law of 18 July 2001.
Signature date at the end confirms 6 January. The 2010 event is `other`, a continuation of
special drug law; no new penalty or score change is inferred.

UN Treaty Collection participant rows were read for both the amended Single Convention and
1988 convention. Both list accession **15 October 2001**. These dates supply the article's
later international context; no claim is made that accession first prohibited cannabis.

MINUSCA's complete webpage body, including date and the final police-spokesperson paragraph,
was returned by the web search index and read:
https://minusca.unmissions.org/fr/communiques-de-presse/le-rapport-du-secretaire-general-des-nations-unies-sur-la-rca.
It is dated **23 February 2022**. Commissioner Serge Ntolo Minko reports an operation in
**Ouandago, Nana-Gribizi**, responding to abuses by a self-defence group: **11 people
intercepted, 11 homemade weapons, over 10 kg of cannabis**, a mid-operation account.
The mission says the population welcomed it. Do not turn the eleven arrests into eleven
cannabis convictions or identify every arrested person as a cannabis user/dealer. The wider
article discusses peace/security activity; unrelated claims are omitted. Direct page/PDF
fetches returned 403, but the indexed webpage supplied the complete readable body. Its related
PDF's indexed page 7 says 17–24 February; the entry uses the verified report date and does
not depend on the PDF's full text having been downloaded.

Radio Ndeke Luka's **6 November 2024** original report, by Martial Steve Mbetissinga, was
opened and read:
https://www.radiondekeluka.org/76428-bangui-la-police-detruit-une-quantite-importante-de-drogues-et-medicaments-nuisibles.
The anti-drug office destroyed cannabis and other seized products **5 November**. Seizures
were **14–20 October**, along the Oubangui, in Boy-Rabe and at Combattant market. No cannabis
weight or arrest/conviction count is supplied. Do not infer one. Category `enforcement`,
tag `enforcement`; **not** crop `eradication`.

Current FCDO drug-penalty paragraph was re-read. It warns about heavy fines and prison for
use/possession. Its general political chronology is stale; it is not used for contemporary
politics or to override the law's specific small-quantity options.

## Limits and next gaps

Earlier national penalties remain unreconstructed. Searches for the 1961 Penal Code and the
previously cited alleged drug law 97.003 did not produce a cannabis-relevant primary text.
The latter number appears in court-of-accounts material; no new drug-law entry is created.
The 2001 event is `other`: a comprehensive law whose direction against its immediate predecessor
has not been verified. The inherited 1926 colonial event remains sourced as before, with a
colonial tag added; the original text and penalty levels still need research.

**The six numerical scores/statuses are preserved, not newly verified.** In particular, the
legacy consumption score 1 presumes light enforcement, and the possession/enforcement scores
also need practice evidence. The statute alone does not show typical sentencing. Do not invent
a dated tightening/easing step from the fact that this batch first recovered the text. A later
score audit should reconstruct earlier national law and obtain sentencing/practice evidence;
then update scores and dated steps together. This batch's chain check only tests structural
consistency, not proof of historical severity or current enforcement frequency.

The UN crop-annex search index lists 2017 Central African eradication/cultivation figures,
but a readable complete source file was not recovered (404/403/empty downloads). No crop
statistic or event is added from a search table alone. The empty failed download has no
research authority.
