- Act XXXVII of 1930 on Jogtár (net.jogtar.hu, read in Hungarian):
  "1930. évi XXXVII. törvény a Genfben 1925. évi február hó 19-én kötött
  Nemzetközi ópiumegyezmény becikkelyezéséről" — section 1 incorporates the
  Geneva Convention into the laws of the country; section 2 prints the
  official Hungarian text; Article 1 defines Indian hemp as the dried
  flowering/fruiting tops of the female Cannabis sativa with resin
  unextracted, under any trade name. Jogtár web pagination ignores the page
  parameter and the print view renders empty, so only the first web page
  (title through Articles 1–4) was read; the hemp trade articles come from
  the League document below.
- League of Nations Advisory Committee, C.434.M.225.1935.XI (Geneva, 2 Nov
  1935), via UN Digital Library PDF (read via pdftotext): Article 11(1)(a)
  special import certificates for hemp resin and ordinary preparations
  (hashish, esrar, chiras, djamba) with stated purposes and no re-export;
  Article 11(1)(b) hemp imports only for medical/scientific purposes;
  numbered registers, export authorisations, bonded warehouses and free ports.
- Antal Nagy, Jogi Fórum 2022 (read in Hungarian): no drug legislation
  before the early 1930s; Hungary confined itself to transposing conventions
  with Act XXXVII/1930 as the first rules; 1961 Act V (public-order crimes,
  substance list by 1957 health decree); 1978 code (small-quantity concept,
  blurred consumer/dealer line).
