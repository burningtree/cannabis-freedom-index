- Read the colonial annual report, Chapter XIV, printed pp. 75–76, and its title
  page confirming 1936 publication. It identifies No. 29 of 1935, treaty purpose,
  replacement of earlier laws and whole-territory application. It does not
  reproduce the cannabis provisions or establish a commencement day.
- The original 1935 ordinance was not obtained. The existing research-paper
  sources remain, but their later account of possession prohibition is explicitly
  distinguished from inspection of the ordinance. The article uses Richard
  Quansah Amissah's actual text and author attribution.
- Read the complete reproduced Supreme Court judgment, Hassan v The State,
  [1962] 2 GLR 150–154. It supplies the 1961 commencement date, comparison of old
  and new provisions, evidential issue and final disposition. The hosting site is
  a law-report reproduction, not the court's own website; no secondary case
  summary was substituted for the judgment.
- The source PDF for Act 64 on GhaLII returned 403. No assertion is made that the
  entire 1961 Act legalised cannabis, and no modern legal advice is inferred from
  the narrow historical possession ruling.
- Earlier 1926 drug legislation appeared as a research lead in a League document,
  but was not added as a cannabis event without confirming its operative scope.
- The original 1935 `before` vector and category are preserved without fresh
  validation. The annual report's replacement language means the claim that
  1935 was the first applicable restriction remains unproven. All new events are
  unscored pending evidence sufficient to reconstruct the wider legal position.
