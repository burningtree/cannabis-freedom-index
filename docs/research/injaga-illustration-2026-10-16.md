# Injaga: Codere's original research photograph

Located and extracted the original Plate 1 associated with Helen Codere's
chapter, “The Social and Cultural Context of Cannabis Use in Rwanda,” in
Vera Rubin (ed.), Cannabis and Culture (Mouton, 1975), pp. 217–226.
The plate is on plate page x, between printed pp. 266–267; it is PDF page 290
(one-based) in the locally supplied book scan. The plates contents page is
PDF page 281. The chapter explicitly refers readers to this plate.

Public digitization supplied by the user:
https://cannalib.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Cannabis-and-Culture-1975.pdf
Publisher's bibliographic edition:
https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110812060/html

The plate's printed institutional photograph credit reads:
“L'Institute pour la recherche scientifique en Afrique centrale”.
The entry normalizes the institution's name to Institut pour la recherche
scientifique en Afrique centrale. No individual photographer is established.
The source does not establish the photograph's capture date; 1959–60 dates the
fieldwork in which it was shown. The requested caption was adjusted from
“photographed during” to “in the photograph used during” to preserve that
important distinction. The alt text otherwise follows the supplied wording.

The source page was rendered and visually inspected. `pdfimages` extracted
its original 1275 × 1650 scan layer; the photograph was cropped at x=162, y=200,
width=780, height=808, preserving the full photographic composition and
original monochrome appearance. No colourisation, enhancement or visual
reconstruction was applied. Asset:

Reuse terms are unconfirmed; no open licence is claimed.

Verified chapter details: printed p. 219 describes 252 Rwandans commenting
freely on six photographs and 113 producing cannabis-related responses to
the Twa man's photograph. Printed p. 223 reports 99 wholly against cannabis,
13 mixed opinions and one unqualifiedly favourable response. These are
opinion counts, not prevalence figures or evidence of pharmacological effects.
Printed p. 221 distinguishes injaga (cannabis) from urumogi (the gourd pipe)
in this account. Removed urumogi from the entry's unqualified `also` aliases
while preserving the explicit discussion of historically situated vocabulary.
Hostile statements are contextualised within social hierarchy and prejudice;
no universal claim about Twa consumption or exclusive community use is made.
The modern Lafforgue fallback was unnecessary.
