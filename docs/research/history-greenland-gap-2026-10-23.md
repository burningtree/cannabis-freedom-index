# Greenland: local legislation and the low-THC import route

Researched 23 October 2026. Five new entries extend Greenland from three events to eight.
The three existing entries are revised, two articles added, and all twelve profile text blocks
converted to inline citations. All six numeric scores, dependency and status fields remain.

## The chronology comes from the actual regulations

The local Act 24 of 25 November 2022 and accompanying regulation 40 of 23 December 2022
both expressly commenced **1 January 2023**. The law replaced the inherited Danish statute;
the accompanying regulation already excluded qualifying cannabis products with no more than
0.2% THC when imported by an authorised legal entity. The 2022 explanatory notes describe
parliament's two 2019 decisions seeking low-THC product/oil access comparable with Denmark.
They also explain why the local Act alone needed an accompanying regulation: the actual
exception depends on the regulation's definitions, not just the enabling law.

An appendix to the October 2024 review mentions the June 2024 regulation, but that is not
the earliest exception. The full official repeal chain was followed backwards:

- Current regulation 61, dated 1 September 2025, replaces regulation 48 of 30 May 2025.
- Regulation 48 replaces regulation 37 of 20 June 2024.
- Regulation 37 replaces regulation 16 of 20 April 2024.
- Regulation 16 replaces regulation 59 of 15 November 2023.
- Regulation 59 replaces regulation 40 of 23 December 2022.

The January 2023 exception appears in regulation 40, sections 1(4) and 9. The November
2023 replacement retained the authorised-importer/THC conditions. Regulation 16 expressly
commenced **22 April 2024** and added import in batches for distribution after receipt at
storage or similar premises in Greenland controlled by the importer. It also specifies product
identification and proof of compliance in applications and applies to pending applications.
Later replacements retain these conditions. The events therefore distinguish the initial
2023 exception from the 2024 distribution/documentation requirement. Later reissues with
no verified cannabis-specific change do not receive duplicate reform events.

A legal entity is a company or other organisation; the exception is not a personal traveller
import allowance based on THC content alone. The importer needs the Chief Medical Officer's
office's permission, and all the definition's conditions must be met. Other product and medicines
rules may still apply. The batch does not infer actual sales, importer numbers or inventories.

The current regulation's title date is 1 September 2025, while its signature block is dated
22 August. It commences the day after publication. Neither signature nor title date is used
to invent a separate commencement event. Its annex's List B no. 59 defines cannabis, excluding
isolated seeds and fibres from that definition. Those exclusions alone are not treated as an
unrestricted seed trade or home-growing right.

## Earlier political and administrative gaps

The government's full 2019 answers to questions 257 and 273 were read. They confirm that
Greenland took responsibility for drug legislation alongside health services in **1992** and
that subsequent Danish changes did not automatically extend there. The actual 1991 health
Act could not be retrieved at the attempted PDF URL, so the event uses the confirmed year,
not a newly inferred exact commencement day. The answer quotes the continuity rule for
existing health legislation. The later local bill's explanatory notes corroborate this account.
The profile's dependency text is retained rather than inventing a constitutional change.

The 2019 parliamentary decisions are documented in the government's actual 2022 explanatory
notes, not merely the earlier questions, which pre-date the autumn decisions. Their exact
voting dates were not independently recovered; the event uses **2019**. The decisions are
calls for implementation, not themselves an effective low-THC exception.

The 2024 review's history section describes the health department's **2016** proposed national
hash plan, covering supply reduction, prevention and treatment. It says the whole plan remained
a proposal while some work proceeded through the public-health programme. The entry is
therefore debate/context, not an adopted comprehensive plan. The June 2026 government response
separately confirms Allorfik's treatment/counselling expansion since 2016. No treatment outcome
or number of patients is inferred.

## The first restriction remains an estimate

The inherited **1955** law is confirmed in the local 2022 Act's repeal clause and government
explanatory notes. The earliest applicable cannabis instrument, including any earlier Danish
controls that may have applied in Greenland, has not been recovered. The original Danish 1955
PDF URL returned 404; search extracts of later Danish amendments are not treated as an original
Greenland commencement record. The pre-existing estimate ID, year, uncertainty window and
FREE vector are preserved. Its description now distinguishes the inherited statute from the
current local law and removes the false implication that the old Danish statute still governs.
It remains an estimate rather than being marked checked as a verified first-ever prohibition.

## Better evidence for the existing debate entries

The actual independent review by Christina Viskum Lytken Larsen and Christine Ingemann,
with WHO collaborator Jakob Manthey, was downloaded. Its cover was visually inspected and
confirms **10 October 2024**, replacing the old year-only date. The background, regulatory
options, trade-offs, conclusions and prevention history were read. The revised entry reflects
the comparison of prohibition, decriminalisation, limited access and commercial sales, rather
than reducing the whole report to a later government's statement about harms to young people.
These are the review's policy findings, not newly established clinical advice. No material is
quoted verbatim. The report's international clinical findings are not independently reanalysed.

The full June 2026 government response was read. It reports funding for three to five additional
customs officers in the 2025 budget and describes prevention, treatment and family support.
Funding is not a verified count of officers actually hired. The profile no longer relies on a
secondary court-case headline or unsupported claims of no products, no licensed cultivation
and a medical route established by Wikipedia. Current statutes supply the permissions and
prohibitions; the 2019 medicines-committee answer supplies the approval authority.

## Profile, scoring and follow-up limits

The low-THC product changes are explicitly unscored: they do not create a whole-point increase
or reduction in personal freedom for intoxicating cannabis. The local 2023 law preserves the
prescription/pharmacy route described in its notes, and no operating flower programme was
verified. Medical remains Limited, access 1. Cultivation 1, possession 1, enforcement 2,
sharing 1 and consumption 1 stay unchanged. Club and visitor classifications remain unchanged.
No draft fine tariff from the bill notes is presented as a binding sentencing table.

The latest Folketing summary of a 20 October 2026 drug-driving proposal was a useful lead,
but the full proposal was not successfully retrieved. It is not used to assert current driving
law or add an event. That remains a follow-up, alongside the first applicable restriction,
original 2019 voting records and practical implementation of the low-THC route.
