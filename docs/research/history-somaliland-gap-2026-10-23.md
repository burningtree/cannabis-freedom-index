# Somaliland: inherited drug laws and a two-entry history gap

Reviewed 23 October 2026. Somaliland had only an estimated first restriction and a 2023
cultivation-arrest report. This batch adds five Somaliland history entries and a corresponding
1964 entry for Somalia, revises three existing entries, and adds three articles. Somaliland now
has seven entries. The first restriction remains estimated.

## Sources actually read

- Downloaded the [UNODC English Penal Code text](https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/som/1962/penal_code_html/Penal_Code_of_Somalia.pdf).
  Read the approving decree, articles 1–3 and 342–343. Trading, possession **for sale**, supplying
  or procuring drugs for another person carry one to three years and a fine in article 342.
  Increased punishment applies to specified recipients, including under-18s. Article 343
  separately covers allowing consumption premises and entering them to consume.
- Read the editorial introduction and relevant code passages in the [Somalilandlaw edition
  hosted by FAO](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/som209614.pdf) through the web PDF extraction.

  **3 April 1964** commencement, replacing the Indian code in the north and Italian code in the
  former Italian territory. [Refworld's catalogue](https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/1964/72335)
  independently records that commencement date. No calculation from the December 1962 signature
  date was used. Refworld's actual PDF also returned 403 locally.
- Re-read the original Italian gazette of [Law 46 of 3 March 1970](https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/handle/2307/26208),
  retained as `tmp/research-progress-pass2/so1970it.pdf` and `.txt`. Relevant printed pages 833–842,
  articles 1–6, 14–19 and Schedule A. The four-page English extract in `so1970.txt` is incomplete,
  so it was not used instead of the original for omitted cultivation and transfer provisions.
- Re-read the enabling text and cannabis entries of [Health Secretary Decree 227 of 26 September 1970](https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/handle/2307/26565),
  retained as `so1970schedule.pdf` and text in the earlier research folder. It names Cannabis /
  Hashish and Indian Hemp / Cannabis. The original schedule is published in the 29 October 1970
  gazette; other listed substances were not exhaustively audited.
- Re-read all seven articles of [Decree-Law 5 of 18 May 1984](https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/handle/2307/29253),
  Somali original, printed pages 394–395. Visually inspected the earlier page394 render and a
  new page395 render. Ten years and 50,000 Somali shillings apply to unlawful manufacture,
  production, import, dealing and supply/procuring for others. Hosting consumption premises:
  five years and 25,000; entering to consume: three years and 20,000. The image confirms 20,000

  regional branches. Article 6 retains earlier penalties for earlier offences. Article 7 provides

- Re-read both articles of [Law 25 of 24 June 1984](https://arcadia.sba.uniroma3.it/handle/2307/28733),
  original gazette printed p. 483. The decree was converted to statute. Its conversion law has a

  controls first began in 1984, or that the conversion changed their substance, was made.

  It reproduces a police statement about an alleged grow in a house yard in Ga’an Libaah,

## Articles and interpretive limits

Three new Somaliland articles meet the writing guide, each with four headings:

- Penal code 1964: 367 words.
- Licensing law 1970: 375 words.
- Drug-offences decree 1984: 365 words.

The articles explain what people faced, distinguish offence groups, and link earlier/later
entries and the Somaliland profile. Every factual sentence has inline source marks.

The original penal code's possession-for-sale offence is **not** treated as an ordinary personal
possession offence. The 1970 special law supplies the broader possession rule. Neither the
1984 venue offence nor the earlier 343 offence is presented as an identical penalty for every
act of private consumption. The 1984 decree's production offence may overlap with the lower1970
cultivation provision; no unverified judicial resolution of that overlap was invented.

The National Security Court provisions are described historically. They are not asserted to be
the present institutional arrangement in Somaliland. The constitutional continuity rule is
conditional, not proof that every old provision survives unchanged. Local amendments, repeal,
compatibility decisions and present sentencing practice still require further recovery. This
batch establishes a firmer inherited statutory baseline; it does not certify a complete current
Somaliland consolidation or modern enforcement tariff.

The English code edition's editor warns about translation quality and notes that Somaliland
fine scales have changed. Articles use the old money amounts only when describing1984 itself;
the profile does not present Somali-shilling fines as modern Somaliland fines.

## Profile corrections

All thirteen existing score/context blocks now cite the recovered statutes, constitution or
published police account. Wikipedia-only support has been removed. The profile now identifies
statutory possession/cultivation penalties, unpaid-transfer rules, consumption-venue offences,
medical authorisation provisions and constitutional safeguards. The arrest block stays tied to
one reported case, with no inferred national total or conviction.

Medical classification changes **none → limited** to recognise the general licensing and
prescription framework in the inherited1970 law and express cannabis schedule, using the same
limited-framework distinction already present in the Somalia profile. No operating cannabis
patient programme, issued licence, supplied medicine or patient-grown route was verified or
asserted. The six numeric scores remain unchanged, and access stays 0. This is a research
correction, not a claim that Somaliland enacted a medical reform in 2026. The Somalia profile
was left byte-identical to its baseline.

All pre-existing score vectors within this batch are preserved. The 1984 increase is unscored:
the existing supply scores already sit at the bottom, and evidence for a separate whole-point
change in personal-use freedom is insufficient. The broader national historical score work
being done concurrently in India, Madagascar and South Africa was inspected and preserved in
explicit snapshot records rather than overwritten or treated as this batch's own changes.
