# Sint Maarten: prosecution policy and legalisation proposals

Researched 23 October 2026. This batch adds six history entries, revises the existing 2018
prosecution entry, writes three articles and converts all twelve profile text blocks to inline
citations. It extends Sint Maarten from two entries to eight, including the retained first-ban
estimate. Tags cover the substance of each event; proposals remain in the debate category.

## Sources read and dates

The official Overheid.nl legislative metadata records the 28 March 1960 ordinance commencing
on **1 June 1964** under the decree of 29 April 1964. The archived Netherlands Antilles
consolidation expressly replaces the ordinance of 30 July 1929. This establishes continuity
of drug controls, but does not recover the earliest cannabis-specific ban. The pre-existing
estimated first restriction and its uncertainty window remain unchanged. Later consolidated
cannabis definitions, exemptions and penalties are not backdated to 1960 or 1964. The current
catalogue's 2015 version date alone does not establish a new penalty event.

The original prosecution guideline PDF was read at pp. 1–2 and 5–6, with relevant surrounding
text, from the retained Dutch Caribbean research files. It applies to Sint Maarten, Curaçao
and the Caribbean Netherlands from **1 December 2018**, replacing July 2015 guidance. The
0–25 g band provides confiscation and potentially conditional dismissal. The 25–50 g band
provides a fine request up to NAf 500; from 50 g the cocaine/heroin requests are halved.
The table overlaps at its printed boundaries, so entries retain its own bands. These are
prosecutors' decisions and sentencing requests, not observed judicial outcomes. Exceptions
include minors, repeat offending, dealing indications, another offence and specified settings.
The separate discussion calls for prosecution of minors even with 0–5 g to support assistance.
Grow cases receive individual assessments. Aruba's different thresholds are not imported.

The full September 2022 government announcement was read in its NEWS.SX reproduction.
It gives 12 September as launch and 5 December as deadline, despite also saying 90 days.
The fixed deadline is used; no duration is inferred. The announcement invites studies and
proposals, and does not document a completed consultant study or its findings.

The full six-page **AB 2024 no. 11** regulation was read as web-extracted PDF text, including
its explanatory notes. Its search metadata incorrectly names petroleum prices; its actual body
is the cannabis committee regulation. It takes effect on signing, **28 February 2024**
(the justice minister's signature is dated 27 February), and was published **6 March**.
It creates an inter-ministerial committee and a programme of research and recommendations;
it does not itself grant adult-use possession, cultivation or retail rights. Its description
of Aruba is a comparison the committee was tasked to consider, not independent verification
of Aruba's current medical access.

Parliament's March notice and April follow-up were read in full. The latter confirms the
13 March 2026 meeting was adjourned and announces a 9 April continuation. The March event
is corroborated by the full SMN News report of Native Nations SXM's presentation. Industry
job, tax and economic forecasts were not treated as established outcomes or included as facts.
No later hearing date is inferred from inconsistent annual-report search snippets.

The full Daily Herald report, syndicated by SXM Talks on 25 August 2026, describes remarks
made during the Monday budget debate: **24 August** is the event date. The full relevant
SMN News report of **14 October 2026** records Claret Connor seeking PAHO assistance.
It establishes a request, not PAHO acceptance or a completed assessment. Ministers' statements
about health risks are reported as their policy concerns; secondary clinical claims are not
used as independently verified medical evidence.

## Profile and score treatment

The current official ordinance was read directly, particularly articles 1, 2a, 3a, 4 and 6–8.
Article 7(3) expressly exempts possession of lawfully obtained amounts needed for personal
medical use, alongside professional medical uses. Article 7(2) covers authorised pharmacists
and practitioners. The profile's medical label changes from **none to limited** to reflect
these statutory exemptions. An operating cannabis patient programme, pharmacy inventory or
routine prescribing pathway was not verified. Unsupported travel-guide claims of a 2019
programme are not repeated. The profile spells out the exemption and the ongoing review.

All six numeric scores, dependency, recreational status, club status and visitor status are
preserved. No proposal is scored as an implemented reform. No broad statutory maximum is
presented as the normal punishment for a small personal quantity. The profile and articles
explain the prosecution policy in terms of requests rather than automatic court sentences.
