## Reading and applications

### Matthews: the Cup as a social occasion and a business

Read selected pages of chapter 9, *Cup Fever*: printed 155–160 and 165–171 in the
2003 edition. Pages 161–164 remain outside credited coverage. The chapter explicitly
identifies the 1998 event. It describes the Pax Party House exhibition, shop tours,
Melkweg ceremony, Herer signing hemp-paper books and complaints about promotional
spending. Applied these details to `culture/traditions/the-cannabis-cup.md`.

Matthews attended the event and interviewed participants. His account of Council,
however, comes from the programme because he missed those sessions. Kept this
distinction in the entry. Recorded complaints are not proof that an award was
purchased; the book’s numbers, legal summaries about Switzerland and medical
claims were not imported. The 1999 acknowledgements also explain that some people
receive pseudonyms, a useful constraint on future biographical applications.

The comparison with the Swiss contest and Bernard Rappaz, pp. 167–169, is a useful
next lead, but its first-event date and the described legal position need original
Swiss records before an event or legal-history change.

### Gautier: serving the preparation before dinner

Examined *The Club of Assassins*, sections 1, 3 and 9 in Stang’s translation.
Applied the sequence in sections 1 and 3 to
`culture/traditions/club-des-hashischins.md`: green paste on saucers, coffee with
grounds and without sugar, then dinner. This supports the social setting of an
existing entry without manufacturing a new dated event. The narrator’s distorted
tastes and visions remain literary representations, not a clinical record.

The modern translator, original author and original publication date are kept
separate. The anthology’s introductory material and its Assassins story are not
independent evidence of an organised medieval drug cult.

### Baudelaire: the earlier moral comparison

Read sections 5–7 of *Wine and Hashish*, especially section 6’s contrast between
wine, sociability and work, and hashish, isolation and loss of will. Section 7
endorses Barbereau’s preference for poetic effort without an intoxicant.
Added the 1851 precursor to `articles/france-paradis-artificiels-1860.md`.
The [Fayard edition notice](https://www.fayard.fr/livre/du-vin-et-du-haschich-9782755507645/)
also dates the original essay to 1851. The local anthology’s copyright page gives
1851 independently. No exact day of original publication claimed.

**Egypt lead:** section 6 asserts that the Egyptian government forbids sale and
traffic in hashish. It supplies no decree, issuer, date or territorial scope.
Do not use this to replace the atlas’s documented nineteenth-century ban dates.
Locate the original French wording and underlying Egyptian measure first. A
contemporary literary assertion may show what Baudelaire believed, while still
being wrong about the law.

### Boullosa and Wallace: enforcement and chronology

Read chapters 2 (*1940s–1950s*) and 5 (*1988*) in full; selected passages of chapter
1 were examined for cannabis and prohibition. Chapter 5 is principally political
context, with no new cannabis-specific application in this pass.

Chapter 2 dates movement of anti-drug enforcement into the PGR to 1947 and links it
to centralisation of federal authority. This is a useful enforcement-history lead,
not proof of a new substantive cannabis offence. Its 1948 Great Campaign is
explicitly described as destruction of **poppies**; it should not become a
cannabis eradication entry merely because cannabis appears elsewhere in the book.
The wartime US–Mexican morphine agreement is presented as disputed by the authors,
not established fact.

Chapter 1 moves quickly from Salazar Viniegra’s 1938 argument through clinics,
embargo and closure. Compare the actual 1940 regulation and existing
`articles/mexico-brief-legalisation-1940.md` before adding dates or claiming
cannabis retail was legalised. Keep opiate maintenance distinct from cannabis
policy. No new timeline event was created from this compressed narrative.

### Carneiro: regulation after prohibition

Read chapter 5’s concluding section, *O fim dos monopólios ou sua extensão à
maconha?*, including its cannabis discussion and note 270. It compares public
alcohol distribution with proposed cannabis distribution through LCBO/SAQ-related
bodies. Useful questions concern who may produce, sell and use, provincial taxes,
public consumption and the limits of legalisation.

The passage still uses a planned **1 July 2018** cannabis date and a November 2017
newspaper reference. It cannot establish actual commencement or subsequent
Ontario retail arrangements. No present-day rules, scores, statistics or clinical
claims were changed. Chapters 2, 8 and 9 remain priority reading leads rather
than credited reading.
