# Togo: the missing 2015 penalty rewrite and later cannabis seizures

Researched 21 October 2026 against the current writing rules, category priority and tags.
Togo began with six history events and one article. This pass adds three tagged events
and two articles, revises two events and rewrites the 1998 article. The country profile
now describes the 2015 small-quantity rule instead of presenting 1998 tariffs as current.

## Primary sources actually read

- [1998 drug law, UNODC SHERLOC reproduction](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/tgo/loi-no--98-008_html/Togo-Loi_98-008.pdf):
  downloaded and extracted. Read definitions/classification and Articles 6–14, 98–113.
  **The hosted file is an excerpt ending midway through Article 113.** It is not a full
  copy of the law and cannot support claims about later treatment or commencement
  clauses. Cover identifies the law of 18 March 1998; retain that signing date for the
  existing event, without claiming to have recovered its final commencement clause.
  Article 112 covers small quantities held, bought or cultivated for personal use:
  non-oil cannabis derivatives **1–6 months AND 25,000–500,000 CFA francs OR either
  alone**; high-risk substances including cannabis oil **2 months–1 year AND
  25,000–1.25 million OR either alone**; lower-risk substances 15 days–3 months AND
  25,000–500,000 OR either alone. No numerical quantity is specified. General high-risk
  cultivation and supply Articles 98–99: **10–20 years AND 250,000–125 million OR
  either alone**. Giving for personal consumption Article 101: **3–5 years AND
  25,000–2.5 million OR either alone**. Article 10 bans cultivation nationwide and 11
  requires landholders to destroy plants. Article 111 bans non-prescribed use and
  allows confiscation without prosecution. The waiver list is described without
  asserting that a promise alone always excuses an adult first offender.
- [2015 criminal code, signed government copy](https://jo.gouv.tg/sites/default/files/publications/LOI_2015_010.pdf):
  full 134-page PDF downloaded, 18,739,762 bytes. Initial transfer timed out; resumption
  completed before extraction and substantive reliance. Read the relevant chapter,
  Articles 248–278, and final 1203–1205. Pages 49 (personal use) and 52 (treatment)
  rendered and visually checked. Article 267 sets **1–3 years AND 500,000–3 million
  OR either alone** for small personal-use quantities held, bought or cultivated. It
  gives no numerical quantity. It replaces the older personal-use category tariffs
  with one range. Its opening cross-reference names 254 and 255, although the clause
  itself expressly includes cultivation; no further judicial interpretation is claimed.
  Articles 253–254: **20–30 years AND 50 million–100 million OR either alone** for
  general high-risk production/supply; 256: **5–10 years AND 500,000–25 million OR
  either alone** for giving/offering for another person's consumption. Article 270
  doubles maxima for listed aggravations, capped at 50 years. It is not an automatic
  50-year sentence for every cannabis offence. Article 278 permits treatment instead
  of punishment for a dependent person prosecuted under 267–269, and in addition to
  punishment for other drug offences. Avoiding ordered treatment carries 1–5 years
  AND 500,000–10 million OR either alone. Article 276 permits territorial exclusion
  of foreigners for the enumerated production/supply offences; it is not evidence of
  systematically harsher personal-use enforcement against foreigners.
- [Togo's report to the Committee against Torture, CAT/C/TGO/3](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=dDYIcuxjHRapvwS7IOreaAdLvjKP8raZbUiAlMvsV%2BhQHJAme28r5OlnkaWFahAwpAhn0Zhk%2FsU170Ch9XZO5g%3D%3D):
  read cover and paragraph 6 through the web tool. Received 11 April 2018; expressly
  says the new code entered into force **25 November 2015**, after signature on 24
  November. Use commencement for the new event. The report also identifies the 2016
  amendment concerning torture; this is not a new cannabis event.
- [Contemporary account of the code reform, 22 June 2015](https://www.republicoftogo.com/toutes-les-rubriques/justice/un-code-penal-tout-neuf):
  full article read. Special parliamentary commission at Kpalimé, Assembly president
  Dama Dramani, justice minister Koffi Esaw and UN human-rights representative present.
  Broader objectives: modernise the 35-year-old code, fill offence definitions,
  implement international commitments and develop alternatives for minor offences to
  reduce prison overcrowding. Do not attribute those broad goals to cannabis-specific
  campaigning or infer an unreported reaction to the drug sentences.
- [ATOP's 17 September 2020 seizure report](https://atop.tg/lutte-contre-la-criminalite-transnationale-une-importante-saisie-de-cannabis-et-trois-presumes-trafiquants-presentes-a-la-presse/):
  web open returned 503 but the full original HTML downloaded successfully and its
  article body was read. Presentation **16 September**; interception night 14–15
  September. 1,784 blocks, **1,541.60 kg**, on a Ghanaian-registered 60-seat bus,
  cannabis hidden among second-hand clothes. Three suspects of Beninese, Ghanaian and
  Togolese nationality; authorities alleged Ghana–Togo–Benin routing. This is an arrest
  and seizure report, **not a conviction or proof of an individual sentence**. ATOP
  describes the cannabis as a high-risk drug, supporting contemporary official use of
  that category. A complete health-ministry scheduling order remains a follow-up lead.
- [January 2026 destruction report](https://www.republicoftogo.com/toutes-les-rubriques/societe/le-togo-detruit-6-tonnes-de-drogues-saisies-en-2024):
  full article read. Published **15 January**; says Wednesday ceremony. Event retains
  the report date and describes what Togo reported. Headline and introduction explicitly
  identify **2024** seizures, despite the body also using “last year.” More than 5,291
  kg cannabis in 5,729.05 kg overall; also 51.77 kg cocaine, 8.5 kg methamphetamine and
  377.78 kg illicit psychotropics. Do not silently relabel these as 2025 seizures.
- [June 2026 seizure report](https://www.republicoftogo.com/toutes-les-rubriques/societe/plus-de-50-tonnes-de-drogues-saisies-l-annee-derniere):
  original HTML downloaded and full article read; publication metadata **28 June 2026**.
  Authorities report over 50 tonnes of drugs seized in **2025**, mainly cannabis,
  52 kg cocaine and psychotropics. No exact cannabis tonnage is supplied. Separate this
  report from the January entry. Port and Abidjan–Lagos corridor context is attributed
  to the report. Seizure weights establish neither consumption prevalence nor how
  frequently individual users receive prison sentences.

## Categories, profile and score boundaries

The 1998 entry becomes `other`: the recovered text establishes a comprehensive law,
without a verified comparison showing whether it tightened or eased the preceding
national regime. It is not treated as the first prohibition. The 2015 entry is
`tightening` because the cannabis personal-use prison/fine ranges rise. It is expressly
unscored: the fine-only option remains and the available evidence does not establish a
whole-point change in the enforcement rubric or another segment. The two seizure
reports and bus case are `enforcement`. All five new/revised entries have valid tags.

All six current scores and recreational/medical status values are **preserved, not
newly validated**. The inherited 1900–1920 first-restriction estimate and all `before`
vectors remain untouched. The score chain is mechanically clean, but the colonial
starting point and current enforcement practice need better evidence. Togo was not
simply treated as a French colony throughout that window.

The profile no longer relies on Leafwell for penalty ranges or claims that foreigners
are especially severely prosecuted. It distinguishes the small-personal-use clause,
giving for consumption, general supply, aggravation and treatment. Unsupported CBD
classification is replaced with Article 5’s rules for preparations and mixtures.
Seed/clone-status claims are removed: neither missing mention of
seeds nor the broad cannabis ban proves the specific seed-trade categories. Club text
now describes the restrictions on collective growing/sharing without claiming to have
surveyed all organisations. Health licensing/prescription language is described from
the primary texts; no operational patient supply scheme is inferred from it.

## Remaining source limits

The full 1998 UN legal-notification copy (E/NL.1998/2) and alternate UN download were
inaccessible through attempted routes. The older 1980 code located in WIPO is a later
consolidation, not a verified as-enacted cannabis restriction: no event invented from
it. The 2016 torture amendment and 2022 domestic/sexual-violence amendment surfaced in
primary-government reports and parliamentary records, but a complete consolidated
criminal code and all health scheduling updates were not recovered. Do not describe
this pass as a comprehensive certification of every amendment through 2026. Travel
advice still repeating a generic twenty-year ceiling does not override the original
2015 thirty-year trafficking range and fifty-year aggravated ceiling.
