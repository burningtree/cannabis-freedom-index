## Important distinctions

The referral to Finance at the top of February p. 11 belongs to the previous
petition, not cannabis. The new text does not misattribute it. November p. 9
directs referral to Health once committees were constituted; a referral does
not approve the petition's substantive requests.

Halake expressly declined to support or oppose the petition while defending
investigation. Do not label that intervention an endorsement of legalisation.
The November petition says Kenya ratified the Single Convention before independence;
the site's checked 1964 ratification event contradicts that claim. The claim is
not repeated as fact. Carrier's notes 63 and 70 say November 27 but link the
November 29 Hansard; the examined document's printed date takes precedence.

The chapter's historical fee figures for Lesotho vary, and contemporary policy,
prevalence and market rankings require newer verification. No present law or score
is inferred from its 2010s discussion. Allegations about violence, clinical benefits
or the effects of cannabis in parliamentary speeches are arguments by participants.
The entry does not treat them as established findings.

Further cultural leads: Parker Shipton's *Bitter Money* for Luo judgments about
cannabis proceeds, and Neil Savishinsky's Baye Faal ethnography. The chapter's brief
references are not enough to create full traditions or imply that all Baye Faal
are Rastafarian or use cannabis. No extra cultural entry padded from a single line.
