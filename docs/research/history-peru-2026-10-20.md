# Peru drug-law history — 20 October 2026

Added articles for Decree-Law 11005 and the missing 1972 consumer/trafficking
change. Added one unscored event and corrected the 1921 law's date and account.

## Originals and discrepancies

Downloaded congressional scans of laws 4428, 11005 and 19505 to ignored
`tmp/pdfs/peru-2026-10-20/`. All are image-only: text extraction was empty.
Rendered and visually read both pages of 4428, all four pages of 11005 and both
pages of 19505. The original 1949 scan, not a later policy summary, establishes
28 March. The 1972 scan repeats March in its heading but uses May in Article 1's
reference to the earlier law; this internal inconsistency does not change the
original's explicit execution date.

Law 4428's actual list includes cocaine, omitted in our former description.
It contains registration and prescription controls as well as penal provisions.
The executive date is 26 November 1921; congressional passage was 21 November.
The original does not name cannabis, but that absence alone does not prove the
absence of every possible contemporaneous restriction elsewhere.

The 1994 national plan's retrospective review was read at printed pp. 389–391.
It gives a conflicting May 1949 date and attributes some features already in
11005 to the 1972 amendment. The new event relies on the actual 19505 text:
Article 1 omits purchase/use, Article 4 substitutes Article 12, and Article 8
creates the separate consumer process. The articles do not adopt the summary's
misleading account of a uniformly punitive expansion in 1972.

No claim is made about the first publication or commencement dates of these
instruments beyond their recorded executive dates. No enforcement frequency is
inferred. The 1972 event omits `before` because the separate judicial process,
including potential confinement, does not establish a simple score vector.
