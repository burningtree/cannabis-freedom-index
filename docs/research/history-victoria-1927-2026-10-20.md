## Evidence and limits

- Downloaded the actual Parliament of Victoria Legislative Council minutes for
  1927. The Poisons Bill entry at p. xxxvii, PDF p. 35, records final agreement
  on 14 December and assent on 21 December, Act No. 3542. Visually checked the
  page against extracted text. The event date records assent, not a verified
  separate commencement day; its title and opening explicitly describe passage.
- Downloaded and read the official 27–29 September 1927 Hansard PDF. Beckett’s
  explanation at pp. 1526–1530 supplies the minister, the wider poison-law
  context, the Commonwealth request to states, the international agreement and
  reference to British law. Visually checked p. 1529, PDF p. 79. Those statements
  describe the government’s rationale; the article does not claim that the
  treaty itself automatically created a Victorian possession offence.
- Read the 4 October debate too, using the official weekly PDF whose filename
  incorrectly ends `19270906`. It mainly discusses cyanide. Its detailed
  provisions and penalties were not transferred to cannabis. Likewise, the
  June 1928 Gazette’s cyanide regulations do not prove when the cannabis rules
  commenced. The initial search snippets contained misleading dates and were
  not used as evidence.
- Downloaded and read `Drug Policy and Results in Australia` (UNODC, October
  2008), especially p. 23. It identifies the Poisons Act 1927 as penalising
  unauthorised use of Indian hemp and resin and supplies the later state
  chronology. Its broad historical account is not an original statutory text.
  The report carries a notice that it is not an official UN document. Its
  evaluative claims about the success of prohibition and causal effects on
  prevalence are not repeated in the article.
- Attempts to obtain the original Act from AustLII failed with access errors;
  the guessed Victorian Legislation title page returned 404. A search-indexed
  Melbourne law-review paper also returned 404, and the old NSW parliamentary
  research PDF redirected to a landing page. None supplied statutory evidence.
  Consequently no precise cannabis penalty, exception, possession offence,
  cultivation rule or complete cannabis definition is asserted here.
- Existing references to 1928 in later secondary histories may concern the
  start of operation or the later consolidation, but neither explanation has
  been verified. The original entry wrongly said Victoria passed its Act in
  1928 and called it the first state control of any kind. Verified assent in
  1927 replaces the passage date; the sourced Western Australian 1899 sales
  controls are linked, without claiming that every state's first sales rule
  and later penal-use prohibition were identical.
- Category is `tightening`: this extends Australian controls beyond the
  existing federal/import and poison-sales history. It is passed legislation,
  not `debate`, and the central change concerns cannabis use, not fibre hemp.
  No cross-cutting tag is needed: this is neither a court ruling nor a treaty
  ratification. Added an explicit unscored explanation; no national score
  impact was inferred from this state law, and existing vectors are preserved.
