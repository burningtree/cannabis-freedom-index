# Faroe Islands: filling a two-entry history

Reviewed 23 October 2026. Before this batch, the territory had only an estimated first restriction
and an April 2019 parliamentary debate. Added five milestones and three articles, revised the
existing debate, and checked current profile claims against primary legal and health sources.

## Earlier controls: a dated order, but no verified first ban

Read the [2015 consolidated Faroese drug order](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2015/1349/Pdf)
in full and its [official Faroese register copy](https://logir.fo/ViewLaw.aspx?id=1711314942).
The preamble expressly identifies Order 347 of **21 December 1956**, followed by amendments in
1969, 1988 and 2014. Section 4 replaces a 1936 opium law and carries its drug controls and
subordinate rules forward.

Added the 1956 order as a context event, category `other`, tag `trade`. Its date is the order's
stated date, not an independently established commencement day. No modern consolidated penalty
or modern cannabis schedule was backdated to 1956.

The original 1956 order and 1969 amendment were not recovered: guessed historical Retsinformation
PDF addresses returned 404. A guessed Logir address returned a search/listing page with HTTP 200,
not the original order. The old estimated first restriction is preserved, including its score
vector and window. It remains unresolved: the 1956 order's reference to earlier law demonstrates
why it cannot simply be relabelled the first cannabis ban. This follows the maintenance rule to
record a recovered later statute as context while leaving a first-ban estimate in place. Recovery
of the original cannabis schedules and their territorial application remains a priority.

## December 2014: sentencing and groups of drugs

Downloaded and read [Order 1321 of 2 December 2014](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2014/1321/Pdf),
including both amendment and commencement clauses. Visually checked the first gazette page.
Its new sentencing direction treats sales, and unpaid transfers for subsequent sale, at
restaurants/nightclubs, concerts, festivals and events mainly attended by children or young
people as significantly aggravating. Drug harmfulness must inform sentencing and fines.
A separate repeated-sale provision concerns particularly dangerous/harmful drugs; it is not
represented as specifically a cannabis-only rule. The update also permits substance-group controls.

Read the [Faroese health ministry's proposal of 26 March 2014](https://www.logting.fo/files/casestate/14449/144.13%20U.t.r.%20om%20euforiserende%20stoffer.pdf),
general remarks pp. 1–2. Police and customs had requested modernisation because newer drugs
escaped the existing lists. Danish amendments did not automatically apply to the territory;
a separate order was needed. This supplies context, not a claim that ordinary cannabis itself
was newly scheduled in 2014.

The order commences the day after local publication. The official consolidation's footnote
records publication in Kunngerðablaðið on **19 December 2014**, giving **20 December 2014**.
The Danish gazette publication on 12 December is not used as the effective date.

Added a tightening event with `sentencing`, `youth` and `retail` tags and an explicit unscored
reason. The recovered directions do not establish a whole-point change to ordinary personal-use
freedom. Added a 372-word article with four headings and per-sentence source marks.

## Campaign and medical access, 2018–2019

Read the complete substantive bodies of the public broadcaster's reports:

- [19 July 2018: new cannabis party](https://kvf.fo/greinar/2018/07/19/new-political-party-aims-cannabis-legalisation).
  Founder Hallur Hjalgrímsson Djurhuus sought 800 signatures, with about 250 collected. His
  objective included growing and using cannabis, with particular priority for patients. The party
  proposed neutrality on unrelated political questions.
- [15 February 2019: signatures submitted](https://kvf.fo/node/91255). The founders handed the
  800 signatures to the Ministry of the Interior. This does not by itself establish ballot
  certification, election results, seats, or that the party caused the later medical change.
- [22 March 2018: eleven medicinal products](https://kvf.fo/greinar/2018/03/22/patients-can-receive-cannabis).
  GPs could prescribe eleven cannabis-containing products, but flower and buds under the Danish
  trial were not then approved in the islands. This is dated background, not a newly invented
  March 2018 authorisation event.
- [3 April 2019: renewed parliamentary debate](https://kvf.fo/greinar/2019/04/03/parliament-makes-u-turn-medical-cannabis).
  Jenis á Rana's earlier proposal had been rejected. Several MPs now supported access while
  arguing that an administrative product-list route could suffice. Health minister Sirið Stenberg
  offered discussion with the social affairs committee. The existing debate was revised to this
  full report date and converted to inline citations.
- [22 July 2019: flower ordered](https://kvf.fo/node/96968). A GP requested cannabis buds, and the
  Pharmacy Service ordered from the Netherlands. The report mentions 61 patients on cannabis
  drops. It establishes an order, not the date of delivery or a broad retail market.

Added a campaign event for July 2018, a medical event for April 2019 and a separate flower-order
milestone for July 2019. Campaign tags cover patients and home growing; no growing permission is
inferred. The campaign article is 368 words, with four headings.

The previous profile left the outcome of the trial debate unconfirmed. Read the [Chief Medical
Officer's account](https://landslaeknin.stps.dk/heilivagur/medisinskt-kannabis), the [Pharmacy
Service's account](https://www.apotek.fo/heilivagur/medisinskt-kannabis), and downloaded the actual
[joint prescribing guidance](https://landslaeknin.stps.dk/Media/638234707850762013/Medicinsk%20cannabis%20-%20Vejledning%20til%20brug%20p%C3%A5%20F%C3%A6r%C3%B8erne.pdf),
PDF pp. 2–3. All confirm the scheme began in **April 2019**. No exact day is established.
An old pharmacy download address returned 404; the medical officer's current page linked the
working PDF. A legacy publication page carries a January 2019 date inconsistent with its April
retrospect; that metadata was not used to date the opening.

The guidance recommends conventional treatment first, documentation, patient involvement and
follow-up. It identifies pain, multiple sclerosis, spinal-cord injury and chemotherapy-related
nausea, with recommended specialist roles. Those recommendations are not recast as absolute
statutory eligibility rules. Obsolete blanket wording about cannabis not being authorised as a
medicine anywhere was not repeated. A 385-word article explains the opening and supply process.

The medical milestones remain unscored: the existing access score already represents limited
prescription supply, including medicines available before April 2019. No unsupported access rise
was assigned to the trial opening or an unfulfilled order.

## Current profile and primary-law checks

Downloaded [the regulations effective15 June 2026](https://www.lovtidende.dk/api/pdf/256275),
read §§1,3–6,16,33 and list B item83, and visually checked the cannabis definition on PDF page14.
These support medical/scientific handling, prescription possession, licensed cultivation and
commercial low-THC cultivation with a **0.2% ceiling**, annual permissions and plots of at least
**0.3 hectares**. Seeds and isolated fibre are excluded from the listed cannabis definition;
cultivation is separately regulated. Corrected the unsupported blanket statement that there is
no licensed production route. The source establishes permissions, not an operating cannabis farm.

The 2026 regulations replace the 2025 regulations. No event claims that every restated cannabis
rule was newly introduced in 2026: that would require comparison with the predecessor.

Read [the current Road Traffic Act](https://logir.fo/Logtingslogarkunngerd/14-fra-02-03-1988-um-ferdslu-sum-seinast-broytt-vid-logtingslog-nr-49-fra-11),
§15(1)–(2). It prohibits driving with **more than 0.001 mg THC per kg of blood** outside the lawful
prescription exception. Use contrary to a prescription is also covered; unsafe driving is
separately prohibited. The new context block is `limit`, not an inference of zero tolerance from
medical advice. Pharmacy guidance separately tells medical-cannabis patients not to drive, and
is attributed as that guidance. The consolidation links relevant changes to Law 74 of 30 April2025;
the original amendment and exact commencement have not yet been recovered, so no dated historical
THC-limit change was added. This is a concrete follow-up lead.

Read the [Faroese Police's 2025 statistics](https://politi.fo/-/media/mediefiler/fo/dokumenter/hagtoel/aarsstatistik-2025.pdf),
foreword and printed p.16. It expressly says small personal quantities are prosecuted when found,
with strategic emphasis on organisers and larger middlemen. There were 62 drug-law reports in 2025,
107 in 2024 and 142 in 2021; these cover all drugs. Hash seizures were 29.58 kg in 2025, 69.69 kg in 2024
and 16.25 kg in 2021. The profile now uses the latest year and distinguishes reports from arrests.

All 16 score/context blocks now have inline key citations. Added planting, driving, arrests and
black-market context. Current six scores, recreational/medical classifications, dependency and
existing club/visitor statuses remain unchanged. No unrelated profile was edited.
