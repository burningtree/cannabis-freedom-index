   **A/HRC/59/24**, paragraph **36**, says the 2015 codes had not been brought

Original profile's FAOLEX copy remains a preserved bibliography entry:

Fawzia Hashim's foreword is dated **15 May 2015**. It describes international
specialists and an Eritrean law-reform committee, followed by consultation
with judges, prosecutors, attorneys, lecturers and police. Keep the event
at **2015**, publication year; do not use the foreword date as proven Gazette
commencement. The foreword/Prelude explains the intention to incorporate
customary principles and respond to newer offences, including narcotics.

The personal-use clauses specify **6–12 months OR 20,001–50,000 nakfa**;
these are the **published replacement's** provisions, not established current
penalties. Article 377 includes giving and transferring within trafficking;
Article 381 excludes merely buying for own use from complicity. Article 376
leaves controlled substances and quantity definitions to separate Drug
Offences Regulations, which were not recovered. Neither statute page alone
establishes the operative cannabis schedule. The revised history article
reports the code's content and its delayed implementation together.

NATLEX record **101051** was read: catalogues the English expression as an

publication as at **September 2017**. This corroborates the publication issue

## The Justice Ministry's download is the older code

Read the ministry's laws page, followed its exact Penal Code link and
retrieved the **295-page** document. It begins **The Penal Code of Ethiopia
1957, Proclamation 158/1957**, not the 2015 text. The file name alone is
ambiguous: local filename `ministry-code-2015.pdf` was the initial research
label before the contents were checked and must not be read as its identity.

https://mojeritrea.net/laws/
https://mojeritrea.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Penal-Code.pdf

Read the complete **Article 510**, visually checked **PDF pp. 164–165**.
It covers unauthorised production/manufacture/transformation/import/export/
transport/acquisition/receipt/storage/offering/distribution/procuring for
another of poisons, drugs or narcotic substances. Its ordinary penalty is
**simple imprisonment ≥3 months AND fine ≤20,000 historical dollars**;
subsection 2 extends the same punishment to knowingly providing premises,
even privately, for drug taking. Subsection 3 allows **rigorous imprisonment
≤5 years AND fine ≤30,000 historical dollars** for organised/professional
traffic or specified knowing supply/premises cases involving vulnerable
people with the specified gain/improper-motive element. Article 105's
ordinary simple-imprisonment range was also read. Do not invent current
nakfa conversion or assume the original money amounts survive unchanged.

The new 1991 article identifies that older text as the underlying system;
it does not certify every current amendment or a modern cannabis-specific
personal-possession tariff. It reports the prison minimum but omits old
fine amounts and unsupported contemporary rates. The ministry's download
is primary evidence of the text it publishes, not a complete modern
consolidation or proof of every subsidiary control rule.
