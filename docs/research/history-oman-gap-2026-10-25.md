# Oman: personal holdings, capital offences and treatment chronology

Research date: 25 October 2026. The previous Yemen pass identified the September
2026 law as a lead. The authoritative checkout already contained that law and an
updated Oman profile, so this pass concentrated on the poorly supported earlier
chronology and on comparing the actual statutes. Oman had six events at baseline.
Two entries and two articles are added; the 1999, 2015 and 2026 entries and the two
existing articles are revised. Maintenance, writing, score-level and book rules
were reviewed. No book was used.

## Primary texts read

- [Original 1974 Penal Code reproduced in Arabic by Qanoon.om](https://qanoon.om/p/1974/rd1974007/).
  Read the commencement text, general punishment classifications and articles
  229–231. The code starts on publication; Gazette 52 was published 1 April 1974,
  after the 16 February decree. Personal holdings/purchase carried three months
  to two years and 50–500 rials. Trading, holdings for paid or unpaid transfer,
  and facilitating use carried three–ten years and 300–500 rials. Medical authority
  or a licensed doctor's written prescription supplied exceptions. The code's
  general drug provisions do not independently define cannabis. This is a context
  step in the history of prohibition, not a verified first cannabis ban. A
  [later English copy held by Police Human Rights Resources](https://policehumanrightsresources.org/content/uploads/2016/07/Penal-Code-Oman-1974.pdf)
  was downloaded/text-extracted as a comparison lead. It renders the personal-use
  fine with “or,” whereas the Arabic text uses “and”; it is not the authority for
  the entry or its fine conjunction.
- [Original 1999 drug law, Arabic transcription](https://qanoon.om/p/1999/rd1999017/).
  Read the decree, articles 1–4, 7, 20, 43–52 and 64–66, and the relevant cannabis
  schedules. This is the original decree page, not the linked consolidated text
  amended through 2023. Article 4 starts the law on publication, 15 March 1999,
  Gazette 643. Article 47 sets a one-year minimum and 500–3,000-rial fine for
  personal holdings and growing; its own wording does not state a three-year
  maximum. The 1974 code's general classifications were checked, but the public
  account keeps the original article's minimum rather than presenting the later
  express range as a verbatim 1999 phrase. Articles 47–48 already allow residential
  and outpatient treatment for established dependence; residence is six months
  to one year. Articles 51–52 already protect voluntary/family treatment approaches
  before arrest/prosecution and confidentiality. Article 43 provides death or life
  for specified import/production/growing offences and mandatory death in listed
  aggravated cases. It contains no 50 kg threshold. Article 44 starts the separate
  trading offence at ten years; article 45 sets five–ten for unpaid supply.
- [2013 Health Ministry Decision 53, Arabic transcription](https://qanoon.om/p/2013/moh20130053/),
  compared with the actual [Ministry of Legal Affairs Gazette 1010 PDF](https://www.mjla.gov.om/modules/legislation/download.php?file=52),
  printed/PDF pp. 23–26. All four relevant pages were read, extracted and rendered
  for visual inspection; the cover verifies publication on 27 April. Article 4
  starts the decision the following day, 28 April 2013. The tables add named
  substances including HU-210, CP-47497 and JWH-018 to group-one schedule one,
  alongside other listed compounds. The decision also lists tramadol separately;
  that is not the cannabis-history claim. The
  [UNODC scientific service's classification](https://www.unodc.org/lss/substancegroup/details/ae45ce06-6d33-4f5f-916a-e873f07bde02)
  was read to verify the three examples as synthetic cannabinoids. No claims about
  current international control, health effects or local market prevalence are
  taken from that dated background page. The event concerns named additions, not
  a claim that ordinary cannabis or every synthetic cannabinoid first became
  controlled then.
- [2015 Royal Decree 34, complete Arabic text](https://qanoon.om/p/2015/rd2015034/).
  Read all replacements and the final clause. Gazette 1118 is dated 11 October;
  final clause five starts the amendments the next day, 12 October. Article 43
  adds smuggling for trade to the mandatory-death cases; the older text already
  allowed death or life for the conduct. Both versions already include officials,
  minors and international gangs among aggravating circumstances. The amendment
  is not a removal of a 50 kg threshold or death for every sale. Article 47
  expressly sets one–three years and 500–3,000 rials, and expressly applies this
  penalty to consumption. Added article 64 bis criminalises sample refusal in
  specified settings with six months–three years and 100–1,000 rials. Treatment
  remains available. Articles 44–45 were not among the replaced provisions.
- [2026 replacement law, Arabic text](https://qanoon.om/p/2026/rd2026067/).
  Read the decree and relevant law provisions, including 1–3, 7–9, 15–16, 27,
  37–44, 51 and 57–69, plus cannabis schedules. The decree repeals the old law
  and starts the new one the day after Gazette 1664 of 6 September: 7 September.
  Article 41 keeps one–three years and raises personal-use fines to 1,000–5,000.
  Article 39 raises unpaid supply to seven–fifteen years/5,000–15,000 rials;
  article 38 raises the separate trading minimum to fifteen years. Capital
  import/production/growing rules remain distinct from that trading offence.
  Treatment and confidentiality are continuations of older routes. Article 43
  extends the family approach to fourth-degree relatives when no second-degree
  relative exists. Article 57 adds a prosecution arrest order and reports by
  relatives to the sample-refusal settings. Article 61's deportation rule has
  a residual-offence exception and also reaches stated accusation/acquittal
  circumstances. Article 60 provides additional measures for convicted Omanis.
- [Royal Decree 24/2023](https://qanoon.om/p/2023/rd2023024/) was read in full
  to audit the consolidated law's amendment history. It moves and renames the
  national committee under the National Security Council, effective 26 April
  2023. It does not change the personal-use or supply penalties compared here;
  no separate event is added for that institutional change.

Qanoon.om is a reproduction service, not the official publisher. The legislative
wording was read through the web reader; local requests to its HTML and decree
PDFs returned 403. The 1999, 2015 and 2026 gazette PDFs were located but not
visually read, and are not described as inspected originals. The 2013 official
ministry PDF was successfully retrieved and visually compared. Search snippets
were leads only. Further official-gazette comparisons remain useful verification.

## Corrections and score decisions

The unsupported assertion that the 1999 death penalty applied only above 50 kg
is removed from the timeline and both existing articles. The blanket suggestion
that every dealer faced death and the claim that treatment first appeared in
2026 are replaced with the actual offence distinctions and the older treatment
route. The 2026 entry now states its concrete changes rather than merely saying
that the law was replaced. All four articles use inline citations and link the
neighbouring legal stages and current profile.

The existing one-point enforcement deduction for capital liability moves from
2015 to the verified 1999 statute. The timeline is now aggregate score 4 before
15 March 1999, then 2; today's six scores remain unchanged. This relocates the
previous deduction and does not claim a measured arrest or imprisonment trend.
The earlier aggregate and its enforcement level remain provisional: the sources
here do not establish historical prosecution frequencies. The 1974 rule already
falls within the restrictive possession/sharing levels; 2013 is a named-substance
extension; the 2015 and 2026 changes stay within the existing whole-point floors.
Each legal context event has an explicit unscored reason.

The profile now directly cites the 2026 law across all six segments. It adds the
available treatment routes, sample-refusal conditions and accurate deportation
scope, and removes unsupported absolute “None” claims about production/products.
The retained sources do not establish a specific medical-cannabis programme;
general controlled-drug prescribing and research licences are explained separately.
The pre-existing driving section and its sources are preserved.

Pre-1974 cannabis controls and the earliest domestic prohibition remain open;
the national 1925–1961 estimate is not replaced with a general penal-code date.
The wording/application of the original code's narcotics definition, additional
schedule amendments, later implementing rules, and representative enforcement
practice are further research priorities. This pass is not a complete 2026
case-law or implementing-regulation audit.
