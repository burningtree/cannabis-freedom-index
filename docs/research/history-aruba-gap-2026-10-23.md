# Aruba: CBD rules, medicinal production and revised prosecution policy

Research completed 23 October 2026. Aruba began this batch with two history entries.
Six additions bring it to eight; the 2021 prosecution entry is revised. Three articles
cover the 2019 CBD regulations, the 2020 agency and the revised 2025 prosecution instruction.
All twelve profile text blocks now use inline citations, with tags on every touched event.

## Primary sources and date decisions

The original **AB 2012 no. 51** was downloaded, read in full and its operative pages visually
inspected. Signed 29 November and published 7 December, it commenced **8 December 2012**.
It expanded cannabis coverage to seeds and leaves unaccompanied by tops, enumerated growing,
processing, sale, supply, transport, possession/use and manufacturing controls, and added police
search powers. A prosecutor can authorise clothing searches for everyone in a designated area
for at most twelve hours after the specified consultations. The entry describes these powers,
not a claim of searches actually carried out or a newly established cannabis-wide ban.
The earlier 1990 consolidation was also retrieved, but its faulty OCR and later amendments
mean it is not used to infer a new first-ban date. The existing first-restriction estimate
and window remain unchanged.

The complete **AB 2019 nos. 82, 83 and 84** were read. Each was signed 20 December, published
23 December and commenced **24 December 2019**. No. 82 designates CBD and preparations,
including combinations with THC, as medicines; its second article addresses supply of products
with no more than 0.2% THC. No. 83 designates cannabis preparations under narcotics law with
an exception for CBD preparations at that ceiling. This is not an exemption for every hemp
plant or low-THC flower. No. 84 made specified external-use products, herbs, supplements, foods
and drinks non-medicinal when they met the ceiling.

**AB 2020 no. 7** withdraws no. 84 only, because it did not fully match the intended policy.
It was signed 14 January, published 16 January and commenced **17 January 2020**. It does not
repeal nos. 82 or 83. The health inspectorate's current Papiamento guidance confirms that the
commercial CBD route is medicinal, with a 0.2% THC maximum and permission for importing,
storing and distributing after assessment of legal, quality and safety requirements. It does
not verify every product marketed locally, pharmacy inventories or patient numbers.

The agency decree in **Landscourant 3 July 2020, pp. 34–36**, was read in full. It was signed
**9 June** and expressly takes retroactive effect from **1 June 2020**, used for the event.
The government agency was assigned permits, quality supervision, records, treaty reporting and
coordination of stocks and trade. The amended 2021 request for proposals was read on both pages;
it describes limited cultivation/processing exemptions following agreements and makes awards
contingent on parliamentary legislative changes. Its projected award date is not evidence of
awarded licences or operating production. No separate event is assigned a publication date
from the filename alone. The revised organisational decree in the January 2021 gazette is
retained as a follow-up source, not used to infer an additional liberalisation.

The official XML body of **ECLI:NL:OGEAA:2025:123**, decided **14 May 2025**, was retrieved
from `data.rechtspraak.nl`. The public HTML page alone did not expose its body. The full facts
and assessment were read. They document a **20 March 2023** refusal of one business's growing
and manufacturing applications, alongside a conditional refusal of CBD import permission while
documents were awaited. The inspectorate later said the CBD refusal became final when documents
were not received. The event is about that decision affecting that business, not a nationwide
refusal policy. The 2025 judgment concerns a later failure-to-decide appeal after an actual
administrative decision had already been made; it does not grant a cultivation licence.

## The 2025 instruction was verified, not assumed from a travel guide

A derivative travel guide led to a reported 30 g revision. The old prosecution webpage and
its PDF still describe 5 g, but the prosecution service's current **Richtlijnen** index links
a separate revised instruction. Its actual six-page scanned PDF was downloaded and all pages
visually inspected; no machine-extracted text was available. Page 6 is blank. The original
2021 PDF and its table were also read, with the currently hosted signed scan visually checked.

The revised cover says evaluated/revised **16 June 2025**, retains the original 2021 effective
date, and the signature on page 4 is **14 July 2025**. The event uses the signed document date,
not an independently verified June commencement. This discrepancy remains visible in the source
locator and article. The source URL's August directory is not treated as commencement.

The new soft-drug table shows 0–30 g surrender/dismissal; 30–100 g AWG 250;
100–300 g AWG 750; 300–500 g AWG 1,500; and from 500 g half the hard-drug requests.
These are prosecution decisions and sentencing requests, not court sentences observed in use.
The printed bands overlap at endpoints; the text preserves the source's bands. Exceptions
include another offence, recidivism, minors and specified circumstances such as carnival,
workplaces and government buildings. The minor discussion still mentions 0–5 g: the broader
dismissal table must not be applied to minors indiscriminately. Resident suspended-sentence
requests and non-resident probation-report guidance are distinct from eligibility for the
quantity policy. The new table's intermediate roles are marked not applicable below 500 g;
the profile does not carry forward the old organiser/intermediary demands into the new bands.

## Scores and classifications

The verified broadening of dismissal and substitution of fines warrants **possession 3 → 4**,
consistent with the 25 g dismissal policies in Sint Maarten and Curaçao. The new 2025 easing
entry carries the exact prior six-score vector. Enforcement remains 4, cultivation 1, sharing 1,
consumption 2 and access 0. No general personal growing or sharing right was verified.
The earlier estimated first restriction's vector is preserved; the score timeline now separates
the 2025 improvement from the older baseline.

The CBD rules and supplement withdrawal are explicitly unscored for intoxicating-cannabis
access. The medical label remains **none**, following the schema's explicit exclusion of
CBD/hemp alone and future proposals; the retrieved production invitation makes awards dependent
on legislative adjustments and agreements. Creation of an administrative bureau alone does not
verify an operating patient programme. The current profile describes the verified CBD route and
production proposals rather than making the old unsupported claim that no legal products exist.
The 2012 entry has an unscored reason: broader controls/search powers without enough evidence
to assign a separate whole-point personal-use enforcement change. The agency and individual
refusal are context events. Recreational status, dependency, club and visitor statuses remain.
