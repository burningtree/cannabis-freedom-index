## Sources retained and read

### Complete 1999 ordinance, including annex

https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/ner/ordonnance-no--99-42_html/ORDONNANCE_No99-42.pdf

Signed **23 September 1999** by **Daouda Malam Wanké**, President of the National Reconciliation
Council. Preamble expressly refers to the transition and proclamation of 11 April 1999.
No separately verified gazette commencement date is claimed.

Read the relevant complete clauses and annex. Visually inspected the personal tier on PDF p. 27
and compared it with the regulator's compilation (below), PDF p. 89, printed p. 79.

- Article 8 expressly prohibits cannabis growing and requires owners/operators/occupiers to
  destroy plants growing on their land.
- Annexed Table I explicitly lists cannabis and cannabis resin; this is actual scheduling text,
  not an inference from the international convention alone.
- Article 145: small personal **possession, purchase AND growing**, no gram or plant limit.
  High-risk plant/substance including cannabis oil **two months–one year OR 500,000–1 million
  CFA OR both**; non-oil cannabis derivative **one–six months OR 250,000–500,000 OR both**.
- Waiver bullets list minority, no recidivism and a solemn undertaking. Their cumulative versus
  alternative relationship is not resolved here. Do not claim a universal adult first-offender
  entitlement merely from the latter two bullets.
- Article 144 prohibits use outside prescription and permits confiscation even without
  prosecution. It does not expressly restate the personal possession penalty for use itself.
- Article 96 general high-risk trafficking: **ten–twenty years OR 1–20 million CFA OR both**.
  Article 98 direct high-risk supply for another person's use: **one–five years OR
  100,000–500,000 CFA OR both**. The previous profile also missed these fine-only options.
- Article 113 optional foreigner exclusion permanently or ten–twenty years for **Articles
  94–102**, plus other restrictions. **Not automatically Article 145 small personal quantities**.
- Article 115 treatment/care instead of/additional to punishment for a dependent person
  convicted under the listed offences, including 145 and 146. Evading care **one–five years
  OR 100,000–500,000 CFA OR both**. Do not call this unconditional voluntary diversion.
- Article 149 expressly repeals the 1974 ordinance and 1977 decree. Its 18 November date for
  the older ordinance conflicts with the original heading and the pharmacy law, which say 8.

**Text-quality problems matter:** Article 94 is headed cultivation/production but its operative
text instead refers to export/international transport in both retained copies. Other cross-
references are inconsistent (145 points to 90/92; 98 points to 16). The UNODC copy's Article
102 contains an implausible `peine de vie` transcription, while the regulator's copy does not.
Do not invent a death-penalty event, silently fix the references, or assert the precise ordinary
cultivation penalty from this corrupt clause. The profile states the clear cannabis-growing
ban and the separate personal tier; full original-gazette reconciliation remains a follow-up.

### National pharmaceutical regulator's 2019 legal compilation

https://anrp.ne/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/recueil_textes_legislatifs-reglementaires-edition-2019.pdf

Downloaded in full. Contains Pharmacy Ordinance **97-002 of 10 January 1997**, as well as the
1999 drug ordinance. Read the pharmacy preamble and Articles 9–14/18, and relevant drug
sections. Rendered/visually inspected PDF pp. 17, 73 and 89, including printed pp. 7, 63 and 79.
The preface presents the collection as a tool for pharmacists, manufacturers, importers,
clinicians and other pharmaceutical actors within the national pharmaceutical policy.

1997 rules:

- Article 9: marketing authorisation from health minister before medicines and equivalent
  products can be sold **or supplied free**.
- Article 10: breach of that rule ten days–six months OR 100,000–500,000 CFA OR both;
  administrative/court closure powers. These are medicine-authorisation penalties, not a
  substitute for illicit narcotics penalties.
- Article 11: lists I/II/narcotics map to tables A/C/B of the **8 December 1977 decree**;
  all listed medicines require prescriptions.
- Article 12: adopts controlled-drug lists of ratified conventions; prescribing/dispensing
  details by decree.
- Articles 13–14: pharmacists in the specified pharmacies/hospitals; separate authorised-worker
  rules for depots and facilities lacking pharmacists. Do not claim no exceptions to the
  pharmacist role or a cannabis-specific patient programme.

The 1997 event is `other`, a pharmaceutical recodification and prescription control, not a
medical-cannabis programme event or recreational legalisation. Article context explains the
relationship with later illicit-drug penalties.

### 1974 original and 1977 schedules: limits of the evidence

UN government-communicated text **E/NL.1976/118**, issued **27 April 1978**:
https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1297267/files/e-nl-1976-118-f.pdf

The web index supplied the actual heading/preamble and Articles 1–3. Heading explicitly says
**8 November 1974**. Preamble identifies Supreme Military Council and the 1974 proclamation/
suspension of the constitution. Article 1 delegates import/export/purchase/sale/possession/use
rules for poisons/narcotics to decrees. Article 3's displayed start has five–less-than-ten years
OR fine OR both for narcotics-regulation breaches, but the **remaining text was not recovered**.
The timeline entry therefore claims only the regulatory law's date, scope and later replacement;
it does not extrapolate its incomplete penalty excerpt to all users or growers.

Direct UN PDF attempts produced empty content/403 and the symbol API returned HTML. No
1974 PDF was rendered and no article based on an assumed complete text is written. The entry's
narrow claims were checked against the readable original heading/Article 1 and the later full
1997/1999 statutory references. The old date **18 November** is a later citation error; do not
use 1978 (UN circulation) as the law date.

The **8 December 1977** decree itself was not downloaded. Its date, implementing role and
A/B/C schedules are explicitly identified in **1997 Article 11**, and repeal in **1999 Article
149**. The entry is limited to those facts. It does not claim to have seen the decree's cannabis
listing, prescription details or penalties. Both earlier events are `other`, not unsupported
first bans or directional amendments.
