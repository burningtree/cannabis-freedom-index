# Venezuela: possession, treatment and sentencing gaps

Research date: 25 October 2026. Venezuela had eight history entries before this
pass. Four entries and three articles are added; three existing entries and the
1993 article are revised. This closes substantial gaps between the early aggregate
restriction estimate, the organic drug laws and the constitutional sentencing cases.

## Sources actually read

- [1964 Penal Code amendment supplied by Venezuela to the UN](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1297967/files/e-nl-1965-50-s.pdf),
  E/NL.1965/50, reproducing Gazette 915 Extraordinary of 30 June 1964. Article 26
  substitutes article 367: marijuana holdings, growing and supply carry four to
  eight years; drug-use meeting places two to five years; attendees six months to
  two years without bail. The full two-page reproduction was read. The year-only
  event does not claim that publication proves commencement or first prohibition.
- [Original 1984 law supplied to the UN](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/55480/files/e-nl-1985-93-s.pdf),
  E/NL.1985/93, Gazette 3.411 Extraordinary, 17 July 1984. Read the offence,
  consumer-procedure and final provisions; compared the
  [UNODC copy](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/ven/ley_organica_sobre_sustancias_estupefacientes_y_psicotropica_spanish_html/Ley_organica_sobre_sustancias_estupefacientes_y_psicotropica.pdf).
  Articles 49–55 already place consumers and immediate personal-dose holders
  under security measures, including residential rehabilitation. The judge uses
  forensic reports; there is no fixed gram limit here. Articles 31–33 provide
  ten to twenty years for supply/growing and six to ten for the separate
  possession offence. Articles 193 and 197 address treatment facilities and
  repeal of the 6 August 1934 law. Legislative signature was 4 July; executive
  signature/publication 17 July. No delayed start was found in the final clauses.
- [1993 reform and explanatory memorandum supplied to the UN](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/214023/files/e-nl-1995-01-02-s.pdf),
  E/NL.1995/1, Gazette 4.636 Extraordinary, 30 September 1993. Read the memorandum
  on articles 36 and 75, statutory articles 34–36 and 75–77, and compared the
  [Justia statutory copy](https://docs.venezuela.justia.com/federales/leyes-organicas/ley-organica-sobre-sustancias-estupefacientes-y-psicotropicas.pdf).
  Both the separate possession offence and consumer route specify twenty grams
  of cannabis. The memorandum expressly describes the sentence cut from six–ten
  to four–six years, inconsistent judicial quantity assessments, and failure to
  supply facilities/professional posts for the earlier treatment regime. It
  reports criminalisation of consumers in practice. This was not the first
  statutory personal-use treatment route.
- [2005 law, corrected December reprint held by the OAS](https://www.oas.org/ext/Portals/33/Files/MLA/Ven_multla_leg_esp_13.doc.pdf).
  The OAS PDF was read through the web reader (local requests returned 403),
  including articles 31–34, 70–71 and publication/signature notices; compared
  the [complete Justia text](https://docs.venezuela.justia.com/federales/leyes-organicas/ley-organica-contra-el-trafico-ilicito-y-el-consumo-de-sustancias-estupefacientes-y-psicotropicas.pdf).
  Article 34 reduces the separate possession offence to one–two years; article
  33 sets growing at six–ten years and wage labour at three–five. Personal use
  is individually assessed under article 70. Initial Gazette 38.287 was
  5 October; reprints followed on 26 October and 16 December. The event uses
  October precision because the original commencement clause was not separately
  established. It does not date the reform to its December reprint.
- [Original 2010 law, Gazette 39.510, 15 September](https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/ven/ley-drogas_html/Venezuela_Ley_Organzia_De_Drogas_R-10-92.pdf),
  read alongside the [November corrected gazette held by Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/sites/default/files/2024-08/14449.pdf)
  and full copies at Caja Venezolana and Sovedagro. The Refworld PDF was read
  through the web reader; local download returned 403. Relevant original and
  corrected pages were compared, including articles 130–132, 141, 149, 151,
  153, 177–178. Final provision eight expressly starts the law on publication:
  15 September, not either corrected reprint date. Individual-dose treatment
  under article 131 differs from criminal possession under article 153, which
  can carry one–two years within its twenty-gram cannabis band. Lower growing
  offences carry six–ten years; supply penalties rose relative to 2005.
- [Constitutional Chamber judgment 387, 1 June 2017, full primary text reproduced by Pandectas Digital](https://pandectasdigital.blogspot.com/2017/06/sentencia-n-383-de-fecha-1-de-junio-de_2.html).
  Read the reproduced judgment, particularly sections I and III–IV. The URL
  contains “383”, but the reproduced judgment is 387. A direct Gazette 41.181
  PDF could not be retrieved; no claim is made to have read that PDF. Decision
  date 1 June differs from Gazette publication 27 June. The court annuls article
  177(4)'s statutory-maximum-six-years condition prospectively. The case concerned
  two men with four-year sentences over 27.7 grams of cannabis material and seeds.
  The remaining general actual-sentence ceiling was five years. Suspension is
  conditional and supervised, not automatic release or repeal of the offence.
  The 2014 entry is reconstructed from this later court's account of binding
  judgment 1859 of 18 December 2014; the original 2014 judgment was not read.

Local PDFs were text-extracted; rendered gazette pages were also inspected to
check the treatment and penalty provisions against column-layout extraction.
Search snippets served as leads, not as verification. No new book was used.

## Data and scoring decisions

The current cultivation and sharing scores each fall from 1 to 0 under the
existing scoring rules: even the lesser growing band carries six–ten years, and
article 149 supply includes uncompensated distribution with lengthy prison terms.
The resulting exact and displayed country score is 10, formerly 13.5. This is a
correction of the assessment of existing law, not a new 2026 legal restriction.
Other current dimension scores remain unchanged. The profile distinguishes the
individual consumer procedure from the twenty-gram criminal-possession band,
corrects foreign expulsion to the criminal accessory rule after serving a sentence,
and incorporates the 2017 suspension ruling.

Historical snapshots now put the personal-use change at 17 July 1984: aggregate
score 3 to 10, with possession 0 to 2 and enforcement 1 to 2. This enforcement step
represents the formal change from a prison offence to treatment measures; it is
not evidence of a measured fall in arrests or imprisonment in 1984. The 1993
memorandum explicitly reports implementation failure, retained in both articles.
There is insufficient representative evidence here to assign a different current
enforcement frequency score. The 1993 change becomes context, since the personal
route already existed; the separate criminal sentence cuts do not cross the
personal-use scoring boundary. The 1964, 2005 and conditional 2014/2017 court
steps have explicit unscored reasons. The 2010 event is classified as tightening
because supply and growing ranges rose, while its personal-use score remains 10.

The pre-1984 restrictive snapshot and the 1925–1950 first-restriction estimate remain
provisional aggregates. The 1984 law confirms repeal of the 1934 law, but its
original cannabis scope and commencement were not verified. The 1964 entry is
not presented as Venezuela's first cannabis ban. Original 1914/1930/1934 texts,
the earliest cannabis coverage, and the transition into the 1964 penalties remain
priority gaps. No claim of an exhaustive late-2026 case-law audit is made.
