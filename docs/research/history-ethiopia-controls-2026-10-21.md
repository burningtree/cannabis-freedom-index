## The 1942 law is now available in full

Recovered UNODC's complete one-page reproduction of Proclamation 24/1942:
https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/eth/proclamation-no-24_html/ethiopia-A_PROCLAMATION_GOVERNING_THE_SALE_AND_IMPORTATION_OF_CERTAIN_DRUGS.pdf.
Downloaded, extracted and visually read the whole page. Its final line
dates the law **23 September 1942**, agreeing with the UN's full indexed
E/NL.1952/102 extract. The previous UN-library access failure is resolved
through this independent primary-text repository.

Sections 2–4 expressly name cannabis indica and its derivatives. Imports
need the Commerce and Industry Minister's permit on the Medical Services
Director's advice. Offers for sale are confined to licensed pharmacies or
drug stores; sales require prescriptions from registered, licensed medical
practitioners. Section 5 allows up to two years OR 2,000 dollars OR both.
The one-page reproduction carries no separate commencement clause; use
its closing date as the dated proclamation, not an invented effective date.

Added an event and a 350–500-word article. Category `tightening` records
the specified import/sale restrictions; it is not labelled the country's
first ban, since the earlier law has not been established. Explicitly
unscored within the existing severe-prohibition chronology. Tags trade,
retail and sentencing correspond to import permits, licensed sale and
criminal penalties. No claim about possession, cultivation or a modern
medical cannabis programme is inferred from this limited text.

## The law numbered 2009 actually took effect in January 2010

Recovered original gazette Proclamation 661/2009 from FAOLEX:
https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/eth94419.pdf, retained as `law-2010.pdf`
with layout text. Read definitions and articles 18–20, 53(1)(f), 54 and
56. Publication is **13 January 2010**, and article 56 commences the law
on publication. Do not infer 2009 operation from the law number. Visually
checked PDF 31, printed 5187, showing the seven-to-fifteen-year AND
30,000–50,000-birr penalty for promoting abuse or illicit production
through inappropriate precursor use.

Article 54 expressly repeals the 1999 law. The replacement keeps special
permits, ninety-day consignment permits, special prescriptions, locked
storage and reports. Article 2(8) supplies the treaty-based controlled-drug
definition. Added an `other` event: the main story is replacement and
continued regulation, not an established net easing/tightening of all
cannabis offences. Tags trade, advertising and sentencing. Updated the
1999 article's ending and citations to tell readers about the repeal.

The 2010 law does not reproduce every separate 1999 facilitating offence
in its advertising paragraph. Do not extend that paragraph to hosting
or assert that repeal removed any overlapping Criminal Code offence.

## The 2019 law spells out dispensing checks

Recovered the complete original bilingual gazette from FAOLEX:
https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/eth208821.pdf, 74 scanned pages, retained
as `law-2019.pdf`. Original Justice Ministry download failed DNS; FAOLEX
provided the actual document. Full layout text and an English-column
extract are retained, but OCR is imperfect: checked the images directly.

Read definition 2(11), articles 31–33, 70(1) and 74. Visually inspected
PDF 32, 34–35 and 74 (printed 11130, 11132–11133 and 11172).
Article 74 normally commences on publication: **28 February 2019**.
The exceptional delayed medicine-labelling/tobacco/alcohol requirements
do not defer articles 31–33. Article 70 repeals the 2010 law only in
matters covered by the replacement; do not describe a total repeal of
all health-professional rules.

Article 31 keeps special manufacturing/import/export permits and ninety-day
consignment permits, locked storage and records. Articles 32–33 require
special prescription paper, no self-prescribing, copies and error retention,
one controlled medicine per prescription, and a fifteen-day validity window.
The dispenser checks the original, prescriber's name, institution stamp,
absence of errors, correct paper, serial number and date. These are
medicine-supply controls; they do not prove cannabis products were
registered or a patient cannabis programme was operating.

Added an `other` event and article, tagged retail and trade. This documents
the replacement law and its explicit controls without assuming the
absence of earlier subordinate prescription rules. No weight/plant threshold
tag: fifteen days is not a cannabis quantity limit.
