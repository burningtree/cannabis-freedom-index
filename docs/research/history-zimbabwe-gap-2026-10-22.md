### Approved hemp farmers, 20 September 2019

[Original one-page Gazette supplement, SI 208 of 2019](https://veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/SI%202019%20-%20208%20Criminal%20Law%20Codification%20%28Persons%20who%20may%20lawfully%20possess%2C%20deal%20in%20or%20use%20industrial%20Hemp%29%20Regulations%2C%202019.pdf). Read all provisions in extracted text and a rendered image. The downloaded page contains duplicate side-by-side copies of the same Gazette page, not separate legal changes.

- **20 September 2019**, date in the original footer.
- Industrial hemp: no more than **0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight**, including parts, seed and derivatives.
- Authorised persons: farmers approved in writing by the agriculture minister and the state/its organs on state farms.
- Lawful acquisition, possession and supply; cultivation for industrial purposes on approved farms.
- No general personal growing or open retail entitlement inferred. Not described as export-only, since the one-page authorisation does not itself impose that restriction.

### Hemp amendment enacted in July 2023

[Government Printer text of Act 10 of 2023](https://veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/CRIMINAL%20LAW%20CODIFICATION%20AND%20REFORM%20AMENDMENT%20ACT%2C%20Act%2010%20of%202023.pdf), especially section 4, printed pages 238–239. Read the Act's title and hemp changes, and inspected the definition/proof rule on rendered PDF page 5. Unrelated political/sexual-offence provisions were not used for cannabis claims.

Read [Veritas's accompanying publication and parliamentary chronology](https://veritaszim.net/node/6459) via the locally downloaded HTML: bill introduced in February, parliamentary passage completed in June, **Gazetted as Act on 14 July 2023**. The previous February 2023 event had cited reporting on a Bill as if the change had already happened. Corrected the date and replaced that citation with the enacted text and publication record.

- Excludes industrial hemp from the **cannabis-plant branch** of the dangerous-drug definition.
- Defines industrial hemp at **no more than 1% delta-9 THC by dry weight**, including seed/parts/derivatives.
- The new proof rule puts the burden on the person asserting that material is **cannabis rather than industrial hemp**. This is not a rule requiring every accused person to disprove a prosecution allegation by proving hemp.
- The list separately retains prepared cannabis and cannabis resin. No blanket claim that all processed cannabinoid products under 1% are free of all drug, agricultural or medicines controls was published.

### Criminal code and the incorrect twelve-year possession claim

[Veritas consolidation to September 2023](https://veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Crimcode%20%28updated%20to%202023.09%29.pdf), read sections 155–161, printed pages 64–67. It contains apparent annotation typos saying Act 10/2025 for two hemp insertions; checked the actual text against enacted Act **10/2023** instead of copying those annotations.

- Section 157: unlawful acquisition/possession, ingestion/smoking/consumption, and growing/production for own consumption: **fine up to level ten OR up to five years OR both**. Treatment can be ordered **additionally or alternatively** where addiction is established.
- Replaced the profile's secondary-only **twelve-year** maximum and clarified that consumption is expressly an offence.
- Section 156 ordinary dealing: fine, up to fifteen years, or both. The aggravated-dealing tier explicitly excludes cannabis plant, prepared cannabis and cannabis resin. No cannabis mandatory fifteen-to-twenty-year sentence was asserted.
- Section 161 includes lawful professional drug-handling permissions. Preserved medical/limited status without claiming unrestricted patient access or a current list of approved cannabis medicines.
- The code is a consolidation, not the original 2004 as-enacted text. No new 2006 commencement event was manufactured from it. A 1 July 2006 commencement lead was found in later judicial/index material but not fully researched into a history entry.

### Colonial survey, 30 March 1955

[UN E/CN.7/286/Add.7](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.7/286/Add.7&l=en&t=pdf). Downloaded the 17-page survey, read the relevant cultivation, medicine, use, prosecution, sentence and legal-control passages. Inspected rendered pages 1 and 9 and the relevant sentence pages. Its OCR repeatedly turns **1953 into 1955** and some numbers into wrong digits; the article's 1953 figures were taken from the inspected page, not the broken OCR.

- Cover dated **30 March 1955**. The introductory July resolution is from **1953**, not July 1955; visual review resolves the apparent chronological contradiction in extracted text.
- **1952: 285 prosecutions**, broken down as 282 in the colonial African category, two classified as Coloured, one European. **1953: 310 prosecutions, 308 men and two women**. These are prosecution figures, not arrest counts or unique modern nationwide-use estimates.
- Small garden/homestead cultivation, largely own use; concealment among maize; police crop destruction and chiefs' duties. The report's descriptions of users and trade are attributed as colonial government/UN accounts, not unqualified present-day demographic facts.
- Traffickers generally imprisoned, smokers generally offered a fine; age, quantity and record affected sentences. Statutory maximum **£1,000 OR ten years OR both**, with special bookkeeping/prescription rules.
- Medical exceptions existed; the report said medical use was very rare and no recent cultivation licences had been issued. This precludes a simplistic claim that no medical cannabis could ever be lawful before 2018. No colonial-origin theory or purported proof of cannabis being indigenous was published from the report's speculation.
