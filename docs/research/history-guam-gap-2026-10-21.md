# Guam: patient growing, adult use and the delayed commercial market

Research began 21 October 2026 and was completed 22 October. The previous goal turn
made verified progress on Togo. Guam was selected from the current country inventory:
four history events and **no articles**, despite medical and adult-use legalisation.
This pass adds six tagged events and five articles, revises three events and replaces
secondary summaries in the profile with the primary legal rules where available.

## Primary evidence and dates

- **2014 vote:** [Guam Election Commission's official summary](https://gec.guam.gov/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/2014-general-summary.htm)
  was downloaded and its referendum table read. **4 November 2014; 19,692 yes (56.48%),
  15,175 no (43.52%)**, 34,867 valid votes. Do not repeat an approximate 56% when the
  exact official tally is available. The election website's certificate failed local
  verification; the public election documents were retrieved using scoped curl `-k`.
  No credentials or private records were involved.
- **Medical referendum text:** [Public Law 32-237](https://archives.guamlegislature.gov/32nd_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_32nd/Public%20Law%2032-237%20-%20Bill%20No%20215-32.pdf),
  14-page legislative reproduction downloaded and extracted. Read cover and original
  Sections 122403–122407. Cover expressly records the 4 November vote and later
  assignment of the public-law number in February 2015. The event is the referendum
  approval, not a claim that dispensaries or registration began on polling day.
  Qualifying conditions include cancer, glaucoma, MS, epilepsy, HIV/AIDS, PTSD and
  rheumatoid arthritis or similar disorders. Written medical certification and
  registration determine protection. Adequate supply was to be set by departmental
  rule, no more than needed for three months, from an intrastate source. This original
  rule is not substituted for later possession limits. Patient certification lasted
  at most one year; minors required additional consent. Certification without a card
  allowed a trial defence, not necessarily immunity from initial arrest.
- **October 2018 home cultivation:** [Public Law 34-125](https://archives.guamlegislature.gov/34th_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_34th/P.L.%20No.%2034-125.pdf),
  nine-page scanned legislative packet. OCR all nine pages and read the substantive
  law and signing letter. Page 6 rendered and visually checked. Signed and immediately
  effective **9 October 2018**. The download helper's filename `medical-import-2018`
  is a research naming error: the actual law is the home-growing permit, not an import
  law. Section 122530: medical certification, application and **$15 annual permit**;
  no operational medical dispensary; **six flowering mature and twelve juvenile plants**;
  indoor secured room, out of public sight; twenty-four-hour notice before inspection.
  Caregivers serving multiple patients have a per-patient allowance with commercial
  requirements above three patients and full licensing/fees above five. Landlords and
  homeowners' associations may prohibit growing. Destruction/reporting rules also
  apply; no open patient sale right is inferred.
- **Government explanation:** [Calvo's 9 October 2018 announcement](https://governor.guam.gov/press_release/news-governor-calvo-signs-home-cultivation-bill-into-law/)
  read in full. Senator Louise Muña promoted the change; governor Eddie Calvo described
  difficulty getting medicine to patients; lieutenant governor Ray Tenorio described
  funding problems and an interim solution. This is the verified development missing
  from the old profile, which stopped at a veto reported for 2017. The veto's original
  document was not recovered, so no new precisely dated veto event is invented.
- **December patient protections:** [Public Law 34-165](https://archives.guamlegislature.gov/34th_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_34th/P.L.%20No.%2034-165.pdf),
  nine-page packet OCR'd; read amended 122504–122505 and signing transmittal. Signed
  **28 December 2018** (transmitted 31 December). The event uses signing, not the later
  transmittal date. It expressly covers home-cultivation patients/caregivers, separately
  counts the allowable amount and permitted plants, protects compliant conduct from
  arrest/prosecution and protects property. Custody/visitation protection excludes
  conduct endangering a child. No unlimited amount of purchased cannabis is inferred.
- **Adult-use law:** [Public Law 35-5](https://archives.guamlegislature.gov/35th_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_35th/P.L.%20No.%2035-5.pdf),
  full 38-page packet downloaded and OCR'd. Read governor's signing letter, Sections
  8102–8105, 8108, 8110–8118 and final Section 19; OCR of all pages is not a claim of a
  comprehensive review of every unrelated code amendment. Signed/immediately effective
  **4 April 2019**. Adults 21+: **one ounce cannabis, eight grams concentrate OR infused
  products containing up to 800 mg THC**. Unpaid adult transfers and immature plants
  explicitly allowed. **Six plants per residence, at most three flowering**. Enclosed,
  locked, not publicly visible, lawful property/consent. Section 8103(b) also protects
  possession of the cannabis produced by those plants on the premises where grown;
  do not erase this harvest exception by describing one ounce as a universal home cap.
  Page 12 was rendered and visually checked. Public use: up to **$100**; location/security violations while otherwise within
  8103(b): up to **$500**. Employer/private-property restrictions and federal enforcement
  preserved. Section 8116 preserves pre-existing penalties/proceedings: no automatic
  expungement claimed. Section 16 preserves medical laws. Governor's [contemporary
  announcement](https://governor.guam.gov/press_release/governor-signs-guam-cannabis-industry-act-of-2019-into-law/)
  was read for the patient/illicit-market explanation, not as an independent efficacy
  finding or evidence of first sales.
- **2022 commercial regulations:** [the judiciary’s codification](https://col.guamcourts.gov/sites/default/files/03gr009.pdf), compiled 19 June 2026, was downloaded and the source note and Sections 9211–9212 and 9801 were read. Its source note gives **30 May 2022 by operation of law**, resolving conflicting May 29/30 dates in reports. The old courts URL returned 404; the new administrative-rules index supplied the working filename `03gr009.pdf`.
  [The board's final submitted rules](https://www.guamtax.com/ccb/FinalApprovedCCBRules2022.02.28.pdf)
  were read through web extraction for 9211–9212 (licence/inspection/permit), 9801
  (testing and accreditation) and related testing provisions. The file retains
  “proposed” headers despite being linked as final approved by the department; do not
  infer commencement from that filename or its 28 February submission date.
- **Testing exemption:** [Public Law 37-116](https://archives.guamlegislature.gov/37th_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_37th/Public%20Law%20No.%2037-116.pdf),
  seven-page packet downloaded, OCR'd and relevant clauses read. Page 7 visually
  checked. Signed **22 July 2024**; Section 3 delays effectiveness by **90 days**, hence
  **20 October 2024**. Originally expires two years after enactment. It adds 8108(a)(7):
  licensed sales while no qualifying laboratory exists, identifying flower/bud labels,
  untested-contaminant and health warnings; cultivator attestation under penalty of
  perjury concerning contaminants. Licensed opening of a laboratory triggers testing
  and ten-day compliance demonstration. Do not turn it into unlicensed home-grown
  sales, universal permission to sell any untested product, or an actual first sale.
  Governor's attached letter gives the investment/market-size reasoning.
- **2026 extension:** [Public Law 38-117](https://guamlegislature.gov/38th_Guam_Legislature/Public_Laws_38th/Public%20Law%2038-117.pdf),
  six-page packet downloaded; signing letter and all three substantive sections read.
  Signed/immediately effective **8 April 2026**. Extends the original two-year period
  to five years; the signing letter says the exemption was otherwise expiring that
  July. Do not restart the entire five-year period at the 2026 amendment or present
  the repeated ninety-day text as a new waiting period for this amendment. Findings
  describe absent laboratories and difficulties getting agency clearances.
- **Current judiciary consolidation:** [11 GCA Chapter 8, compiled 5 May 2026](https://col.guamcourts.gov/sites/default/files/11gc008_Q.pdf),
  eleven pages downloaded. Relevant personal-use, cultivation, sharing and testing
  clauses compared with the originals, including the compiler's 2024/2026 source notes.

## Licences, permits and actual supply

[Isla Public Media's February report](https://www.islapublic.org/news/2026-02-25/guams-first-cannabis-establishment-licenses-approved)
was read in full. **25 February 2026** publication/body Wednesday and June follow-up
support the decision date; a photo caption says February 26. Record the substantiated
25 February date, not the isolated caption. Pacific Root LLC / Pacific Cultivation
received a licence; Guam Real Deal LLC / Deep Green Guam received conditional approval.
An operating permit was still the next step. Chair Vanessa Williams Cruz described
an agency/applicant learning curve.

[Isla Public Media's June report](https://www.islapublic.org/news/2026-06-24/cannabis-control-board-issues-its-first-permit-for-a-guam-cannabis-business)
and [Pacific Island Times' account](https://www.pacificislandtimes.com/post/cannabis-board-approves-guam-s-first-permit-to-operate-12-years-since-pot-legalization)
were read. **24 June 2026**, unanimous operating-permit vote after inspections for Deep
Green Guam. The tax department says additional steps before production and sales.
Do not convert this approval into a recorded harvest, shop opening or completed sale.
The Pacific Island Times incorrectly calls Guam the first US territory for adult-use
legalisation; that comparison is excluded because CNMI had legalised in 2018.

The department's scanned February/June/August minutes were located but repeated direct
connections timed out; screenshot attempts did not yield readable evidence in this
session. They are **not claimed as read**. Searches found later meeting notices but no
verified first consumer sale. The profile now dates the June evidence and avoids the
old blanket claim that no shop had opened as of October. Current numeric access is
retained provisionally; actual later supply still needs evidence.

## Scope and scoring

All nine new/revised entries have valid tags. The referendum date becomes exact and
uses the official tally. Patient cultivation/protection events are `medical`, with
explicit unscored reasons: patient-only rules do not establish a whole-point shift
for general adult growing or access without growing. The commercial rules and
individual licences/permit approvals are `other`. The testing exemption and extension
are `easing`, explicitly unscored without verified consumer supply on those dates.
The 2019 event remains `legalisation`, with its exact original `before` vector.

All current scores, recreational/medical status and dependency metadata are preserved,
**not newly certified**. The home-harvest possession exception should be considered
in a later assessment of the current possession rating. The 1925 first-restriction
estimate is also unresolved: treaty dates do not prove a local Guam law. Neither the
estimate nor any `before` vector is changed in this pass. The two-step historical
chain is mechanically clean, which is not a validation of that inherited baseline.

The profile now includes medical growing, the adult residential plant ceiling, the
800 mg product clause, the harvest exception, public-use fines and preservation of
old penalties. Unsupported “no clubs are known” text is removed. Legal-rule facts
are separated from dated licence/permit evidence. The 2016 rewrite, 2017 amendment
and long 2018 medical-rules packet were downloaded as leads; their whole texts have
not been reviewed and are not published as newly checked events in this batch.
