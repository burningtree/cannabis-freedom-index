# Tanzania follow-up: the 1995 penalties and Zanzibar's 1986 bhang provisions

26 October 2026. Follow-up to [the Tanzania pass](history-tanzania-gap-2026-10-26.md). That pass
left the 1995 Act unscored because its original penalties had not been compared with later
amendments, and left the operative Zanzibar provisions unread. This note closes both reading gaps.
No score changed.

## The 1995 Act and its 31 July 2002 consolidation

The original text is Act 9 of 1995. Section 16 made a single general offence of producing,
possessing, transporting, importing, selling, purchasing, using, cultivating or manufacturing
narcotic drugs, including cannabis. On conviction it allowed a fine of ten million shillings,
life imprisonment, or both, with a larger fine at the court's discretion. Section 19 created a
separate small-quantity tier: 300,000 shillings or ten years for cocaine, morphine, diamorphine
and other specified drugs, and 200,000 shillings or seven years, or both, for the residual group
that included cannabis. The burden of proving personal use lay on the holder.

The TanzLII consolidation "as at 31 July 2002" applies Acts 9 of 1996, 17 of 1996, 31 of 1997 and
9 of 2002. Its section 12 now separates cultivation from the general offence and sets a fine of
one million shillings or three times the market value of the plant, whichever is greater, or up to
twenty years in prison, or both. Section 16 keeps life imprisonment for possession and for
trafficking, with a minimum twenty-year term for trafficking, and section 17 keeps the original
small-quantity penalties. The consolidation notes that Act 6 of 2012 had not been applied, so it
is not the final pre-repeal text.

The change matters for the record. The original Act's general penalty allowed life imprisonment for
cultivation, harsher than the 1926 ordinance's twelve-month maximum, while the 2002 consolidation
reduced the cultivation maximum to twenty years. The existing post-1926 vector scores cultivation
at 1 and is inherited from the old timeline, not reconstructed from the 1995 statute. Moving
cultivation to 0 on this text alone would re-base the 2015 step and would also require a fresh look
at possession, sharing and consumption under the same general penalty. The record here does not
support that full reconstruction, so the 1995 entry stays **checked but unscored**, with the
comparison recorded rather than listed as missing.

## Zanzibar's 1986 bhang provisions

The 1986 Pharmaceuticals and Dangerous Drugs Act (Act 6 of 1986) was read from the Zanzibar
Assembly's scan. It was assented to on **21 May 1986** and came into operation on assent. Section
17 provides:

> (1) It shall be unlawful to cultivate or grow bhang.
> (2) Any person who has in his possession bhang for purposes of sale or otherwise shall be guilty
> of an offence.

The definition in the same section says bhang "includes cannabis indica, cannabis sativa, or the
India hemp or any other leaves, plants or seeds of the like character in effect by whatever name it
is called." Section 32(3) sets the penalty: an educational centre for not less than five years and
not more than ten, with corporal punishment not exceeding six strokes. The Act repeals the
Dangerous Drugs Decree, Cap. 76, but does not print the 1927 decree's original cannabis provisions.

A 1984 amendment had already substituted section 28A of Cap. 76 with a similar offence covering
growing or selling bhang, or possessing it for sale or otherwise, and the same five-to-ten-year
educational-centre sentence with up to six strokes. The 1986 Act thus restated a prohibition that
was already in force, rather than creating it. The 1927 decree's original schedule and penalties
remain missing, so neither the 1984 amendment nor the 1986 Act can be dated as Zanzibar's first
cannabis ban.

No Zanzibar entry was added. The country profile's cannabis score is explicitly for mainland
Tanzania, and putting a separate Zanzibar statute into the same scored timeline still requires a
decision about how the two jurisdictions should be represented. The 1986 text is recorded here so
that decision can be made with the operative section and penalty in hand.

## Remaining gaps

- The 1927 Zanzibar Dangerous Drugs Decree, its original cannabis schedule and its penalties are
  still unrecovered. The 1971 Decree No. 11/71 also remains unread.
- The 1986 Act's section 17(3) and its relationship to the separate narcotics provision need a
  cleaner copy of the printed page; the scan's margin notes and numbering are ambiguous.
- The 1995 Act's 2012 amendment was not applied in the 2002 consolidation and was not read. The
  entry's unscored rationale relies on the 2002 text and the original Act, not the final pre-repeal
  version.
- The original 1924, 1926 and 1935 gazettes and German-era Tanganyika law remain unread, as recorded
  in the main note.
