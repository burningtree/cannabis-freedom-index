## Evidence and limits

- Read the original unofficial English translation hosted by the Council for
  the Development of Cambodia: title/adoption statement, Articles 1–14,
  selected penalty passages, Articles 96–99 and 111. Visually checked PDF p. 3
  (cultivation and dealings), p. 6 (research permission), and pp. 47–48 (the
  complete personal-use penalty and customary-consumption discretion).
- The translation credits the Legal Assistance Unit of the Cambodia Office
  of the High Commissioner for Human Rights and says the Khmer text is
  official. It contains errors such as “cannabis saliva”; the article uses
  cannabis without reproducing that apparent botanical typo or claiming an
  independently verified Khmer translation.
- Downloaded the English reproduction of the royal Kram from Open Development
  Cambodia. Read and visually checked p. 1's promulgation heading and p. 28's
  adoption colophon and annex Table I, which lists cannabis and resin. The
  heading and the government NCDD catalogue both record 24 January 1997.
  This is promulgation, not a separately verified publication or commencement day.
- The Kram reproduction's preamble says adoption on 6 December, but its own
  colophon says 9 December, agreeing with the CDC translation's opening and
  closing statements and the later NACD compilation. Retained 9 December for
  the existing adoption record; did not silently harmonise the conflicting text.
- The NIPH-hosted NACD compilation is a 2007 edition incorporating May 2005
  amendments. Its cover, contents and opening English edition statements were
  inspected; its amended penalties were not backdated into the original law.
- Article 98's discretion is conditional and concerns very small quantities
  and defined customary village use. It does not supply evidence that police
  actually tolerated all cannabis use or that courts routinely used discretion.
  Removed the old unsupported claims about international pressure, open market
  sales and years of lax enforcement. Earlier prohibitions remain unverified;
  the article does not claim this was Cambodia's first-ever cannabis ban.
- No current profile, score or score baseline was changed by this pass. The
  existing 1996 score vector was preserved without fresh validation; new
  promulgation event has no `before` vector.
