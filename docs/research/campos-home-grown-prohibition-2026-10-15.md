Verified from the title and copyright pages: University of North Carolina Press, 2012, ISBN
978-0-8078-3538-8. Chapter 8 (pp. 181–202) read in full.

## Added (context entries; local laws in a federation do not move the national score)

- `mexico-oaxaca-ban-1882` — Díaz as governor bans selling "Rosa María" (p. 193).
- `mexico-cosala-ban-1888` — sale and use banned in a Sinaloa town (p. 193).
- `mexico-state-of-mexico-ban-1891` (p. 193).

## Checked against existing entries

- `mexico-ban-1920`: Campos dates the council's proclamation of the "Dispositions on the Cultivation
  and Commerce of Products that Degenerate the Race" to 2 March 1920 (p. 199); the entry follows
  Schievenini's 15 March. Kept 15 March, noted Campos's date in the description, added the source.
  Campos also states that the dispositions banned cultivation and commerce completely and required
  permits for importing opiates and cocaine.
- `mexico-sanitary-code-1891` agrees with Campos (pp. 191–192): prescription for "dangerous"
  medicines including canabina; herbolarias could sell marijuana only to pharmacists.
- The 1883 Regulation of Pharmacies was sent to the Interior Ministry, set aside over constitutional
  doubts and superseded by the 1891 code (pp. 185–191); not added, since it never took effect.
