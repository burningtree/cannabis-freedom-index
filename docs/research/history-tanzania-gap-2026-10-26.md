# Tanzania: dating the first restrictions, 26 October 2026

This pass returned to Tanzania after the 21 October review left an estimated first restriction, an
undated 1935 ordinance and an unscored 1995 Act. The UN compilation of Tanganyika's revised laws
was obtained as a scan, so the 1924 customs notice, the 1926 noxious-plants ordinance and the 1935
dangerous-drugs ordinance could be read directly rather than through an indexed extract. The pass
replaced the 1900 estimate with two dated events, scored them, checked the 1935 and 1995 records,
and wrote an article on the 1926 ban. No country-profile score or segment text changed. A
[follow-up note](history-tanzania-followup-2026-10-26.md) compares the 1995 Act with its 31 July
2002 consolidation and reads the operative Zanzibar section 17 and penalty.

## The documents

### 1924: the customs import prohibition

**United Nations, E/NL.1952/74–83, 11 September 1952.** The compilation reproduces Tanganyika's
*Revised Edition of the Laws, 1947*. Chapter 192 is the Customs Ordinance. Its "Imports
(Prohibitions and Restrictions)" orders, made by the Governor under section 113, begin: "The
importation into the territory of the following articles is prohibited." Item (12), marked
**G.N. 210 of 1924**, is:

> Cannabis sativa (known as Hemp, or True Hemp, and in Swahili as Bhang or Bangi).

The item is on printed p. 66 (E/NL.1952/83). The page image and the OCR were both read. This is an
import prohibition only. It does not create a domestic offence of growing, possessing or using
cannabis, and the revised edition gives no day or month for the notice, so the entry is dated
`1924`.

The notice is the first dated restriction found for Tanganyika. It is not proof that no earlier
German-era or territorial rule existed; the German period was not searched in this pass.

### 1926: the noxious-plants ordinance

**Tanganyika, Cultivation of Noxious Plants (Prohibition) Ordinance, 8 of 1926**, chapter 134 of
the 1947 revision, printed p. 62 (E/NL.1952/81), dated **18 June 1926**. The page scan was read at
200 dpi. Section 2 made it an offence to plant, grow or cultivate any plant in the schedule, and to
consume, use or possess such a plant, any substance obtained from it, or any compound or admixture
of it. The penalty was a fine of up to 2,000 shillings or imprisonment of either kind for up to
twelve months, and the court could order the plant or substance destroyed. Section 3 allowed a
magistrate or administrative officer to issue a warrant to enter and search by day or night.
Section 4 allowed any police officer to arrest without a warrant a person found committing an
offence. Section 5 let the Governor add to or vary the schedule by gazette order. The schedule
named "Cannabis Sativa (known as True Hemp and in Swahili as Bhang or Bhangi)."

This is the first comprehensive domestic ban: it covers cultivation, possession, use and
consumption, and it supplies search, arrest and destruction powers. The 1995 Act later repealed it
by name (chapter 134) in section 63.

### 1935: the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance

**Tanganyika, Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, 28 of 1935**, chapter 95 of the 1947 revision, dated
**12 July 1935**, printed pp. 1232–1235 and 1247 (E/NL.1952/74). The ordinance consolidated the
law on opium and "certain other dangerous drugs". Section 7 prohibited importing or exporting resin
from *Cannabis sativa*. Section 8 prohibited possessing, producing, selling or dealing in the resin
or preparations based on it; cultivating the plant; and possessing, selling or dealing in the whole
or any part of the plant, excluding medicinal preparations. Section 23 set the general penalty at
up to 20,000 shillings or ten years, or both, on conviction by the High Court, and up to 5,000
shillings or twelve months, or both, in a subordinate court of the first class, with forfeiture and
possible destruction.

The entry was previously dated only `1935`, categorised `other`, and described through a later
government notice that did not reproduce the ordinance. It is now dated `1935-07-12`, categorised
`tightening`, and describes the ordinance itself. It remains **unscored**: the ordinance
consolidated existing controls and raised the statutory maxima, but the record read here does not
show a change in enforcement practice. The 1926 ordinance remained in force alongside it until
1995.

### 1995 and 2015

**Tanzania, Drugs and Prevention of Illicit Traffic in Drugs Act, Act 9 of 1995.** The original
text was read. Section 63 expressly repeals both the Cultivation of Noxious Plants (Prohibition)
Ordinance (chapter 134) and the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance (chapter 95). The Act created a separate
small-personal-use tier, with a 200,000-shilling fine, seven years, or both in the original text,
while the main growing and supply offences could bring life imprisonment or a much larger fine. It
took effect on 1 January 1996. The entry remains **unscored**; the follow-up note compares the
original penalty alternatives with the 31 July 2002 consolidation and leaves the inherited score
vector in place pending a full-vector review.

**Tanzania, Drug Control and Enforcement Act, Act 5 of 2015.** Sections 11, 15 and 17 were read.
The Act set at least thirty years for unlawful cultivation and a residual personal-use penalty of
at least 500,000 shillings, three years, or both. It replaced the 1995 Act. The existing 2015
step, 13 → 8, is retained.

### Zanzibar: a separate legal history

Zanzibar is not covered by the Tanganyika ordinances. The 1931 Zanzibar annual report records
"Government Notice No. 49 … Notice under Dangerous Drugs Decree, 1927", which identifies the
principal decree by name and year, and it describes 1931 amendment decrees. The decree itself was
not recovered, so its original cannabis schedule and penalties remain unknown.

Two later Zanzibar texts were read:

- **Dangerous Drugs (Amendment) Act, 1984 (No. 9 of 1984).** It amends the Dangerous Drugs Decree,
  Cap. 76, replacing section 28A. The new section makes growing or selling bhang, or possessing
  bhang for sale or otherwise, an offence. On conviction the offender may be sent to an educational
  centre for five to ten years, with corporal punishment of up to six strokes. The Act also repeals
  Decree No. 11/71. The 1984 text is a lead, not a scored event: the principal decree's earlier
  cannabis provisions are still missing.
- **Pharmaceuticals and Dangerous Drugs Act, 1986.** Its arrangement of sections includes section
  17, "Cultivation and possession of bhang prohibited", and it repeals the Dangerous Drugs Decree,
  Cap. 76. The operative section and penalty are read in the follow-up note.

No Zanzibar entry was added. Scoring one would require the 1927 decree and a decision about whether
Zanzibar's separate law belongs in the same national timeline as the mainland. The 1931 report and
the 1984 amendment are recorded here so the next pass can pick them up.

## The score chain

The old estimate was `tz-first-restriction-estimate`, dated 1900, marked `estimate`, with a
100 → 13 step and a window of 1900–1920. It was removed and replaced by two dated events:

- **1924: 100 → 98.** The import ban cuts off lawful imports, so `access` falls from 10 to 8. The
  other five segments stay at 10 because the notice did not criminalise domestic growing, sale,
  possession, sharing or use. Access is not scored at 0: the notice left the domestic market
  unregulated, and a zero would say there was no lawful source at all. The 8 follows the site's
  treatment of the Netherlands' import-only ban of 1928, which is also 100 → 98 with access 8.
- **1926: 98 → 13.** Before: `{10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 8}`. After:
  `{possession: 1, cultivation: 1, enforcement: 3, sharing: 1, consumption: 1, access: 0}`. This is
  the post-ban vector the Tanzania timeline already used; the pass attaches it to the dated 1926
  law rather than inventing a new one. The statute's fine-or-twelve-months penalty, warrantless
  arrest, search power and destruction order support a severe restriction. The `enforcement: 3` is
  still an editorial estimate: the text does not show how often prison was imposed in practice.
- **2015: 13 → 8.** Unchanged.

`node scripts/data-status.cjs chain tz` reports the three steps and **0 flags**. `scores:triage --
tz` reports **3 scored, 0 open**. The 1935 and 1995 entries are checked but unscored.

## The article and the source-list fix

`articles/tanganyika-noxious-plants-ban-1926.md` is new: 352 words, four sections, three sources
after consolidation, and 16 citation marks. It opens with the June 1926 ban, explains the 1924
customs notice, describes what the ordinance prohibited, covers the search, arrest and schedule
powers, and closes with the 1935 consolidation, the 1995 repeal and the 2015 cultivation sentence.
It links to the Tanzania profile.

The first draft listed the 1924 notice, 1926 ordinance and 1935 ordinance as three sources. They
share one PDF URL, and the site identifies a source by its URL. On a page that cited more than one
of them, all three collapsed into one reference: a `[@2]` could render as `[1]`, and the other
references disappeared. The three entries now use a single UN source with a locator covering the
relevant pages:

> Revised Edition of the Laws, 1947, chapters 192, 134 and 95; E/NL.1952/83, printed p. 66, item
> (12); E/NL.1952/81, printed p. 62, sections 1–5 and Schedule; printed pp. 1232–1235 and 1247,
> sections 2, 7–8 and 23.

The 1924 entry now has one source; the 1926 entry has the UN compilation and the 1995 Act; the
article has the UN compilation, the 1995 Act and the 2015 Act. The 1935 entry keeps its own
ordinance-specific source and the 1995 Act. Every listed source renders, and the citation numbers
match the reference numbers.

## Remaining gaps

- The original 1924, 1926 and 1935 gazettes were not read. The 1947 revised edition reproductions
  were used, so textual variants between the original ordinances and the revision are unchecked.
- The 1935 ordinance's penalty maxima are higher than the 1926 ordinance's, but there is no
  enforcement record here to show whether that changed sentencing in practice. The 1935 and 1995
  entries remain unscored.
- The 1995 Act's 2012 amendment and final pre-repeal text were not read; the 31 July 2002
  consolidation is compared in the follow-up note. The 1995 entry remains checked but unscored.
- Zanzibar's 1927 Dangerous Drugs Decree, its original cannabis schedule and the 1971 Decree No.
  11/71 remain unread. The 1984 amendment and the 1986 Act's section 17 penalty are recorded in the
  follow-up note.
- German-era Tanganyika law was not searched. The 1924 notice is the first dated restriction found,
  not a demonstrated first restriction.
- The 2016 small-quantity regulations and later changes to the 2015 Act were outside this pass. The
  2015 score step is retained as previously reviewed.
