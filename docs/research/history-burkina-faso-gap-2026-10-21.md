## Primary texts actually read

- Public Health Code: Law 23/94/ADP, adopted **19 May 1994**, promulgated **13 June 1994**,
  gazette **13 October 1994**, as reproduced in the justice ministry's annotated legal
  compilation at https://cil.bf/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Loi-025-2018_CODE-PENAL.pdf.
  Header printed p. 369; drug text printed pp. 397–399, PDF pp. 409–411. The compilation is
  selective: it omits Articles 274–290. It identifies a 2010 amendment; the reproduced drug
  clauses are not marked as that amendment. Do not invent the omitted licensing details.
  Article 292: possession without legitimate reason six days–six months, 100,000–1 million CFA,
  or either alone. Article 291: group use/facilitation one–five years, 5–10 million, or either.
  Article 293 doubles the latter penalties for growing/manufacture and the stated minor cases.
  Residence ban five–ten years mandatory for foreigners convicted of facilitating use, not all
  foreign users. Category `other`: national recodification, earlier national penalty comparison
  not recovered; no invented first ban or score direction.
- Penal Code: Law 43-96/ADP, **13 November 1996**, promulgated **18 December 1996**.
  Presidency-hosted consolidated text (header records 2004 amendments):
  https://presidencedufaso.bf/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Code_Penal.pdf.
  Complete drug section Articles 439–448, PDF pp. 67–68, read and rendered. The drug clauses
  are not marked as amended in 2004. Personal use/buy/possession three–six months,
  150,000–300,000 CFA, or either alone. Direct supply one–five years AND 500,000–1.5 million;
  general local supply five–ten AND 1–10 million. Production one–five AND 10–50 million.
  Organising a drug group life AND 10–50 million. Court-ordered detox can include hospitalisation.
  Mixed changes against 1994: higher possession minimum, but lower ordinary production range
  and different fine levels; `other`, no fabricated single-direction score movement.
- Complete 1999 Drug Code: **29 April 1999**, 22-page UNODC attachment recovered through
  the SHERLOC document record:
  https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/bfa/loi-17-99_html/loi_17-99.pdf.
  Adoption date appears at start and finish. A separate police-academy booklet reports
  promulgation 23 June 1999, decree 99-213; adoption is the entry date, not claimed commencement.
  The later annotated compilation says 22 April in its heading, contradicted by the original.
  Article 61 on PDF p. 13 explicitly overrides Articles 44–54 for small personal buying,
  possession AND cultivation. High-risk plants/substances including cannabis oil: two months–
  one year OR 100,000–500,000 CFA OR both. Non-oil cannabis derivative: one–six months OR
  50,000–250,000 OR both. No numerical threshold. Article 44 ordinary production and Articles
  45–46 trafficking ten–twenty years OR fine OR both. Article 50 direct supply two–five years
  OR fine OR both. Article 47 general personal use/possession two–five years OR fine OR both.
  Article 72 treatment can replace or supplement punishment in its listed cases. Waiver bullets
  have potentially ambiguous cumulative/alternative conditions; no universal adult waiver claimed.
  Category `other`: mixed recoding, small-grow exception and much higher ordinary production
  penalties compared with 1996; the score records the clear personal-growing distinction.
- Penal Code adopted **31 May 2018**, original 304-page text:
  https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/loi-no-025-2018-an-portant-code-pnal_html/LOI_N2018-025-AN.pdf.
  Annotated ministry edition preface records gazette publication **26 June 2018**, special 07.
  Adoption is the entry date; do not label it commencement. Article 900-2 expressly repeals
  the 1996 Penal Code and **1999 Articles 44–72 and 82**, not the entire 1999 classification
  and lawful-control law. Article 385-2 small quantities: high-risk including oil one month–
  one year AND 250,000–600,000 CFA; non-oil cannabis derivative one–six months AND same fine.
  Fine-only alternative is absent from these clauses. Do not infer that every possible general
  sentencing discretion was abolished. Generic personal use/possession Article 381-4 two–five
  AND 1–5 million. Ordinary production/trafficking eleven–twenty-one AND the stated fines;
  direct supply two–five AND 500,000–2 million. Article 387-9 retains court-ordered treatment
  instead of/additional to punishment for its specified offences. Article 387-7 additional
  foreigner exclusions cover 381-1–384-5, not automatically the separate 385-2 small tier.
  Category `other`: fines increase and fine-only options disappear, while high-risk small-tier
  minimum falls from two months to one. Cultivation remains at 1; no further whole-point score
  justified by the retained practice evidence.

**Important current-text follow-up:** parliament's own account says 71 members unanimously
adopted a replacement Penal Code on **20 October 2026**, yesterday relative to this research:
https://assembleenationale.bf/972. It gives no cannabis/drug clauses, promulgation or commencement.
The profile identifies that adoption and dates the detailed penalties to the 2018 code. Do not
claim that the 2018 text is a fully consolidated October 2026 law. Do not infer that the new
capital-punishment clauses include cannabis: the report does not name their scope. No standalone
cannabis event is added without a cannabis-relevant provision. Recover the adopted/full gazetted
2026 text, commencement and any drug amendments before making a further legal/score claim.
