# Portugal's 1876 official cannabis standards

Research date: **16 October 2026**. Added one event and article:
`portugal-cannabis-pharmacopoeia-approved-1876`.

## Original source retrieved and read

Downloaded the [official Pharmacopêa portugueza, Lisbon, Imprensa Nacional, 1876](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Pharmacop%C3%AAa_portugueza_%28electronic_resource%29_%28IA_b20407105%29.pdf),
a digitized Wellcome copy, Internet Archive identifier **b20407105**. Read the
opening decree, relevant general notices and all three cannabis entries. Text
extraction was followed by rendered-page inspection of:

- **PDF p. 9:** royal decree approving the pharmacopoeia, dated **14 September
  1876**, including the legal replacement and delayed-operation wording.
- **Printed p. 92 / PDF p. 150:** *Cânhamo*, Indian/European hemp descriptions
  and the explicit instruction about the material to supply as hashish.
- **Printed p. 187 / PDF p. 245:** *Extracto de cânhamo, alcoólico*.
- **Printed p. 417 / PDF p. 475:** *Tinctura de cânhamo indiano*.

Also read **Advertências VII–VIII, pp. XLVII–XLVIII**, addressing identification
of plant parts used in formulas. The volume was not read cover to cover.

The [Imprensa Nacional's historical account](https://imprensanacional.pt/history/farmacopeia-portuguesa-1876/),
published **6 December 2021**, independently confirms the official edition,
appointment of the commission by decree of **15 November 1871**, and approval
of its project by decree of **14 September 1876**. The University of Coimbra's
[catalogue](https://am.uc.pt/item/56606) was located as another institutional record,
but its page did not load reliably and was not needed as a substantive source.

## Date and legal significance

The original decree orders the new pharmacopoeia to replace the *Codigo
pharmaceutico lusitano* approved by decrees of **6 October 1835** and
**14 February 1861**, **for all legal purposes**. It expressly delays execution
until **six months after publication of the decree in the Diário do governo**.

The event therefore uses **14 September 1876 as the approval date**, clearly
labelled. The relevant original gazette publication was not retrieved, so no
exact commencement day or immediate effect is asserted. An approval event is
retained without a score vector rather than inventing a dated score transition.

## Cannabis-specific standard

The p. 92 entry distinguishes Indian hemp, with flowering and partly fruiting
tops, from the European variety, described through fruits containing oily seeds.
The Indian material is described as resin-bound compressed masses in commerce.

Its note expressly instructs that these **tops** should be supplied as
**Haschich**, unless the compound buttery preparation made from them in the
East is specifically indicated. This is an identification/substitution standard
within the official medicinal work. It should not be silently translated into
a modern definition of separated cannabis resin.

The extract and tincture entries supply pharmaceutical preparation standards.
No modern cannabinoid potency or clinical-efficacy claim is inferred from them.
No first-ever recognition is claimed because the predecessor codes were not
compared. The sources do not establish a general cannabis ban, unrestricted
retail supply, possession/cultivation offences or enforcement frequency.

## Italian lead tested, not added

Downloaded the original [Farmacopea ufficiale del Regno d'Italia, 1892](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/34/Farmacopea_ufficiale_del_regno_d%27Italia_%28electronic_resource%29_%28IA_b20406630%29.pdf),
Internet Archive identifier **b20406630**. Read the extracted opening approval
text, searched the extract for cannabis/hemp terms and examined the extracted
regulatory lists around printed **pp. 378–388**. Searches did not establish a
medicinal cannabis entry. A hemp-oil reference in a soap formula is not evidence
that medicinal cannabis was a scheduled poison.

This was a limited text-based retrieval check, not an exhaustive visual review
of every page or proof that Italy had no cannabis controls. No Italian 1892
cannabis-specific event was added. Generic Italian health/pharmacy legislation
of 1888–1889 was also not assumed to cover cannabis without a verified link.
The Brazilian 1886 sanitary regulation likewise did not yield an explicit
cannabis match in this pass, so no entry was added from it.
