Seeds and cuttings fall between the cracks of drug laws, and countries treat them very differently. [Italy's](/country/it/) highest court held [in 2012](/history/italy-seeds-ruling-2012/) that offering seeds for sale is not a crime, and in the United States the drug agency wrote [in 2022](/history/us-dea-seed-letter-2022/) that seeds within the hemp definition are not federally controlled. Elsewhere seeds are banned outright, as cannabis.

Where adults may grow at home, how they get seeds becomes part of the law: [Morocco's](/country/ma/) legal programme [imported its first seeds in 2023](/history/morocco-first-seeds-2023/). These sections give four statuses each: whether seeds and clones may be held and planted, and whether they may be sold. The figures differ widely between countries; see the overview of [the legality of cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_cannabis).[@1]
