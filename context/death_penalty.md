A few countries still allow execution for cannabis, almost always for trafficking rather than use. Harm Reduction International counts 36 countries that keep the death penalty for drug offences and more than 1,200 people executed for them in 2025.[@1] The international drug treaties do not require it: the [Single Convention](https://www.unodc.org/pdf/convention_1961_en.pdf) asks for penal provisions but leaves the punishment to each state.[@2] For the background see [capital punishment for drug trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_for_drug_trafficking).

The law and the practice differ. [Singapore](/country/sg/) made trafficking more than 500 g of cannabis a capital offence [in 1990](/history/singapore-cannabis-death-penalty-1990/) and [hanged a man for it in 2023](/history/singapore-tangaraju-execution-2023/); [Iran](/country/ir/) [raised its thresholds in 2017](/history/iran-death-threshold-raised-2017/) so that cannabis now needs 50 kg.
