Modern cannabis science began with chemistry done under prohibition: in Israel, [Raphael Mechoulam](/culture/people/raphael-mechoulam/) [isolated THC in 1964](/history/israel-mechoulam-thc-1964/) from hashish supplied by the police. Later work on the plant's [cannabinoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabinoid) and the body's own receptors grew from there.[@1] Yet because cannabis sits in the strictest treaty schedules, scientists in many countries still need special licences to obtain it, and in some there is no legal route at all.

These sections say who studies cannabis in each country, what they found and what the law allows researchers to do. The map shows where research is open, licensed, restricted to narrow routes or blocked. On the advice of the World Health Organization, the UN [moved cannabis out of its most restrictive schedule in 2020](/history/un-cannabis-rescheduled-2020/).
