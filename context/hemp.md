Hemp is cannabis grown for fibre, seed and, increasingly, CBD, and the law separates it from the drug by a THC limit. In the European Union hemp must not exceed 0.3% THC and be grown from varieties in the EU catalogue; it goes into textiles, food, building materials, paper and cosmetics.[@1] The [United States](/country/us/) adopted the same 0.3% line [in 2018](/history/us-farm-bill-2018/), as did [China's](/country/cn/) [Yunnan province in 2010](/history/china-yunnan-hemp-2010/), while [Czechia](/country/cz/) [raised its limit to 1% in 2022](/history/czechia-hemp-one-percent-2022/).

The line is under strain. Semi-synthetic compounds such as [HHC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexahydrocannabinol) were sold openly as legal alternatives until countries began to ban them: [Denmark in 2023](/history/denmark-hhc-banned-2023/), [Germany in 2024](/history/germany-hhc-banned-2024/), [Czechia](/history/czechia-hhc-banned-2026/) and the [Netherlands](/history/netherlands-hhc-banned-2026/) in 2026. These sections describe each country's THC limit, its rules for CBD, and loophole products; for the oldest use of the plant see [hemp and the beginnings of paper](/culture/traditions/early-chinese-hemp-paper/).
