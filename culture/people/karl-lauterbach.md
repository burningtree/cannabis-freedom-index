Karl Lauterbach is a doctor who used to be against legalising cannabis. "I was against legalizing
cannabis for years," he said in October 2021. "But now, as a doctor, I have come to a different
conclusion." Two and a half years later, as Germany's health minister, he saw through the law that
made it legal for adults to possess and grow it.

He was born in 1963, studied medicine in Aachen, San Antonio and Düsseldorf, and became professor of
health economics and epidemiology at the University of Cologne. He has sat in the Bundestag for the
Social Democrats since 2005, and became widely known during the COVID-19 pandemic as a television
expert and a prolific commentator online.

## The change of mind

What changed his view, he told the *Rheinische Post* in October 2021, was police reports of other
substances being mixed into cannabis sold on the street. A legal supply, he argued, would protect
consumers. He said so while his party, the Greens and the Free Democrats were negotiating to form a
government, and urged them to legalise. The three parties' coalition promised a regulated market
for adults. Lauterbach became federal minister of health on 8 December 2021.

## From shops to clubs

On 26 October 2022 he presented the key points of a law for the controlled sale of cannabis to
adults. It did not survive contact with European law. In
[April 2023](/history/germany-two-pillar-plan-2023/), after talks with the European Commission, he
and other ministers announced a narrower plan in two pillars: home growing and non-profit
associations first, licensed shops only as regional trials later.

The Bundestag [passed the Cannabis Act](/history/germany-bundestag-vote-2024/) on 23 February 2024.
From [1 April](/history/germany-cannabis-act-2024/) adults could carry 25 grams, keep 50 at home
and grow three plants, and from [July](/history/germany-cultivation-associations-2024/) join
non-profit growing associations. There are still no shops, and passing cannabis to another person
remains a crime.

## After the ministry

Lauterbach was health minister until 2025. The second pillar, licensed shops in regional trials,
was not part of the act, and adults in [Germany](/country/de/) still have no shop to buy from.
