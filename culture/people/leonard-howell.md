Leonard Percival Howell was the first to preach in Jamaica that Haile Selassie, crowned Emperor of Ethiopia in 1930, was the returned Messiah. He is often called the first Rasta. The settlement he founded at Pinnacle, in the hills of St Catherine, was the first Rastafari community, and ganja was among its crops.

He was born in 1898 in Clarendon and spent some twenty years abroad, as a seaman and in Panama and New York, where he joined Marcus Garvey's Universal Negro Improvement Association. Deported from the United States, he returned to Jamaica in December 1932 and began preaching the next year.

## Preacher and prisoner

The colonial authorities treated his preaching as sedition. In 1934 he was tried for it, defended himself with a photograph of Haile Selassie, and was sentenced to two years in prison. His tract *The Promised Key*, published under the name G. G. Maragh, attacked the churches and colonial rule. In 1938 he was sent to the Bellevue asylum in Kingston.

## Pinnacle

In 1940 he bought land near Sligoville, Jamaica's first free village, built after the end of slavery, and founded Pinnacle on its hilltop. The residents farmed, kept livestock and ran a bakery and a school, and sold their surplus outside. According to his son Monty, interviewed by the *Jamaica Observer* in 2014, the crops included ganja.

Jamaica had [banned ganja in 1913](/history/jamaica-ganja-law-1913/), and in [1941](/history/jamaica-mandatory-prison-1941/), the year after Pinnacle was founded, the colony made a prison term mandatory for it. The police raided Pinnacle that same year, arrested many of its people and called it a communist experiment; Howell was caught, tried again for sedition and jailed for two more years.

## The raids

Between 1941 and 1954, Monty Howell recalled, the police raided Pinnacle as a matter of course, seizing the community's cash as the supposed proceeds of ganja and arresting people on ganja charges, a woman of seventy among them. In 1954 they came in force, burned houses, including Howell's, and drove the people off the land; many were jailed on ganja charges. The *Gleaner*, looking back in 2022, describes the village as completely destroyed. Some residents returned, but Pinnacle never recovered.

The people who left took the faith with them, to the slums of west Kingston and elsewhere. The sacramental use of ganja that Pinnacle practised became part of Rastafari across the island and beyond; its history is in [the Rastafari sacrament](/culture/traditions/rastafari-sacrament/).

## Afterwards

Howell spent later years in and out of court over the ownership of Pinnacle and died in Kingston in 1981, months after he was beaten and slashed at Tredegar Park, not far from Pinnacle. The government later declared the lot where his house stood a national monument, and in 2022 awarded him an Order of Distinction. Who owns the land is still disputed.
