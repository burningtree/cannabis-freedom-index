Peter Tosh turned the demand to legalise cannabis into a reggae song, and then said it to
Jamaica's rulers to their faces. A founder of the Wailers with Bob Marley and Bunny Wailer, he
went solo in 1976 with an album called *Legalize It*. Its title track travelled the world among
reggae audiences, [Rastafari](/culture/traditions/rastafari-sacrament/) and campaigners for
legalisation.

He was born Winston Hubert McIntosh in Westmoreland, Jamaica, in 1944, was passed between relatives
as a child, and at fifteen moved to Trenchtown in Kingston, where he trained as a welder and learned
music from the singer Joe Higgs, who taught young people for free. There he met Marley and Bunny
Wailer. He wrote or co-wrote many of the Wailers' best-known songs, among them "Get Up, Stand Up".

## Legalize It

The album was recorded in Kingston in 1975 and 1976 and released by CBS in
[June 1976](/history/jamaica-legalize-it-1976/). Ganja was illegal in Jamaica, where mandatory prison
for possession had [ended only in 1972](/history/jamaica-mandatory-prison-ended-1972/). The title
track became a favourite at his concerts.

## The Peace Concert

In April 1978 Tosh played the One Love Peace Concert in Jamaica. With the political leaders Michael
Manley and Edward Seaga in the audience, he
[lit a spliff and lectured them](/history/jamaica-tosh-peace-concert-1978/) for failing to legalise
cannabis. According to *The Gleaner*, he became a regular target for the police. In September he was
arrested at Half Way Tree, accused of smoking marijuana and attacking a police officer, and reported
being beaten so badly in custody that he suffered a broken hand and head injuries needing stitches.

## Afterwards

Tosh kept smoking openly on stage, at New York's No Nukes concerts in 1979 among others. He was
killed in 1987, during a robbery at his home. In 2012 Jamaica awarded him the Order of
Merit, and in 2015 his estate declared 20 April International Peter Tosh Day, to honour what it
called his "philosophy of responsible cannabis consumption for medicinal and spiritual health
benefits". That year Jamaica [made possession of small amounts](/history/jamaica-ganja-reform-2015/)
a ticketable offence and recognised Rastafari sacramental use, fourteen years after a national
commission had [reported public support](/history/jamaica-ganja-commission-2001/) for allowing it.
