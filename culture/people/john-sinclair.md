John Sinclair was a Detroit poet, jazz writer and political organiser whose prison sentence for two marijuana cigarettes made him the best-known cannabis prisoner in the [United States](/country/us/) of the early 1970s. In 1969 a Michigan judge gave him nine and a half to ten years. Two and a half years later fifteen thousand people came to an arena in Ann Arbor to demand his release, and in March 1972 the Michigan Supreme Court reversed his conviction.[@1,2,4]

His case belongs to the law as much as to the counterculture. It showed how heavily a state could punish possession of a small amount, and it became the occasion for an annual gathering, the [Hash Bash](/culture/traditions/hash-bash/), that still meets.[@1,5]

## Poet, manager, Panther

Sinclair was born in Flint in 1941. In Detroit in the mid-1960s he wrote poetry and jazz criticism and managed the rock band MC5 from 1966 to 1969. In 1968 he founded the White Panther Party with Pun Plamondon, a white radical group conceived as a counterpart to the Black Panther Party.[@4]

He was also a target. In December 1966 he was arrested for giving two joints to an undercover police officer.[@4]

## Ten years for two joints

At trial the charge of selling was dismissed on grounds of entrapment, and he was convicted of possessing the two cigarettes. On [28 July 1969](/history/us-sinclair-sentence-1969/) he was sentenced to nine and a half to ten years in prison.[@1]

The length of the sentence for so small an amount made him a cause, and a "Free John Sinclair" campaign grew through his years in prison.[@2,3]

## The Freedom Rally

On [10 December 1971](/history/us-john-sinclair-freedom-rally-1971/) the John Sinclair Freedom Rally filled the Crisler Arena in Ann Arbor. [John Lennon](/culture/people/john-lennon/) and Yoko Ono headlined, with musicians, poets and political speakers. Lennon sang a song he had written for the occasion, "John Sinclair", later released on *Some Time in New York City*.[@2,4] The concert did not overturn the conviction, but Sinclair was freed three days later, having served twenty-nine months.[@4,5]

## People v. Sinclair

On [9 March 1972](/history/us-sinclair-conviction-reversed-1972/) the Michigan Supreme Court reversed and set aside the conviction and ordered his discharge. The justices wrote separate opinions rather than one, and the case is cited as *People v. Sinclair*, 387 Mich. 91.[@1] The ruling is remembered as the decision that struck down Michigan's marijuana statutes.[@4]

On 1 April 1972 the first Hash Bash gathered on the University of Michigan's Diag in Ann Arbor. It has met every year since, on or near the first Saturday of April.[@5]

## Later life

Sinclair went on writing, performing poetry with musicians, and broadcasting. He worked as a disc jockey in New Orleans, lived for years in Amsterdam, where he founded a foundation in 2004 and from 2005 ran a radio show, and kept campaigning for cannabis. On 1 December 2019, when legal sales began in Michigan, he was among the first customers. He died in Detroit on 2 April 2024, and that year's Hash Bash was held in his memory.[@4,5]
