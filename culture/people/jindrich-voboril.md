Jindřich Vobořil was twice Czechia's national drug coordinator, the official who steers the
government's drug policy. In his second term, from 2022 to 2024, he set out to make the country the first
in the European Union to license the growing and sale of cannabis for adults.
Coalition politicians would not go that far, and he left office under pressure. A year later his think
tank drafted the amendment behind Czechia's legal home growing, which took effect in 2026.

## From harm reduction to government

Vobořil was born in Brno on 29 September 1966. He studied pedagogy and theology at the theological
faculty of Palacký University in Olomouc and later took a postgraduate diploma in applied psychology at
Liverpool John Moores University. His working life began in the non-profit sector: he led Podané ruce, an
organisation for drug prevention, treatment and harm reduction, which credits him with
founding its foundation. He is a member of the centre-right Civic Democratic Party (ODS) and has stood,
unsuccessfully, for the European Parliament and for Brno's city council.

| Term | Dates | Ended |
|---|---|---|
| First | 29 June 2010 – 31 July 2018 | Left for personal reasons; the press linked it to disagreements with Andrej Babiš |
| Second | 14 February 2022 – 31 August 2024 | Left after coalition leaders agreed his departure with Petr Fiala |

His first term began in the year Czechia's new criminal code
[set amounts](/history/czechia-thresholds-2010/) below which possession was only an administrative
offence.

## A licensed market

Back in office under Petr Fiala's government, Vobořil announced in 2022 that he was preparing a law to
[regulate the cannabis market](/history/czechia-regulated-market-plan-2022/). Growers would need
licences, licensed shops might sell to adults, purchases would be capped, and buyers might have to
register. In April 2023 the government approved an addiction plan that included a regulated market,
alongside more prevention, treatment and higher taxes on addictive substances. At a Prague conference in
May he said the bill should pass by the end of the year, with shops for adults over 18, clubs, and
licensed home and commercial growing. He admitted that another member state might take Czechia to the
European Court of Justice, since no other EU government planned a commercial market. Prohibition, he
said, had failed.

The coalition did not agree. The [draft presented in January 2024](/history/czechia-cannabis-draft-2024/)
kept home growing, clubs, licensed sales, exports and taxes, but not the open commercial market.
"I am not giving up. There is a chance," he said.

He applied the same idea to new substances. He pushed an amendment creating a category of
"psychomodulatory substances", so that products such as kratom and HHC could be regulated rather than
banned, and opposed proposals to ban them outright. When HHC was banned anyway, he asked the president to
pardon ordinary users.

## Pushed out, then the law passed

On 9 July 2024 the coalition leaders agreed that Vobořil would step down at the end of August. The
Christian Democrats had long demanded it, and the opposition leader Andrej Babiš had called him a
"magor", a nutcase. Vobořil said he had faced pressure from big industries in gambling, tobacco and
drugs, and had received threats. Fiala kept him on as an expert adviser.

He carried on through his think tank, Racionální politiky závislostí (Rational Addiction Policies). In 2025
parliament passed a reform of the criminal code with an amendment that set limits for possessing and
growing cannabis for personal use, made growing three plants legal and abolished the offence of holding
drugs for someone else. According to Seznam Zprávy, his think tank had drafted it. When deputies of the
Mayors and Independents (STAN) submitted it, he accused them of crudely stealing it. STAN's Barbora
Urbanová called the accusation a lie. "I'm glad it passed," Vobořil said.

[The new rules](/history/czechia-home-grow-2026/) took effect on 1 January 2026: adults may grow three
plants and keep 100 grams at home. In June 2026 Babiš, now prime minister again,
[announced a tougher drug policy](/history/czechia-tightening-plan-2026/). Vobořil called it a return to
before 1989.
