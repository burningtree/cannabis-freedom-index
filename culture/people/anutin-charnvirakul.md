Anutin Charnvirakul is the politician most closely tied to Thailand's decriminalisation of cannabis.
As Thailand's minister of public health he campaigned for cannabis as medicine and as a
crop, and in June 2022 the flower was taken off the country's narcotics list. For three years
Thailand had thousands of cannabis shops and almost no rules for them.

He was born in Bangkok in 1966 into a wealthy family, studied at Hofstra University and at
Thammasat University, and ran the family construction company before entering politics in 1996.
In 2012 he succeeded his father as leader of the Bhumjaithai Party. Under Prime Minister Prayut
Chan-o-cha he became deputy prime minister and minister of public health, the post from which he
also managed the country's response to COVID-19.

## A million plants

Thailand had allowed [medical cannabis in 2019](/history/thailand-medical-2019/), the first country
in South-East Asia to do so. Anutin wanted more: cannabis as a cash crop and Thailand as a regional
centre for medical cannabis. On 8 May 2022 he announced on Facebook that the government would give
away a million cannabis plants to households, from 9 June, to encourage growing at home without a
licence.

On [9 June 2022](/history/thailand-decriminalisation-2022/) the flower left the narcotics list.
Possessing and growing it stopped being offences, and about 4,200 prisoners were released. Extracts
and products with less than 0.2 per cent THC became legal; stronger ones still needed a prescription.

## A grey market

Sales were meant to be medical only, but the dispensaries that had begun trading in April 2022
mostly asked for no prescription, and their numbers grew fast. No law regulating the new market
followed, and calls for one grew louder.

The policy outlasted Anutin's time at the health ministry, but not by long. In 2023 he moved to the
interior ministry. In June 2025 his party left the governing coalition, and within days the
health minister, Somsak Thepsuthin of the Pheu Thai Party, [reclassified the flower](/history/thailand-prescription-only-2025/)
so that buying it required a prescription.

## Prime minister

Anutin became prime minister in September 2025. A year later his cabinet approved a
[Cannabis and Hemp Act](/history/thailand-cabinet-bill-2026/) that would write the prescription rule
into law, make growers register their plantations and raise penalties. The bill still has to pass
parliament.
