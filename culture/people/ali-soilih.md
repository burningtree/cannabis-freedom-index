For three years in the 1970s the Comoros, an island state in the Indian Ocean, allowed cannabis. The man who allowed it was Ali Soilih, who seized power less than a month after independence and tried to remake an old Muslim society into a modern socialist state, with the young as his allies. Cannabis was one of the things he gave them. It went when he did.

He was born in 1937 in Majunga, in Madagascar, and educated there and in France. In the early 1960s he moved to the Comoros to work in agriculture and development, was elected to the National Assembly in 1967, and became minister of equipment and tourism. He turned against France, the colonial power, and against religion; he renounced Islam and became an atheist.

## The revolution

The Comoros declared independence from France in July 1975. On 3 August Soilih overthrew the first president, Ahmed Abdallah, with the help of the French mercenary Bob Denard, and in January 1976 became head of state. His programme mixed Maoism with Islamic philosophy.

| Soilih's reforms | |
|---|---|
| The young | Voting age lowered to fourteen; teenagers put in positions of authority |
| Cannabis | [Legalised](/history/comoros-legalisation-1975/) |
| Women | The removal of the veil encouraged |
| Custom | The costly *anda*, the grand marriage, and traditional funeral ceremonies abolished |
| Security | The Moissy, a youth militia modelled on China's Red Guards |

The legalisation of cannabis and the campaign against the veil were among the reforms meant to win over the young. In a referendum in 1977, 56.63 per cent of voters endorsed his presidency.

## The fall

The revolution made many enemies. France cut off all aid. Traditional leaders resented the loss of their customs, and the teenage Moissy behaved as a law unto themselves, raping and killing with impunity, until most Comorians saw them as a force of repression. There were four failed coups.

On 13 May 1978 Denard returned, now working for Abdallah, with about fifty mercenaries, and overthrew Soilih. On 29 May he was shot dead, according to the government while trying to escape from house arrest. He was forty-one. Abdallah reversed his decrees, renamed the country the Islamic Federal Republic of the Comoros, and [banned cannabis again](/history/comoros-ban-restored-1978/).

## Afterwards

Cannabis has stayed illegal in the Comoros since. The [penal code of 1995](/history/comoros-penal-code-1995/) names hashish and Indian hemp and punishes growing or selling them with up to ten years in prison. Soilih's social reforms left marks that are still visible, above all on the island of Anjouan.
