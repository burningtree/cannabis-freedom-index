Barbara Wootton was a sociologist and criminologist who spent her life on the boundary between social science and law. In the late 1960s she chaired the British government committee on cannabis. Its report, published in January 1969, said that moderate use did no lasting harm and that prison was the wrong answer to possession of a small amount.

Born Barbara Adam in Cambridge in 1897, she took a first in economics at Girton College at a time when a woman could not put "BA" after her name. She taught economics, worked for the Trades Union Congress and the Labour Party, sat on royal commissions and governed the BBC. In 1958 she became one of the first four women life peers, as Baroness Wootton of Abinger, and later the first woman to sit on the Woolsack as a deputy speaker of the Lords.

## The committee

The Home Office asked a sub-committee of its Advisory Committee on Drug Dependence, under Wootton, to look at cannabis and LSD. It was working quietly when, on [24 July 1967](/history/uk-soma-times-advertisement-1967/), a full-page advertisement in *The Times*, placed by the campaign group SOMA, argued that the dangers of cannabis had been exaggerated and that the law on it was socially damaging, if not unworkable. The report says that this advertisement, and the debate it set off, led the committee to drop LSD and report on cannabis first.

## The report

Its central findings, published in [January 1969](/history/uk-wootton-report-1969/), were plain:

| The committee found | |
|---|---|
| Harm | The long-term consumption of cannabis in moderate doses has no harmful effects |
| Comparison | Cannabis is less dangerous than opiates, amphetamines, barbiturates and alcohol |
| Crime | No evidence that it was causing violent crime |
| The law | Possession of a small amount should not normally be punished by imprisonment |

The Home Secretary, James Callaghan, told Parliament the committee had been over-influenced by the lobby behind "that notorious advertisement", and rejected the report. Yet the drug law his government then prepared did what Wootton had asked in one respect. The Misuse of Drugs Act, which [took effect in 1973](/history/uk-misuse-of-drugs-act-1973/), placed cannabis in Class B, below heroin, and halved the maximum sentence for possession from ten years to five.

## Afterwards

Wootton died in 1988. The argument her committee started has not settled: Britain [moved cannabis down to Class C](/history/uk-class-c-2004/) in 2004 and [back to Class B](/history/uk-class-b-2009/) in 2009, and possession of a small amount still carries a possible prison sentence.
