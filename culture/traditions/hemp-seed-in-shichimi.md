In restaurants across Japan a shaker of *shichimi tōgarashi*, "seven-flavour chili", often stands on
every table: a blend of ground red pepper and six other seasonings. In the old recipes one of the seven
is always *asa no mi*, the seed of the cannabis plant. It is sprinkled on udon and soba, on beef
bowls and pork soup, in a country whose cannabis penalties are among the strictest in the world.

## Medicine for the table

The blend was born among apothecaries. In 1625 Nakajima Tokuemon founded a shop at Yagenbori in Edo, a district of doctors and medicine sellers, and
mixed a seasoning on the model of Chinese herbal medicine: a medicine to be eaten with one's food.
Two other old houses followed, each beside a famous temple: Shichimiya by Kiyomizu-dera in Kyoto,
and Yawataya Isogorō at Zenkō-ji in Nagano, which began in 1736 when a man from the village of
Kinasa started selling the blend in the temple precincts. Kinasa was then known for its hemp and
its paper.

| The three old houses | Where | Founded |
|---|---|---|
| Yagenbori Nakajima | Edo; now beside Sensō-ji, Tokyo | 1625 |
| Shichimiya (first called Kawachiya) | Before Kiyomizu-dera, Kyoto | By the 1650s; renamed 1816 |
| Yawataya Isogorō | Zenkō-ji, Nagano | 1736 |

The recipes differ. According to the Japanese Wikipedia, the old houses share chili,
*sanshō* pepper, hemp seed and sesame, and vary the rest: poppy seed, seaweed, ginger, citrus peel.
Yawataya Isogorō labels its hemp seed 麻種, read *otane*. Street sellers once kept each ingredient
in its own jar and mixed the blend to the customer's taste, with a spoken patter describing each
ingredient that became a small street performance of its own. It can still be heard at some
Tokyo fairs.

## A seed the law leaves out

When the [Cannabis Control Act](/history/japan-cannabis-control-act-1948/) was passed in 1948,
hemp was still an ordinary crop, grown on some [25,000 farms](/history/japan-hemp-farms-1950/)
two years later. The law defines "cannabis" as the plant and its products, but excludes its seeds
and mature stalks. That clause survived the [reform of 2023](/history/japan-reform-passed-2023/)
and remains in the act, now renamed the Act on the Regulation of Cannabis Plant Cultivation. Since
[December 2024](/history/japan-use-criminalised-2024/) using cannabis has also been a crime, under
the narcotics law.

The seed is free only once it cannot grow. A licensed grower who passes seeds to anyone but
another grower must first treat them so that they cannot germinate. Untreated seed may not be
imported without the health minister's permission, and treated seed may be imported only with a
certificate that it has been treated. Growing the plant without a licence carries one to ten years
in prison. The hemp and poppy seeds in shichimi are, as a rule, treated in this way.

## Abroad

Outside Japan the seed is a problem. The Japanese Wikipedia warns that taking the domestic blend abroad,
for one's own use or as a present, can lead to confiscation or arrest. House Foods and S&B, two of
the largest makers, produce shichimi for export without hemp seed, keeping the seven by using both
black and white sesame. In 2017 Yawataya Isogorō announced that it too would begin exporting.
