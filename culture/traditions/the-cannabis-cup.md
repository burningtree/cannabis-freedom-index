The Cannabis Cup is a festival and competition run by the American magazine [*High Times*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Times). Every year since 1988, usually in November and usually in Amsterdam, judges have sampled the cannabis entered by seed companies and coffeeshops and awarded trophies for the best. It gave the new trade of cannabis breeding its prizes and its celebrities, and it fixed the names by which much of the world's cannabis is still sold.

## Founding

The Cup was founded by Steven Hager, the *High Times* editor who also [traced the word 420](/culture/language/420/) to the Waldos. It was held in Amsterdam, where since the [law of 1976](/history/netherlands-toleration-1976/) the [coffeeshops](/culture/traditions/the-coffeeshop/) could sell cannabis openly.

The first Cup was held in [November 1988](/history/netherlands-cannabis-cup-1988/).

## The contest

| Element | |
|---|---|
| Judges | A panel, from many countries, who sample the entries and vote |
| Categories | Best Indica, Best Sativa, Best Hybrid; Best Hash and Best Nederhash, for hashish made in the Netherlands; Best Imported Hash; later Best Concentrates and Best Edibles |
| Other prizes | Best New Product, Best Booth, Best Glass |
| Around it | Live music, seminars, a trade fair for cannabis products |
| Since 1997 | Induction into a Counterculture Hall of Fame: Bob Marley was the first, Louis Armstrong the second |

The winners show the history of the plant over a decade.

| Year | Overall winner |
|---|---|
| 1988 | Skunk #1 |
| 1990 | Northern Lights #5 |
| 1994 | Jack Herer |
| 1995 | White Widow |
| 1998, 1999 | Super Silver Haze |

A Hindu Kush cross took the "Master Kush" prize in 1992 and 1993 (see [kush](/culture/language/kush/)). The judging categories follow the trade's division into [indica and sativa](/culture/language/indica-and-sativa/).

## Trouble at home

In 2010 the Dutch police raided the Cup's venue. In 2014, after 27 years, *High Times* held what seemed to be its last Amsterdam Cup, citing the growing uncertainty about Dutch cannabis law, in the years of the [residents-only rule](/history/netherlands-residents-only-2012/), the new legal markets in the United States, and the after-effects of the raid. The Cup later returned to Amsterdam.

## To America

The first American Cup was held in 2010, along with a Medical Cannabis Cup in San Francisco for states that allowed medical use. After [Colorado and Washington legalised](/history/us-colorado-washington-2012/) adult use, Cups followed in Denver, Seattle, Michigan, California and elsewhere, with entries tested by independent laboratories for THC and CBD.

The move reversed the original logic. The Cup went to Amsterdam because cannabis could be sold there and not in America. Now it is held in American states with licensed markets, while the Netherlands still has only toleration.
