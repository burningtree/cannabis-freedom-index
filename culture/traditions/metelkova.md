Metelkova is an autonomous cultural zone in Ljubljana, Slovenia, formed through the occupation of former military barracks. Its clubs, visual art and activist spaces place it alongside other European experiments in collectively organised urban life. Cannabis features in published observations of its nightlife, although the evidence supports a more qualified connection than the long-running market at [Pusher Street](/culture/traditions/pusher-street/).

Calling it autonomous describes how people organise and defend a cultural space. It does not mean that Metelkova is an independent jurisdiction or that Slovenian drug law stops at its entrance.

## From barracks to cultural space

The government-backed Culture of Slovenia portal documents a network of more than two hundred organisations seeking cultural uses for the site after the Yugoslav army departed. During the night of [10–11 September 1993](/history/slovenia-metelkova-occupation-1993/), artists and activists occupied the complex to prevent demolition.

What developed was a collection of venues and initiatives rather than a conventional institution with one programme. Concerts, clubs, studios and community activity gave the site a continuing social life. Its defence of space for alternative culture is the central reason it belongs in the history of European autonomous places.

## What the cannabis evidence shows

A first-person travel report published in *Harmony* in September 2017 describes cannabis, especially hashish, being sold openly around Metelkova. That is a dated visitor's observation. It documents an association between the place and a cannabis market at that time; it does not establish the extent of the trade, the views of every organisation there or present conditions.

The same report describes artistic work and inclusive social projects. Reading its cannabis passage alongside that wider account avoids turning an entire community into a shorthand for street dealing. Neither the report nor the cultural portal establishes a special legal exemption for cannabis at Metelkova.

## Different meanings of freedom

The useful comparison with [Ruigoord](/culture/traditions/ruigoord/) and Christiania concerns space, participation and the coexistence of nightlife with cultural activity. Their legal histories and cannabis practices differ. A documented competition in Ruigoord, a decades-long market in Christiania and an observation of sales in Ljubljana are different kinds of evidence.

Slovenia's [2024 cannabis referendum](/history/slovenia-referendum-2024/) belongs to the national political history. Its personal-use question was advisory: a favourable vote did not itself create legal cultivation rights inside or outside Metelkova. Cultural autonomy and changes in national drug policy need to be followed separately.

Metelkova's inclusion here recognises a documented overlap between cannabis and European alternative urban culture. The surviving evidence is strongest on the cultural site's history and thinner on the continuity of its cannabis practices. It supports that limited account without implying that all autonomous cultural spaces are cannabis enclaves.
