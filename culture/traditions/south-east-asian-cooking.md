In mainland South-East Asia cannabis was a kitchen plant before it was a drug in anyone's law. It was a seasoning and a household medicine. Prohibition arrived from outside, in the twentieth century, and was enforced late or not at all. The "happy" restaurants that tourists know are the visible end of an older and quieter custom.

## A herb among herbs

Cannabis is thought to have come to South-East Asia from India around the sixteenth century. The Thai name, *kancha*, is the Indian [ganja](/culture/language/ganja/); the Khmer, Lao and Vietnamese names are the same word.

It had four uses: an ingredient and condiment in cooking, a medicine, a source of fibre, and a comfort for hard work. Labourers took it to relax their muscles. It was given to women to ease the pain of childbirth.

In Cambodia it stayed a common ingredient in food, and Cambodians think of it as something for older people; it is not popular with the young.

## Outlawed on paper

| Country | First ban | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| [Thailand](/country/th/) | The [Cannabis Act of 1935](/history/thailand-cannabis-act-1935/) made possessing, growing, selling and using it crimes | Followed by the [Narcotics Act of 1979](/history/thailand-narcotics-act-1979/) |
| [Cambodia](/country/kh/) | Formally by 1961, to comply with the Single Convention; then by [a drug law in 1996](/history/cambodia-ban-1996/) | Unenforced for decades. Cannabis was sold openly in markets |
| [Laos](/country/la/) | Illegal under the [Law on Narcotics](/history/laos-law-on-narcotics-2007/) | Openly sold in some businesses despite the law |
| [Vietnam](/country/vn/) | South Vietnam [condemned it in 1968](/history/vietnam-ban-1968/) and American advisers began [spraying fields in 1969](/history/vietnam-eradication-1969/) | The spraying was resented, and was called off in areas whose loyalty the government needed |

The Vietnamese case shows how the plant looked from the village. When American officials began destroying cannabis in the Mekong Delta, they were warned off the lands of the Hòa Hảo sect for fear of driving its people to the other side in the war.

## Happy pizza

In Phnom Penh, Siem Reap and Sihanoukville, restaurants with "Happy" in the name sell pizza and other dishes cooked with cannabis, or with it as a garnish. The same is found at Vang Vieng in Laos, where bars offer a "happy" menu.

They are commonest where the tourists are. They grew up in countries where cooking with cannabis was already ordinary and the ban was not enforced. The United Nations drugs office said in 2009 that cannabis cultivation had "ceased to be a major concern" in Cambodia. Since 2017 Cambodia has run [harder anti-drug campaigns](/history/cambodia-drug-campaign-2017/), and Laos applies the death penalty in some drug cases.

## Elsewhere

Cooking with cannabis is not peculiar to the region. It has its own entries where it belongs to a particular custom: the drinks and sweets of [bhang at Holi](/culture/traditions/bhang-at-holi/) in India, Morocco's *majoun* (see [kif and the sebsi](/culture/traditions/kif-and-the-sebsi/)), the hashish sweets of [Egypt](/culture/traditions/hashish-in-egypt/), and the hemp-seed soup of Silesia, [siemieniotka](/culture/traditions/siemieniotka/). What sets South-East Asia apart is that the leaf was an everyday seasoning, not a festival food or a preparation for its effect.

## Thailand comes back to it

In [2019](/history/thailand-medical-2019/) Thailand became the first country in South-East Asia to legalise medical cannabis, and in [2022](/history/thailand-decriminalisation-2022/) it took the plant off its narcotics list altogether. Thousands of shops opened.

The opening was partly reversed in [2025](/history/thailand-prescription-only-2025/), when new rules classed the flower as a controlled herb, to be sold only on prescription, including the prescription of a practitioner of traditional Thai medicine. The old use is thus the one the law now recognises, and the new recreational trade the one it is trying to close.
