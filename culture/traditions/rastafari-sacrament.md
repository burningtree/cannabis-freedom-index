For Rastafari, smoking ganja is the principal ritual of the faith. The herb is a sacrament, smoked in company with prayer and long discussion, and held to be commended by the Bible. For most of the movement's history that made every observant Rasta a criminal. Since 2015 a number of Caribbean states have written the sacrament into their law, and one Rastafari community has refused the offer.

## The herb

[Rastafari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari) arose in Jamaica in the 1930s. Its followers call the plant ganja, callie, Iley, "the herb" or "the holy herb". They find it in Genesis, the Psalms and Revelation, where the leaves of the tree of life are "for the healing of the nations", and they credit it with healing, with bringing "peace and love", and with an inward attention that lets the smoker discover the divinity within. Some hold that its smoke is an incense against the wickedness of society.

Not every Rasta smokes. Many who abstain say they have reached a state of awareness that no longer needs it, and some regard heavy smoking by others as excess. It is also drunk as tea, cooked with, and used as medicine.

## How it is smoked

The setting is a *grounding*: a gathering in a yard or commune, presided over by an elder, with drumming, chanting, hymns and poetry. Its centre is *reasoning*, an unhurried discussion of the faith and of the news in which each speaker is meant to carry the group's understanding a little further.

| Form | What it is |
|---|---|
| [Spliff](/culture/language/spliff/) | A large hand-rolled cigarette. A prayer to Jah is said while it is rolled, and it is lit only when the prayer is finished |
| Chalice | A water pipe; kinds include the kutchie, the chillum and the steamer |

The pipe goes round the circle counter-clockwise.

The largest gatherings, once called groundations and now Nyabinghi Issemblies, last from three to seven days in the open air: food, ganja and reasoning by day, drumming and dancing round fires at night. They are held on dates tied to Ethiopia and to Haile Selassie, among them his birthday on 23 July and his coronation day on 2 November.

## Where it came from

Nobody knows how cannabis entered the faith, and scholars offer three routes. One is Kumina, a religion founded in Jamaica by Bakongo people from Central Africa, in which cannabis was smoked to invite possession by ancestral spirits. A second is Hindu practice: [indentured Indians brought ganja](/history/jamaica-ganja-arrives-1855/), and the word, to the island. A third is simply that smoking was common among poor Black Jamaicans in the early twentieth century, and the first Rastas sanctified a habit they associated with the peasant life.

What is certain is that it was established by the 1940s. [Pinnacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Howell), the hilltop commune founded in 1940 by the early preacher Leonard Howell, was known for growing it.

## A sacrament against the law

Jamaica had [banned ganja in 1913](/history/jamaica-ganja-law-1913/) and in 1941 [made a year in prison mandatory](/history/jamaica-mandatory-prison-1941/). Academics have noted that the harshest laws came in years when the authorities feared unrest among the poor. Pinnacle was raided again and again from 1941, and in 1954 a raid broke it: houses were demolished and hundreds of residents arrested. By 1960 the commune had collapsed, and its scattered residents carried the faith across the island.

Rastas came to see smoking as itself a protest against the laws of "Babylon". In the United States thousands have been arrested for possession.

## Written into law

| Country | Year | What the law does |
|---|---|---|
| [Italy](/country/it/) | [2008](/history/italy-rastafari-ruling-2008/) | The highest court acquitted a Rastafarian caught with 97 grams, accepting that he smoked it as a meditative herb |
| [Jamaica](/country/jm/) | [2015](/history/jamaica-ganja-reform-2015/) | Rastafari may use ganja as a sacrament |
| [Antigua and Barbuda](/country/ag/) | [2018](/history/antigua-cannabis-act-2018/) | Religious bodies, Rastafari included, may register to grow and hold more than the ordinary limits |
| [Barbados](/country/bb/) | [2019](/history/barbados-medical-2019/) | Sacramental use at places of worship |
| [Saint Kitts and Nevis](/country/kn/) | [2023](/history/saint-kitts-freedom-of-conscience-2023/) | Registered groups may grow and use it at their places of assembly; the prime minister apologised to the community |

Not everyone wanted this. In [Saint Vincent](/country/vc/) in 2018, Rastafari representatives [told parliament to drop its religious-use bill](/history/saint-vincent-religious-bill-dropped-2018/): the state, they said, cannot grant a right to their sacrament.

Elsewhere the courts have refused. Kenya's High Court [dismissed a Rastafari petition](/history/kenya-high-court-rastafari-petition-2026/) in 2026. In [Ethiopia](/country/et/), where Rastafari settled on land the emperor gave them at [Shashamane](/history/ethiopia-shashamane-settlement-1965/), the criminal code makes no exception.
