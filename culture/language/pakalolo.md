*Pakalōlō* is a Hawaiian name for cannabis. The [Pukui–Elbert dictionary](https://wehe.hilo.hawaii.edu/?q=paka%20l%C5%8Dl%C5%8D), available through the University of Hawaiʻi's Wehewehe Wikiwiki, records it as *paka lōlō*: marijuana, pot or grass, literally “numbing tobacco.” The name refers to cannabis generally, rather than a single Hawaiian cultivar.

The vocabulary is well documented; the sources used here do not establish when the compound was coined or who first used it. Its literal meaning should not be expanded into an unsupported story about ancient cultivation or a particular religious practice.

## Two forms on the page

The Hawaiian dictionary writes the term as two words and marks the long vowels in *lōlō*. The joined form *pakalolo*, without those marks, appears in local usage. Both are useful when looking for references, but preserving the dictionary form makes the Hawaiian spelling visible.

“Numbing tobacco” is a translation of the name, not a botanical classification. Cannabis and tobacco are different plants. As with many vernacular names, one familiar substance supplies the vocabulary used to describe another.

## Hawaiian and Pidgin

[e-Hawaii](https://e-hawaii.com/pidgin/pakalolo/) also records *pakalolo* in its Pidgin vocabulary. Hawaiian and Hawaiʻi Pidgin are distinct languages; the appearance of this Hawaiian compound in Pidgin shows a word travelling between local speech communities. A dictionary entry in one does not make all usage belong exclusively to that language.

## The local word and federal law

Hawaiʻi is part of the [United States](/country/us/), whose [1970 Controlled Substances Act](/history/us-controlled-substances-act-1970/) placed marijuana in Schedule I. The local name does not define a separate legal category or confer an exemption. It identifies the plant in a regional vocabulary that continues alongside the terminology used by federal law.
