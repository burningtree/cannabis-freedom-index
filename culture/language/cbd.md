*CBD* stands for cannabidiol, one of the compounds of the cannabis plant. It is not the one that
intoxicates; that is THC. For most of its history the word was known only to chemists. Since the
late 2010s it has been printed on oils, sweets, drinks, creams and flowers sold in ordinary shops,
and it has become one of the hardest words for the law to place.

## In the laboratory

Chemists tried to isolate the active ingredients of cannabis in the nineteenth century. Cannabidiol
was studied in 1940, from wild hemp gathered in Minnesota and from Egyptian cannabis resin, and its
structure and stereochemistry were worked out in 1963, by
[Raphael Mechoulam](/culture/people/raphael-mechoulam/). *Cannabidiol* is its international
non-proprietary name, the official generic name of a medicine; *CBD* is the shorthand that went
to market.

## From hemp to the high street

In the United States the [2018 Farm Bill](/history/us-farm-bill-2018/) removed hemp, defined as
cannabis with no more than 0.3 per cent THC, together with its extracts and cannabinoids, from the
federal drug law. The same year the Food and Drug Administration approved a cannabidiol medicine,
Epidiolex, for two forms of epilepsy. CBD foods and drinks had been on sale since about 2017. But
CBD itself was never declared safe as a food ingredient, so selling it in food or as a dietary
supplement stayed illegal under federal food law, and the FDA sent dozens of warning letters to
companies selling it as a cure. Its own tests found many products mislabelled; in 2020 half of 147
products it studied contained THC.

The boom did not last. According to the Wikipedia account, the American CBD industry stalled
during the pandemic and by 2024 had collapsed, for want of investors, of an FDA ruling and of
consistent state laws.

## What the law calls it

| Place | Year | What CBD became |
|---|---|---|
| [Bulgaria](/history/bulgaria-cbd-certificate-2019/) | 2019 | A food supplement certified for open sale |
| [Taiwan](/history/taiwan-cbd-rule-2020/) | 2020 | A medicine on prescription, with almost no THC allowed |
| [Slovakia](/history/slovakia-cbd-delisted-2021/) | 2021 | No longer a psychotropic substance |
| [France](/history/france-cbd-flower-2022/) | 2022 | Hemp flower lawful to sell, by order of the Council of State |
| [China](/history/china-cbd-precursor-controls-2024/) | 2024 | A precursor chemical under state control |
| [Andorra](/history/andorra-cbd-ruling-2025/) | 2025 | A drug, if it contains any THC at all |

In the European Union, the European Commission said in 2019 that CBD counts as a "novel food",
since it was not eaten before May 1997, and so needs a safety assessment before it can be sold as
one. Sweden's Supreme Court ruled the same year that CBD oil with any THC in it falls under the
narcotics law. The Andorran case shows what that can mean: a resident caught at the border with
half a kilo of flower sold as CBD spent more than a year in prison.

## Hemp again

In November 2025 the US Congress [rewrote the hemp definition](/history/us-hemp-thc-limit-enacted-2025/)
from 12 November 2026, counting all THC and capping a finished product at 0.4 milligrams of THC
per container. Its target was the intoxicating products that had grown up under the same hemp
definition, such as [delta-8](/culture/language/delta-8/).
