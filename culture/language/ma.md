*Má*, 麻, is the Chinese word for hemp and among the oldest names for the plant recorded anywhere. For most of Chinese history it meant cloth, rope, oil and medicine. The same character also means numb, which is the nearest the classical language comes to admitting what else the plant can do.

## The character and its compounds

| Written | Read | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 麻 | má | Hemp; also numb, tingling |
| 大麻 | dàmá | "Great hemp": the hemp plant, cannabis |
| 麻木 | mámù | Numb |
| 麻醉 | mázuì | Anaesthetic, narcotic |
| 麻蕡, 麻花, 麻勃 | máfén, máhuā, mábó | Terms in the medical texts for particular parts of the male and female flowers |
| 大麻 | taima (Japanese) | Hemp; borrowed from the Chinese |

The plain word *má* is often joined with *dà*, great, because by itself it carries the suggestion of numbness.

The botanist [Li Hui-lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui-lin_Li) drew the obvious conclusion. The stupefying effect of the plant, he argued, was "commonly known from extremely early times", and the secondary meanings of the word come from the fruits and leaves, "which were used as infusions for medicinal purposes".

## In the classics

Hemp has been grown in China since the Neolithic. Cords of it were pressed into the wet clay of Yangshao pots to make their pattern. The *Book of Odes* mentions *má* many times; one early song has young women weaving it into clothing.

The classics speak of it as fibre, clothing and food, and say nothing of intoxication. The exceptions are the medical writers and the Taoists, who record that the flowers taken in quantity make a person "see demons" (see [hemp in the Taoist censer](/culture/traditions/hemp-in-the-taoist-censer/)). Archaeology fills the gap: braziers from [a 2,500-year-old cemetery in the Pamirs](/history/china-jirzankal-study-2019/) held the residue of cannabis rich in THC, burned at burials.

## Trade

In the nineteenth century Xinjiang was a large producer of hashish, with Yarkand as its centre, and exported it legally to British India, where it was sold as [charas](/culture/language/charas/). The Chinese authorities [ended the trade in 1934](/history/china-xinjiang-hashish-trade-ended-1934/).

## The law

| Year | Measure |
|---|---|
| [1985](/history/china-narcotic-1985/) | China joins the UN drug conventions and classes cannabis as a narcotic |
| [1998](/history/china-yunnan-eradication-1998/) | Yunnan sets out to make the province "cannabis free" |
| [2010](/history/china-yunnan-hemp-2010/), [2017](/history/china-heilongjiang-hemp-2017/) | Yunnan, then Heilongjiang, license industrial hemp |

The two meanings of the word have become two legal categories. Smoking *dàmá* brings ten to fifteen days of detention and a fine. Growing *má* for fibre has never been prohibited, and the seeds have been listed in the Chinese pharmacopoeia without a break.

## In Japan

Japanese took the compound as [*taima*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Japan), and with it the plant's standing as an everyday crop. The same two characters name the paper talisman that the Ise shrine sends to households, the *Jingū taima*, which began as a purification wand (see [hemp in Shinto](/culture/traditions/hemp-in-shinto/)). Japan's [Cannabis Control Act of 1948](/history/japan-cannabis-control-act-1948/) is in Japanese the *Taima* Control Act.
