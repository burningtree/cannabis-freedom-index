*Delta-8* is short for Δ8-tetrahydrocannabinol, a close chemical relative of the familiar THC,
delta-9. It is moderately less potent, but it intoxicates. The plant makes only a little of it. What made *delta-8* a word that ordinary
Americans knew was not the plant but a law.

## A chemists' compound

Roger Adams and his colleagues at the University of Illinois published a partial synthesis of the
compound in 1941, and tested it on volunteers the following year. Raphael Mechoulam achieved a
total synthesis in 1965, and in 1966 chemists at the University of Delaware described its structure
from cannabis itself. Early papers often called it delta-6-THC, a name now abandoned. For decades it
mattered mostly as an impurity to be measured in the pharmaceutical THC sold as dronabinol.

## Made from hemp

The [2018 Farm Bill](/history/us-farm-bill-2018/) defined hemp as the cannabis plant and "all
derivatives, extracts, cannabinoids, isomers" with no more than 0.3 per cent of *delta-9* THC. It
said nothing about delta-8. Producers began converting cannabidiol from legal hemp into delta-8 by
acid-catalysed cyclisation, and selling it as distillate, vape cartridges and edibles, marketed as
legal alternatives to cannabis. It reached head shops, smoke shops, dispensaries, petrol stations
and convenience stores.

## In court

The products lived in what one account called a "hazy legal landscape". In
[2022](/history/us-delta-8-appeals-ruling-2022/), in a trademark case, the Ninth Circuit treated
them as lawful hemp. Other federal courts upheld states that regulated or banned them, among them
Virginia and Wyoming. Independent tests often found more delta-9 THC in the products than the
0.3 per cent allowed, and sellers were raided and charged in several states: a shop owner in
Wisconsin faced up to fifty years in 2021. The Food and Drug Administration, which has never
evaluated delta-8 for safety, acted against companies selling it as a cure or in packets that
looked like ordinary chips and cookies.

## The end of the loophole

On 12 November 2025, to end a government shutdown, Congress passed a spending law that
[rewrote the hemp definition](/history/us-hemp-thc-limit-enacted-2025/) from 12 November 2026. It
counts all THC, not only delta-9, leaves out cannabinoids made outside the plant, such as delta-8
converted from CBD, and allows no more than 0.4 milligrams of THC per container of a finished
product. In one assessment quoted by Wikipedia, that would make products such as delta-8
"incredibly difficult, if not impossible, to sell legally".
