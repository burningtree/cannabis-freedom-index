*Zatla* is the everyday Tunisian word for cannabis. An older word, *takrouri*, is the one in the decrees of the French protectorate and is still used in some regions. Zatla is also the word at the centre of one of the Arab world's sharpest arguments over drug law, because of a statute that for twenty-five years sent every user to prison for a year.

## Two words

| Word | Use |
|---|---|
| Zatla (زطلة) | The national, colloquial word |
| Takrouri (تكروري) | Regional, and the term of the colonial law |
| Kif | Used as an equivalent in official texts of the protectorate |

## An old habit

Cannabis is thought to have come to Tunisia with the Arab conquests, between the ninth and twelfth centuries. In 1550 [Leo Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Africanus) described what he had seen in Tunis:

> They have here a compound called Lhasis, whereof whosoever eateth but one ounce falleth a laughing, disporting, and dallying, as if he were half drunken.

It was eaten, then, as a confection, as [hashish](/culture/language/hashish/) was across the Muslim world before smoking arrived.

## Sold by the state

Under the beys and then under France, takrouri was regulated, not forbidden.

| Year | Measure |
|---|---|
| [1884](/history/tunisia-hemp-decree-1884/) | A beylical decree laid down rules for "Indian hemp and takrouri" |
| Protectorate | A state monopoly sold takrouri alongside tobacco |
| [1953](/history/tunisia-takrouri-ban-1953/) | A decree of 23 April ended the sales and prohibited growing and use |

Independent Tunisia kept the ban of 1953.

## Law 52

In 1992 Tunisia passed [Law 52](/history/tunisia-law-52-1992/). It set a minimum of one year in prison, and a fine, for using or possessing any quantity of an illegal drug, and it forbade judges to reduce the sentence. Use could be proved without any drug being found: the country still relies on urine tests.

The law filled the prisons with young people convicted for zatla.

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| [2017](/history/tunisia-law-52-amended-2017/) | Parliament let judges weigh mitigating circumstances for a first or second offence and order treatment instead of prison |
| [January 2021](/history/tunisia-kef-30-year-sentences-2021/) | A court in El Kef gave three young men thirty years each for smoking in a sports stadium, the place adding twenty years to the sentence. Hundreds protested in Tunis |
| [March 2021](/history/tunisia-kef-sentences-reduced-2021/) | The sentences were cut to two years on appeal |
| [2026](/history/tunisia-law-52-committee-hearing-2026/) | A parliamentary committee heard the interior ministry on a bill to amend the law. The ministry counted about 15,253 drug cases the year before, involving some 27,000 people, nearly nine in ten of them aged 19 to 40 |
