*Mota* is the everyday word for marijuana in Mexico, and in Guatemala, El Salvador and Costa Rica as well. People say *fumar mota*, to smoke mota, and argue about *legalizar la mota*. It is informal but not obscure: El Colegio de México's dictionary of Mexican Spanish and the Royal Spanish Academy both list it.

It is also a much older Spanish word that meant something else entirely. The Academy's dictionary gives the origin as unknown, and no one has shown how the old word became the new one.

## A speck of fluff

In general Spanish, a *mota* is a small thing that does not belong. The Academy's first senses are a little knot that forms in woollen cloth and has to be picked off with tweezers, a bit of thread that sticks to clothes, a small dot or spot, and a slight flaw. In Mexico it is also the fluff on wool or cotton, a ball of cotton, and a powder puff.

The linguist Luis Fernando Lara, author of the *Diccionario del español de México* at El Colegio de México, found Spaniards using the word in the eighteenth century for the fuzz left over from making linen. The drug sense comes later. "I don't know how it arrived in Mexico," he told the news agency AFP in 2016, but from the end of the nineteenth century it meant marijuana, and it still does.

A connection between fluff and dried, crumbled leaves is easy to imagine, and so is one with a small amount. Neither has been documented, and the dictionaries do not offer one.

## The words around it

Lara was adding about fifty words from the drug world to the second edition of his dictionary, from newspapers, police reports and medical files going back decades. Those for marijuana include a small family built on *mota*.

| Word | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| *mota* | Marijuana | Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Costa Rica |
| *moto*, *motorolo*, *pacheco* | A smoker of it | Mexico |
| *churro* | A joint, named after the fried pastry | Mexico |
| *toque* | A drag | Mexico |
| *grifo* | High | Mexico |
| *tabique* | A pressed brick of it | Mexico |
| *bareta* | Marijuana, in slang | Colombia |
| *maría*, *juana* | Marijuana | Listed by the Academy as synonyms |

The Academy treats *mota*, *bareta*, *maría* and *juana* as synonyms of *marihuana*. It records the marijuana sense of *mota* only for the four countries above, and gives *bareta* as Colombian slang.

## The law's word

The law does not say *mota*. Mexican decrees and rulings speak of *marihuana*, and lately of *cannabis*; for the history of that word, see [marijuana](/culture/language/marijuana/). The Mexican dictionary's own definition of the plant ends with a plain statement: "Es una droga tradicionalmente popular; su cultivo y su uso están prohibidos." It is a traditionally popular drug; growing and using it are forbidden.

The ban in that sentence dates from [1920](/history/mexico-ban-1920/), when a federal decree removed marijuana from the medicines recognised by the sanitary code and penalised its sale and possession. It has been loosened since. In [2021 the Supreme Court](/history/mexico-supreme-court-2021/) declared the ban on adult personal use unconstitutional, but Congress never passed the law to regulate it, and there is still no legal market. The dictionary's sentence is out of date in part; the word it defines is not.
