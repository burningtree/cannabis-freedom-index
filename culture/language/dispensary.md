A *dispensary* is, first of all, a place where medicine is prepared or handed out: an institution
that gives out medical supplies and advice, like the charitable dispensaries that treated the poor
in eighteenth-century Britain, or, in an older sense, the pharmacy of a hospital, which sells
nothing. In the United States the word now also means a shop that sells cannabis, to patients
and, where the law allows it, to any adult. Its slang form is *dispo*.

## Medicine as the way in

The word came to cannabis through the medical marijuana movement of the 1990s, because medicine was
the only door the law left open. In San Francisco, [Dennis Peron](/culture/people/dennis-peron/)
had watched cannabis ease the nausea and pain of his partner, Jonathan West, before West died of AIDS. With "Brownie Mary" Rathbun and other activists he turned the informal help given to sick
friends into the Cannabis Buyers Club, which sold to people with AIDS, cancer and other illnesses,
openly and against both state and federal law. It had no licence; there was none to be had.

Ed Rosenthal's photographs of 1994 show what it was like inside: paper cranes hanging from the
ceiling, the painted sign of Peron's old café, The Island, a crowded counter, and a handwritten
menu of grades, tinctures, edibles and even cuttings to grow at home. People came to talk as well
as to buy. The early clubs were meeting places and support networks for the sick at least as much
as they were shops, and they called their customers patients and their product medicine.

In November 1996 California voters passed [Proposition 215](/history/us-california-medical-1996/),
which Peron had helped write, protecting patients with a doctor's recommendation who possessed or
grew cannabis. It did not set up a system of shops, but the dispensaries that grew up under it
became the first openly tolerated cannabis outlets in the country, and their medical name stuck.

The medical name did not protect them from federal law. In
[2001](/history/us-oakland-cooperative-2001/) the Supreme Court ruled against the Oakland Cannabis
Buyers' Cooperative, holding that federal drug law leaves no room for a defence of medical
necessity.

## From clinic to shop

When Colorado [opened the first licensed shops](/history/us-colorado-first-shops-2014/) for adults
on 1 January 2014, the name stretched to cover them, though by then many sold to anyone over 21 and
had nothing clinical about them: in the United States, outlets called
dispensaries now sell for medical use, for adult use or for both. The
largest, opened by Planet 13 in Las Vegas, covers 112,000 square feet.

Other countries chose other words for the same counter, and the word often says what the law
allows.

| Where | What it is called | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Dispensary | A licensed shop, medical or adult-use |
| Canada | Cannabis store | A shop licensed by the province, private or state-owned |
| Netherlands | [Coffeeshop](/culture/traditions/the-coffeeshop/) | A café allowed to sell small amounts under a policy of tolerance |
| Spain, Malta, Uruguay, Germany | [Cannabis social club](/culture/traditions/cannabis-social-clubs/) | A non-profit association that grows for its members and does not sell |
| India | Bhang shop | A licensed seller of [bhang](/culture/language/bhang/) |

## Exported with the trade

The word has travelled with the legal market. In Thailand, cannabis
[dispensaries began trading](/history/thailand-dispensaries-open-2022/) in April 2022, two months
before the flower was taken off the narcotics list. In Barbados, Island Therapeutics opened in
[June 2025](/history/barbados-first-dispensary-2025/) as the first licensed medicinal cannabis
dispensary, more than five years after the law that allowed it. In both, the American word was
the one chosen.
